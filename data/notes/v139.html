The deepest practical takeaway

I think there are really two powerful self-checks from this satsang.

The first comes from Verse 138:

How shock-absorbent am I becoming?

Not:

“How do I behave when everything goes well?”

Anyone can be peaceful when:

  • everyone respects them,
  • plans work,
  • health is good,
  • seva goes their way,
  • relationships are smooth.

The better test is:

What happens when the road becomes rough?

Do I immediately:

react?

Or:

collapse into self-pity?

Or can I gradually:

respond?

Gurudev's teaching isn't:

“Make sure nobody hurts you.”

It's much stronger:

Develop yourself so that every contact doesn't hurt you.

Find the bruise within rather than labeling the whole world painful.

And the second self-check comes from Verse 139:

How much Moh is actually reducing?

Because after studying this much Atmasiddhi, we could know an enormous amount about spirituality.

But Gurudev keeps pulling the measurement away from information:

Are desires weakening?
Does respect matter less?
Does criticism disturb me less?
Are likes/dislikes weakening?
Is body-identification reducing?
Is the world losing its power to dictate my happiness?

That is much harder to fake.

And I think the progression from yesterday's Thermometer Verse into today's Jnanidasha is perfect:

Verse 138 asks:
Are the right virtues awakening?

Then Verse 139 asks:

And as those virtues awaken, is Moh becoming quieter?

Because ultimately:

Spiritual progress isn't measured by how much more I can say about the soul.

It is measured by:

how much less delusion controls the soul.

And that is why Gurudev finds this verse so beautiful: Param Krupalu Dev explains Jnanidasha—the state of Knowledge—without needing to praise knowledge at all.

He simply asks:

What happened to Moh?

That is the test.
