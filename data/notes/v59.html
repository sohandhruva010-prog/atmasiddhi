1) Where we are in the Atmasiddhi flow

  • Up to Verse 58: the Guru answers the disciple’s doubts about the existence of the soul using logic + examples (not scripture quotations).
  • Verse 59 is the disciple’s response:
    • He accepts the Guru’s clarification.
    • He does something very important: he pauses to contemplate (antar karye vichar).
    • Only after that, he declares: “It seems possible.” (sambhav)

This verse is basically:

“I am not reacting emotionally. I reflected deeply, and now I accept the possibility of soul’s existence.”

2) Verse 59 — Word-by-word meaning (from transcript)

Key phrase:

“Atmana astitvana Aape kahya prakar, antar karye vichar, sambhav teno thay chhe.”

A) “Atmana astitvana”

  • Atma = soul
  • Astitva = existence

So: “regarding the existence of the soul”

B) “Aape kahya prakar”

  • Aape = “You” in the highest respectful form (not casual “tu”, not even “tamey”)
  • This matters because it shows:
    • the disciple is not debating as equals
    • he is speaking as a disciple to a Guru, with reverence and humility

Meaning:

“In the way You explained (with proofs/logic/examples)…”

C) “Antar karye vichar”

  • Antar = within / internally
  • Vichar = reflection / contemplation

Meaning:

“After reflecting deeply within…”

D) “Sambhav teno thay chhe”

  • Sambhav = possible / plausible / acceptable Meaning:

“It seems possible (and acceptable) that the soul exists.”

✅ Short meaning (as teacher says):

By internally contemplating over Your clarification about the soul’s existence, it seems possible.

3) Why this verse is NOT a small statement — it shows the disciple’s qualities

The teacher highlights that Verse 59 reveals traits of a “true disciple”:

Trait 1: Contemplation (not just listening)

He didn’t just say “okay cool, next topic.”

He took time, reflected, and checked:

  • “Am I convinced?”
  • Not “How will I argue with others?”
  • Not “How can I defeat the Guru next time?”

Trait 2: Humility (no ego-defense)

A common ego pattern is:

  • “I was saying it that way because…”
  • “My intention was…”
  • “That’s why I argued that way…”

But this disciple does:

  • “I contemplated and I saw where my thinking was wrong.”

That’s humility + honesty.

Trait 3: Honesty (the word “sambhav” is huge)

He could have used dramatic praise:

  • “You proved it completely, you are God, no doubts ever.”

Instead he says:

“It seems possible.”

That reveals intellectual integrity:

  • My doubt was intellectual
  • My conviction is intellectual
  • I have not yet experienced the soul directly

So:

✅ intellectual conviction ≠ Self-realisation

He is honest about where he is.

4) What exactly changed in the disciple’s understanding

The teacher explains what he is now convinced about:

A) Distinction between “external I” and the inner animator

  • External manifestation = body (visible, sense-perceptible, material)
  • Inner entity = soul (invisible, beyond senses, spiritual)

He is now convinced:

  • body and soul are not the same thing
  • the body is like the “outer expression”
  • something else animates it

B) Why he couldn’t accept before (and why he accepts now)

Before:

  • He trusted only what is seen by senses
  • Soul cannot be “seen,” so he denied it

Now:

  • He sees the logical force of:
    • death
    • knowingness
    • examples that separate “matter” from “knower”

6) The BIG TEACHING of this video: Contemplation creates lasting impact

A) The stone vs cloth analogy (very important)

Teacher’s point:

If you only listen (no contemplation)

It’s like water on stone:

  • stone gets wet briefly
  • water runs off
  • dries fast

Meaning: you feel convinced in the moment… then it fades.

If you listen + contemplate

It’s like water on cloth:

  • cloth absorbs
  • water penetrates
  • stays longer

Meaning: teaching sinks into the heart.

Core takeaway:

Without contemplation, even the best discourse leaves no lasting transformation.

B) Another warning example: “orator spell”

Sometimes in a powerful lecture:

  • you nod
  • you raise your hand in agreement
  • you feel “yes yes yes”

Then later alone:

  • “Wait… does this even make sense?”

Teacher says:

  • don’t declare too quickly
  • take time, contemplate
  • declare only after internal digestion

This is the major narrative section. It’s basically:

“This same conversion-by-logic happened before too.”

Context

  • Source: Raipaseni Sutra, one of the Jain Agams.
  • Characters:

10) King Pardeshi’s atheistic arguments + Keshiswami’s counters (with examples)

Argument 1: “My grandfather never returned from hell”

King says:

  • If soul + hell exist, my irreligious grandfather should have come back to warn me.
  • He never came → so no soul/hell.

✅ Keshiswami’s counter-example: Criminal punishment

  • If someone commits adultery with the king’s queen, king would execute him immediately.
  • Would king allow him to go warn family? No.

So:

  • A being in hell is under extreme bondage and suffering.
  • Not free to travel, warn, or visit.

Four reasons beings in hell cannot come:

1. severe pain/agony

2. protectors/guards of hell don’t allow freedom

3. karmas not exhausted → cannot leave state

4. cannot even end life to escape

Conclusion:

No visit ≠ no soul. It only shows constraint.

Argument 2: “My grandmother never returned from heaven”

King says:

  • My grandmother was religious → should be in heaven.
  • She loved me → should come and persuade me.
  • She didn’t → so no heaven/soul.

✅ Counter-example: Temple vs toilet

  • If you are dressed to go to temple and someone invites you to sit in toilet:
    • You refuse because it’s dirty/disgusting.

Similarly:

  • heavenly beings are absorbed in heavenly pleasures
  • human realm is unattractive/impure to them

Four reasons celestial beings don’t come:

1. engrossed in heavenly pleasures, no interest in human pleasures

2. cut off from human relationships, absorbed in new celestial relations

3. long lifespan → “I’ll go later” but humans die quickly

4. cannot tolerate smell/impurity of mortal world

Conclusion:

No visit ≠ no heaven/soul. It shows disinterest + mismatch of realms.

Argument 3: “Thief died in sealed iron box, no hole, so soul didn’t leave”

King says:

  • If soul left, box should show a hole or break.
  • No hole → soul doesn’t exist.

✅ Counter-example: Light & sound

  • Light passes through glass without leaving holes.
  • Sound can come out of an enclosed room even if “no hole is visible” in the way you imagine.

Key principle used:

  • Solid matter needs physical passage.
  • Non-solid/subtle entities can pass through without “holes” like material objects.

Conclusion:

Soul is formless/subtle, not a solid body — so your “hole logic” is wrong category reasoning.

Argument 4: “After days, body decomposed with worms; no entry path; so worms were born from body (soul-body one)”

King’s inference:

  • No external entry → worms generated from body itself.

✅ Counter-example: Heat entering iron ball

  • Heat permeates iron even if there are no holes.
  • Iron becomes red hot without visible openings.

Similarly:

  • subtle souls can enter and take birth as worms.

Conclusion:

The presence of worms doesn’t prove “soul = body.”

It shows subtle entry/interaction not captured by crude observation.

Argument 5: “Weight before/after death is same, so soul didn’t leave”

King says:

  • If soul left, weight should reduce.

✅ Counter-example: Air in leather bag

  • Empty bag vs bag filled with air can show negligible weight difference.
  • Yet air is clearly present.

Then he adds metaphysics:

  • Weight belongs to matter (touch-based qualities)
  • Soul is formless, non-material → not weighable

Conclusion:

Same weight ≠ no soul. Weight is not the right test for a formless substance.

Argument 6: “I cut the dead body into pieces, didn’t find soul, so no soul”

King claims:

  • Dissection didn’t reveal soul → soul not present.

✅ Counter-story: Fire in Arni wood

  • Fire exists in wood, but you can’t “see fire” by chopping into pieces.
  • Fire is subtle; it manifests by proper method (rubbing to kindle)

Lesson:

Wrong method can’t disprove subtle reality.

Conclusion:

Soul is subtle — cannot be “seen” by gross cutting. You need correct inference/method.

Final proof question: “How can you be sure soul exists?”

✅ Leaves moving / air inference

  • Air is invisible, yet you infer it from effects (leaves moving).

Similarly:

  • life-activities and movement show an animating principle.

Conclusion:

You infer soul from effects, not direct sense sight.
