He recaps the entire “true seeker” (ātmārthī) section:

  • Verses 34–36 – What lives in the heart & head of a true seeker before Sadguru:
    • 34: What he wants in a Sadguru (Self-realised, saintly).
    • 35: How he wants to surrender to a living Sadguru (pratyaksh Sadguru).
    • 36: His conviction that the supreme path is one in all times; any practice that leads to it is worth accepting/respecting.
  • Verse 37 – Once he meets Sadguru: “Em vichārī antare, shodhe Sadguru yog;
    Kaam ek atmārthnu, bījo nahi mannrog.”
    • He seeks Sadguru and has only one aim – ātmārth (spiritual welfare).
  • Verse 38 – What worthiness looks like:
    • Kashayni upshānttā – passions calmed,
    • Matra moksh abhilāsh – only desire for liberation,
    • Bhavey khed – grief of rebirths,
    • Pranidayā – compassion for all beings. → This is ātmārth nivās, the “house” fit for Self-realisation.
  • Verse 39 – If that state is not there:
    • You cannot benefit fully from Sadguru association (lahey nahi jog),
    • You do not attain the path of liberation,
    • Your inner disease (antar rog – delusion) is not cured.
  • Verse 40 – If worthiness is there:
    • Sadgurubodh suhay – His teachings become effective,
    • From that, suvicharna – right thinking – arises,
    • And from that, sukhday – bliss of the soul’s nature – starts to dawn.

Now comes Verse 41, which he calls:

“The whole path of liberation in one verse.”

2. What Verse 41 is doing

He sets up the verse’s purpose:

Having explained how right thinking (suvicharna) arises in a worthy seeker (from worthiness + Sadguru association → nimit & upādān), now Param Krupalu Dev explains: What is the fruit of that right thinking? What happens after right thinking develops?

He says:

  • Verses 39–40 got us to:
    • Worthiness + association of Sadguru → right thinking.
  • Verse 41 now answers: “From right thinking → what all happens up to Nirvan?”

He also adds:

  • In one verse, Param Krupalu Dev compresses:
    • The entire 14 guṇasthānak (stages of spiritual development) journey:
      • From 1st stage (worldly, deluded)
      • To 14th (complete liberation).

He maps:

  • First line of verse 41 → from right thinking to Self-realisation (roughly up to 4th guṇasthān).
  • Second line → from Self-realisation to complete liberation, Nirvan (up to 14th guṇasthān).

3. The verse: “Jya pragate suvicharna, tya pragate nij jnān…”

He breaks it word-by-word.

3.1 “Jya pragate suvicharna…”

  • Jya – where / when.
  • Pragate – arises, manifests, appears.
  • Suvicharna – right thinking:
    • Thoughts born from Sadguru’s teachings,
    • Not from delusion.

So: “Where (or when) right thinking appears…”

We’re talking about:

  • The point at which Sadgurubodh suhay (verse 40) has happened,
  • And the seeker’s thinking is no longer:
    • “I = body, mine = relatives & objects,”
    • But aligned with truth and Guru’s insight.

3.2 “Tya pragate nij jnān”

  • Tya – there / then.
  • Nij jnān – knowledge of the Self:
    • Self-realisation,
    • Enlightenment (SR),
    • Samkit – right faith,
    • Direct experience of the Self.

So: “Where right thinking arises, there arises Self-realisation.”

Sequence so far:

1. Worthiness (verse 38),

2. Sadgurubodh suhay (verse 40),

3. Suvicharna (verse 40),

4. Nij jnān (verse 41).

He emphasises:

“Right thinking is the soul’s wake-up call. Once the soul stirs from sleep, Self-realisation arises.”

4. “Je jnāne kshay moh thai…” – What nij jnān does to delusion

Second half:

“Je jnāne kshay moh thai, pamey pad nirvan.”
  • Je jnāne – by that very knowledge (Self-realisation),
  • Kshay moh thai – delusion (moh) gets destroyed,
  • Pamey pad Nirvan – one attains the state (pad) of Nirvan:
    • Complete liberation,
    • State beyond birth and death.

He nuances:

  • At nij jnān:
    • Delusion is not fully destroyed yet.
    • What gets destroyed at Self-realisation is:
      • Darshan moh – faith-deluding karma.
    • What remains:
      • Charitra moh – conduct-deluding karma.

So:

  • At Self-realisation:
    • You are liberated from:
      • Wrong belief / ignorance (faith-delusion),
    • But still not from:
      • All inner tendencies and behaviours, which fall under conduct delusion.

He explains:

  • Darshan moh (faith-delusion) gone → nij jnān arises.
  • Charitra moh (conduct-delusion) remains → still pulls you away from constant abidance.

Then:

  • “Je jnāne kshay moh thai…” means:
    • By that same knowledge, sustained and deepened,
    • Eventually all delusion is annihilated:
      • Both faith-delusion and conduct-delusion.

And then:

“Pamey pad Nirvan” – “He attains the state of complete liberation.”

He distinguishes:

  • Already at Self-realisation:
    • There is partial liberation:
      • Liberation from ignorance/wrong belief.
  • At Nirvan:
    • There is complete liberation:
      • No karmas,
      • No body, mind, senses,
      • Only the pure Soul in its fullness.

5. Why is the soul wandering? The cycle of delusion and thinking

He sets up a practical question:

“What makes the worldly soul wander in cycles of birth and death?”

From the verse’s angle:

1. Absence of right thinking (no suvicharna).

2. Because of moh (delusion), all thinking arises from:

  • “I am the body,”
  • “My happiness is in objects & relationships.”

He describes the vicious cycle:

  • Delusion produces thoughts:
    • Those thoughts are:
      • Of I & mine, in a wrong sense.
  • Thinking based on delusion:
    • Generates feelings (love/attachment or hate/aversion),
    • Those feelings strengthen wrong beliefs:
      • “I do get happiness from this person / thing / title.”
  • Stronger beliefs → stronger delusion → next round of deluded thinking.

So:

“Delusion → wrong thinking → deeper delusion → more wrong thinking…”

If someone is very smart, and that smartness is entirely in worldly directions:

“More intelligent, more refined his deluded thinking, more dense becomes his delusion.”

He points out:

  • Thinking less doesn’t automatically reduce delusion (it’s already there),
  • Thinking more but wrongly just thickens it.

So it looks like there is no way out of this loop.

He answers:

“There is a way: Sadguru association + worthiness + teachings that penetrate → right thinking.”

Once Sadguru’s teachings penetrate:

  • New layer emerges between:
    • Delusion and
    • Thoughts.
  • Now thoughts start arising from:
    • Sadguru’s bodh, not from raw delusion.

Then:

“From this layer of teachings, right thought arises and begins to work on delusion.”

So:

  • Earlier:
    • Delusion → thinking → more delusion.
  • Now:
    • Sadguru’s teaching → right thinking → cutting delusion.

6. Two types of delusion: Darshan moh & Charitra moh

He clearly divides delusion into two:

1. Darshan moh – faith-delusion:

  • Delusion related to:
    • Wrong beliefs about:
      • Self,
      • Sadguru,
      • Path,
      • What brings true happiness.
  • When this is destroyed:
    • Samkit / nij jnān / Self-realisation arises.

2. Charitra moh – conduct-delusion:

  • Delusion that:
    • Hinders living in constant abidance,
    • Pulls into wrong action even after knowing what’s right.

Map:

  • Destroy Darshan moh → Self-realisation.
  • Destroy Charitra moh → Nirvan (after stages of restraint & ladders).

So:

  • Verse 41 line 1:
    • “Where right thinking arises, there arises Self-realisation” → Darshan moh destroyed.
  • Line 2:
    • “By that knowledge delusion is destroyed, and Nirvan is attained” → Charitra moh eventually destroyed.

7. The role of Sadguru’s teachings in this phase

He reiterates:

  • Sadguru’s teachings may last only one hour, but:
    • Reflecting on them can fill 24 hours (even your dreams).

By:

  • Constantly reflecting on His teachings:
    • Your equations change:
      • Happiness now = within, not outside.
    • Your belief system changes:
      • Satguru’s view = truth;
      • Your old view = mistake.

Therefore:

  • Your interest in spirituality & longing for Self-realisation increases.

He uses a scale image:

  • On one side:
    • Interest in spirituality goes up.
  • On the other side:
    • Darshan moh goes down.

It’s common sense:

“If faith increases, faith-delusion decreases.”

You don’t even need technical jargon to understand:

  • More faith in:
    • Sadguru,
    • Self,
    • Path,
  • Means less confusion, less doubt, less wrong faith.

When Darshan moh is fully gone:

“Completely destroyed – you attain Self-realisation.”

8. The experience of Self-realisation – nectar and imprint

He describes nij jnān experience:

  • It’s not just emptiness:
    • Though some may describe it as “void” or “no-thought.”
  • It is: “A blissful taste – like nectar.”

That bliss: Leaves a deep imprint in the mind.

He gives a vivid analogy:

  • Imagine:
    • After a hot Palitana yatra in summer, descending in the heat,
    • You reach the bottom, very thirsty.
    • You drink ice-cold lemonade – tangy, sweet, cooling.
  • That sensation is so soothing, it gets deeply imprinted.
  • Later:
    • Even in winter, you may crave lemonade,
    • Because your mind remembers: “That was amazing.”

Similarly:

  • During Self-realisation:
    • You are actually beyond mind & senses,
    • But the mind later registers: “That was the best state ever.”
  • Now the mind:
    • Longs for that state again and again:
      • “I want to go back into that no-mind, still, blissful state.”

From this: Begins the longing for unbroken, unobstructed, ceaseless abidance in the Self.

This leads into:

  • Samadhi – trance-like abidance:
    • Savikalp samadhi – with thought,
    • Nirvikalp samadhi – beyond thought.

Post-enlightenment:

  • You no longer have to push yourself into meditation:
    • “Come on, it’s 4am, sit.”
  • Rather:
    • You are pulled into the Self, like gravity.

9. Working on Charitra moh – from Self-realisation to Nirvan

After Darshan moh is gone:

  • Self-realisation has occurred (4th guṇasthān).
  • But Charitra moh remains:
    • It stops you from:
      • Staying steadily in inner abidance,
      • Being uniformly pure in conduct.

So:

  • Now you need:
    • Sadguru’s further guidance (Gurugam – special personal instructions),
    • On how to increase constant awareness & alertness.

You get:

  • Keys on:
    • How to keep awareness unbroken,
    • How not to let mind slip away.

By practising:

  • Awareness + alertness → Charitra moh becomes:
    • Weaker and weaker, until:
      • Fully destroyed at higher stages.

He briefly touches guṇasthān ladder (for those who know it):

  • From 4th (Self-realised),
  • Through 5th, 6th,
  • Into 7th – apramatta saiyam (vigilant restraint):
    • “Apramatta” – non-indolent, non-lazy,
    • “Saiyam” – restraint, not outer renunciation:
      • Focus held within,
      • Mind rarely steps out.

Then:

  • Climbing kshapak shreni (annihilation ladder):
    • 8th → 9th → 10th guṇasthān,
    • At the last moment of the 10th stage:
      • Charitra moh is fully destroyed.

After that:

  • The other three ghāti karmas (knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, obstacle-causing):
    • Are destroyed within 48 minutes.
  • Because:
    • Once Delusion is gone, they voluntarily fall off,
    • They no longer have a base.

Then:

  • At 13th stage – Kevaljnān (omniscience) manifests.
  • Remaining aghāti karmas (body-related):
    • Also get exhausted/destroyed.
  • Finally: The soul attains Nirvan – the end of becoming (but not the end of being).

He simplifies:

“If you don’t want all technical levels, just remember:

  • Right thinking → Self-realisation,
  • That knowledge → destroys delusion,
  • Then, in time, other karmas fall away,
  • And Nirvan – complete liberation – is attained.”

10. The whole path in one verse

He highlights three aspects:

1. Whole path – from:

  • Stage 1 (no worthiness) → To Stage 14 (Nirvan) All present in one verse.

2. Whole sequence – not just “eventually you get liberation”, but:

  • Step → step → step:
    • Right thinking → Self-realisation → destruction of faith-delusion → progressive destruction of conduct-delusion & other karmas → Nirvan.

3. Exact guidance – you know:

  • Where you are,
  • What needs to be done next,
  • How liberation follows naturally if sequence is honoured.

He summarises beautifully:

“Right thinking is the soul’s wake-up call. Once the soul wakes up, right conduct will follow. With right thought and conduct, delusion is destroyed. In its pristine beauty and radiant glory, Enlightenment occurs; Liberation follows.”

Those last four words are his compressed version of verse 41:

Enlightenment occurs; liberation follows.
