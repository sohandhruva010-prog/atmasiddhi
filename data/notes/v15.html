Extensive Study Notes ( Swachhand — the biggest hindrance )

Built from your Video 15 transcript. Designed to read smoothly and print as a PDF handout.

1) Where Verse 15 sits in the flow

  • 9–10: Need of Guru + five marks of a Sadguru.
  • 11–12: Why living Sadguru’s upkār matters; only through His teaching does devotion to Jina become fruitful. 13–14: Crisis handling: (13) no Guru → scripture as support; (14) have Guru but no constant company → study His assigned texts deeply, daily, dropping opinions.
  • 15–18: Four-verse unit on destroying swachhand (self-will). Verse 15 defines and proves why ending swachhand is decisive for liberation.

2) Verse 15 — short meaning (as conveyed)

“If the soul stops swachhand (self-will), only then it will certainly attain mokṣa; infinitely many have done so—said by the faultless Jina.”

“To” (only then) → necessary condition. “Avaśya” (certainly) → sufficient assurance: remove this one poison and the fruit is sure.

  • Authority: Jina nirdosh (faultless Conqueror) is quoted.

Not just Self-realisation (target); the verse promises liberation (goal) when swachhand is annihilated.

3) What is swachhand? (five clear lenses)

1) Doing what I want (not what is right/assigned).

2) Living by moods: decisions swing with inner weather.

3) Intending what I enjoy rather than what purifies.

4) Attachment to my viewpoints/attitudes—I must prefer my lens.

5) Self-willed behaviour/attitude: stubborn insistence, even if externally obeying.

Why it’s so strong: It is an age-old habit (time-immemorial). Because it is appealing, undoing it feels like swimming upstream; hence the verse’s emphatic “only then.”

4) Why swachhand blocks liberation

  • Breeds ego, pride, self-conceit, arrogance, and mood-attachment. Produces the dark phase of repeated failure → frustration → giving up.
  • Lowers worthiness (pātratā): the Guru cannot begin His work—no receptivity, no “space” to receive love, grace, or command.
  • Turns religion into mechanical ritualism or dry intellectualism—lots of activity, zero spiritual welfare.

Any religious act done with swachhand does not contribute to liberation.

5) The logic of the line

  • “Roke jīva to … pāmey avaśya mokṣa.” Stop swachhand → certain liberation.
  • “Pāmyā em anant chhe — bhakhyū Jina nirdosh.” Infinitely many have attained by this very renunciation; this is the Jina’s statement.

6) Recognising swachhand in disguise

  • “I’m just being frank.” (Verse 8’s yogya—appropriateness—is missing.)
  • Selective obedience: “I’ll do nityakram when I feel like it—and then nicely.”
  • Private reservation under outer compliance: I obeyed, but I should have been allowed my way.
  • Study without application or without dropping opinions.

7) Remedy: emptying the mind for receptivity

  • Love the living Sadguru enough to notice swachhand and confess it within.
  • Practise opinion-renunciation first (before external renunciations).
  • Restore nitya (daily) rhythms regardless of mood; lovers are regular.
  • Prefer ājñā over options; stop disguised guidance.

8) Micro-practices (start today)

A. Daily (12–15 min): 1) 2-minute stillness: remember the Guru; intend to notice self-will. 2) Swachhand spot (3 min): write one moment today when mood/viewpoint tried to rule. 3) Counter-move (3 min): one tiny obedience that opposes that mood. 4) Nityakram pledge (4–7 min): keep a minimum daily anchor (reading, contemplation, inner turning) regardless of mood.

B. Weekly (20–30 min): - Audit: Which of the five lenses showed up most? What concrete pattern change will you test next week?

C. Relationship drill: Before “being frank,” check yogya: Is it true? helpful? timely? my role? If not all yes, keep silent.

9) Working definition to use across the text

Swachhand = self-willed behaviour/attitude that privileges my preference over right view/ ājñā, often hiding under sincerity, frankness, or zeal.

10) One-page recap

  • Claim: Ending swachhand is the turning key—only then → certainly mokṣa.
  • Why hard: it is ancient, appealing habit; feels like going against the grain.
  • Guru’s role: His love exposes swachhand; receptivity lets Him work.
  • Your part now: daily anchors, opinion-drop, mood-transcending obedience.

11) Reflection prompts

1) Which of the five faces of swachhand is most active in me right now?

2) What single ājñā will I honour despite mood for the next 24 hours?

3) What truth will I not say today because it fails the yogya test?
