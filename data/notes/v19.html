Verse 19 — “Je Sadguru updeśthī, pāmyo kevaljñān; Guru rahyā chhadmasth pan, vinay karey Bhagwān.”
Big idea in one line

Even if a disciple attains omniscience (kevaljñān) through the True Guru’s guidance, he continues to revere his Guru—who may still be non-omniscient (chhadmasth). That is the zenith of humility (vinay).

How Verse 19 fits the flow (15 → 18 → 19–20)
  • 15–17: showed swachhand (self-will) as the great blocker and the Guru’s refuge as its remedy, culminating in right faith (samkit).
  • 18: gave an unfailing method: inner enemies led by pride (mān) cannot be destroyed by one’s own efforts; they fall “with slight effort” in the Sadguru’s refuge.
  • 19–20: turn to the path of humility (vinay)—what humility looks like in a worthy disciple (19) and why Jinas praise this path (20).

Verse 19, then, depicts humility’s peak: the disciple, now Bhagwān (omniscient), still bows—in heart and acknowledgement—to the Sadguru whose teachings carried him there.

Verse 19 unpacked (word-by-word the teacher used)
  • Je … updeśthī — “He who, by a Sadguru’s teaching…”
  • Pāmyo kevaljñān — “…attained omniscience.”
  • Guru rahyā chhadmasth — The Guru remains non-omniscient (pre-kevali;
    “covered,” not yet at the 13th stage).
  • Vinay karey Bhagwān — “Even then the now-omniscient disciple continues to show reverence to his Guru.”

The teacher clarified chhadmasth: literally “covered state,” i.e., any stage before omniscience (1–12).

The spiritual arc the teacher highlighted (9–18 → 19–20)

From verse 9 onward the text has stressed the indispensability of a Sadguru; verse 18 sealed the “alp prayāse” promise. Now 19–20 display the fruit: humility—the disciple’s unshakeable gratitude to his source.

Why this is the “zenith” of humility
  • The disciple could think, “I surpassed my Guru.” Instead, he never forgets the debt: “All this is because of Him.”
  • The teacher called this spiritual maturity (beyond just “humility”): acknowledging one’s source is wisdom; grabbing credit is immaturity.

Practical read: If God (a Kevalī) keeps crediting his Guru, what about us in ignorance? Our reverence should be greater, not less.

A clear three-stage model (the teacher’s board example)
  • Stage 1 — Ignorance (1st guṇasthān): A true seeker bows because the Guru is higher (min 4th). Basic manners → reverence start here.
  • Stage 4 — Enlightened (4th guṇasthān): The disciple reaches the Guru’s level; reverence increases, not decreases—the present experience only deepens gratitude.
  • Stage 13 — Omniscience: No “formalities” remain (the Omniscient is beyond ritual gestures), yet he continues to acknowledge: “It is because of Him.” That acknowledgement itself is vinay at 13.

Analogy the teacher used: The billionaire never forgets the millionaire mentor who taught him the craft—even after surpassing him.

Stories the teacher gave (scriptural illustrations)

1) Gautamswāmī and the Omniscients

Gautamswāmī initiated many who attained omniscience before him (his deep attachment to Mahāvīra delayed his own kevaljñān). In the samavasaraṇ (assembly), he headed to the monks’ section, expecting his disciples to follow—but Mahāvīra corrected him: “Do not disrespect the Omniscients.” Point: A Guru still chhadmasth, disciples omniscient—and yet humility all around; no pride, no assertion of status. (This perfectly embodies Verse 19.)

2) Sādhvī Mrugāvatī and Guruni Chandanbālā

Mrugāvatī (disciple/aunt) returned after sunset (a code-breach) and received reprimand from Guruni Chandanbālā; she accepted without excuse, fell into deep remorse, and attained omniscience. That night, seeing (with inner light, not eyes) a snake near Chandanbālā’s hand, she gently moved it aside. Chandanbālā realised: “She has attained omniscience.” Mrugāvatī, now Omniscient, still served her Guruni with vinay; Chandanbālā, moved, later attained omniscience too. Point: Even after kevaljñān, the disciple’s reverence continues—Verse 19, lived.

What counts as “showing respect” at each stage (the teacher’s nuance)
  • At 1: Basic manners (gentle words/gestures), inward deference, obedience.
  • At 4: More reverence—experience has confirmed the Guru’s grace.
  • At 13: No outer “formalities”; constant acknowledgement of the Guru as source of insight is the respect.
The teacher’s “tests of humility” (how Gurus refine disciples)

A Guru may challenge you—sometimes even call a right fact “wrong”—to check humility:

  • Do you prize being factually right, or do you prize your Guru?
  • Do you insist, prove, brandish evidence—or can you remain respectful while clarifying? These tests reveal whether you’ve attained vyavahār samkit moving toward śuddh samkit—or whether ego still rules. (Verse 20 will add: only few fortunate truly grasp the path of vinay.)
Why vinay accelerates progress

The virtue of reverence for a Sadguru:

  • Transforms quickly (“rapid rise”)—from worthiness → Self-realisation → even omniscience.
  • Turns ego-traits into virtues when tuned to the Guru (steadfastness vs stubbornness).
  • Keeps you safe from swachhand and pride, the very inner enemies flagged in 18.
Key definitions (as used here)
  • Chhadmasth — any state before omniscience (1–12).
  • Vinay — humility/reverence manifested as obedience, acknowledgement, devotion.
  • Samkit — right faith (17 called the Guru-aligned state samkit in vyavahār sense).
Pocket recap (read in one breath)

Verse 19 paints humility’s summit: Even after becoming Omniscient by the Sadguru’s teachings, the disciple keeps revering the Guru who is still chhadmasth. This seals the Sadguru-centric arc (9–18) and opens the humility path (19–20): vinay is maturity, gratitude, and the fast lane to liberation.

Micro-practice (to live Verse 19 today)

1. Acknowledge the source: In any praise you receive, add the simple truth: “This is my Guru’s grace.”

2. Choose respect over ‘being right’ once today; clarify gently if needed.

3. Bow in stages: a small outward gesture; a concrete act of obedience; an inner sentence of gratitude.

4. Recall the parables (Gautamswāmī, Mrugāvatī) when ego rises.

Where this sits in the text
  • Verse 19 (Importance/peak of humility) and Verse 20 (the path of humility praised by the Vītrāgs) close the ‘Sadguru & humility’ segment before the text turns to bigotry vs true seeking (24–33, 34–42) and the Six Fundamentals.
