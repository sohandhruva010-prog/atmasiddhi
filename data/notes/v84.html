Topic: Worldly differences prove karmic fruition (Soul is the receiver)

Where Verse 84 fits (context chain)

The Guru explicitly sets the sequence:

  • Verse 82: Introduced bhaavkarma (impure feelings) + dravya karma (material karmas), and explained bondage + “programming/stamping”.
  • Verse 83: Proved that insentient (lifeless) matter can still produce effects, without knowing/intending—via poison/nectar and other examples.
  • Verse 84 (this verse): Uses a familiar, common-life observation to prove that karmas do give fruits, and therefore the soul must receive them.

✅ In short: 82 = programming, 83 = lifeless matter can give effect, 84 = evidence from real-world disparities (cause–effect).

1) What Guru says Verse 84 is proving

He says Verse 82–83 already established:

  • karmas are bound and programmed during bondage,
  • and when the time is right, accumulated karmas give fruits on their own.

Now Verse 84 adds a different style of proof:

“Common-eye” proof

Even an ordinary person can observe the world and see that:

  • There are differences, disparities, diversities everywhere.
  • These are effects.
  • No effect exists without a cause.
  • That cause is: past shubh–ashubh karmas maturing (fructifying).

2) Verse 84 — the verse idea + word meanings (as Guru teaches)

The Guru first unpacks vocabulary.

Key words

Raank

  • Raank = pauper / beggar
  • He even gives a memory trick: P–P = Pauper and Prince

Nrup / Nrupati

  • Nrup = king
  • He explains the Sanskrit breakdown:
    • “nru” = human/man
    • “pati” = lord/master
    • So nrupati = ruler/lord of subjects

✅ Core contrast of the verse:

  • Ek raank = one person is poor
  • Ek nrup = another person is a king
  • Such differences are seen everywhere.

The causation line (the “law statement”)

He points to the key logic:

  • Karan vina na karya te → Without a cause, no effect happens.

So:

  • these differences are effects
  • therefore they must have causes
  • and the cause is: shubh–ashubh karma-phala being experienced.

3) Guru’s “poetry / name-weaving” aside (he includes it, so keep it in notes)

He goes into a literary aside about “nrup” and how poets weave their name into verses.

Points he makes

  • “Nrup” appears in Param Krupalu Dev’s compositions in many places.
  • He references phrases like:
    • “Nrupati jitata jitiye…” (example of “nrupati” usage)
  • He connects:
    • “Rai” (in Raichand) also means king
  • He mentions a poetic convention:
    • When composing songs/poems, the poet’s name is woven in (often near the end)
    • Example he gives: Meerabai style (“…Giridhar Nagar” idea: Krishna as her Lord)

Mokshmala reference

He mentions the Mokshmala composition:

  • “Shubh shitaltamay chhay rahi…”
  • And a line like:
    • “Nrupchandra prapanch anant daho…”
  • He interprets:
    • Nrupchandra = “king + moon” → linking to Rajchandra / Raichandra
    • He notes: early Param Krupalu Dev poems followed this system, later he didn’t always follow it.

✅ Why this matters in the lecture: It’s not the main proof, but he’s ensuring students understand “nrup/raank” and related usage cleanly.

4) The verse’s main proof method: Observe disparities → infer karmas

The Guru’s approach is very direct:

Step A — Observe

Look at the world:

  • one is poor
  • one is rich
  • one is intelligent
  • one is foolish
  • one is healthy
  • one is sick
  • one is beautiful
  • one is ugly
  • etc.

Step B — Recognize these are “effects”

These are not random. They are outcomes.

Step C — Apply causation law

No effect without a cause.

Step D — Identify the cause

The cause is:

  • shubh karmas (auspicious/meritorious)
  • ashubh karmas (inauspicious/demeritorious)
  • specifically: manifestation of past karmas (uday / fruition)

✅ This is how Verse 84 supports the 4th Fundamental Truth: If karmas are fructifying, someone must experience them → that experiencer is the soul.

5) “Born rich/poor” vs “became rich/poor” (important distinction he makes)

He makes a sharp point:

  • If you ask: “Why are you rich/poor at 30?”
    • You can cite visible reasons: work ethic, career choices, etc.
  • But the deeper question of the verse is:
    • Why were you BORN into a rich family?
    • Why not born somewhere else entirely?

Strong example he uses

  • “Why were you born in USA?”
  • “Why not born in Dharavi (slum)?”
  • His point: Your entire life trajectory changes because of birth circumstance—so the cause must be deeper than “this life’s effort.”

✅ For him, Verse 84 is mainly pointing toward birth-conditions, capacities, circumstances as karmic effects.

6) The “Three D’s” (he explicitly instructs this)

He says: write the three D’s:

1. Differences

2. Disparities

3. Diversities

He repeats: you will see these three everywhere, in every possible state.

7) Wealth disparity example (Rushit) — “not even aware of extent of wealth”

He uses a playful but pointed illustration.

What he says

  • Some people don’t even know:
    • how many houses they have,
    • how much jewellery,
    • how many shares,
    • how many loans they gave out that are pending repayment.

He contrasts it with ordinary people:

  • “We know ₹500 in wallet and ₹100 in bank—that’s it.”

His key message

He says (importantly):

  • don’t assume it means the wealthy person looted people
  • it may be the fruit of past devotion/religious feelings
  • he’s showing a disparity, not accusing.

✅ Purpose: To show how extreme worldly outcomes can be—and they demand a deeper “cause” explanation.

8) “Unfair / unjust / discriminatory” — why people reject God (common reaction)

He says: to a common eye (without karmic science), these disparities look:

  • unfair,
  • unjustifiable,
  • discriminatory.

And people respond:

  • “There is no God. If God exists, why injustice?”

He gives a sharp contrast:

  • here, people waste food
  • elsewhere, people fight over basic food (he uses khichri context)

✅ His point: If you don’t understand karmic causation, the world looks morally chaotic.

9) The “Bay area / khichri / Bombay / bom-bay” aside (he includes it, so here it is)

He shifts into humor and general-knowledge style:

Bay area joke

  • They had a khichri collection area
  • later it became a meeting area
  • now there are puddles/drainage water → calling it “bay area”
  • “Dirty Bay, not Bombay.”

Bombay general knowledge

He claims:

  • Portuguese had islands
  • they were given as dowry to British when a Portuguese girl married a British boy
  • Bombay = “good bay”
  • He links:
    • French “bon” (good)
    • Portuguese “bom” (good)

✅ Purpose (in the flow of talk): Not directly the verse proof, but he’s keeping the room engaged while still talking about “bay / good bay / bom-bay” as a playful aside.

10) Intelligence disparity example (photographic memory vs blank mind)

He gives a very relatable “same setup, different output” example.

  • Two students:
    • same parents, same tuition, same atmosphere
  • yet:
    • one can answer instantly (pitter-patter)
    • another revises 5 times and still goes blank

He jokes:

  • someone makes chits and then forgets where the chits are

✅ Purpose: To show disparities exist even when external causes look “equal.” So the cause must include karmic imprint differences.

11) “Why me?” — Guru’s strong correction

This is a major teaching moment.

He says:

  • If someone who claims refuge in the Dispassionate One (Vitarag philosophy) keeps saying:
    • “Why did this happen to me?”
    • “Why at such a young age?”
  • Then:
    • “the first day of refuge has not begun.”

Because:

  • karmic philosophy directly answers “why me”
  • saying “why me” after learning karma is like not absorbing the basics.

✅ His intended practice outcome: Replace “why me” with:

  • “This is the fruit of my own causes.”

12) “No two are identical” — from humans to ants to crows to horses

He expands the disparity observation beyond just rich/poor.

Human count

He mentions:

  • ~7.5 billion humans (as audience said)

He claims: even among ants

  • no two ants are identical

Birds / crows

  • “A flock of birds… every crow is different.”

Horses

  • horse lovers can recognize their horse instantly
  • while outsiders just label:
    • brown/black/white/mixed
  • yet none are identical.

✅ Purpose: To show the world is built on individual variation, not sameness—suggesting individualized karmic histories.

13) The “trait list” of differences (very good to memorize)

He lists where differences show up:

  • height
  • complexion
  • size
  • strength
  • facial features
  • handprint
  • mental capacities
  • concepts
  • emotions
  • aspirations

Example he gives for aspirations:

  • one wants a bigger car
  • another wants to retire

✅ He’s showing: Disparity exists on physical + psychological + motivational levels.

14) The “dog in a mansion” example (very vivid karmic-fruit illustration)

This is one of his strongest modern-life illustrations.

What he describes

Sometimes you see dogs that:

  • live in huge mansions
  • choose what food they want
  • rest as much as they want
  • have people designated to walk them
  • helpers carry bags to clean up after them
  • they get car rides, etc.

Meanwhile the owner:

  • lives in big house but small office (i.e., trapped in work)
  • doesn’t get time to eat
  • doesn’t get time to rest
  • can’t even take a 3-day Maldives vacation (he uses this type of line often)

His punchline

On paper, the owner seems fortunate. But in experienced life, the dog looks more free from tension.

He even adds a Covid-era contrast (as his illustrative claim):

  • human has to wear a mask
  • dog is “barking freely”
  • no social distancing, no handwashing, no PPE suit knowledge, etc.

He asks:

  • “What is PPE?” → Personal Protective Equipment

✅ Purpose: To show that “fortune/misfortune” is not always where you assume it is— and still it reflects karmic fruition in lived experience.

15) The core conclusion: disparities are karmic effects

He brings it back tightly:

  • These are effects
  • differences in effects imply differences in causes
  • the cause is:
    • manifestation of past shubh–ashubh karmas

He says:

  • don’t just say “past karmas exist”
  • specifically: past karmas must manifest for effects to appear

16) “As you sow, so shall you reap” — and his special method: go backwards

He states the karmic law:

As you sow, so shall you reap.

Then he gives a very practical psychological tool:

Why going backwards helps

  • sowing (cause) is invisible
  • reaping (effect) is experiential/visible

So to build faith in karma philosophy:

  • don’t start with “If I do X now, I’ll get Y later”
  • instead reflect:
    • What I am experiencing now is what I myself sowed earlier

✅ This is his “eraser” for the mind: It increases your ability to erase:

  • “why me, why me, why me.”

17) Epic references: Bhishma, Gandhari, Sita, Draupadi (all bore their own karma)

He says:

  • Bhishma bore fruits of his own karma
  • Gandhari bore fruits of her own karma
  • Sita bore fruits of her own karma
  • Draupadi bore fruits of her own karma
  • and likewise: “I am bearing fruits of my own karmas.”

✅ Purpose: To remove the tendency to blame external forces and strengthen personal karmic accountability.

18) Karmic “blindness” and “good fortune”

He says:

  • We can’t see many things because of jnanavarniya karma (knowledge-obscuring karma)
  • but because of saubhagya (good fortune karma), we received Param Krupalu Dev’s teaching on karma philosophy.

✅ Purpose: Even understanding karma philosophy is treated as a karmic fruit (merit/good fortune).

19) Intensity, duration, and disproportion (small act → long fruit)

He states an important karmic principle:

  • You must bear fruits:
    • in the manner you created the cause
    • with the intensity you created
    • and according to time/duration parameters

He gives a disproportion example:

  • sometimes you do something for 1 second
  • and bear consequence for 1 year

Rushabhdev Bhagwan example (Varshitapa)

He references:

  • Varshitapa (year-long fast austerity)
  • mentions 13 ghadi leading to 13 months of consequence/result
  • clarifies:
    • 1 ghadi = 24 minutes

(He’s using this as a “scale” example: small measured cause → long measured effect.)

✅ Purpose: To show karmic causation is not always “equal time-length”; intensity and structure matter.

20) What Verse 84 finally establishes (the actual proof)

The verse is answering the disciple’s first argument (lifeless karma giving fruit) by adding:

“Even if you don’t understand the mechanism fully…”

You can’t deny karmic fruition because:

  • differences are obvious
  • effects need causes
  • karmas are the only fitting cause
  • hence:
    • shubh–ashubh karmas are experienced
    • and the experiencer is the soul (bhoktā)

21) The Guru’s wrap-up: “First argument answered” (82–84 together)

He summarizes the 3-verse package (this is very exam/notes-friendly):

In three verses (82–84), Param Krupalu Dev explained:

1. How karmas are associated (bondage + bhaav/dravy link)

2. Though lifeless, karma can inflict results (non-sentient matter shows effects)

3. It does show results — evident even to common eye (world disparities prove fruition) ✅ Conclusion: So the disciple’s first doubt/first argument is answered.
