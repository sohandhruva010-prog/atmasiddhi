Detailed Notes — Verse 120: “Bhasyu Nijswaroop…”
The Disciple Experiences Himself as Pure Consciousness — Ageless, Immortal, Imperishable & Beyond the Body

This lecture continues the “Expression of the Disciple’s Enlightenment” section of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra. Verse 119 was the disciple’s first expression after Self-realisation: “Through the teachings of my Sadguru, I attained an unprecedented realisation.”

Verse 120 now answers the next question:

What exactly did the disciple experience when he realised the Self?

The answer is incredibly direct:

I experienced myself as pure consciousness — ageless, immortal, imperishable and distinct from the body.

This is no longer philosophy that the disciple believes because the Guru told him. The crucial word is “bhasyu” — experienced. What had previously been understood intellectually has now become his own direct experience.

1. Where We Are in Atmasiddhi

Bapa first establishes that this entire section is specifically about the disciple after Self-realisation.

The title given to the section is:

Shishya Bodhbeej Prapti Kathan
  • Shishya — disciple
  • Bodhbeej — Self-realisation
  • Prapti — attainment
  • Kathan — expression/account

So this is not Bapa merely interpreting that the disciple attained Self-realisation between verses 118 and 119. The section itself is presented as the disciple’s expression of the attainment of Self-realisation.

The nine verses of this section are divided into two parts:

Verses 119–123: expression of the disciple’s experience of Self-realisation.

Verses 124–127: expression of his joy, gratitude and indebtedness toward the Sadguru.

So we are still in the first portion:

What did I experience when I realised the Self?

2. Connection With Verse 119

Verse 119 essentially said:

“I realised my true Self within, which I had never experienced before, and my self-delusive ignorance was destroyed — all through the teachings of the Enlightened Mentor.”

Even though the disciple himself contemplated, turned within and experienced the Self, he gives the credit to the Guru.

From the technical perspective, the Guru may be called the instrumental cause.

But from the devotee’s heart:

“Without You, this would never have happened.”

Bapa puts it beautifully:

Without Your teachings, inspiration and motivation, “the giant would have never been awakened.”

Verse 120 now continues:

Verse 119: I attained unprecedented Self-realisation through You.

Verse 120: Gurudev, let me tell You what I experienced.

3. From Wrong Knowledge to Right Knowledge

Before Self-realisation, the disciple already possessed knowledge.

He could:

  • think,
  • reason,
  • understand the Guru,
  • study the Six Fundamental Truths,
  • distinguish concepts,
  • contemplate the Soul intellectually.

But that knowledge was still functioning as wrong knowledge, because his fundamental identification remained wrong.

He still experienced:

“I am this body.”

At Self-realisation, something fundamental changes.

The knowledge itself becomes right knowledge.

Why?

Because for the first time, the knower correctly recognises himself.

The disciple now experiences:

“I am not the body. I am pure consciousness.”

So Bapa describes Self-realisation as a transformation from:

wrong knowledge → right knowledge

and from:

intellectual conviction → experiential conviction.

4. The Five Descriptions of the Self in Verse 120

The disciple experiences himself as:

1. Shuddha Chetanaroop

Pure consciousness

2. Ajar

Ageless / unchanging

3. Amar

Immortal / deathless

4. Avinashi

Imperishable / indestructible

5. Dehatit Swaroop

Beyond / distinct / separate from the body

So the short meaning of the verse is:

“I now experience my Self as pure consciousness, which is ageless, immortal, indestructible and distinct from the body.”

Or:

I am free from old age, death and destruction because these belong to the body, not to the Self.

5. The Most Important Word: Bhasyu

Bapa spends special attention on one word:

Bhasyu — experienced.

This word proves that the disciple is not merely repeating what he learned.

Earlier in Atmasiddhi, the same word was used in connection with false identification:

Due to dehadhyas, one experiences the Soul and body as the same.

That experience was:

“I am the body.”

Now the same bhasyu appears again — but the experience has completely reversed.

Before:

“I experience myself as the body.”

Now:

“I experience myself as pure consciousness, separate from the body.”

That one word captures the entire spiritual transformation.

6. From Intellectual Conviction to Experiential Conviction

This distinction is central to the lecture.

Before Self-realisation, the disciple had already intellectually understood:

  • the Soul exists,
  • the Soul is eternal,
  • the body cannot be the true Self,
  • previous impressions cannot be explained merely through this present body,
  • consciousness is different from matter.

This intellectual conviction itself was significant.

Bapa acknowledges how difficult even that is because our mind is filled with:

  • concepts,
  • opinions,
  • desires,
  • insistence,
  • “I am right,”
  • deeply ingrained identification.

But through the grace of the Sadguru:

The truth moves from intellect into experience.

The disciple no longer says:

“I believe I am not the body.”

He says:

“I experience that I am not the body.”

7. A Leap From What Appears to What Actually Is

Bapa gives a beautiful description of Self-realisation:

“A leap from what appears to be, to what actually is.”

What appears?

The body.

It is visible.

It ages.

It moves.

It eats.

It becomes sick.

It feels sensations.

It eventually dies.

And because that is what appears most obviously before us, we conclude:

This is me.

But Self-realisation breaks through the appearance.

The disciple rises above the barrier of the body and breaks the chains of limited understanding.

He awakens from what Bapa calls the:

deep slumber of ignorance.

Until now, illusion had made the unreal identification appear completely real.

Self-realisation is awakening from that dream.

8. Verse 120 Revises the First Two Fundamental Truths

There is another beautiful structure hidden inside the verse.

First line → First Fundamental Truth

The Soul exists.

The disciple says:

I experience myself as pure consciousness.

Therefore:

I exist as Soul.

Second line → Second Fundamental Truth

The Soul is eternal.

The disciple says:

I am ageless, immortal, imperishable and separate from the body.

Therefore:

I am eternal.

So for us, Verse 120 is a revision of the first two Fundamental Truths.

But for the disciple, it is not revision.

It is experience.

Bapa makes this distinction very clearly:

For us it is a revision; for the disciple it is an experience which he is expressing.

9. Why Does the Disciple Express His Experience?

An interesting question arises.

If he has attained Self-realisation, why speak about it?

Why not simply remain absorbed within?

Bapa says that expressing and recounting an experience can:

enhance the continuity of that experience.

When something beautiful happens and we recount it, the experience becomes alive again.

Likewise, the disciple is recounting his realisation before the Guru.

But this expression also serves another purpose for us:

Through the disciple’s description, we get a concise revision of the Six Fundamental Truths.

So there are two perspectives:

For the disciple: expression extends the continuity of his experience.

For us: his expression teaches and revises the Fundamental Truths.

10. Shuddha Chetanaroop — Pure Consciousness

The first thing the disciple experiences is:

“I am pure consciousness.”

Not merely:

“I am conscious.”

But:

Shuddha chetna — pure consciousness.

Bapa separates both words.

Chetna — consciousness

This immediately distinguishes the Self from jada — inanimate matter.

The body is material.

The Self is conscious.

So:

Chetna tells me what I am.

Shuddha — pure

This distinguishes the Self from all impurities:

  • attachment
  • aversion
  • anger
  • pride
  • deceit
  • greed
  • likes
  • dislikes

So:

Shuddha tells me what I am inherently free from.

Together:

I am pure consciousness.

11. No Coating and No Contamination

Bapa uses two memorable words:

Coating

The association of the Self with the non-self:

  • body
  • senses
  • breath
  • mind
  • intellect
  • karmic personality
Contamination

The impure modifications:

  • attachment
  • aversion
  • anger
  • greed
  • likes
  • dislikes, etc.

The disciple experiences the Self as independent of both.

So:

Pure consciousness = no coating + no contamination.

The Self is experienced independently of:

external/material association and internal/impure feelings.

12. The Lamp Example — Light and Smoke Are Not the Same

Bapa gives the example of a burning lamp.

A lamp can produce:

  • light
  • smoke

Both may appear together.

But light is not smoke.

Similarly, in the worldly state we observe:

  • knowledge/consciousness
  • impure feelings

They appear together in the Soul's impure state.

But they are not identical.

Therefore:

The existence of anger in my present state does not mean anger constitutes my eternal nature.

The existence of attachment does not mean attachment is the Self.

The disciple now experiences the original nature beneath these modifications:

Pure consciousness.

13. Not “I Became Pure” — I Was Always Pure

This distinction is extremely important.

The disciple is not saying:

“I used to be an impure Soul, and Self-realisation has now made my Soul pure.”

Instead:

“My nature was always pure consciousness.”

The impurities belonged to the state/modifications, not to the eternal nature.

Bapa emphasizes:

Was, is, and always remains pure consciousness.

So Self-realisation does not manufacture purity.

It reveals purity.

This connects directly to earlier verses:

Nature = eternally pure

State = may be impure Self-realisation allows the disciple to stop defining himself through the temporary state and recognise his eternal nature.

14. The Disciple’s Relationship With His Own Experience

Bapa points out something beautiful about a genuine spiritual experience.

When the disciple actually experiences the Self:

He becomes quiet.

He does not immediately want to announce:

“Look what I experienced!”

The experience draws him inward.

There is:

  • peace
  • withdrawal
  • stillness
  • little desire to speak
  • little desire to celebrate externally

Yet something completely different happens when he speaks about the Guru.

Then he cannot remain quiet.

He may not want to describe his own spiritual state, but when it comes to the Guru:

“O True Guru! Boundless ocean of compassion! What favours You have bestowed!”

His own attainment makes him silent.

His Guru's greatness makes him speak.

Bapa describes this apparent contradiction as part of the sheer joy of devotion.

15. A Devotee Can Hold Apparent Contradictions

Bapa makes a playful but deep observation about devotion.

A devotee may say:

“I miss Him so much.”

and simultaneously:

“I constantly feel Him with me.”

Logically, someone might ask:

How can both be true?

But bhakti is not always expressed through dry logic.

A devotee may:

  • cry in happiness,
  • dance where another would cry,
  • become silent,
  • suddenly overflow,
  • intensely miss the Guru,
  • yet feel the Guru constantly present.

Bapa calls this the sheer joy of being a devotee.

The deeper point is that Self-realisation has not dried the disciple emotionally.

Jnan has deepened bhakti.

16. Ajar — Ageless / Unchanging

Now the disciple describes the eternality of the Self.

First:

Ajar

Jar refers to wearing out, ageing or decay.

A-jar therefore means:

  • ageless
  • unchanging
  • not subject to old age
  • not subject to wear and tear

The body passes through:

birth → childhood → youth → old age → deterioration

But these are:

modifications of the material body.

They are not modifications of the eternal nature of the Soul.

Therefore:

The body becomes old. I do not.

17. “How Old Are You?”

Bapa playfully points out that when someone asks:

“What is your age?”

and we immediately answer with the body's age, we are explaining ourselves through the body.

The Self-realised disciple instead experiences:

“I am ageless.”

Not merely:

“I intellectually know the Soul is eternal.”

But:

“I found myself ageless.”

The body may be 20, 50, 75 or 90.

But consciousness itself does not become “old” in the way the material body does.

18. The Body Is Subject to Wear and Tear

The body is a compounded material entity.

Therefore it is subject to:

  • growth
  • deterioration
  • injury
  • disease
  • ageing
  • decomposition

Bapa gives ordinary examples:

  • muscle tears
  • grey hair
  • wrinkles
  • declining vitality
  • difficulty walking

And he makes the point humorously with three claps:

Childhood. Youth. Old age.

How quickly three claps finish is how quickly life seems to pass.

You hardly realise when one stage becomes another.

Why?

Because:

The nature of the body is decay and decomposition.

19. The Soul Is an Original Substance

Why does the Soul not decay like the body?

Bapa explains that the Soul is not something newly produced through the combination of parts.

The body is composed through combinations and therefore can decompose.

But the Soul is described here as:

an original / natural substance.

Therefore:

It is not subject to decomposition.

The disciple has shifted identification from:

“I am this ageing material body”

to:

“I am an ageless conscious substance.”

20. Amar — Immortal / Deathless

The next word:

Amar

Mar → death A-mar → no death

Therefore:

  • immortal
  • deathless
  • endless

Bapa makes a crucial distinction:

Death happens to the body.

The Soul does not die with the body.

From the framing of the lecture, the Soul remains associated with a particular body for the duration determined by ayushya karma — lifespan-determining karma.

When that association ends:

The body dies; the Soul does not.

The Soul leaves that body and, until liberation, continues elsewhere according to karma.

So:

Bodies perish.

The Soul continues.

21. The Difference Between Knowing “I Am Immortal” and Realising It

Someone could object:

“But the Soul was immortal before Self-realisation too.”

Correct.

The disciple did not become immortal upon Self-realisation.

He realised that he had always been immortal.

That is the entire point.

Before:

Immortal in nature, but identifying with the mortal body.

After:

Immortal in nature and knowing/experiencing himself as that immortal Self.

Bapa beautifully phrases the shift:

“You were immortal only!”
“Yes, but I realise now!”

22. Why Fear of Death Arises

If I identify myself with something that must die, death naturally becomes terrifying.

If:

Body = I

then:

Death of body = end of me.

Therefore body-identification produces:

  • fear of death
  • anxiety about ageing
  • anxiety about illness
  • anxiety about future births
  • insecurity about who will care for me
  • attachment to maintaining the karmic personality

But the Self-realised disciple experiences:

“I am immortal.”

Therefore, according to Bapa's explanation, the Enlightened Person does not carry the same fear of death because death is understood as an event concerning the body, not destruction of the Self.

23. Avinashi — Imperishable / Indestructible

Now comes the third expression of eternality:

Avinashi

Bapa distinguishes this from amar.

Amar:

I do not die.

Avinashi:

I cannot be destroyed, broken or divided.

Avinashi therefore means:

  • imperishable
  • indestructible
  • unbreakable
  • indivisible

The Soul has travelled through innumerable embodiments.

Yet:

Not one part of the Soul has broken away.

It remains an indivisible conscious entity.

24. Infinite Wandering Has Not Damaged the Self

Bapa gives a simple worldly analogy.

When we repeatedly move physical belongings from one place to another, eventually something may:

  • crack,
  • break,
  • get lost,
  • become damaged.

But the Soul has undergone an unimaginably greater journey:

body → body → body → body

across countless embodiments.

And yet:

Nothing has broken away from the Soul.

It remains:

  • whole
  • indivisible
  • indestructible

The changing bodies do not damage the eternal conscious substance.

25. Nothing External Can Destroy the Soul

Bapa connects this with the Bhagavad Gita teaching that weapons cannot cut the Soul, fire cannot burn it, water cannot wet it, and wind cannot dry it.

The point is not merely consolation at someone's death.

The teaching is supposed to transform one's own identification while living.

Instead of merely saying after someone dies:

“The Soul does not die,”

the spiritual seeker should realise:

“I am that Soul which cannot be destroyed.”

Bapa's question then becomes:

If I genuinely know what I am, why this fear?

26. Ajar, Amar and Avinashi — The Distinction

These three words may sound repetitive, but each attacks a different aspect of body-identification.

Attribute Meaning What belongs to body instead

Ajar Ageless / unchanging Ageing, wear and tear

Amar Immortal / deathless Death

Avinashi Imperishable / indestructible Destruction, breaking, decomposition

Together they establish:

I am eternal.

And that is why the second line of Verse 120 beautifully revises the Second Fundamental Truth — the Soul is eternal.

27. Dehatit Swaroop — Separate From the Body

The final description ties everything together.

Dehatit

Deh — body Atit — beyond

So:

Beyond the body / distinct from the body / separate from the body.

Why am I ageless?

Because ageing belongs to the body.

Why am I immortal?

Because death belongs to the body.

Why am I imperishable?

Because destruction belongs to material compositions.

The common root is:

I am not the body.

28. The Coconut Example

Bapa gives a beautiful visual example.

When a coconut dries, its inner kernel separates from the outer shell.

If you shake it, you can actually hear the inner portion moving independently.

The shell and kernel are together, yet distinctly separate.

Similarly, the disciple now experiences:

“I am completely distinct from the body.”

Previously:

I operated as the body.

Now:

I am the knower of the body.

That is a massive shift.

Before:
I = body
After:
Body = something known by me

And if I am the knower of the body, the body cannot be the knower itself.

29. From “I Am the Body” to “I Know the Body”

This is perhaps the simplest way to understand Verse 120.

Before enlightenment:

I am this body.

After enlightenment:

I know this body.

The body changes from subject to object.

Earlier:

body = me Now:

body = known by me

And:

I = the conscious knower

That is why Bapa describes the disciple as having crossed the barrier of the body.

30. “Pure Consciousness” Goes Even Deeper Than “Consciousness”

Earlier in Atmasiddhi, the existence of consciousness helped establish:

I am not inanimate matter.

But now the disciple says something deeper:

I am not merely consciousness. I am pure consciousness.

Why is that important?

Because someone may accept:

“Yes, I am a conscious Soul.”

but still identify deeply with:

  • anger
  • greed
  • attachment
  • pride
  • likes and dislikes.

Verse 120 goes deeper:

These impurities are not my eternal nature either.

So Self-realisation distinguishes the Self from both:

1. The material non-self

body, senses, etc.

2. The impure modifications anger, attachment, aversion, etc.

What remains?

Shuddha Chetna — Pure Consciousness.

31. Millions of Attributes — One Soul

At the end, Bapa adds another subtle point.

The Soul possesses innumerable attributes.

Yet Self-realisation is not an experience in which someone separately experiences:

“Here is my knowledge.”

“Now here is my perception.”

“Here is my bliss.”

“Here is my vigour.”

The Soul is experienced as one indivisible entity possessing all these attributes.

Bapa gives the chocolate example.

A piece of chocolate may contain many ingredients:

  • cocoa
  • sugar
  • butter
  • etc.

But when you eat it, you experience:

one chocolate.

Similarly:

The Soul has innumerable attributes but is experienced as one conscious entity.

32. The Transformation Contained in One Word

Returning to bhasyu, Bapa says that this single word encompasses a transformation in:

  • knowledge
  • beliefs
  • thought processes
  • feelings
  • responses
  • identity

Think about how enormous the difference is:

Before

“I am the body.”

After

“I am pure consciousness.”

That change cannot remain merely theoretical.

If the fundamental identity changes, then the way one relates to:

  • ageing
  • death
  • pain
  • pleasure
  • praise
  • insult
  • attachment
  • relationships
  • possessions

must gradually change too.

That is why Bapa calls it a story of transformation.

33. The Devotional Undercurrent Is Still There

Even though Verse 120 primarily explains the experience of the Self, the devotion of Verse 119 continues underneath it.

The disciple could say:

“I worked hard, contemplated and realised the Soul.”

Instead, his inner feeling remains:

“By His grace, I realised.”

Bapa gives the analogy:

“I was just on the flight. The flight took me there.”

The devotee feels:

I remained in His refuge, and He transported me from “I am body, senses and breath” to “I am pure consciousness.”

That is not the technical language of causation.

It is the language of gratitude.

34. The Full Journey From Verse 119 → 120

These two verses fit beautifully together.

Verse 119 — How did this happen?

Sadguruna updeshthi. Through the teachings of my Sadguru.

Verse 120 — What happened?

Bhasyu nijswaroop. I experienced my true nature.

What did I experience?

Shuddha Chetanaroop — I am pure consciousness.

Ajar — I am ageless.

Amar — I am immortal.

Avinashi — I am imperishable.

Dehatit — I am distinct from the body.

So the disciple is essentially saying:

“Gurudev, through Your grace, what I once merely understood has become my experience. I now know myself not as this ageing and perishable body, but as the pure, eternal consciousness that knows it.”

35. Practical Reflection From the Lecture

Although the verse describes the state of a Self-realised disciple, there is a very practical question for the seeker:

What am I identifying with throughout the day?

When the body changes:

“I am changing.”

When the body is hurt:

“I am hurt.”

When someone attacks my personality:

“They attacked me.”

When anger arises:

“I am angry.”

When anxiety arises:

“I am anxious.”

Verse 120 points toward a completely different understanding:

The body is known.

The emotion is known.

The thought is known.

The impurity is known.

I am the conscious knower.

The disciple has moved from being lost in the known to recognising himself as the knower.

36. The Central Contrast of the Entire Lecture

Before Self-realisation After Self-realisation

“I am the body.” “I am the knower of the body.”

“I am ageing.” “The body ages; I am ajar.”

“I will die.” “The body dies; I am amar.”

“I can be destroyed.” “I am avinashi.”

“My anger is me.” “Impurity is a modification; I am pure consciousness.”

Intellectual understanding Experiential conviction

Soul and body experienced as one Soul experienced as dehatit

What appears What actually is

Dehadhyas Atmadrashti

Wrong knowledge Right knowledge

Key Lines to Remember

Bhasyu — experienced. This is no longer intellectual knowledge.

“A leap from what appears to be, to what actually is.”

Shuddha Chetanaroop — I am pure consciousness.

No coating, no contamination.

The body and other material associations are not me; attachment, aversion and other impurities are not my eternal nature either.

Ajar — the body ages; I am ageless.

Amar — the body dies; I am deathless.

Avinashi — material things break and perish; I am indestructible and indivisible.

Dehatit — I am completely distinct from the body.

Before: I was operating as the body. Now: I am the knower of the body.

The disciple did not become eternal or pure after Self-realisation. He realised that this had always been his nature.

Verse 120 = First Fundamental Truth + Second Fundamental Truth, now experienced rather than merely understood.

One-Line Essence

Verse 120 is the disciple’s first direct description of what Self-realisation actually revealed: “Gurudev, I no longer merely believe that I am the Soul. I experience myself as pure consciousness — untouched by impurity, untouched by age, untouched by death, indestructible, and completely distinct from this body.”

And when placed beside Verse 119, it becomes even more beautiful:

Verse 119: “Gurudev, this happened because of You.”
Verse 120: “And this is what You made me realise I truly am.”
