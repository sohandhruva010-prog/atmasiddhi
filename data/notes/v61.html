1) Where we are (the link from Verse 60 → Verse 61)

  • The disciple already presented Verse 60 doubt:
    • Under Charvaka influence: soul is not avinash (indestructible).
    • Soul is created with the body and destroyed with the body (gross modification viewpoint).
  • Now the teacher says: Verse 61 is a second, separate argument (not continuation).
    • The word “Athva” = OR
    • Meaning: “I have another reason to doubt soul’s eternity.”

2) Verse 61: the disciple’s second argument (momentariness)

Core claim:

Everything changes every moment → therefore everything is kshanik (momentary) → so the soul is not eternal.

The teacher frames it as:

  • This is under influence of Buddhist philosophy, specifically the doctrine of momentariness.
  • Perspective: subtle modifications of life:
    • moods and inner states change continuously:
      • anger → happiness → sadness
      • auspicious feelings (shubh bhaav) ↔ inauspicious feelings (ashubh bhaav)
  • Therefore the “knower/feeler” seems momentary.

Very clear contrast he repeats:

  • Verse 60: soul exists for a limited time period (like lifetime).
  • Verse 61: soul exists only for one moment — then “dies” next moment.

3) Key Gujarati terms in Verse 61 (as he explains)

Athva

  • Means: Or
  • Signals: new argument, not the previous one.

Vastu

  • Means: substance (not just soul—any substance)
    • sentient / non-sentient
    • living / non-living
    • animate / inanimate

Kshan / Kshanik

  • Kshan = one moment
  • Kshanik = momentary / transient / transitory

Kshane kshane paltay

  • Changes every moment
  • He says: even science will accept “constant change.”

E anubhavthi pan

  • “Even from experience too…”
  • Important: Here the disciple claims he doesn’t even need deep logic—experience alone shows constant change.

Nahi atma nitya janay

  • “The soul does not appear eternal.”

Short meaning he gives:

“Substances are seen to undergo modifications every moment, hence all are momentary; from experience too, the soul doesn’t seem eternal.”

4) The key philosophical confusion: “substance vs. state”

The teacher warns: you (Jain listener) may feel confused because Jainism says:

  • Substance remains while states change.

But the disciple (under Buddhist flavor) is mixing them:

  • He treats modification itself as the substance
  • So if there is modification, he concludes:
    • old substance “died”
    • new substance “was born”
    • therefore everything is “utpad-vyay” (creation-destruction) every moment
Teacher’s diagnosis of the disciple’s mistake:
  • He cannot differentiate:
    • dravya (substance)
    • vs paryay / state (mode/state of substance)
  • He thinks: “if it changed, it became a new substance.”

He says Jainism’s stance (the correction) is:

It’s the state that changes; the substance remains.

5) “Change is the law of the universe” — examples he uses

He stacks multiple “experience proofs” to make the disciple’s argument feel very strong.

A) Clock example (wear and tear)

  • He asks: How long will a clock last? (Made in India—he jokes)
  • Even if kept perfectly, after long time it decays.
  • If misused, it can fail quickly.
  • Conclusion: wear and tear is law of nature; time makes “new” become “old.”

B) Body aging / gym example

  • Even if you go to the gym and stay fit:
    • you won’t stay the same forever
    • either you’re “expanding” or “decaying”
  • Law of nature: constant change.

C) Dharamshala wear-and-tear example

  • In one year in Aradhana dharamshala:
    • so much wear and tear that people won’t believe it’s “only one year old”
  • Again: change is everywhere.

D) History example: Indus Valley Civilisation

  • If change/decay weren’t real, ancient civilizations would still be intact.
  • He mentions:
    • Mohenjo-daro (movie reference; he visited it)
    • Harappa
    • Lothal near Ahmedabad
  • His point: everything eventually becomes dust → decay is inevitable.

E) Orange juice example (minute change)

  • Fresh orange juice left for evening:
    • smell, taste, color change
  • Change isn’t sudden; it happens gradually but continuously.

F) People changing (Premarpits example)

  • He describes people who used to run/manage departments now:
    • wheelchair, stick, memory slips, calling someone by wrong name
  • Shows psychical + physical change.

G) Satsang / shibir “inner change” example

  • Beginning of satsang vs middle vs end: you’re different
  • First shibir vs last shibir: you’re different
  • Even “better joy” is still change (little joy → lot of joy)
Overall conclusion he puts in disciple’s mouth:

I don’t see anything unchanging—physical or psychical. So I infer: everything is momentary.

6) The “moot point” the disciple misses: the still center

This is a major teaching moment from the speaker (before he gets to Guru’s reply).

He says the disciple has thought well but missed the key:

  • the still center
  • the unchanging behind all changes

He uses the Chakki Chalasana / grinding stone analogy:

Chakki (grinding stone) analogy

  • A chakki has two stones:
    • one is stationary
    • one rotates
  • You can easily notice the rotating top,
    • but you miss the stable base.
Meaning:
  • The disciple observes only the “rotation” (changing states),
  • but doesn’t recognize the “stable base” (unchanging substance/center).

He jokes:

  • give fancy names to chores = “salt-filling posture”
  • olden-day women did tons of household work and stayed fit—because work itself was their “yoga.”

But the philosophical payload is:

There is a stable substratum that makes change observable.

7) Buddhist doctrine as he explains it (simple but accurate to his framing)

He tries to keep it “as a query,” but he still explains it.

Buddhist “momentariness” (as he presents)

  • Everything is momentary.
  • Soul is destroyed every moment and reborn again due to vasanas (strong unfulfilled desires).
  • Cycle continues:
    • birth → death → rebirth
    • like a flame that keeps coming back as long as there is fuel

Lamp-oil analogy

  • As long as you keep pouring oil, flame continues.
  • When desires end completely, no reason to be reborn again.

Candle analogy (again)

  • Candle flame seems extinguished then comes again (he compares to “reappearing flame” style candles).
  • That’s how Buddhism explains moment-to-moment rebirth of consciousness.

Stream of consciousness vs “soul”

He says Buddhism:

  • says stream of consciousness is beginningless
  • but “soul” is not beginningless as a fixed entity; it’s more like repeated arising/ceasing.
  • He uses term: santan pravah (legacy/continuity):
    • father dies, son continues the legacy
  • That “legacy” is what people mistake as “soul,” but really it’s continuity of impressions/stream.

River analogy

  • “You cannot step in the same river twice”:
    • water is always new
  • Ganga flows for thousands of years, but the water at 11:10 is not there at 11:11.
  • So “river” seems constant, but the constituents keep changing.

Samskara counterpoint (his personal teaching style)

  • Sometimes he uses “river” idea to remove guilt: “that moment is gone.”
  • But with Nemi he used a stricter point:
    • it leaves a samskara (impression)
    • if not eradicated, it reproduces mistakes again
  • So he blends “moment passed” with “impression remains.”

8) His critique of Buddhist moksha concept (why it’s not appealing)

He says Buddhist nirvana is:

  • destruction of misery (ending birth-rebirth)
  • but not “attainment of happiness” in the way Jainism promises.

His money example (very memorable)

  • If I say: “Hang yourself and I’ll give you 20 million.”
  • But if you’re dead, who gets the money? He parallels:
  • If liberation = death of soul, then:
    • who experiences moksha?

He contrasts with Jain framing:

  • Jainism promises infinite happiness (not just ending misery)
  • so you can give up small pleasures (pizza/ice cream) because “you” will enjoy the infinite happiness.

9) Major guidance on Guru-disciple alignment (the “footnote faith” warning)

This is a big side-teaching in the transcript.

He warns:

  • You can’t be “too open-minded” after surrender.
  • If what Guru believes is only in your footnote (secondary), and your main mind favors another philosophy, you’re not aligned.

He says:

  • You may think “non-absolutism means I can keep Charvaka/Buddhist in footnotes,”
  • but then what are the Six Fundamentals for?

Key line:

  • You can’t claim devotion while internally preferring the opposite doctrine.
  • You’re not cheating the Guru—you’re cheating yourself.

He references Atmasiddhi intro teaching:

  • “Swachhand… Mat darshan agrah taji”
    • give up self-willed attitude
    • give up stubborn insistence on personal opinions/philosophies

He gives an example of complete erasure of ego/opinion:

  • Lalluji Muni (he says the monkhood identity itself got erased)
  • “I am not a monk” said by someone with 14 years monkhood—meaning truth dawned.

11) Argument 1 answer broken into 1A / 1B / 1C (Verses 62–67)

He tells you exactly how to mark your text:

1A: Verses 62–63

Guru’s first move is a single devastating question:

“Who is the knower?”

  • You say: soul is created and destroyed.
  • Who knows the soul was created/destroyed?

He argues:

  • Only two substances: body and soul
  • Body is inert; it doesn’t even know its own existence.
  • A clock doesn’t know when it was born or will die. So if not body, then only chetan (soul).

But then the logical problem:

  • If soul dies, how can the soul “know” it died?
  • A dead soul knowing its own death is “unthinkable.”

So 62–63 aims to expose:

your claim has no real knower / no direct known evidence.

1B: Verses 64–66

He says this section will involve:

  • “sperms and ovum” (biological reproduction point)

Main logical test:

If soul is created from some substance, do they share characteristics?

He uses:

  • Hydrogen/nitrogen example:
    • If soul is produced from material components,
    • those components should contain the property of knowingness.

He uses:

  • Baby inherits traits example:
    • if a couple has a baby, baby shows some traits
    • if baby has none of either parent’s traits, you’d suspect something is off So if soul arises from inert matter,
  • inert matter should show at least seed-form knowingness—otherwise how create a totally different property?

This is the “property continuity” challenge.

1C: Verse 67

He previews:

  • Guru will give authentic evidence
    • something carried forward from past birth
    • something that continues into next birth

So:

  • 1A = knower problem
  • 1B = cause-property mismatch
  • 1C = positive evidence of continuity/transmigration

12) Big “take-home summary” of Verse 61 (disciple’s position)

The disciple is saying:

1. I observe constant change everywhere—physical and psychical.

2. I see “creation-destruction” (utpad-vyay) moment to moment.

3. Since soul is accepted as a substance, it too must follow this law.

4. Therefore soul is not eternal; it appears momentary.

And he asks for remedy:

  • He’s confused and humble—he wants the Guru’s clarity.
