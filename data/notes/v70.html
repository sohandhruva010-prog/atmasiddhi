Main Theme: Universal Law of Conservation → proves soul is eternal

This verse is the conclusion of the Guru’s rational proof from Verse 62 → Verse 70 establishing: ✅ Soul is eternal (nitya / everlasting)

He literally says: “This concluding verse is the pinnacle.” Even above Verse 69.

1) Why Verse 70 is “Peak” (according to Guruji)

From Verse 62 onward, Guru gave multiple rational solutions:

  • Who is the knower of soul’s creation/destruction? (big logic question)
  • If something isn’t created by any association, there’s no reason it can be destroyed
  • No evidence for soul creation/destruction
  • Consciousness cannot arise from matter (and vice versa)

But Verse 70 is the strongest because it is based on something: ✅ “Indisputable and accepted by science” → Law of Conservation

So the disciple can’t escape it by saying:

  • “That’s Jain philosophy” Because it isn’t just philosophy here — it’s universal logic + science.

2) The Guru’s style: doesn’t demand blind faith

This is emphasized early:

  • Guru doesn’t say “Believe me.”
  • He says: “Prove it or examine it.”
  • He commands respect because he exposes how baseless the argument is.

So this whole section is like: ✅ “Think for yourself. Test it. Examine it.”

That’s why he calls the verse homework-style.

3) The verse itself (the core line)

“Kyāre koi vastu-no keval hoy nāsh;

Chetan pāme nāsh to kema bhaley tapas.”

Word-by-word meaning (important)

Kyāre

= never / at no time

Koi vastu

= any substance (anything that exists as a “real thing”)

Keval

= completely / absolutely / totally

Hoy nāsh

= can be destroyed

➡ So: ✅ No substance can ever be completely destroyed.

Next line:

Chetan

= conscious substance = soul

Pāme nāsh

= gets destroyed

To

= if (hypothetically)

Kema bhaley tapas

= then go ahead, examine and find out (challenge/homework)

➡ Meaning: ✅ If you insist the soul is destroyed, then find what it transforms into.

4) The main scientific principle used (LAW OF CONSERVATION)

Guru says this is: ✅ universal ✅ accepted by science ✅ undeniable

The rule:

Nothing that exists can be entirely destroyed. It only changes form.

He even describes learning it in first standard:

  • “Substance can never be created or destroyed, it only changes form.”
  • solid → liquid → gas (basic school science)

📌 The point is not “religion vs science.” It’s: science confirms the nature of substances.

5) The key discrimination: “Form destroyed” vs “Substance destroyed”

This verse is ALL about one distinction:

✅ What can disappear?

  • Form / shape / state / mode (paryay)

❌ What cannot vanish?

  • Original substance (dravya)

So the disciple is mistaken because he sees:

  • body form changing
  • emotions changing
  • thoughts changing and concludes: ❌ “substance itself dies”

But Guru says: ✅ That’s only destruction of form, not destruction of existence.

6) Guru’s challenge to the disciple (this is the “tapas” part)

Guru basically says:

“Okay, you say the soul is destroyed. Fine.”

Then: ✅ Tell me what it becomes.

Because if something is destroyed completely, it can’t just go into:

  • nothing
  • thin air
  • vanish like magic

He even jokes:

“This is not K Lal’s magic show.”

So the disciple must answer:

If soul gets destroyed, it must convert into something else:

  • Does consciousness become matter?
  • Does it merge into non-living substance?
  • Does it become nothingness?

Guru’s point: ❌ You can’t show any valid transmutation of consciousness into non-consciousness.

So the claim “soul is destroyed” fails.

7) His examples to make it obvious (everyday proof)

He gives multiple examples where form disappears but substance remains:

Nature example cycle:

  • seed → sapling → flower → fruit → seed At each stage, old “form” is gone, but nothing becomes “nothing.”

So: ✅ destruction is only of form, not substance.

Human-life example:

  • childhood → adulthood → old age The “stage” is gone but the being continues.

Clay pot example (very important)

  • pot breaks → what is destroyed? ✅ shape (form)
  • what remains? ✅ clay particles

So you can say:

  • “the pot is destroyed” but you cannot say:
  • “the clay vanished completely”

8) Final logical step: “If matter can’t be destroyed… why would soul be?”

This is the strongest closing move:

Premise 1:

Science accepts matter/substance is indestructible (as substance).

Premise 2:

Soul is also a substance (but conscious).

Conclusion:

✅ If unconscious matter is indestructible, then conscious substance (soul) is also indestructible.

He even says:

Consciousness is far superior than matter.

And he uses the buttermilk vs ghee story:

  • Save ghee because it’s “more superior.”

That’s how he frames it: ✅ If the inferior (matter) doesn’t vanish, the superior (soul) definitely won’t.

9) How Verse 70 completes the disciple’s doubt (Verse 61)

Disciple’s doubt was:

Everything changes every moment → therefore everything is momentary → soul is momentary.

Guru’s closure in 3 steps (68–70):

Verse 68:

✅ Soul is eternal as substance, changing as states (Anekant viewpoint)

Verse 69:

✅ “Only momentary” is illogical because the knower/speaker must continue

Verse 70:

✅ Conservation law: no substance can be totally destroyed → if soul is a substance, it cannot be destroyed → soul is eternal

So Verse 70 is the final seal.

10) The “spiritual takeaway” he sneaks in (very important)

Even though this is a scientific proof, Guru extracts spirituality from it:

If you understand:

✅ “I am the substance (pure soul)” and ✅ “anger is only a passing state (form/modification)” then your anger starts reducing.

Because you stop identifying with the temporary state.

This is the practical use:

Discriminative knowledge (vivek)

  • “This is a phase passing through me”
  • “I am not that phase”
  • “I remain as pure soul”

11) The one-line conclusion of Verse 70

✅ No substance is ever completely destroyed; it only changes form. Since the soul is a substance (conscious), it also cannot be destroyed. Therefore, the soul is eternal.
