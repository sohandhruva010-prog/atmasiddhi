1) Aptness of the Title — Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra

Historical note: The first copy (Aso Vad Ekam) bore only “Atmasiddhi.” By Aso Vad Dasam, in a letter to Lalluji Muni, the composer himself writes “Atmasiddhi Shastra.” “Shri” is an honorific of reverence for a scripture.

Three key words: Siddhi – Atma – Shastra

a) Siddhi — accomplishment / establishing
  • Generic meaning: accomplishment, establishing, proving one’s standpoint.
  • Hence many texts across traditions use siddhi: e.g., Brahmasiddhi, Advaitasiddhi (Vedānta); in Jain tradition Sarvārthasiddhi, Anekāntasiddhi, Sarvajñasiddhi, etc.
b) Atma — the soul
  • Here, siddhi pertains to establishing the truth about the soul via the Six Fundamentals (Chha Pada):

1) Atma chhe — The soul exists

2) Atma nitya chhe — The soul is eternal

3) Atma kartā chhe — The soul is the doer of karmas

4) Atma bhoktā chhe — The soul is the enjoyer/sufferer

5) Mokṣa chhe — Liberation exists

6) Mokṣa upāy chhe — There is a path to liberation

  • Twofold siddhi: (i) Intellectual conviction through logic, reasoning, and examples. (ii) Experiential guidance—the path to Self-realisation.
c) Shastra — status & function
  • A shastra (scripture) inspires, instructs, and prepares the seeker with do’s & don’ts; it cannot inspect—that role is the Sadguru’s.
  • Atmasiddhi Shastra both motivates and hands you to the Sadguru for inner inspection and completion.
  • Though the composer was a householder, Shastra is tied not to robe but to Samyak Jñān (Right Knowledge). Truth issuing from enlightenment is authoritative—hence “Shastra.” Parallels: Mokṣamārga Prakāśaka (Pandit Todarmal, householder), Nātak Samayasāra (Banārisīdās), Chha Dhālā (Kavi Daulatram) are also revered as shastras.
  • Eminent scholar Pandit Sukhlalji lauded Atmasiddhi Shastra as an “Adhyātma Upaniṣad / Adhyātma Gītā” and an “Encyclopaedia of spiritual science.”

Summary of aptness: Atma-siddhi = establishing conviction about the soul and leading to its attainment; Shastra = a scripture flowing from Self-experience, expressed with logical clarity.

2) Subject Matter — What is the text about?

  • Core theme: The Six Fundamentals (shat-pada)—presented logically and experientially.
  • Not primarily a manual on Guru–disciple etiquette (though these are explained); the subject is Ātman and the path to its realization.
  • Non-sectarian, universal appeal: No sect-markers (Śvetāmbara/Digambara, Sthānakvāsi/Derāvāsi). Focus is on truth, not labels. Cause–effect analysis: The text traces delusion → effects and remedies → results, making the path practical & realistic.
  • Sufficiency: Like “encompassing the sea in a bowl,” the essentials are adequately and completely presented; a genuine seeker needs no other source to attain the goal.
  • Mastery in five verses: The disciple’s realisation (119–123) concisely captures the entire path.
  • Two indispensable emphases: Self-effort (Jo iccho parmarth to, karo satya puruṣārth) under the guidance of a Sadguru.

Section-wise outline of the 142 verses

1. Verses 1–23: Obeisance; necessity of giving up self-will; the path of humility.

2. 24–33: Traits of matārthī (bigot): kriyajāḍ (mechanical ritualist) & śuṣkajñānī (dry intellectualist); give up opinionatedness.

3. 34–42: Traits of the atmārthī (true seeker); v.42 announces six fundamentals (Guru–disciple dialogue form).

4. 43–44: Naming the six fundamentals and noting their relation to the six schools (ṣaḍ-darśana).

5. 45–58: Fundamental 1 — Soul exists.

6. 59–70: Fundamental 2 — Soul is eternal.

7. 71–78: Fundamental 3 — Soul is doer.

8. 79–86: Fundamental 4 — Soul is enjoyer/receiver.

9. 87–91: Fundamental 5 — Liberation exists.

10. 92–118: Fundamental 6 — There is a path to liberation.

11. 119–127: Disciple’s attainment; gratitude; resolve to live Guru’s ajñā.

12. 128–142: Fifteen reflective verses; pitfalls to avoid; concluding obeisance.

3) Style / Method (Shaili)

  • Dialogue form: Guru–disciple conversation raises doubts and resolves them patiently and logically.
  • Simple yet profound language; compact verses dense with meaning.
  • Logical demonstrations with everyday examples; cause–effect tracing of delusion and its remedies.
  • Practical realism: Designed for seekers with courage & patience to walk the path.
  • Universal, non-sectarian tone: devotional yet rational; rational yet deeply devotional.

“Not merely a creation of logic; it flows from Self-experience.”

4) Explanations & Translations (Vivechano ane Bhāṣāntaro)

  • Aim: Preserve the meaning, logic, and experiential thrust.
  • Guiding stance: Respect the dialogue and the build-up of conviction; keep the verses memorizable and the sense unambiguous.
  • Practice: Use clear English while retaining key Gujarati terms (atmārthī, matārthī, samyak jñān, ajñān, puruṣārth, ajñā), glossed on first use.
  • Spirit: Commentary should inspire and instruct, and then hand the seeker to the Sadguru— mirroring the shastra’s own role.

5) Mangalācharaṇ — Patit Jan Pāvani

  • Purpose: Align the heart in reverence so it becomes receptive to subtle truth.
  • Function: From bhakti (devotion) to viveka (discernment) to samkit (right vision).
  • The series will explain this invocation before entering Verse 1, tuning the listener’s inner instrument.

Key Verses (for orientation)

Je swaroop samjya vina, pamyo dukh anant;
Samjavyu te pada namun, Shri Sadguru Bhagwant. Without understanding the true Self, one suffers endlessly; I bow to the Enlightened Master who revealed that truth. — Atmasiddhi, 1

Upje te suvicharna, mokshmarg samjay;
Gurushishya-samvādthi, bhākhū shatpad āhī. Right thinking arises, and the path of liberation is understood; hence I state the six fundamentals here in Guru-disciple dialogue. — Atmasiddhi, 42 ‘Atma chhe’, ‘Te nitya chhe’, ‘Chhe kartā nijkarma’;
‘Chhe bhoktā’, vali ‘Mokṣ chhe’, ‘Mokṣ upāy sudharma’. The soul exists, is eternal, is the doer and enjoyer; liberation exists, and there is a path—true religion. — Atmasiddhi, 43

Practical Takeaways

  • Let title clarity become study clarity: Atma-siddhi aims at both conviction and experience.
  • Study with self-effort and under Sadguru’s guidance; avoid opinionatedness.
  • Keep the section map handy as you read; track how each doubt is raised and resolved.
