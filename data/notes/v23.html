Verse 23 — “Hoy matārthī tehne, thāy na ātma-lakṣh; te matārthī lakṣhaṇo, ahīṁ kahyā nirpakṣh.”
One-line essence

A sectarian/bigot (matārthī) simply does not develop ātma-lakṣa—the steady focus/awareness of the Self. Therefore, he cannot reach Self-realisation or liberation. The text now announces: the traits of a matārthī will be presented impartially in the coming verses (24–33) for the seeker’s benefit.

1) Where Verse 23 sits in the flow (the end of the Introduction)

  • 9–18: Primacy of a living Sadguru; inner enemies fall “with little effort.”
  • 19–20: Humility (vinay)—its zenith and its purpose.
  • 21: Warning about asadgurus who exploit devotion (catastrophic delusion).
  • 22: The decisive fork—mumukshu understands rightly; matārthī misreads.
  • 23 (today): States the outcome of matārthī-ness (no ātma-lakṣa → no real progress) and previews the next section: traits of the matārthī (24–33). After that will come the traits of the ātmārthī (true seeker), taught in parallel so we don’t get “negative-only” and discouraged.

2) Key definitions (as used here)

  • Matārthī: sectarian/bigot—devoted to sect-beliefs and belonging, not to truth.
  • Ātmārthī / Mumukshu: truth-centered seeker whose only aim is Self-realisation (and liberation).
  • Ātma-lakṣa: sustained self-awareness / the Self as the living focus throughout activities (the practical precursor to Self-realisation).

3) The verse in two lines (sense and intent)

A) “Hoy matārthī tehne, thāy na ātma-lakṣh”
  • A sectarian never puts the Self in focus.
  • Because Self is not his aim, self-awareness doesn’t grow; without that, how will Self-realisation or liberation arise?
  • He can be very active religiously, but the basic error remains: no Self in view.
B) “Te matārthī lakṣhaṇo, ahīṁ kahyā nirpakṣh”
  • The text will now list the traits of sectarianism objectively—with no person, no sect, no hidden agenda in mind—solely to help the seeker recognise and remove such traits in himself.

4) Atmārthī vs Matārthī — quick contrast recap

Ātmārthī (true seeker)

1. Reverence for truth (not just tradition).

2. Conducts all activities with a spiritual purpose (alignment to Self).

3. Grows ātma-lakṣa everywhere—listening, speaking, eating, serving—so awareness becomes continuous.

Matārthī (sectarian)

1. Interested in sect-beliefs & belonging, not truth (societal standing, ashram “fit,” group identity).

2. Activities are external & mechanical—ritual for show/scorekeeping, not for inner shift.

3. Self isn’t on the radar—no inquiry into soul, no contemplation; even virtue-cultivation is neglected (“I don’t want anger/greed/deceit/pride” isn’t on the list).

  • Vivid image: neem tree at the edge of a sugarcane field—standing next to sweetness, yet only growing bitterness.

5) What “no ātma-lakṣa” looks like in real life

  • Religious sincerity without spiritual aim: fasts, samayik, mālās, seasonal vows—counted, displayed, but not used to soften ego or increase Self-awareness.
  • Community-driven practice: “I do this because in our society/ashram/mission you have to,” not because it serves awakening.
  • No questions about fundamentals (Does the soul exist? Is it eternal? What continues after death? How does transmigration work?).
  • Even virtue-hunger is absent (no felt need to reduce anger, pride, deceit, greed). Thus transmigration increases despite “religiosity.”

Tragic twist: if he were openly worldly, he might one day make an about-turn. But misplaced religiosity convinces him he’s “far ahead,” while he’s actually going nowhere.

6) Why the upcoming list of matārthī-traits matters

  • It’s not to attack anyone; it’s a mirror so a sincere person can spot, admit, and correct sectarian tendencies in himself.
  • Aim: spiritual welfare, strengthening the Self-realisation goal. If a trait is seen, recognise → remove. That’s all.

7) Self-check: am I drifting into matārthī-mode?

Ask yourself, honestly:

  • When I practice, is my target the Self—or membership/approval/appearance?
  • After today’s ritual/satsang, did my self-will thin and Self-awareness rise—or did I just add one more tick to my tally?
  • Do I seek explanations, meanings, fundamentals—or do I avoid them as “difficult” and stay on the surface?
  • Am I cultivating at least basic virtues (less anger/ego/deceit/greed) week by week?

If the answers sting, that’s good news—you’re seeing where to work.

8) How to live Verse 23 (today)

1. Name the aim: Before any practice, whisper the aim: “This is to increase ātma-lakṣa.”

2. One-minute anchoring: After each session, sit one minute—feel the knower quietly aware of breath/body/mind.

3. Tie the ritual to inner shift: For every outer act, write the inner change it serves (e.g., prostration → “reduce self-will, receive ājñā”).

4. Virtue micro-goal: Choose one kashāya (anger/pride/deceit/greed) and track one visible softening today.

5. Satsang filter: When you hear a teaching, ask, “What is the intent (āśay)? How does this move me toward ātma-lakṣa?”

9) Compact recap (one breath)

Verse 23 says it plainly: a matārthī doesn’t grow Self-focus, so he cannot get Self-realisation—no matter how many religious acts he performs. Therefore, the scripture now lists the marks of sectarianism impartially (24–33) so a sincere person can recognise and remove them, and return to the ātmārthī track where vinay, ājñā-following, and ātma-lakṣa steadily carry him home.
