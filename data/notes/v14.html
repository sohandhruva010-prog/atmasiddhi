Extensive Study Notes (How to progress when constant association with the living Sadguru is absent)

Built from your Video 14 transcript. Structured for smooth study and printing as a PDF. Verse 13 covered the crisis of no living Sadguru. Verse 14 covers the crisis of having a living Sadguru but lacking continuous company—what to do between meetings.

1) Position in the flow

  • 9: Necessity of Guru.
  • 10: Five qualities of a Sadguru.
  • 11–12: Living Sadguru’s upkār; only through His teaching does devotion to Jina become fruitful.
  • 13: If no Sadguru is found, take śāstra as support (soul & non-soul fundamentals) till you meet Him.
  • 14: If you have a Sadguru but lack constant samagam, do three things with what He recommends.

2) The verse — short meaning (from the talk)

“Athvā (or): The texts that the Sadguru has recommended should be 1) studied deeply (avgahan kaj),

2) contemplated regularly/daily (te te nitya vicharvā), 3) with personal prejudices/opinions renounced (kari matāntar tyaj), for the soul’s benefit.”

Given a choice: choose the Guru’s guidance over mere scriptural study—because scripture informs, but the Guru destroys ego and delusion. Verse 14 is what you do in His physical absence, not a replacement for Him.

3) Why this verse matters

A true Guru is rare; continuous proximity is rarer. Without a plan for the in-between, zeal leaks and confusion creeps in. Verse 14 gives a concise, actionable rule-of-life for the gap.

4) The three instructions unpacked

A) Avgahan kaj — Study deeply

Aim: Everything you study is for Self-realisation (not prestige, debate, or hobby).

The Four R’s (from the talk): 1) Read – receive the words. 2) Reflect – what changed in my understanding and what must I do today because of it? 3) Ruminate – dwell till it sinks; revisit lines, analogies, causes & effects. 4) Reinforce – codify as a small vow or behaviour; check it next sitting.

Deep study ≠ collecting quotes/notes or admiring logic. It ends in application. Foundations formed this way make you steady in any life-role.

Securing the right literature: Ask, “O Lord, in Your absence, what should I read?” Accept His exact picks and order, even if surprising.

Illustrative caution (from the talk): The Master withheld Ātmasiddhi and Samayasāra from a seeker and first prescribed Yogavāsiṣṭha Rāmāyaṇa (2 chapters) to build detachment and yearning. Right text at the right time prevents dry intellectualism.

B) Te te nitya vicharvā — Contemplate daily/regularly

  • Regularity over mood. Lovers are regular. Make a fixed daily slot, however small.
  • Cadence suggestions:
  • Daily (10–20 min): Read + Reflect + one Reinforcement vow.
  • Weekly: Study circle or satsang; share applications, not just insights.
  • Analogy: If you are faithful with medical pills, be more faithful with spiritual pills.

C) Kari matāntar tyaj — Renounce opinions & prejudices

This is the first renunciation on the path (before clothes/house): drop opinions, fixed notions, sectarian habits, traditional rigidities.

Posture: - “My Guru is always right; if I don’t understand, I will change my understanding.” - Stop disguised guidance (“May I do it this way?”). Seek ājñā and obey. - Let go of attachments to persons/sects/styles that hold your ego in place.

Why this matters: Without dropping opinions, study turns dangerous—fuel for dry intellectualism. With opinions dropped, one line can transform a life.

5) Practical blueprint for the “gap” (between two meetings)

Daily (15–25 min): - 5 min grounding (silence; recall Guru’s presence). - 5–10 min Four R’s on the assigned text. - 3–5 min report to journal (what I applied, where I resisted, what to correct).

Weekly (45–60 min): - Group review of the same assigned text; each shares one application and one ego-opinion dropped.

Monthly (30–40 min): - Audit: regularity, application, and opinion-dropping. Adjust plan per honest findings.

Always: Keep asking for next reading order. If you lack explicit order, re-study the last assignment more deeply instead of roaming texts.

6) Red flags & course-corrections

Red flag: Studying broadly without applying narrowly → course-correct by shrinking scope and deepening practice.

  • Red flag: Frequent debates; urge to correct the Guru’s method → course-correct with matāntar tyāg reflection and obedience drills.
  • Red flag: Sporadic sittings tied to mood → course-correct by fixing a tiny, immovable daily slot.

7) One-page recap

  • Context: You have a living Sadguru but lack continuous association.
  • Instruction: On His recommended texts, practise (1) deep study, (2) daily contemplation, (3) opinion-renunciation—all for the soul’s benefit.
  • Spirit: Choose the Guru whenever you have a choice; Verse 14 is the bridge between His visits.
  • Outcome: Zeal sustained, delusion weakened, foundations strengthened; you keep moving toward Self-realisation even while physically apart.

8) Reflection prompts

1) What single opinion will I drop this week to honour matāntar tyāg? 2) Which one sentence from today’s reading will I reinforce as a vow for 24 hours? 3) When will my immovable daily slot be? (Start small; never miss.)
