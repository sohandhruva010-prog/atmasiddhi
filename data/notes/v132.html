Verse 132 of the Upsamhar, and it completes the teaching begun in Verse 131.

Verse 131 warned:

Don't hear “I am the pure soul” and therefore abandon spiritual practices. Keep the pure Self as the goal, but continue the means required to realize it.

Verse 132 now explains why both Nishchay and Vyavhar are necessary and makes an even deeper point:

Neither viewpoint by itself is the complete truth.

The verse is essentially:

“Naya nishchay ekantthi, ama nathi kahel;
Ekante vyavhar nahi, banne sath rahel.”

Meaning:

In Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra, neither the absolute viewpoint nor the relative viewpoint has been presented one-sidedly. Both have been maintained together in harmony. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 132: Nishchay + Vyavhar — Both Must Travel Together

Gurudev opens with a beautiful summary of the path:

Awareness of the Pure Self + Right Conduct = Moksh Marg

That's the combination.

The path itself is simple, Gurudev says.

“The path is simple, but we are complex beings.”

And therefore we take something simple, misunderstand it, become one-sided, and go astray.

That's why the Upsamhar saves us—it exposes the subtle mistakes we can make with otherwise correct spiritual teachings. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

1. The problem isn't Nishchay — it's EKANT Nishchay

Gurudev emphasizes this carefully.

Nishchay is right.

The absolute viewpoint is not the problem.

The problem is:

Nishchay Ekant

One-sided insistence on Nishchay alone.

Similarly:

Vyavhar is right.

But:

Vyavhar Ekant
One-sided insistence on Vyavhar alone

is also incomplete.

Param Krupalu Dev says:

Neither has been taught one-sidedly in Atmasiddhi.

Instead:

“Banne sath rahel.”

Both remain together. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

2. One may be speaking while the other remains in awareness

This is an important distinction.

Suppose Param Krupalu Dev is describing the eternal nature of the soul.

Then He may say:

Pure.

Knowledgeable.

Full of consciousness.

Self-luminous.

Abode of bliss.

That's Nishchay.

But suppose He is talking about our current spiritual state.

Then:

You need renunciation.

You need detachment.

You need to pacify passions.

You need spiritual practice.

That's Vyavhar.

So one viewpoint may be in the foreground, but the other hasn't been rejected.

It simply isn't the purpose of that particular statement. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

3. Gurudev's simple quiz trick 😂

Gurudev gives a useful way to distinguish the two.

If the statement describes something ETERNAL:

Something that:

was, is, and always will be

→ think Nishchay / absolute viewpoint.

For example:

The inherent nature of the soul is pure consciousness.

If the statement involves CHANGE:

impure → pure

passions → pacification

bondage → liberation

spiritual development

→ think Vyavhar / relative viewpoint.

Because now we're describing the state/modifications of the soul rather than its eternal inherent nature. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

That distinction is very helpful.

4. Naya vs Praman

Now Gurudev introduces another important concept.

To understand reality, Jain philosophy speaks of:

Naya = viewpoint

and

Praman = complete knowledge/truth of the object

Gurudev simplifies it:

Naya = partial truth
Praman = complete truth

A viewpoint doesn't necessarily say something false.

It says something partially.

That's a huge distinction. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

5. BOTH Nishchay and Vyavhar are partial truths

This is probably the most important philosophical point in the satsang.

Nishchay is a Naya.

Therefore:

partial viewpoint.

Vyavhar is also a Naya.

Therefore:

partial viewpoint.

So if I say:

“The soul is pure.”

True.

But from a particular viewpoint.

If I say:

“My present state is deeply impure.”

Also true.

From another viewpoint.

Neither statement alone gives the entire reality of the soul. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

6. “But how can I be PURE and IMPURE?”

This sounds contradictory until we understand viewpoints.

Nishchay:

My inherent eternal nature is pure.

Vyavhar:

My present modifications/state contain attachment, aversion, passions and karmic bondage.

Therefore:

Pure in nature.

Impure in present state.

No contradiction.

The contradiction appears only when we demand that every statement must describe reality from the same viewpoint.

7. Gurudev's UNCLE example

Gurudev uses ordinary relationships to explain this.

He can be:

  • someone's paternal uncle,
  • someone's maternal uncle,
  • someone's son,
  • someone's father.

Are these false?

No.

But each identity exists in relation to someone else.

That's why it's a relative viewpoint.

If someone says:

“You're an uncle.”

That's true in a particular relation.

But that isn't the complete description of his existence.

Likewise:

“The soul is impure.”

may describe one dimension/state.

Don't mistake one relational truth for the entire substance. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

8. The GLASS OF WATER example

This is Gurudev's best everyday example.

Someone asks:

“What is this?”

We say:

“A glass of water.”

But Gurudev says:

Wait. The glass isn't made of water.

It's made of steel.

So is it:

a glass of steel

or

a glass of water?

Answer:

Both are correct from different viewpoints.

From the material/substance perspective:

Steel glass.

From practical usage:

Glass of water.

The practical statement isn't false simply because it isn't philosophically exhaustive. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

9. “The station has arrived!”

Gurudev gives another great example.

You're sleeping on a train.

Someone wakes you:

“Get up! The station has arrived!”

Technically:

The station did NOT arrive.

The station hasn't moved an inch.

The train arrived at the station.

So should you respond:

“Your statement is philosophically false. I refuse to collect my luggage.”

😂

No.

In practical life:

“The station has arrived”

is perfectly understood.

So:

Vyavhar doesn't have to be rejected merely because Nishchay can describe something more precisely. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

10. “You are a girl” — true or false?

Gurudev gives another example.

From Vyavhar:

“You are a girl.”

Meaning:

the soul is currently associated with a female body.

Perfectly acceptable practical language.

From Nishchay:

You are not fundamentally male or female.

The body is female.

You are the soul distinct from the body.

Also true.

So:

“You are a girl.”

→ true from relative viewpoint.

“You are not a girl; you are pure soul.”

→ true from absolute viewpoint.

Again:

Viewpoint resolves apparent contradiction. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

11. Then why do we even NEED viewpoints?

Gurudev asks an excellent question.

If everyone wants the complete truth — Praman, why bother learning partial viewpoints?

Why not just state everything completely every time?

Because:

Language cannot express every dimension simultaneously.

You may know both simultaneously.

But when you speak, language unfolds sequentially.

You have to emphasize one aspect first, then another. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

This is a really important philosophical point.

12. Even the Omniscient Lord has to SPEAK sequentially

Gurudev says:

Omniscience can know all dimensions simultaneously.

But speech has a limitation.

When expressing something verbally:

first one statement,

then another.

So when the Enlightened One speaks from Nishchay:

Nishchay becomes Mukhya — emphasized.

Vyavhar becomes:

Gaun — subordinated.

Then when the purpose changes:

Vyavhar becomes emphasized.

Nishchay becomes subordinated.

But:

Subordinated ≠ rejected. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

That distinction is central to this satsang.

13. Gurudev's Hansa example 😂

Gurudev remembers asking someone:

“Is Hansa your wife or not?”

Instead of simply saying:

“Yes.”

the person apparently began:

“From the absolute viewpoint…”

😂

Gurudev's point:

Don't unnecessarily complicate Vyavhar.

If someone asks:

“Is she your wife?”

the practical answer is:

Yes.

You don't need a lecture on soul-body distinction every time.

Use the viewpoint appropriate to the purpose of the conversation. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

14. Mukhya and Gaun — emphasized and subordinated

This gives us a really useful framework.

At any given moment:

One viewpoint may be Mukhya

emphasized because it's relevant.

The other becomes:

Gaun

present but subordinated.

The mistake is turning:

Gaun

into:

Nonexistent / false / rejected.

Gurudev says:

To subordinate is correct.

To negate is wrong knowledge. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

15. The BUTTER-CHURNING analogy

This is probably Gurudev's most beautiful analogy in the lecture.

Imagine churning curd/buttermilk to obtain butter.

You have a rope with two ends.

One end = Nishchay.

Other end = Vyavhar.

You pull:

this side

then:

that side

then:

this side

then:

that side.

One becomes prominent while the other moves back.

Then they reverse.

And through this alternating movement:

Butter emerges.

Similarly:

by correctly using both viewpoints, realization of the Self emerges. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

16. “You are PURE!”

Suppose someone becomes completely crushed by guilt:

“I'm terrible.”

“I'm impure.”

“I've messed everything up.”

“I'm hopeless.”

What viewpoint may Guru emphasize?

Nishchay.

You are pure consciousness.

Your inherent nature remains untouched.

Return to your Self.

Why?

Because that's what the seeker needs at that moment.

17. “You need to purify yourself!”

Now suppose the same seeker becomes complacent:

“I'm already pure!”

“Why discipline myself?”

“Why reduce jealousy?”

“Why follow Ajna?”

“Everything is just the body anyway.”

Now Guru switches:

Vyavhar.

Look at your jealousy.

Look at your comparison.

Look at your passions.

Your present state is impure.

Make effort.

Same Guru.

Same soul.

Different emphasis.

Because the spiritual requirement changed. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

This is a really beautiful explanation of why Guru may sometimes seem to say opposite things.

18. “Jya jya je je yogya chhe…”

Gurudev expresses the principle essentially as:

Whatever is appropriate, wherever it is appropriate.

That's how the Enlightened One teaches.

Not mechanically:

Nishchay always.

Not:

Vyavhar always.

But:

What does this seeker need right now to move toward truth?

That viewpoint becomes emphasized.

The other remains present in the Enlightened One's knowledge. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

19. The RAILWAY TRACK analogy

This may be the easiest analogy to remember.

Think of railway tracks.

Two rails run for:

  • hundreds,
  • thousands,
  • even several thousand kilometers.

They never merge.

But:

The train cannot move without BOTH.

Gurudev says:

One track = Nishchay.

One track = Vyavhar.

They remain distinct viewpoints.

But:

The train of spiritual pursuit advances only with the support of both. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

This perfectly summarizes verses 131–132.

20. One-sided Vyavhar is ALSO wrong

Verse 131 focused heavily on someone intoxicated with Nishchay.

But Gurudev makes sure we don't swing to the opposite extreme.

Someone may say:

“I'm impure.”

“I need austerities.”

“I need meditation.”

“I need charity.”

“I need prayers.”

“I need to pacify passions.”

“Eventually I'll become pure.”

Then they say:

“Why do I need all this talk about an eternally pure Self?”

That's also one-sided.

Because now spiritual practice has lost sight of what you're actually trying to realize. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

21. Two opposite extremes

So we now have:

Extreme 1 — Vyavhar only
“I am impure. I must do things to become pure.”

No recognition of eternal pure nature.

Extreme 2 — Nishchay only
“I was, am and always will be pure.”

Therefore:

bondage and liberation are merely imagination.

no practice necessary.

Both sound logical within their own framework.

But:

Both are partial truths.

And Gurudev says:

Your train needs both tracks. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

22. The CHESS example

Gurudev asks:

Two people playing chess:

Friends or enemies?

If you say:

Friends

then:

Why are they attacking each other's queen?

😂

If you say:

Enemies

then:

Why did they voluntarily sit together to play?

Answer:

Both.

From one viewpoint:

friends.

Within the game:

opponents.

You need two viewpoints to describe the complete situation.

Likewise Gurudev asks:

Is the soul pure or impure?

Answer:

Both — from different viewpoints. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

23. Atmasiddhi itself demonstrates perfect balance

Gurudev says Param Krupalu Dev didn't merely teach the balance.

The entire Atmasiddhi Shastra embodies it.

At some points He paints the:

ideal eternal nature

Pure.

Conscious.

Blissful.

Indestructible.

Then elsewhere:

practical present condition

Pacify passions.

Cultivate renunciation.

Develop detachment.

Follow spiritual practices.

Purify your state.

And Gurudev says the movement between these is so smooth that we often don't even notice the viewpoint has changed. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

24. Ideal vs Practical

Gurudev gives another simple formulation:

Nishchay = IDEAL

This is what I fundamentally am.

Vyavhar = PRACTICAL

This is where I currently stand and what I must do.

And Param Krupalu Dev beautifully balances:

Focus on the ideal.

Work on the practical. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

That may be the cleanest one-line summary of the satsang.

25. Meditation vs introspection

Near the end Gurudev makes this extremely practical.

Suppose you're sitting for:

Meditation.

Then Nishchay is powerful:

I am pure.

I am consciousness.

I am peaceful.

I am blissful.

You're turning toward your eternal nature.

But suppose you're doing:

Introspection.

Then you cannot sit there saying:

“I'm already pure. Nothing wrong here!”

😂

You have to examine:

Here's jealousy.

Here's comparison.

Here's ego.

Here's attachment.

Here's anger.

That's Vyavhar.

Otherwise introspection becomes useless. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

26. Contemplation helps us move between the two

Gurudev gives a beautiful progression:

Introspection

recognize the current impurity.

↓

Contemplation understand what is Self and what is non-Self; understand the teachings correctly.

↓

Meditation

turn toward and abide in the pure Self.

So spiritual practice isn't:

“I'm impure, impure, impure…”

forever.

Nor:

“I'm pure, pure, pure…”

while ignoring what's actually happening within.

There is a movement between honest awareness of the present state and awareness of the eternal Self. Pasted text(20261110-213044)

How Verses 131 and 132 fit together

These really are a pair.

Verse 131 asks:
What should I DO?

Keep Nishchay in focus.

Continue the spiritual means.

Goal + practice.

↓

Verse 132 asks:
Why?

Because:

Nishchay alone = partial truth.

Vyavhar alone = partial truth.

The complete spiritual understanding requires:

both viewpoints in harmony.

The deepest practical takeaway

I think the most useful teaching from this satsang is:

Don't confuse emphasis with rejection.

Sometimes BAPA may emphasize:

“You are the pure soul.”

That doesn't mean:

“Your present jealousy, anger, attachment and ego don't matter.”

And sometimes He may strongly point out:

“Look at your ego. Look at your impurity. Work on this.”

That doesn't mean:

“You are fundamentally worthless or impure.”

The viewpoint has changed because what you need at that moment has changed.

That is why Gurudev's butter-churning analogy is so good:

Nishchay ←→ Vyavhar
Nishchay ←→ Vyavhar

One becomes emphasized.

Then the other.

But neither disappears.

And through that movement:

the butter of realization emerges.

The railway analogy makes the same point even more simply:

The two rails never become one.

But remove either one:

the train derails.

So if I become obsessed only with my flaws:

“I'm jealous, I'm angry, I'm terrible, I'm impure…”

I need Nishchay:

That isn't your eternal nature. Turn toward the pure Self.

But if I become spiritually arrogant:

“I'm pure consciousness. Nothing touches me.”

while comparison, jealousy and ego continue internally…

I need Vyavhar:

Look honestly at your present state and do the work.

That creates an incredibly healthy spiritual balance:

Never let your present impurity make you forget your pure nature.

But equally:

Never let knowledge of your pure nature make you deny your present impurity.

The first creates despair.

The second creates delusion/complacency.

The middle path gives us:

Hope from Nishchay.

Work from Vyavhar.

Or in Gurudev's beautifully simple formulation:

Focus on THAT.

Your pure eternal nature.

Work on THIS.

Your present state.

And that integration of the ideal and practical is the path of liberation Param Krupalu Dev is preserving throughout Atmasiddhi.
