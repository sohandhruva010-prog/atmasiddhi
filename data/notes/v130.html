This is Verse 130 of the Upsamhar, and now the warnings Gurudev introduced at the beginning of Section 12 really start.

Verse 129 gave us the treatment for Self-delusion:

Disease → Atmabhranti Doctor → Sadguru Regimen → Guru Ajna Medicine → Vichar + Dhyan

Verse 130 now asks:

You know the treatment—but are you actually going to DO it, or are you going to find sophisticated spiritual excuses for not doing it?

The verse is essentially:

“Jo iccho parmarth toh, karo satya purusharth;
Bhavsthiti adi naam lai, chhedo nahi atmarth.”

Meaning:

If you truly desire the supreme spiritual goal, put forth the right effort. Do not obstruct your spiritual welfare by using destiny, time and similar factors as excuses. Pasted text(20261109-181803) Pasted text(20261109-181803)

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 130: If You Truly Want It, Make the Right Effort

Gurudev says the Upsamhar is now going to expose one pitfall after another—mistakes that can make us incapable of curing the Self-delusion described in verse 129.

The first major warning is:

Don't use spiritual philosophy to justify spiritual laziness. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

1. “Jo iccho parmarth toh…” — IF you really desire it

Gurudev begins with the first word:

Jo — IF.

That “if” matters.

IF you desire Parmarth…

Parmarth can mean the:

  • Supreme Truth,
  • Self-realisation,
  • enlightenment,
  • liberation,
  • ultimate spiritual goal.

Gurudev essentially asks:

Do you actually want it?

Because it's very easy to say:

“Of course I want Moksh.”

“Of course I want Self-realisation.”

“Of course I want BAPA's rajipo.”

But when something else attracts us, our behavior may reveal what we actually prioritize.

So Gurudev says we can almost use this line as a mantra to challenge ourselves:

“If I really want Parmarth… then what is this nonsense I'm doing?” Pasted text(20261109-181803)

Not to condemn ourselves.

But to stop turning weaknesses into excuses.

2. Gurudev describes a very specific spiritual danger

This verse isn't primarily for someone who has never met Guru or never cared about spirituality.

Gurudev describes someone who:

1. was spiritually lost,

2. received the grace of meeting an Enlightened One,

3. found direction,

4. found a goal,

5. began satsang, bhakti and sadhana,

6. genuinely wanted Guru's rajipo and Self-realisation…

and then:

started slipping.

That's what makes this verse so relevant. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

3. The Four P's that pull the seeker back

Gurudev says after progressing for some time, the seeker can begin slipping back toward the 4 P's:

Position

Status, recognition, importance.

Power

Influence, control.

Possessions

Money, accumulation, parigraha.

Pleasures

Sensual/worldly enjoyment.

The seeker's focus quietly changes.

Originally:

Guru's rajipo + Self-realisation.

Slowly:

position + power + possessions + pleasures.

And often we don't immediately admit:

“My priorities have changed.”

Instead:

we begin justifying ourselves. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

4. Drifting itself isn't the biggest problem — EXCUSING it is

Gurudev is actually quite compassionate about fluctuation.

He says he doesn't expect every day to be spiritually identical.

There may be periods where:

only tears for Guru arise,

and then periods where:

sensual pleasures strongly attack.

You may experience:

  • laziness,
  • weaker awareness,
  • different associations,
  • drifting.

The important thing is:

Don't disguise the drift as philosophy.

Recognize:

“Yes, I am slipping.”

Then recharge:

“No. This isn't my goal.”

Rather than inventing an intellectual explanation for why no effort is required. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

5. Param Krupalu Dev doesn't say “tremendous effort”

This is an important distinction.

The verse doesn't say:

Khub purusharth — lots of effort.

Nor:

Prachand purusharth — tremendous effort.

It says:

Satya Purusharth — RIGHT / TRUE effort.

So the issue isn't merely:

“How much am I doing?”

The issue is:

“Am I doing the thing that actually moves me toward Self-realisation?” Pasted text(20261109-181803)

6. What is NOT necessarily Satya Purusharth?

Gurudev gives examples.

Suppose I say:

“I'll fast for eight days.”

Or:

“I'll fast for sixteen days.”

Or perform:

  • enormous physical austerities,
  • mechanical rituals,
  • hours of scriptural study,
  • hours and hours of satsang.

Gurudev says:

That alone is not necessarily Satya Purusharth.

He isn't rejecting fasting, bhakti, satsang or scriptural study.

He's asking:

What are they producing within you? Pasted text(20261109-181803)

7. Satya Purusharth = increasing Self-awareness

Gurudev gives the actual definition.

Right effort means:

Listen to Sadguru.

↓

Understand what He teaches about the Supreme Truth.

↓

Recognize the unfathomable glory and splendour of the soul.

↓

Increase its importance within yourself.

↓

Direct your energies toward practicing it.

↓

Increase Self-awareness.

↓

Stabilize that awareness.

↓

Experience bliss beyond senses and thought.

That is Satya Purusharth. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

So fasting can be right effort if it increases Self-awareness.

Satsang can be right effort if it increases Self-awareness.

Bhakti can be right effort if it increases Self-awareness.

The activity is not the final measurement.

Inner movement toward the Self is.

This directly connects with the earlier teaching:

Without inner purity, even your seva and projects can miss the path.

8. Guru can guide you — but YOU have to walk

This is another major theme.

Gurudev emphasizes the word:

“Karo” — DO.

Param Krupalu Dev doesn't say:

“I'll do it for you.”

He says:

YOU do the right effort.

Guru can:

  • guide,
  • instruct,
  • inspire,
  • create a conducive atmosphere,
  • hold your hand,
  • awaken you through His words.

But ultimately:

You have to wake up.

Gurudev says:

“I'll shout so that you can get up from your slumber. But waking up is your effort.” Pasted text(20261109-181803)

9. The person drowning example

Earlier Gurudev describes us as a lost person wandering around Earth.

Then, due to past merits:

An Enlightened Being holds your hand.

Like someone drowning whose hand is suddenly caught.

Everything changes.

Now:

  • you have direction,
  • someone redirects you when you wander,
  • someone guides you,
  • someone tells you right from wrong.

But Guru isn't holding you so that you remain spiritually dependent forever.

Eventually:

you have to learn to walk. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

10. Sardar Patel and Gandhi example 😂

Gurudev tells a story about Gandhi and Sardar Patel.

A young man apparently wrote Gandhi a very devotional letter essentially saying:

“Bapu, I have full faith in you. You will have to do my welfare.”

Gandhi asks Patel how he should respond.

Patel gives a characteristically sharp response:

essentially, “Nobody is sitting free to do everything for you.”

Gurudev uses it humorously to make the spiritual point:

You have to stand on your own feet.

Guru will help you up.

But you cannot expect to be carried forever. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

11. Spiritual maturity changes the relationship with Guru

Gurudev makes a beautiful distinction.

Early in the path:

“Guruji, tell me.”

“Guruji, guide me.”

“Guruji, what do I do?”

That's natural.

But after years of association, Guru wants maturity.

Gurudev expresses the feeling almost as:

“Guruji, now I have grown. I can walk straight on my own. Everything You gave me, I now want to live for You.”

The Guru has trained you so that eventually you can put forth right effort yourself. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

This does not mean becoming independent of Guru spiritually.

It means becoming less helpless and more capable of living what Guru has already taught you.

12. “Only Your grace can do it” can ALSO become an excuse

This is subtle.

Saying:

“Everything happens through Guru's grace.”

can be a beautiful expression of humility.

But Gurudev says if after years of association we use:

“Only if You do it…”

to avoid effort, then the same language has become spiritually unhealthy.

At that stage, such language should help eliminate:

ego and doership

—not:

knowledge, responsibility or confidence.

Guru's grace should not become an excuse for passivity. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

13. The second half: DON'T block your Atmarth with excuses

Now comes:

“Bhavsthiti adi naam lai, chhedo nahi atmarth.”

Essentially:

Don't take concepts such as destiny, time, etc. and use them to obstruct your own spiritual welfare.

Gurudev makes an important distinction:

These concepts are NOT false.

The mistake is using true concepts wrongly.

That's much more dangerous. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

14. Excuse #1 — “Moksh isn't possible in the fifth era anyway”

Someone says:

“Liberation isn't possible in the fifth era.”

Then:

“So why should I put in intense effort?”

Gurudev says the premise may contain a valid doctrinal point.

But what are you using it for?

To justify indolence.

That's the problem.

A truth has been moved from the background into the foreground to justify not making effort. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

15. Excuse #2 — “There are no Tirthankars today”

“Where is Bhagwan's Samavasaran?”

“There are no living Tirthankars around me.”

“How am I supposed to progress?”

Again:

circumstance → excuse → reduced effort.

16. Excuse #3 — “True Enlightened Ones are too rare”

This one is especially interesting because Gurudev acknowledges:

Yes. Sadgurus ARE rare.

Even if you meet one:

  • circumstances may prevent constant proximity,
  • responsibilities may interfere,
  • the conducive environment may not remain,
  • you may not receive unlimited time.

All true.

But again:

Does this truth inspire greater effort—or justify less effort?

That's the test. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

17. Excuse #4 — Krambaddh Paryay

Gurudev briefly introduces:

Krambaddh Paryay

Modes occurring in sequential order.

Someone reasons:

“The Omniscient One has already seen when everything will happen.”

“My liberation is already going to occur at a certain point.”

“So what difference does my effort make?”

Again Gurudev says:

The doctrine isn't necessarily wrong.

Your interpretation is wrong.

Why?

Because your understanding is producing:

less effort.

A correct spiritual understanding should inspire right effort—not become permission for laziness. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

18. Excuse #5 — Bhavsthiti / destiny

This is the main word in the verse.

Gurudev gives the simplified meaning:

Bhavsthiti = the number of births destined before liberation.

Suppose someone asks Guru:

“How many births until my liberation?”

And hypothetically receives:

“15 births.”

Then the mind says:

“Great. It's fixed.”

“Whether I put in effort or remain lazy, liberation will happen after 15 births anyway.”

Therefore:

Why bother?

And Param Krupalu Dev says:

NO.

Do not take the name of Bhavsthiti and use it to abandon Purusharth. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

19. The real issue: the excuse isn't totally FALSE

This point is extremely important.

Gurudev says:

“What he's saying is not totally wrong, but he is using it wrongly.”

That's exactly why these pitfalls are dangerous.

If someone said:

“Moksh doesn't exist.”

Easy.

We know that's contrary to the teaching.

But someone says:

“Karma exists.”

Correct.

“Destiny exists.”

Correct.

“Time matters.”

Correct.

“The fifth era creates limitations.”

Correct within the framework Gurudev is teaching.

Then quietly adds:

“…therefore I don't need to make much effort.”
THAT is where truth becomes an excuse. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

20. The Five Causal Factors — Samvay Karan

To explain why effort isn't the only factor but is still essential, Gurudev introduces the Jain framework of five causal factors.

An effect materializes when the relevant causes come together.

He lists:

1. Kaal — Time

2. Swabhav — Nature

3. Bhavitavyata — Destiny / destined occurrence

4. Karma — Past effort

5. Purusharth — Present effort All five matter. Pasted text(20261109-181803) Pasted text(20261109-181803)

This is extremely important because Gurudev is continuing the Anekant / balance theme from verse 128.

21. Cause 1 — KAAL: Time

Some things require a certain amount of time.

Gurudev gives several examples.

Pregnancy

You can't say:

“I have tremendous enthusiasm. Let's deliver the baby in four months.”

Effort doesn't override the required time.

Mango tree

Plant it today.

You can't say:

“I'll spend ₹1 crore. Give me mangoes this season.”

The cause needs time to mature.

Yogurt

You add the culture to milk.

Five minutes later:

“Why isn't the yogurt ready?!”

Because:

Kaal hasn't matured. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

22. Spiritual application of Kaal

You may be putting tremendous effort toward Samyak Darshan.

Yet the result hasn't appeared.

What do we immediately think?

“I'm doing something wrong.”

Maybe.

But Gurudev says:

Perhaps the time hasn't ripened.

He compares:

  • Lord Mallinath attaining Omniscience extremely quickly,
  • Lord Mahavira taking twelve and a half years.

So:

Don't become frustrated merely because effort hasn't immediately produced the visible result.

Purusharth isn't the only cause. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

23. Cause 2 — SWABHAV: Nature

The substance must possess the potential to produce that result.

Gurudev gives simple examples:

Mango from lemon tree?

Impossible.

Why?

Its nature doesn't contain that potential.

Likewise:

Table → liberation?

Impossible.

It is an inanimate substance.

Soul → liberation?

Possible.

Because consciousness possesses that potential.

So:

The nature of the substance must support the effect. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

24. Cause 3 — BHAVITAVYATA

Gurudev gives the mango-flower example.

A mango tree produces many flowers.

Some become mangoes.

Others perish.

Why does one develop and another not?

Within this framework:

Bhavitavyata — the effect occurs when it is destined to occur.

Gurudev doesn't go deeply into the philosophy here, but includes it as another causal factor that cannot simply be ignored. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

25. Cause 4 — KARMA = past effort

Gurudev explains karma here almost as:

Past Purusharth.

Someone seemingly less capable may suddenly become wealthy.

Someone brilliant may struggle financially.

Why?

The present visible effort isn't the only variable.

Past karmic causes are also operating.

Gurudev therefore says we can't judge everything simply through:

“Who worked harder today?” Pasted text(20261109-181803)

26. The Mansarovar example

Gurudev gives the example of people who were extremely disciplined about:

  • diet,
  • exercise,
  • health,

yet suffered serious altitude/oxygen issues at Mansarovar.

Meanwhile others with apparently less disciplined habits remained relatively fine.

His point is not that healthy habits are useless.

It's:

Present effort is not the ONLY cause determining an outcome.

Karma is another causal factor within the framework he is explaining. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

27. Cause 5 — PURUSHARTH = present effort

Finally:

Karma = past effort
Purusharth = present effort

All five causes operate together.

And Gurudev emphasizes:

You cannot disregard any.

You cannot negate any.

You cannot overglorify one and forsake the others. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

This is pure continuation of Verse 128's Anekant teaching.

28. Then why does Param Krupalu Dev emphasize Purusharth?

Here's the key.

Gurudev asks:

Do you know your Kaal?

No.

Do you know exactly when the right time will mature?

No.

Do you fully know Swabhav?

No.

Do you know Bhavitavyata?

No.

Do you know the exact karmas remaining?

No.

But:

What is in your hands RIGHT NOW?

Purusharth.

That's why Param Krupalu Dev emphasizes it.

Not because:

“Only effort exists.”

But because:

“Effort is the factor currently available to you.” Pasted text(20261109-181803)

That's a huge distinction.

29. This is NOT “effort determines everything”

Gurudev explicitly clarifies:

“I'm not saying you will get exactly the fruit of your present Purusharth.”

All five factors work.

So this isn't:

Work hard enough and you can force any result.

Rather:

You cannot control all the causes, but you are responsible for the cause that IS within your control.

That is Purusharth. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

30. A beautiful balance: Effort WITHOUT frustration

This creates a very mature spiritual attitude.

Regarding effort:

Give everything.

Because Purusharth is in my hands.

Regarding results:

Don't become frustrated.

Because Purusharth isn't the only cause.

That means:

100% responsibility for effort.

0% entitlement toward timing/results.

I think that's one of the strongest teachings in this satsang.

31. Gurudev's “live WITH You vs live FOR You” example

Near the end Gurudev recalls asking a young devotee:

“Do you want to be with me, or do you want to live for me?”

Her answer:

“I want to live for You.”

And Gurudev says he was very happy with that response.

That's spiritually mature devotion.

Instead of:

“Why isn't Guru calling me?”

“Why don't I get more physical proximity?”

“Why hasn't this happened for me yet?”

the orientation becomes:

Let me use the circumstances I have to live for Him.

That is Purusharth. Pasted text(20261109-181803)

32. The biggest sign that Parmarth isn't intense: “BUT…”

Gurudev says some people constantly have:

“But…”

or:

“Lekin…”

Every spiritual instruction gets followed by an explanation for why it doesn't apply right now.

And Gurudev says:

Where there is intense longing, there is little room for excuses.

If excuses dominate, Param Krupalu Dev is asking us to examine:

How intensely do I actually want liberation? Pasted text(20261109-181803)

33. The final formula: no excuses

Gurudev ends with:

Where there is a will, there is a way.

But his spiritual version is stronger.

If there is genuine yearning:

Make the right effort.

Don't put in the foreground:

  • Kaal,
  • fifth era,
  • absence of Tirthankars,
  • rarity of Sadguru,
  • Bhavsthiti,
  • Krambaddh Paryay,
  • karma,
  • circumstances.

These factors may be real.

Respect them.

But don't turn them into excuses for abandoning the one thing currently in your hands:

Satya Purusharth.

And Gurudev says even Self-realisation has a “price tag”:

Firm determination
Perseverance
Zero excuses Pasted text(20261109-181803)
How Verses 128–130 are building on each other

We're now starting to see exactly what Gurudev meant when he said the Upsamhar is about pitfalls and balance.

Verse 128 — Don't become one-sided intellectually

Different viewpoints may contain partial truths.

Don't:

reject everything

or

accept everything blindly.

Understand the viewpoint.

→ Anekant.

↓

Verse 129 — Treat the actual disease

Your fundamental problem is:

Atmabhranti.

Treatment:

Sadguru + Ajna + Vichar + Dhyan.

↓

Verse 130 — Don't use philosophy to avoid the treatment

You may know:

  • destiny exists,
  • karma exists,
  • time matters,
  • circumstances matter.

But don't say:

“Therefore, why make effort?”

Because now a correct doctrine has become a spiritual excuse.

The deepest practical takeaway

I think the most useful way to remember this entire satsang is:

Control the effort. Respect the causes. Release the result.

Gurudev is simultaneously correcting two opposite mistakes.

One person says:

“Everything is destiny. Why do anything?”

Wrong.

Another says:

“Everything depends entirely on my effort. If I haven't attained the result, I must be failing.”

Also wrong.

The balanced understanding is:

Many causes determine the result.

Therefore I shouldn't become arrogant when things work or destroyed when they don't.

But:

Purusharth is the cause currently in my hands.

Therefore I have no excuse not to use it fully.

And Satya Purusharth isn't simply keeping myself busy with religious activity.

Gurudev gives us the actual measurement:

Is my Self-awareness increasing?

If fasting increases it → fast.

If satsang increases it → satsang.

If bhakti increases it → bhakti.

If seva increases it → seva.

But don't confuse the activity with the goal.

The goal is that increasingly, through everything BAPA gives me, my orientation moves from:

role → Self
world → Self
self-will → Ajna
excuses → effort
dependence → spiritual maturity

And whenever I catch myself saying:

“Yes, but…”

this verse almost interrupts:

“Jo iccho parmarth toh…”
If you really want the Supreme Truth…

then:

“Karo satya purusharth.”

Make the right effort.

Everything else can remain a factor.

It just cannot become my excuse.
