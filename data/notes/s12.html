Section 12 — Upsamhar
The Conclusion of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra — Introduction

And Gurudev makes clear that “conclusion” does not mean merely summarizing what came before. These final 15 verses (128–142) are extraordinarily dense: they protect the seeker from subtle errors that can arise even after understanding the spiritual path. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

1. We have just received the extraordinary four verses of Guru-bhakti

Gurudev begins by looking back at verses 124–127, the “Aho! Aho!” verses.

He calls them outstanding because they reveal:

  • what devotion toward Guru is,
  • what reverence looks like,
  • how gratitude is expressed,
  • what words a disciple uses,
  • and most importantly, what feelings lie behind those words.

They are so powerful that they were taken from Atmasiddhi and incorporated into Dev Vandan, to be recited morning and evening.

Gurudev jokingly says they are therefore:

“In and out.”

Inside Atmasiddhi, but also “out” as an independent daily prayer. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

And after giving us this extraordinary picture of Guru-bhakti, Param Krupalu Dev now proceeds toward the completion of Atmasiddhi.

2. “Upsamhar” = Conclusion

Upsamhar means conclusion.

Gurudev admits that nobody really likes hearing:

“We're reaching the end.”

Especially because this spiritual study has penetrated so effortlessly that rather than thinking:

“Finally it's ending,”

the feeling is:

“We don't want this to end.”

But factually, Param Krupalu Dev is now completing Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

3. Only 15 verses — yet almost an independent scripture

The Upsamhar consists of:

Verses 128–142 = 15 verses

But Gurudev says these 15 verses are so rich and complete that they possess the potential to stand as an independent scripture.

He cites Pandit Sukhlalji's assessment:

“This conclusion seems to be an independent scripture.”

Meaning you could almost extract these fifteen verses from Atmasiddhi and study them separately, and they would still feel spiritually complete. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

That immediately tells us something:

Don't treat the Upsamhar as an appendix.

It isn't:

“Okay, the important teaching is over; here's a closing summary.”

Some of the weightiest philosophy and practical guidance in Atmasiddhi is still coming.

4. “Every word of an Enlightened One contains infinite scriptures”

Gurudev connects the Upsamhar with something they had studied in Letter 166:

Every sentence and every word of an Enlightened One is filled with infinite scriptures.

Earlier, this might sound like devotional exaggeration.

But Gurudev says:

Look at the Upsamhar and it becomes evident.

A single verse can contain the essence of an entire spiritual principle.

For example, Gurudev mentions the coming line:

“Upadannu naam lai, e je taje nimit…”

He says that if given freedom, he could potentially write an entire book explaining that one verse.

That's how compressed these final verses are. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

5. Simple on the outside, incredibly rich inside

Gurudev uses a nice food analogy:

One spoon of dudhpaak.

It looks small.

But it is extremely rich.

That's what these verses are like.

The language is simple enough that almost anyone can understand the surface meaning.

But once Gurudev begins unpacking it, enormous philosophical principles emerge.

And as the verses progress:

128 → 129 → 130 → 131 → 132…

Gurudev says we will actually feel the fabric of the text becoming more and more profound. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

6. Heavy spiritual subjects are made LIGHT

Some of the concepts coming are extremely deep:

Nishchay

Absolute viewpoint.

Vyavhar

Relative viewpoint.

Upadan

Principal/substantive cause.

Nimit

Instrumental cause.

These can become complicated philosophical discussions.

Yet Param Krupalu Dev makes these “weighty” subjects feel light.

They penetrate effortlessly.

That's part of what Gurudev finds so extraordinary about the Upsamhar: profound Jain spiritual philosophy is being compressed into simple verses without losing its depth. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

7. Spiritual PRINCIPLES + spiritual PRACTICE

This is one of the most important things Gurudev tells us about the structure of the Upsamhar.

Param Krupalu Dev doesn't merely give:

“Here is the correct philosophy.”

He also shows:

“Here is how you can misuse that philosophy.”

So throughout these verses we will repeatedly see:

Principle

What is spiritually true?

and Practice

How should I actually live according to that truth?

Or conversely:

What mistake should I NOT make?

Gurudev says Param Krupalu Dev has skillfully woven spiritual principles and spiritual practices together. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

8. Example: Upadan and Nimit

Gurudev gives us a preview.

The spiritual principle will essentially be:

Upadan + Nimit are both needed.

Upadan = principal cause
Nimit = instrumental cause

But then the next teaching warns:

“Upadannu naam lai, e je taje nimit…”

Meaning someone may intellectually learn:

“The principal cause is what really matters.”

And then use that truth to reject the importance of the instrumental cause.

Gurudev says:

That is the wrong practice.

So notice what Param Krupalu Dev does:

Correct principle: understand upadan and nimit properly.

↓

Warning: don't take one side of that truth and use it to reject the other.

That pattern is going to become central to the Upsamhar. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

9. The real purpose: What is BENEFICIAL and what is HARMFUL to a seeker?

Gurudev gives a very useful way of understanding the entire conclusion:

For a true seeker, the Upsamhar helps determine:
What is beneficial to me?

and

What is harmful to me?

That's why these final verses aren't merely theoretical.

They're almost like guardrails for a mumukshu.

You've learned the path.

Now:

How do you make sure you don't misunderstand it?
How do you make sure you don't become one-sided?
How do you avoid thinking you're progressing when you're actually going astray?

That's what the Upsamhar is going to address. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

10. Gurudev divides the 15 verses into TWO parts

Gurudev says that, for purposes of understanding, these fifteen verses can be divided into two sections:

Part 1 — Verses 128–136

This section primarily cautions the seeker.

It identifies:

  • pitfalls,
  • dangers,
  • one-sided beliefs,
  • spiritual misunderstandings,
  • places where the seeker can go astray.

And importantly:

You may even be doing the RIGHT thing in the WRONG spirit.

That's much more dangerous than an obvious mistake.

You may sincerely believe:

“I'm following Jineshwar Bhagwan.”

“I'm following Sadguru.”

“I'm following spirituality.”

And yet somewhere within your understanding, something has become distorted.

The Upsamhar exposes those subtle errors. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

11. Part 2 — Verses 137–142

In the later verses, Gurudev says Param Krupalu Dev depicts the states of three kinds of people.

1. The hypocrite

Gurudev previews:

“Mukhthi jnan kathe ane, antar chhutyo na moh…”

Someone may speak knowledge beautifully from the mouth…

but internally:

Moh — delusion/attachment — hasn't actually left.

So:

Talking spirituality ≠ living spirituality.

2. The worthy aspirant / true seeker Gurudev previews qualities such as:

  • Daya — compassion
  • Shanti — peace
  • Samta — equanimity
  • Kshama — forgiveness
  • Satya — truth
  • Tyag — renunciation
  • Vairagya — dispassion

These become markers of genuine spiritual development.

3. The Enlightened One

Finally, Param Krupalu Dev describes the Jnani's state.

Gurudev previews:

“Sakal jagat te ethvat, athva swapna saman…”

The entire world is experienced almost like something discarded or like a dream.

This is Jnanidasha — the state of the Enlightened One. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

12. And where does the ENTIRE Atmasiddhi finally end?

After:

  • understanding the seeker,
  • finding Sadguru,
  • understanding the soul,
  • resolving doubts,
  • understanding the Six Fundamental Truths,
  • attaining Self-realisation,
  • experiencing Guru-upkar,
  • surrendering to Guru,
  • navigating the pitfalls of the path,
  • distinguishing hypocrisy from true seeking,
  • understanding the Jnani's state…

Atmasiddhi ends with:

“Ho Vandan Aganit.”

My innumerable obeisance.

Gurudev is deeply moved by these final three words.

He says that in his own English exposition/book, after translating them, he wanted the Gujarati:

Ho Vandan Aganit

written again in bold, and then essentially:

Nothing.

A blank page.

Don't add something after it.

Because Gurudev says:

“That's the end of a devotee's journey.”

The devotee becomes completely fulfilled simply in bowing at the Feet of the Enlightened One. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

This is beautiful because Atmasiddhi doesn't end with:

“Therefore, I know everything.”

It ends with:

Obeisance.

Knowledge culminates in humility.

13. But apparently Atmasiddhi could have ended at verse 127

This is one of the most fascinating parts of Gurudev's introduction.

He talks about Param Krupalu Dev's original handwritten manuscript.

After verse 127:

“E Upkar Amaap” — this favour is immeasurable Param Krupalu Dev appears to have composed another verse that would have functioned as a closing verse, referring to Shri Saubhagya, Shri Achal (Dungarshibhai), and other mumukshus, saying this teaching had been given for their spiritual welfare.

But then:

He crossed that verse out.

It wasn't included in the first copies.

And then Param Krupalu Dev continued writing what became the Upsamhar. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

14. Gurudev's lesson about the number 142

Gurudev makes a side point that I actually think is nice.

Param Krupalu Dev didn't seem concerned with:

“I should make this exactly 150 verses.”

or:

“Let's finish on a nice round number.”

Atmasiddhi ends at:

142.

Why?

Because:

The work was finished.

Gurudev connects this with his own book. His book ended up at 506 pages, and there was discussion about perhaps adding a few pages to reach a “better” number.

He briefly considered it and then thought:

If Param Krupalu Dev finished when His work was finished, why am I adding unnecessary material just to make the number prettier?

So he left it.

The little lesson is:

Completeness matters more than cosmetic symmetry. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

15. Why did Param Krupalu Dev continue?

Now comes the most interesting part of the introduction.

Gurudev is very careful here.

He explicitly says:

This is his personal feeling. He has no proof.

So we should preserve that distinction.

The fact is:

  • there was a proposed concluding verse,
  • it was crossed out,
  • Param Krupalu Dev continued,
  • and verses 128–142 became the Upsamhar.

Gurudev's personal devotional interpretation is what comes next. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

16. Gurudev imagines Param Krupalu Dev getting up…and sitting back down

By verse 127, everything was already complete.

Subject complete:

The nature of the soul and Six Fundamental Truths had been explained.

Dialogue complete:

Guru spoke → Guru became silent.

Disciple spoke → disciple became silent.

Emotional journey complete:

The disciple had expressed gratitude and surrendered himself.

So Gurudev imagines:

Param Krupalu Dev finished.

Put down His pen.

Got up.

Took a few steps…

And then spiritually “saw” what could happen to seekers 50 years, 100 years into the future.

Again, Gurudev explicitly labels this as his feeling, not a historically established event. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

17. He “saw” the dangers future seekers could fall into

Gurudev imagines Param Krupalu Dev recognizing:

People are going to misunderstand these teachings.

They may become trapped in:

Ekant Nishchay

One-sided/absolutist understanding of the absolute viewpoint.

Ekant Vyavhar

One-sided understanding of the relative viewpoint.

Ekant Upadan

One-sided emphasis on principal cause.

Ekant Nimit

One-sided emphasis on instrumental causes.

They may:

  • become hypocritical,
  • mistake intellectual knowledge for realization,
  • incorrectly identify Enlightened Ones,
  • even start believing they themselves are Enlightened merely because they can speak spiritual knowledge.

And Gurudev's feeling is:

Param Krupalu Dev's compassion for future seekers made Him “sit back down.” Pasted text(20261109-180644)

18. This changes how we should understand the Upsamhar

The Upsamhar isn't simply:

“Here's what Atmasiddhi taught.”

It's closer to:

“Now that I've taught you the path, let Me protect you from misunderstanding it.”

That distinction is huge.

Because sometimes the danger isn't:

I don't know spirituality.

The danger is:

I know just enough spirituality to misunderstand spirituality.

I learn Nishchay, then reject Vyavhar.

I learn Upadan, then reject Nimit.

I learn:

“I am the soul,”

then perhaps use that as an excuse to ignore proper conduct.

I learn spiritual vocabulary and begin speaking like a Jnani, while attachment and delusion remain untouched inside.

I may even be doing something genuinely religious, but not in the right spirit.

That is the seeker Param Krupalu Dev is now protecting. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

19. Why the conclusion may actually be MORE relevant after learning Atmasiddhi

There's an interesting paradox here.

Before learning Atmasiddhi, perhaps I simply don't know.

But after learning it, I now possess concepts like:

  • Atma,
  • Self-realisation,
  • Nishchay,
  • Vyavhar,
  • Upadan,
  • Nimit,
  • Jnani,
  • Moksh,
  • Nijshuddhata.

That knowledge is incredibly valuable.

But now another danger appears:

My ego can appropriate the knowledge.

I can become knowledgeable about Self-realisation without being Self-realised.

I can speak about non-attachment while remaining attached.

I can quote the absolute viewpoint while using it to dismiss my responsibilities.

I can believe I understand the Jnani because I understand the definition of a Jnani.

So the conclusion becomes almost like a spiritual safety manual:

Before you walk away with everything I have just taught you, understand how NOT to misuse it.

That is the feeling Gurudev is giving to this section.

So going into the next satsangs, I would keep this map in mind:

Nishchay ↔ Vyavhar
Upadan ↔ Nimit

Principle and proper practice.

What is beneficial?

What is harmful?

Where can I unknowingly go astray?

Verses 137–142
Show me the actual spiritual spectrum
Hypocrite

↓

Worthy / true seeker

↓

Enlightened One

↓

And finally:

“Ho Vandan Aganit.”
Innumerable obeisance at the Feet of the Enlightened One. Pasted text(20261109-180644)

The deepest message of this introduction I think Gurudev is preparing us to understand that learning the path is not the same as safely walking the path.

The previous sections answered:

What is the soul?
Who am I?
What is bondage?
What is Moksh?
What is Moksh Marg?
Why do I need Sadguru?
What has Sadguru done for me?

But the Upsamhar asks a different question:

Now that you know all this… can you avoid fooling yourself?

Can I understand Nishchay without becoming one-sided?

Can I honor Vyavhar without mistaking external practice for the ultimate truth?

Can I understand Upadan without rejecting Nimit?

Can I listen to Guru without merely collecting language that makes me sound spiritual?

Can I distinguish between:

someone who speaks knowledge,

someone who genuinely seeks,

and

someone who actually knows?

And perhaps most importantly:

Can all this knowledge make me humbler rather than more convinced of my own spiritual greatness?

Because look where Atmasiddhi ultimately ends.

Not:

“Now I understand everything.”

Not:

“Now I am a Jnani.”

Not even:

“Now I have mastered the path.”

It ends:

“Ho Vandan Aganit.”

At the Feet of that Enlightened One, my innumerable obeisance.

So in a way, Section 12 is Param Krupalu Dev making sure that everything we've learned in Atmasiddhi actually takes us toward the Jnani's Feet—not toward a bigger spiritual ego.

And that makes it a very fitting conclusion to the entire scripture.
