He starts by zooming out:

  • Atmasiddhi is all about:

“The means and method of Self-realisation.”

We’ve completed about one-third of the text:

1. Verses 1–23 – Upodghāt (Introduction):

  • Guru-devotion,
  • Need for Sadguru,
  • Praise, prayer, and setting the tone.

2. Verses 24–33 – Traits of a matārthī (bigot/sectarian).

3. Verses 34–42 – Traits of an ātmārthī (true seeker):

  • Inner mindset before Guru,
  • His attitude toward living Sadguru,
  • Worthiness, right thinking, Self-realisation, liberation sequences.

Now, in Verse 42, Param Krupalu Dev said:

“I will state the six fundamental truths necessary for Self-realisation/liberation, in the form of a Guru–disciple dialogue.”

Today’s talk sets up:

  • Where these six fundamentals come from,
  • Why they are so important,
  • How the next section (verses 43–44 and then 45+) will work.

2. Six Fundamentals (Shatpad): What and Why?

He reminds:

  • Param Krupalu Dev told Muni Lalluji (in the famous “Chhā-pada no patra” – the letter on the six fundamentals) that:

Unwavering faith in these six fundamental truths results in Self-realisation (samyak darshan).

He states:

  • These shatpad – six fundamental truths – are:
    • The basis of spiritual knowledge,
    • The basis of Jainism itself.

As a result, a natural doubt arises for many listeners:

“I’ve gone to pāṭhshālā, learned Jain classes as a child, I’ve never heard of these ‘chha pada’ as basic Jainism. I was always taught that the fundamental basis is Nav tattva (nine elements). Where did these six come from?”

3. Nav tattva vs Shatpad – apparent clash

He summarises Nav tattva as traditionally taught:

1. Jīva – soul / self

2. Ajīva – non-self

3. Puṇya – merit

4. Pāp – demerit

5. Āśrav – influx of karma

6. Bandh – bondage of karma

7. Samvar – stoppage of influx

8. Nirjarā – shedding of karma

9. Moksh – liberation

Sometimes:

  • 7 tattvas are given:
  • Punya–pāp merged into āśrav.

So:

“Everyone familiar with Jain philosophy knows Nav tattva or 7 tattva as the basis. And now Param Krupalu Dev is saying: These six fundamentals are the basis of Jainism & Self-realisation. So where do they come from?”

This is what he resolves next: Shatpad are not an invention. They come from canonical roots.

4. Canonical roots: Dwadashāngi & Drashtivād

He takes us into Jain canonical structure:

  • Lord Mahavira’s sermons:
    • He didn’t write anything,
    • But His teachings were initially compiled into 12 Angas (Dwadashāngi):
      • 1st: Āchārāng,
      • 2nd: Sūyagadaṅg,
      • 3rd: Sūtrakṛutaṅg,
      • 4th: Thānaṅ,
      • … and so on.
    • The suffix “aṅg” appears in the names, hence “Dwadash-angi” (12 Angas).
  • 12th Anga: Drashtivād:
    • Now lost for over 2000+ years.
    • We only know about it from references in another Anga:
      • Samavāyāṅga Sūtra.

From Samavāyāṅga:

  • We learn:
    • Drashtivād had 14 Purvas (14 subdivisions).
    • The 7th Purva was called Ātmāpravāda Purva.
    • And the subject matter of Ātmāpravāda Purva:

The six fundamental truths about the soul (shatpad).

So:

Shatpad = subject of the 7th Purva of the 12th Anga Drashtivād. Drashtivād = Lord Mahavira’s scripture, now lost, but referenced in Samavāyāṅga Sūtra.

He emphasises:

  • So when Param Krupalu Dev presents six fundamentals, He is: “Bringing out, into light, something whose roots lie in Jināgam – the Jain Canons.”

Not a new invention at all.

5. Mention of Shatpad in later Acharyas’ works

He adds more evidence:

1. Acharya Siddhasen Divakar – In his text Sanmati Tarka Prakaran:

  • Clear references to shatpad.
  • In his Atmasiddhi commentary & thesis days, he found multiple references there.

2. Upādhyay Yashovijayji Maharaj (~300–400 years ago):

  • Composed “Samyaktva Śatsthan Chaupāī”:
    • A text of about 124–125 verses.
    • “Śatsthan” = Six Pada, Six Fundamentals.
  • The whole text:

Propounds six fundamentals as the basis of Samyaktva (Right Faith).

He narrates how he found this text:

  • Last time they went to Palitana for Diwali shibir (around 1997–98).
  • His mother had a major surgery, was depressed; he was serving her.
  • Day before Shibir, she didn’t want to stay alone in Bombay → he took her by air.
    • Flight: Bombay → Ahmedabad,
    • Due to airport issues, diverted to Bhavnagar.
    • Bhavnagar → Palitana = only 40 minutes by car.
  • In Bhavnagar:
    • There was Acharya Pradyumnasuri Maharaj in chāturmās.
    • He went for darshan and asked for prasād (blessing).
    • As a sadhu, his “prasadi” was:
      • Two texts:
        • Śamayaśataka (used later in one Paryushan), and
        • Samyaktva Śatsthan Chaupāī.

He read Samyaktva Śatsthan Chaupāī on the 40-minute drive from Bhavnagar to Palitana:

  • He was shocked: “Six fundamentals so beautifully expounded!”

Thus:

Shatpad is present in multiple authoritative Jain works. It remained mostly unknown because the Jain community became:

  • Overly ritualistic,
  • Uninterested in deep metaphysical truths.

Shrimad Rajchandra:

“Did not bring shatpad from nowhere;
He brought it from Jināgam & Acharya tradition back into living focus.”

6. Shatpad as the core subject of Atmasiddhi

He recaps:

  • Introduction (Upodghāt),
  • Matārthī & Ātmārthī traits: “All that was preparation – to inspire you to develop worthiness and create the right state to receive the main teaching.”

The main subject of Atmasiddhi is:

Six Fundamentals – shatpada / chha-pada.

  • “Atmasiddhi” has a double meaning:

1. Understanding the nature of the soul intellectually,

2. Experiential attainment of that understanding (Self-realisation).

For that:

  • The root of Self-realisation is: “Reflection on the Self” (atma-chintan, atambhāvanā).
  • But if you don’t know the Self at all:
    • How can you reflect?
  • Therefore:
    • Knowing the six fundamental truths about the soul is essential.

7. The new section: “Śatpad-Nāmkathan” – naming & purpose

He points to the organiser / handwritten Atmasiddhi:

  • After verse 42, next verses:
    • 43, 44, 45…
  • But in the original handwriting:
    • There was no explicit section heading between ātmārthī section and the next.
  • In the first printed Vachanamrut (VS 1961 – 1905 CE):
    • Edited by Ambalalbhai and Mansukhbhai,
    • A new small section heading appears:
      • “Śatpad-Nāmkathan” – Naming of the Six Fundamentals.

This section = two verses:

  • Verse 43 – names of the six padas.
  • Verse 44 – purpose of studying them.

From verse 45 onward:

  • Fifth section:

“Disciple’s doubts about the first aphorism (existence of soul) and the Sadguru’s solution.”

About Ambalalbhai’s addition:

  • He assures us:
    • Ambalalbhai’s reverence was such that:
      • He would not add/delete without discussing with Shrimad when alive.
    • Possibly:
      • Originally Shrimad did not mark this as a separate section.
      • Later, after discussions, He may have accepted:

“A small ‘naming’ and ‘purpose’ section will help familiarise seekers.”

He ties it to his own teaching style:

“Before starting a deep Q&A, I first want one line: Why are we talking? What’s today’s subject? So verse 43 = names of the six, verse 44 = purpose of studying them.”

Thus:

  • 43 – What we’ll study (names of the six fundamentals).
  • 44 – Why we’ll study them (purpose: right thinking, right view, liberation).

No explanations yet, only:

43 – Names 44 – Purpose

8. How the main debate will proceed (from verse 45)

He explains the style of the upcoming core section:

  • From verse 45, we enter:
    • Guru–disciple dialogue about the first truth:

Existence of soul.

  • The disciple:
    • Has full faith in Guru as Sadguru,
    • But does not understand the nature of the soul.
  • His attitude:
    • “I know you are Self-realised;
      so there must be something like a soul.

But I don’t understand it – please explain.”

So the dialogue will be:

1. Disciple role (Shrimad speaking as humble seeker):

  • Expresses doubts fully:
    • With logic,
    • Without masking simplicity,
    • Without false politeness like:

“Sorry if I’m disrespectful, sorry if I’m wrong.”

  • He opens his heart fully:

“If I see a clock and a book, but not the soul, I feel soul does not exist. Kindly resolve me.”

2. Guru role (Shrimad speaking as Sadguru):

  • Answers each doubt one by one,
  • With cogent clarity – no loose ends,
  • No further sub-doubts remain,
  • Not just mental satisfaction:
    • Each answer:
      • Clarifies,
      • Deepens conviction,
      • Moves the disciple forward on the path of liberation.

3. Truth voice:

  • Sometimes beyond both roles,
  • Just pure statement of reality itself.

He emphasises:

“Shrimad plays both roles:

  • When asking, He is the disciple,
  • When answering, He is the Guru,
  • Sometimes He is just Truth.”

9. Teaching method: gentle persuasion vs rigid imposition

He highlights the style Param Krupalu Dev chooses:

  • Not:
    • “Truth is this; accept or you’re an atheist.”
  • But:
    • Gentle persuasion:

“Look at it from this viewpoint, think it through this way and see.”

He says this method:

  • Is far superior to rigid imposition:
    • It:
      • Creates interest,
      • Is easy to follow,
      • Is more graspable,
      • Is comprehensive (addresses doubts, angles).

He references 4 types of communication (just naming them):

1. Head-to-head

2. Head-to-heart

3. Heart-to-head

4. Heart-to-heart

He doesn’t unpack fully, but hints:

  • Deepest is heart-to-heart:
    • Both are silent,
    • Love passes without words.

Here though:

  • The disciple wants logic and clarity,
  • So Sadguru happily gives a fully reasoned, logical dialogue.

He says:

“He won’t just speak mystical love;
He will give you a logical, step-by-step demolition of wrong conclusions and replacement with right understanding.”

10. The flavour of the coming dialogue (tiny preview)

He previews the disciple’s first big doubt:

“If I can see a book and clock with my eyes, then why can’t I see the soul? Therefore, I conclude soul does not exist.”

The logic will be:

  • “I accept Fiji exists:
    • Because:
      • It’s written on a map,
      • My friend went there.”
  • But then:
    • If scriptures mention soul,
    • And Enlightened Ones “have been there,”
    • Why dismiss soul?

Param Krupalu Dev will:

  • Show:
    • Where the disciple’s thinking went wrong,
    • That:

“The seer of eyes cannot be seen by the eyes. The one who sees through the eyes is the soul.”

He says:

“In just 2–3 verses, the entire basis of the doubt is gone.

The disciple himself feels: ‘Yes, why was I demanding to see the seer through the seen?’”

This showcases:

  • Shrimad’s awesome ability:
    • To reveal:
      • The flaws in reasoning,
      • The blind spots in logic,
    • Using a few simple words.

11. Final condensation: what Verse 42 / this talk is doing

This satsang sets up the transition:

  • We’ve understood the atmārthī:
    • His mindset,
    • His worthiness,
    • His right thinking,
    • His path from Self-realisation to Nirvan.

Now we ask:

“What content should fill that right thinking?”

Answer:

The six fundamentals (shatpad).

Verse 42 says:

  • To generate right thinking, and
  • To understand Mokshmarg clearly,
  • Param Krupalu Dev will now state the six fundamentals:
    • In a Guru–disciple dialogue,
    • So the seeker can:
      • Reflect on them,
      • Attain Self-realisation,
      • And ultimately liberation.

He ends with:

Root of Self-realisation = reflection on the Self. To reflect rightly, you must know the Self. To know the Self, you must understand the six fundamentals of the soul. That is what the next section will unfold— lovingly, logically, and in the living language of Guru and worthy disciple.
