• He reminds: the disciple’s Verse 46 argument had two layers:

1. Body / senses / breath can be equated to “soul.”

2. There is no independent sign (endhan) of the soul—no clear evidence—so believing in a separate soul is false.

  • He says: Verse 53 already handled part 1 (body/senses/breath are not the soul).
  • Now Verse 54 is specifically answering part 2: “If soul is a separate substance, show me the sign.”

0:40–1:11 — He introduces Verse 54 as the direct reply

  • He frames it very clearly:
    • Disciple: “No sign of soul is evidently known; therefore separate soul is false.”
    • Guru: “I’ll give you the sign.”

1:11–2:41 — Word-by-word breakdown of the verse (he builds the meaning from vocabulary)

He starts unpacking the verse phrase by phrase:

“Sarva avasthane vishe”

  • Sarva = all
  • Avastha = states / conditions / modifications / changes
  • So: “in all states” (all bodily/mind-related conditions)

“Nyaro”

  • Nyaro = separate (bhinn)

“Sada janay”

  • Always known / always evident as separate

“Pragatroop chaitanyamay”

  • Pragatroop = evident, manifest, clearly present
  • Chaitanyamay = made of consciousness (consciousness-ness)

“E endhan saday”

  • Endhan = sign / indication / characteristic
  • Saday / sada = always, permanent

So he lands the core:

  • The sign the disciple wants is: consciousness/knowingness, and it is always present.

2:41–4:54 — Short meaning: in every changing state, soul remains separate, continues even after state ends;
knowingness is the never-destroyed sign

He gives “short meaning” but it’s already deep and structured:

  • The soul exists through all states, but it remains separate from those states.
  • He gives examples of “states” so you feel what “avastha” means:

States of mind

  • waking state
  • dream state
  • deep sleep state

States/changes of body

  • childhood
  • youth
  • old age

Then he emphasizes the key move:

  • The soul does not merge into the state.
  • It continues to exist even after the state is gone:
    • sleep ends → “it” is still there
    • waking ends → “it” is still there
    • dream ends → “it” is still there

And he adds the crucial point:

  • It is also the one that knows the state.
  • Therefore:
    • the ever-present sign is knowingness / consciousness
    • this sign is never destroyed

4:54–6:47 — Meta-moment: “A few moments ago we doubted existence… now we’re discussing nature”

He pauses and makes you notice the progression of the Shastra:

  • “Just a few moments back” (in the flow of the text),
    • the existence of soul was questioned.
  • Now, very quickly, the conversation has moved to:
    • qualities, nature, characteristics of the soul.
  • He highlights how satsang works:
    • once the mind starts moving in the right direction, you don’t remain stuck at “does it exist?”
    • you immediately want to know: “what is it like?”

7:02–9:11 — The “most significant thing”: soul’s nature is changeless + indestructible + timeless

He shifts from “functions” of soul to “nature” of soul:

  • Earlier (Verse 52), we saw soul as:
    • integrator / harmoniser
    • supplier of significance to objects/occurrences
  • Now he says: the most significant thing is not just what soul does, but what it is:

Three strong statements he repeats

1. Nature of soul is changeless

2. Nature of soul is indestructible

3. Nature of soul is timeless

Then he applies it to all the states again:

  • body alive or body dies → soul remains
  • dream/sleep/waking → nature remains the same
  • childhood/youth/old age → nature remains the same

And he notes (very important nuance):

  • knowingness may look “regulated” in its expression (memory weak/strong),
  • but the nature does not change.

He brings back the “regulator” imagery (like previous videos):

  • old age → regulator seems on 1 (“I can’t recollect…”)
  • youth → regulator seems on 5 (“Apurvaji knows the next sentence…”)
  • but it never becomes zero.

9:11–11:28 — Humour + reinforcement: “Don’t know” is also knowing; “doing nothing” is also a kind of doing

He makes a playful but precise philosophical point:

  • “I don’t know” still contains the verb know.
  • “I don’t recollect” still implies you know that you don’t recollect.
  • “I’m not doing anything” is still a kind of “doing” (he uses it to show how subtle language is).

Then he goes bold:

  • “Even if I pass away due to Covid, what difference does it make? I am sure to remain.”
  • Not said as drama—said as faith in the Fundamental Truth:
    • timeless, changeless.

12:22–13:39 — He extracts “two spiritual practices” from the verse: nyaro + knowing

This is where he turns verse meaning into sadhana:

  • From “nyaro sada janay” he pulls out a practical inner routine:

1. Nyaro → I am separate (bhinn)

2. Sada janay → knowingness is always present (I am always knowing) He says:

  • even though the line “literally” means “always known as separate,”
  • he takes it as a practice formula:
    • “I am always separate.”
    • “I am always knowing.”

So now Verse 54 becomes not only philosophy but meditation-direction.

14:22–16:47 — He returns to Verse 46 and shows how Guru answers using the disciple’s own word: “endhan”

He explicitly revisits the disciple’s demand:

  • Disciple: “Show me an endhan (sign).”
  • Guru replies with the exact same word:
    • “E endhan saday” — “This is the permanent sign.”

He highlights the beauty of the Guru:

  • Guru takes the disciple seriously,
  • uses the disciple’s language,
  • answers in a few words, in a wondrous style.

16:47–20:44 — What is an “attribute” (guna)? He gives the definition and locks consciousness as the soul’s attribute

He asks a key conceptual question:

  • What is an “attribute” (guna)?

Then he gives a clean definition:

Attribute must satisfy two conditions

1. It must exist in all parts of the substance

2. It must remain through all states/modifications So “attribute” = everywhere + every time (his phrase)

Then he applies it:

  • Soul’s attribute is consciousness / knowingness
    • not just “top part has consciousness”
    • but whole soul-substance, all the time

He jokes lightly:

  • by the end you’ll add “philosophy” on your visiting card—so much philosophical stuffing—but keep it enjoyable.

20:44–23:41 — Existence and consciousness are linked: if soul never loses existence, it never loses knowingness

He ties existence and consciousness together tightly:

  • Soul never loses existence → therefore it never loses knowingness.
  • He anticipates the objection:
    • “But in deep sleep, we don’t know anything… so how can it be ‘sarva avastha’ (all states)?”
  • He says he’s “bound by this one word” sarva avastha—he has to explain it properly.

23:41–27:40 — Big meditative teaching: Enlightened focus on unchanging nature; you don’t change situations—just change focus; “just be”

Here he turns Verse 54 into a direct instruction about meditation:

  • The soul passes through changing states,
  • but the nature is unchanging.
  • The Enlightened One’s drashti (focus) is on the unchanging nature,
    • and he remains a witness to changing states.

Then he simplifies enlightenment:

  • To become a doctor is difficult,
  • to become enlightened is “simple”:
    • you don’t have to change nature,
    • you don’t even have to change situations,
    • you only change your focus.

He makes a sharp point about meditation style:

  • Don’t “overdo” meditation.
  • If you put too much effort and struggle:
    • it becomes wrong meditation
    • you start hating meditation, soul, even Sadguru.
  • If you enjoy meditation:
    • gratitude increases, and it feels natural.

And then his Zen-style line:

  • Don’t even say “just know.”
  • Say “just be.”
  • Because:
    • existence itself is consciousness
    • and consciousness is knowingness
    • so to exist is to know
    • therefore: just be / just exist

27:54–33:37 — “Sarva avasthane vishe” explained via 3 states: waking, dream, sleep (with witness analogy + mosquito + freshness)

Now he returns and gives the promised explanation of sarva avastha:

What does “vishe” mean?

  • It means: the soul is present in those states (it “exists there”)

He uses the courtroom humour:

  • A witness must be present at the crime scene.
  • You can’t be in Singapore while murder happened in London and still be the witness.

So, soul must be present in:

  • waking
  • dream
  • sleep

Waking

  • easiest: you are clearly knowing something.

Dream

  • you know the dream while dreaming,
  • and after waking you at least know “I dreamt.”
  • Memory may fail because the “regulator” is low, but the knower is there.
  • He adds the Mother Trishala reference:
    • Digambars say 16 dreams, Shwetambars say 14,
    • and she could narrate them—so dream-knowing is real.

Deep sleep

This is where his examples become very “alive”:

  • Mosquito lands while you’re asleep…
  • and suddenly your hand moves to stop it / you scratch.
    • That shows a knower is present even in slumber.
  • After waking you say:
    • “I had good sleep”
    • “I feel fresh”
    • meaning you know something about that sleep-state afterward.

He ties it back to the regulator/fan:

  • It doesn’t go to zero.
  • It moves between 1 to 5.
  • Coffee example with Atul:
    • wakes at 2, calls Beena → 3, asks coffee → 4, drinks coffee → 5 (“I’m awake now”)
  • Point: expression changes, but knower never switches off.

The decisive proof of separateness

  • You can report across states:
    • “I was dreaming… now I’m awake.”
    • “I slept well… now I’m awake.”
  • If you were not separate, how could you know the previous state after it ended?
  • So:
    • present in the state (vishe)
    • yet separate (nyaro)
    • and knowing (janay)

33:37–34:23 — Final landing: Verse 54 gives the “endhan” (sign) as the ever-present knowingness

He closes by restating the verse’s conclusion:

  • The evidence/sign of soul is:
    • pragatroop chaitanyamay (evident consciousness)
    • e endhan saday (the permanent sign)
  • So Guru “proves” the soul—especially by:
    • the ever-present attribute of knowingness (across all states),
    • and the soul’s separateness from every changing condition.

Then: “Let’s sing.”

Quick 5-line “revision sheet” for Verse 54 (matches his teaching)

  • Avastha changes (waking/dream/sleep; child/youth/old age).
  • Soul is present in all states (vishe) but remains separate (nyaro).
  • Soul is always knowing (sada janay)—even “don’t know” is a form of knowing.
  • An attribute (guna) must be everywhere + every time → consciousness qualifies.
  • Therefore consciousness/knowingness is the permanent endhan (sign) of the soul.
