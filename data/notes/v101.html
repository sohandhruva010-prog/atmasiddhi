Verse: 101 (Atmasiddhi Shastra) How this video fits: Verse 100 explained the path through negative assertion (what must reduce / not be there). Verse 101 explains the same path through positive assertion (what must increase / be established).

1) Quick recap of Verse 100 (the launching pad)

The speaker begins by restating Verse 100’s “acid test”:

Progress on the path of moksha is proportional to reduction in:

  • Likes (raag / attachment)
  • Dislikes (dwesh / aversion)
  • Ignorance (ajnan / delusion)

So even if you are doing:

  • rituals / religious activities
  • austerities
  • maun (silence)
  • scriptural study

…if these practices do not reduce raag–dwesh–ajnan, then (per this framework) they are not functioning as moksha-marg for you.

Key lens: Don’t measure spirituality by activity count. Measure it by inner transformation markers.

2) Negative assertion vs Positive assertion (the “twin verses” idea)

The talk gives a very practical explanation:

A) Negative assertion (Verse 100)

You explain something by stating what it is not:

  • “If you’re progressing: there should not be likes, dislikes, ignorance (increasing).”
  • Or: “If these are increasing, you’re not on the path.”

The speaker compares it to identifying a person:

  • “Not this one, not this one…” → narrowing by elimination

B) Positive assertion (Verse 101)

Now we define the path by stating what should be present / increasing:

  • awareness of the pure Self
  • abidance in the pure Self
  • increasing identification with the soul’s pure attributes

Important: The speaker calls 100 and 101 “twin verses” because:

  • 100 = what should decrease
  • 101 = what should increase …and the two move together.

3) Verse 101 — the core statement (as taught here)

The discourse presents Verse 101 as defining:

(1) The pure nature of the soul in three attributes

1. Sat

2. Chaitanyamay

3. Sarvabhaas rahit

(2) The path of liberation as:

The way by which one attains the pure Self (keval / pure) and increasingly abides in it — that is moksha-marg.

This is the positive version of the path:

  • Not just “reduce impurities”
  • But “increase Self-abidance”

4) The three attributes — what they mean (with the speaker’s emphasis)

4.1 Sat — Eternal existence

The speaker insists: don’t define “sat” vaguely as “it is.”

Sat = eternal existence. Meaning:

  • The soul is an independent substance, not a temporary product of elements.
  • It is indestructible / imperishable.
  • It exists through changing modes, but its existence is continuous: past–present–future.

He uses a very direct reflection:

  • People say “so-and-so is off / passed away” (body reference)
  • But the teaching says the Self (sat) cannot be “off.”
  • So: If something ends, it cannot be the “I” described as sat.

Practical takeaway: When you’re pulled into body-identity, remember “sat”:

“What I truly am must be that which continues.”

4.2 Chaitanyamay — Full of consciousness (knowingness)

Chaitanya = consciousness / knowingness May = full of So chaitanyamay means:

  • The soul is entirely consciousness (not partly conscious).
  • Philosophically, the soul is never devoid of consciousness.

He addresses a common confusion:

  • In medical language someone becomes “unconscious.”
  • But here the point is: the soul’s consciousness may be unexpressed, but it is not absent.

A very important refinement he adds

He says:

  • Knowingness is your only natural activity
  • Likes/dislikes are not the soul’s inherent activity They are impure modifications / acquired habits / conditionings, not the core nature.

So the direction is:

  • Knowingness remains
  • Likes/dislikes should dissolve (because they’re not your “core committee,” so to speak)

4.3 Sarvabhaas rahit — Devoid of all illusions

He breaks the word:

  • Sarva = all
  • Abhaas = illusion / appearance
  • Rahit = devoid of

So the pure soul is:

Free from all illusory identification and appearances.

What counts as “illusion” here?

He emphasizes two big categories:

1. Body-identity (“I am the body”)

2. Impure feeling-identity (“I am anger / I am dislike / I am attachment”) He makes a sharp point: Even if the body-not-self idea is intellectually understood, people still think:

“At least my anger / my likes-dislikes are me.”

Verse 101 says: No—those too are not the true Self.

Crystal + colored flower analogy (excellent teaching tool)

He gives an image:

  • A crystal appears red if a red flower is placed behind it
  • The “redness” looks real, but it’s an appearance due to association
  • The crystal remains colorless in its nature

Similarly:

  • The Self appears like body / emotions due to close association
  • But the soul is sarvabhaas rahit—not actually those appearances

Practical takeaway: When body or emotions feel like “me,” treat that as abhaas (appearance), not essence.

5) The “positive path” formula in this talk

The speaker repeatedly condenses the method into a progression:

1. Understand the pure nature (through Sadguru/scripture)

2. Awaken to it (shift from intellectual knowing → lived recognition)

3. Develop constant awareness

4. Reach abidance in the Self

And he adds an important flexibility:

  • If meditation helps this awakening/abidance → meditation becomes a valid “means”
  • If bhakti helps → bhakti is a valid “means”
  • If study helps → study is a valid “means”

But the test is not the tool. The test is: Is awakening + awareness + abidance happening?

6) Witnessing consciousness and the “increase/decrease” linkage

A key practical bridge in this video:

If witnessing awareness increases…

  • likes/dislikes naturally decrease
  • ignorance naturally weakens
  • intense passions loosen

He gives a real-life measurement example:

  • “Only during meditation I’m in awareness”
  • Then “for 10 minutes after meditation it continues” → That extension itself shows progress.

He also gives a reality-check:

  • You can’t say “I was witnessing from 3:00–3:30” and then “at 3:35 I blasted”
  • That suggests you were doing a practice mechanically or chanting, but not truly established in witnessing mode.

Core diagnostic: True witnessing is not a “mood.” It has a stabilizing effect.

7) A crucial ethical/spiritual correction: how you see yourself and others

He applies Verse 101 not only to “me,” but also to “others.”

Seeing yourself:

Instead of:

  • “I am angry”
  • “I am egoistic”

Train the inner identity toward:

  • “I am sat”
  • “I am chaitanyamay”
  • “I am sarvabhaas rahit”

Seeing others:

He gives examples of judgmental perceptions people might hold (including a harsh stereotype about women as an example of wrong seeing). The intended correction is:

Even if someone seems unrefined, immature, or limited in some capacity:

  • their soul-nature is still sat–chaitanyamay–sarvabhaas rahit in essence
So the test becomes:
  • If you cannot see self/others at least sometimes through this “pure-soul lens,”
  • and you are only seeing labels, defects, roles, identities, then per the satsang framing: you are not moving steadily on moksha-marg.

8) “Spark” teaching: soul as what powers life

He introduces a vivid reminder:

  • The soul is the “spark” that:
    • makes the eyes see
    • makes the ears hear
    • makes the body-mind function

A dead body has:

  • the same organs
  • the same structure …but lacks that “spark,” so nothing functions.

He even remarks that the cremation reality (body treated differently after death) points toward:

  • something essential is absent (“spark is no more”)

Purpose of this segment: To strengthen conviction that:

  • the true “me” is not the body-machine
  • the true “me” is the conscious principle

9) Acceptance and stillness: the surprisingly “tool-less” doorway

This is one of the most practical parts.

He says:

  • To experience the soul (the “spark/light”), you don’t need fancy tools.
  • You need:
    • acceptance
    • stillness

And he links it to the intensity of likes/dislikes:

  • When acceptance arises, intense raag/dwesh reduces.
  • Subtle preferences may remain, but intensity falls.

The verification question he asks

  • Is your acceptance and stillness increasing?
    • If yes → moving toward liberation
    • If no → bondage-direction

This is extremely “audit-friendly”:

  • It’s not about counting mantras
  • It’s about the felt inner direction

10) “Sakshi karta” and the triangle model (very memorable)

He uses a powerful phrase:

  • sakshi karta = “witness-doer”
    • action happens
    • yet you remain witness

He gives a mental model:

  • Triangle

1. Body

2. Mind

3. “Me” in witnessing mode

Let body do, mind do… But “I” stays in witness-awareness.

Result (as he repeats):
  • ignorance weakens
  • intense passions weaken
  • knots loosen
  • gradually cut → liberation / pure bliss

12) One-page revision summary

Verse 100 (negative):

✅ Progress check = raag/dwesh/ajnan decreasing

Verse 101 (positive):

✅ Progress check = identification with pure Self increasing Pure Self described by 3 attributes:

1. Sat = eternal existence

2. Chaitanyamay = full of consciousness/knowingness

3. Sarvabhaas rahit = free from all illusory identities (body + impure feelings)

Path instruction (as presented here):

  • Understand → awaken → constant awareness → abide
  • Any practice is valid only insofar as it increases this.

Verification:

  • If witnessing awareness increases → intense likes/dislikes decrease
  • If acceptance and stillness increase → liberation-direction
  • If likes/dislikes/ignorance increase → bondage-direction
