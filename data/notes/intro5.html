1) Architecture: 142 Verses • 12 Sections • One Seamless Journey

  • Atmasiddhi Shastra comprises 142 gāthās (“How many? 142.”)
  • Carefully planned into 12 sections (“Method in the matter”): though written in one sitting, it is well-charted for a seeker.
  • Continuity & flow: every verse meshes with the previous and the next; the reader’s thought expands and deepens with each step.
  • Outcome: complete clarity — whether on eternity of the soul or the means to liberation.

Upje te suvicharna, mokshmarg samjay;
Gurushishya-samvādthi, bhākhū shatpad āhī. Right thinking arises and the path of liberation is understood; thus I state the six fundamentals as a Guru–disciple dialogue. — Atmasiddhi, 42

2) The 12 Sections (with Study Plan)

1) Verses 1–23 — Introduction (Upodghāt) → January

2) Verses 24–33 — Traits of the bigot / sectarian (Matārthī-lakṣaṇ) → February

3) Verses 34–42 — Traits of the true seeker (Atmārthī-lakṣaṇ) → February

4) Verses 43–44 — Naming the Six Fundamentals → March

5) Verses 45–58 — Fundamental 1: Soul exists → March

6) Verses 59–70 — Fundamental 2: Soul is eternal → April

7) Verses 71–78 — Fundamental 3: Soul is doer → May

8) Verses 79–86 — Fundamental 4: Soul is enjoyer/receiver → May

9) Verses 87–91 — Fundamental 5: Liberation exists → June (start)

10) Verses 92–118 — Fundamental 6: Path to liberation → June (end) – July

11) Verses 119–127 — Disciple’s enlightenment (expression of joy & gratitude) → August

12) Verses 128–142 — Conclusion / Pinnacle (Upsamhār) — 15 reflective verses → September (Paryushan)

‘Atma chhe’, ‘Te nitya chhe’, ‘Chhe kartā nijkarma’; ‘Chhe bhoktā’, vali ‘Mokṣ chhe’, ‘Mokṣ upāy sudharma’. — Atmasiddhi, 43

3) Method & Style (Shaili): Dialogue → Curiosity → Conviction

Lively exchange over lecture. The text unfolds as a Guru–disciple conversation (question–answer), raising authentic doubts and patiently resolving them with logic, examples, and experience. Benefits: - Curiosity is kindled; attention focuses.

  • Opposing views are presented fairly and then refuted.
  • The reader participates in the inner debate, arriving at personal conviction.

Scriptural lineage of Q&A style (as highlighted):

  • Jain canon: Bhagavati Sūtra (36,000 Qs of Gautam to Mahavira), Jñātadharmakathā, Vipāka Sūtra, etc.
  • Later Jain works: Viśeṣāvaśyak-bhāṣya (Ācārya Jinabhadra), Vichār Ratnasār (Gani Devchandraji).
  • Non-Jain: Tripiṭaka dialogues; Bhagavad Gītā (Arjuna–Krishna); Upaniṣads; Śaṅkarācārya; Gujarati poets (Akho, Dasi Jivandas, Dayaram).

Form & language. Simple Gujarati, doha-chhand couplets → easy to memorise, yet profound in import; penetrating, heart-touching and attractive.

4) Shat Darshan (Six Schools) — Why Their Gist Appears Here

  • Indian “philosophy” = systematic study (philo + sophia), often technical and complex (terminology of Jain, Bauddha, Sāṅkhya, Nyāya, etc.). The speaker’s journey: Jain principles → adhyātma → languages → Ṣaḍ-darśana (’89–’91) → Nyāya → academia — yet the end result was not dry philosophising but living devotion (“became Sufi, not ‘philsophy’”).
  • Atmasiddhi presents the essence of the six systems within a devotional-rational dialogue — accessible to laypersons, useful to scholars, elevating for devotees.

Shat-darshan pan teh… — the six fundamentals encompass the six systems. — alluded in Atmasiddhi, 44

5) Compact & Condensed → Need for Vivechan (Explanation)

  • Each verse is dense with layers of meaning; one verse can be explained in many ways.
  • Hence vivechan (exposition) aids seekers to reach the essence.
  • The style keeps the satsang alive: a “lively exchange of ideas,” not a dry blackboard lecture.

6) Publication History • Explanations • Translations

First publications

  • During the composer’s lifetime: not published; only four copies circulated privately.
  • After His passing, V.S. 1961: compiled works printed as “Shrimad Rajchandra” with Atmasiddhi included.
  • V.S. 1964 / 1908 CE: Atmasiddhi Shastra printed as an independent book for the first time.

Explanations (Selected)

Detailed (sample): 1943 (Kanjiswami’s discourses), 1961 (Bhogilal G. Sheth), 1986 (Dr. Tarulatabai Mahasatiji), 1992 (Dr. Bhagwandas Mehta), 2001 (Atmasiddhi Shastra Vivechan, 4 vols, ~3000 pp, Pujya Gurudevshri), 2009 (Jayantilalji Maharajsaheb), 2011 (Bhanuvijayji Maharajsaheb).

Short (sample): 1905 (Ambalalbhai — concise meanings, seen by the composer), 1923 (Rai Bahadur J. L. Jaini), 1943 (Brahmachari Govardhandasji), 1965 (Songadh Trust), 1976 (Dahyabhai C. Mehta), 1985 (Dinubhai Patel), 1993 (Girdharbhai; Dhirubhai Pandit), 2003 (Manubhai Doshi), 2006 (Chandrikaben), 2010 (Short explanation by Pujya Gurudevshri), 2015 (Sayla), 2019 (Benshri Ratna Prabhu).

Ambalalbhai’s concise meanings carry special respect as they were reviewed by the composer.

Translations (Selected)

1919 Sanskrit (Pandit Bechardasji) — rare case of Gujarati → Sanskrit.

  • 1927 Marathi (anonymous).
  • 1967 English (Fr. Francis).
  • 1974 Hindi (Shri Sahajanandghanji, Hampi).
  • 1985 English (Brahmachari Govardhandasji; written earlier).
  • 1996 Hindi (Mahopadhyay Chandraprabh Sagarji); 1996 Bengali (Bhawarlal Nahata).
  • 2001 Kannada (Dr. A. N. Upadhye; Dr. M. A. Jaychandra).
  • 2006 English (Chandrikaben); 2006 Hindi (Nijhawan).
  • 2019 Hindi (Benshri Ratna Prabhu).
  • Available today in seven languages: Gujarati, Sanskrit, Marathi, English, Hindi, Bengali, Kannada — with more in pipeline (French, Spanish, German, Arabic, Mandarin).

Many verses are also elucidated in the composer’s letters; Gandhiji wrote on Atmasiddhi in English (lost in a London bus).

7) Why the Title & Structure Matter for Study

The title directs our aim (intellectual conviction → experiential attainment).

  • The structure provides a map; the style provides the vehicle.
  • The explanations/translations form bridges for modern seekers across languages and backgrounds.

Key Orienting Verses

Je swaroop samjya vina, pamyo dukh anant; Samjavyu te pada namun, Shri Sadguru Bhagwant. — Atmasiddhi, 1 Upje te suvicharna… bhākhū shatpad āhī. — Atmasiddhi, 42 ‘Atma chhe’… ‘Mokṣ upāy sudharma’. — Atmasiddhi, 43 Shatsthanak saṅkṣhepma, shatdarshan pan teh… — Atmasiddhi, 44

Practical Takeaways

  • Keep the 12-section map handy as you read; track how each verse builds on the last.
  • Lean into the dialogue — let doubts surface and be resolved; avoid parroting.
  • Use vivechan alongside memorisation to draw out depth.
  • Remember: self-effort under Sadguru’s guidance is the winning combination.
