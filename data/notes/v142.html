This is the final satsang on Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra — Verse 142, and Gurudev treats it not as a sad ending, but as a feeling of completion.

Everything has now been given:

  • the disciple's doubts have been resolved,
  • the Six Fundamental Truths have been established,
  • Moksh Marg has been clearly defined,
  • the subtle pitfalls of the path have been exposed,
  • the Mumukshu and Jnani have been distinguished,
  • and Verse 141 told us what to do next: understand the first five truths, live the sixth, and attain the fifth—liberation.

So what remains?

Gratitude. Devotion. Surrender.

And Shri Atmasiddhi ends exactly where it began:

At the Feet of the Enlightened One.

The final verse is:

“Deh chhata jeni dasha, varte dehatit;
Te Jnanina charanma, ho vandan aganit.”

In essence:

Though associated with a body, the Enlightened One's state remains beyond the body. At the Lotus Feet of such an Enlightened One, may there be my innumerable salutations.

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 142: “Ho Vandan Aganit”

1. Atmasiddhi begins with devotion and ends with devotion

Gurudev points out an old spiritual tradition:

An author begins a sacred scripture auspiciously by bowing to the revered Lord, and concludes it the same way.

Look at Atmasiddhi:

Verse 1:

“Namu…” — I bow.

Verse 142:

“Vandan…” — obeisance.

Additional verse traditionally sung:

“Pranam…” — salutations.

Different words, but the same devotional movement.

Gurudev says beautifully:

It started with devotion, and it ended with devotion.

In between?

An ocean of Jnan.

The scripture is packed with logic, philosophy, arguments and spiritual principles—but all that knowledge is held between two acts of devotion.

And Gurudev turns this into a lesson for our own journey:

Start with devotion. End with devotion.

The final feeling should be:

“Thank You. Thank You. Thank You.”

2. Don't feel sad that Atmasiddhi is ending — feel COMPLETE

Gurudev says he specifically prayed and meditated beforehand so that he wouldn't become emotional or low because the Yajna was ending.

His beautiful line is:

“There is nothing like departure when it is with an Enlightened One.”

The satsangs may finish.

But the Enlightened One's teaching doesn't leave.

So Gurudev asks us not to end with:

“It's over 😔.”

Instead:

“It's complete.”

That's a very different feeling.

3. Look at what has been completed

Gurudev almost performs a final checklist:

Confusions?

Resolved.

Path of liberation?

Clearly defined.

Pitfalls?

Depicted.

Don't become confused regarding:

  • Nishchay and Vyavhar,
  • Upadan and Nimit,
  • destiny and Purusharth,
  • verbal knowledge and Self-realisation.
Disciple's realization?

Expressed.

Guru's benevolence?

Acknowledged.

Guru thanked?

Yes.

Everything necessary has been given.

Gurudev says:

“Only a complete Enlightened One can complete this way.”

So there isn't a feeling:

“I wish Param Krupalu Dev had explained one more thing.”

The only thing remaining is:

Put it into practice.

4. The entire essence of spirituality is hidden in the FIRST line

Gurudev says this final verse contains the essence of the path taught by:

  • Sarvajna Bhagwan — the Omniscient Lords,
  • Vitrag Bhagwan — the Dispassionate Lords,
  • Nirgranth Bhagwan — the Disentangled Lords.

And all of it is captured in:

“Deh chhata jeni dasha, varte dehatit.”

Though the body remains, His state is beyond the body.

That is the Jnani.

The body exists.

But:

body-identification doesn't.

5. Body existing ≠ body-identification existing This distinction is crucial.

Gurudev separates two things:

Existence of the body

and

Identification/attachment with the body

These are not the same.

The body exists because of:

Karma.

Identification and attachment exist because of:

Moh — delusion.

Therefore an Enlightened One may still possess a physical body because past karma continues.

But that does not mean:

“I am this body.”

or:

“This body is mine.”

remains within Him.

This is the entire meaning of:

Dehatit — beyond the body.

6. The COCONUT analogy returns

Gurudev brings back the coconut example.

Inside a mature coconut:

  • shell,
  • kernel,
  • water.

When the water dries, the kernel becomes clearly separated from the shell.

You shake it and can actually feel:

these are distinct.

Likewise:

Body and soul were ALWAYS distinct.

The Enlightened One didn't create that distinction.

But since beginningless time:

we never experienced it.

Body-identification acted like the water holding everything together.

When Dehadhyas — body-identification — dries, one experiences:

“The body is here…and I am distinct from it.”

This connects beautifully with the earlier verse:

“Myan thaki talvarvat…”

Sword and sheath.

Atmasiddhi has repeatedly brought us back to the same central realization:

I am not the body. I am the soul.

7. Saubhagyabhai's extraordinary humility

Gurudev recalls Saubhagyabhai describing an experience of the body and soul as distinctly separate.

But then comes something even more beautiful.

He essentially tells Param Krupalu Dev:

If YOU confirm this is genuine, I will accept it.

Why?

Because he knows pseudo-enlightenment is possible.

He doesn't immediately announce:

“I experienced something! I'm Enlightened!”

He leaves open the possibility:

Maybe this is still an illusion.

Maybe it's a projection of contemplation.

If You say it isn't genuine, I won't call it realization.

Gurudev is deeply moved by this humility.

Even spiritual experience is placed at Guru's Feet for verification.

8. Guru's humility naturally percolates into the disciple

Gurudev connects Saubhagyabhai's humility with Param Krupalu Dev Himself.

The Guru has tremendous humility toward:

  • the Omniscient Lords,
  • Bhagwan Mahavir,
  • His spiritual tradition.

So naturally:

that humility enters the disciple.

This connects all the way back to Gurudev's earlier teaching:

People should be able to behold your Guru through your behavior.

The disciple begins reflecting the Guru.

9. The HORSESHOE MAGNET analogy

This is probably the main analogy of the satsang.

Imagine:

Horseshoe magnet = Karma

Now bring a:

Piece of iron

near it.

The iron gets affected.

Gurudev says:

Iron = ordinary worldly soul.

Karmic circumstances arise and we immediately become internally affected:

  • happy,
  • sad,
  • angry,
  • excited,
  • depressed,
  • attached,
  • averse.

Now take:

Piece of wood.

Put it near the magnet.

Nothing happens.

Gurudev says:

Wood = Liberated Soul.

No karmic influence.

Now comes the important one.

Attach:

iron + wood

together.

Bring them near the magnet.

What happens?

The:

iron gets affected.

But the:

wood doesn't.

Gurudev says:

This is the embodied Enlightened One.

The body may still be affected by karma.

But the inner state remains unaffected.

That's an excellent way to understand:

“Deh chhata jeni dasha, varte dehatit.”

10. The Jnani can be physically sick without becoming spiritually sick

So Gurudev says carefully:

He may be sick.

The body can experience:

  • illness,
  • pain,
  • discomfort,
  • karmic effects.

But:

body affected ≠ Self affected.

The iron is affected.

The wood remains untouched.

Therefore the Jnani can remain inwardly:

  • peaceful,
  • blissful,
  • detached,
  • established in Self,

even while the body passes through its karmic circumstances.

11. Jeevan Mukti — liberated while living

Gurudev connects this to Verse 113.

Earlier we learned:

When uninterrupted awareness/abidance in one's nature manifests, Omniscience arises.

And Gurudev uses:

Jeevan Mukti

Liberated while living.

The physical body hasn't yet fallen.

Yet spiritually:

the fundamental work is complete.

The person is:

“Embodiment of liberation.”

That's important for understanding why Param Krupalu Dev chooses this particular Jnani for His final salutations.

12. Why doesn't Param Krupalu Dev bow to the Siddhas in the final verse?

Gurudev asks this explicitly.

Param Krupalu Dev could have concluded:

I bow to the Liberated Lords.

Or:

I bow to the Omniscient Lords.

But instead He specifically chooses:

An Enlightened One who still possesses a body yet lives beyond it.

Why?

Because that Jnani is the living demonstration of Atmasiddhi's objective.

The seeker can look at Him and see:

This is what I am trying to become.

13. Out of ALL the Jnani's virtues, why choose “Dehatit”?

The Jnani possesses so many virtues:

  • peace,
  • compassion,
  • tranquility,
  • forgiveness,
  • detachment,
  • wisdom,
  • equanimity.

Yet Param Krupalu Dev chooses one:

Beyond the body.

Why?

Gurudev answers:

“Because that is the purpose of this scripture.”

The scripture is called:

Atma-Siddhi

Realisation of the Self.

The disciple originally approached Guru to understand:

Who am I?

So the final verse points to the One who has actually realized:

“I am not the body. I am the Self.”

This makes the ending incredibly precise.

14. Param Krupalu Dev is still giving us HOMEWORK in the last verse

Verse 141 already told us:

Contemplate Five. Act on Six. Attain Five.

You might think:

“Okay, homework received.”

But Gurudev says Param Krupalu Dev cares so much that even in the final verse He essentially says:

Where should you start?

Of all the things taught in Atmasiddhi, focus on:

Self-awareness.

Begin cultivating:

“I am distinct from the body.”

When circumstances change, observe:

Did it affect the body?
Did it affect the mind?
Or did I allow it to affect me?

That is an extremely practical final instruction.

15. Physical pain, mental happiness/sadness—and the observer

Gurudev distinguishes:

Physical level:

What happens to the body.

Mental level:

Happiness, sadness, emotional reactions.

Then asks:

Can I remain aware:
“I am the observer of these.”

Not:

“I must never feel sadness.”

But:

“Sadness is being experienced; sadness is not my pure nature.”

That awareness is what Param Krupalu Dev has spent the entire scripture cultivating.

And Gurudev says beautifully:

Till the last line, He is inculcating the importance of Self-awareness within us.

16. Self-awareness is a SPIRITUAL value

Gurudev distinguishes:

Moral values:

honesty, etc.

Spiritual value:

Self-awareness.

Self-awareness is specifically central for:

  • Jignasu,
  • Mumukshu,
  • Atmarthi,
  • seeker of liberation.

Why?

Because Moksh doesn't merely require becoming a good person.

It requires ultimately knowing and experiencing:

who I actually am.

17. The BODY is not the cause of transmigration

Gurudev gives another very important clarification:

Body ≠ cause of transmigration.

Otherwise every embodied Enlightened One would necessarily remain spiritually bound.

The actual cause is:

Ekatva — identification

“I am this body.”

and:

Mamatva — attachment/possessiveness

“This body is mine.”

Those arise from Moh.

So if identification and attachment have disappeared:

So what if the body remains?

The body can continue because of karmic causes while the soul is already spiritually beyond it.

18. The Jnani becomes the “Embodiment of Liberation”

Gurudev says he prefers:

Embodiment of Liberation

rather than merely:

embodiment of divinity.

Why?

Because this is exactly what the seeker needs.

The Jnani:

  • has attained the state we seek,
  • embodies the destination,
  • knows the path,
  • can lead us along it.

Therefore:

He becomes the leader of my path to liberation.

This connects beautifully to Verse 129:

Disease → Atmabhranti
Physician → Sadguru

The physician is qualified because He Himself embodies spiritual health.

19. “Te Jnanina…” becomes “Aa Jnanina…”

Now Gurudev becomes deeply devotional.

Param Krupalu Dev wrote:

“Te Jnanina…”

At the Feet of THAT Enlightened One…

But Gurudev says:

If the Enlightened One is right in front of you, why say “that”?

Basic grammar 😂:

Far away:

that.

Near:

this.

So devotion personalizes it:

“Aa Jnanina…”

At the Feet of THIS Enlightened One.

This continues a theme we've seen throughout the final verses:

Sadguru → Prabhu
Te Prabhu → Aap Prabhu

That Enlightened One → This Enlightened One.

Spirituality becomes personal.

20. Bowing becomes NATURAL when you see your goal embodied

Why does the seeker bow?

Not because:

“Scripture says I must bow.”

Not because he took a vow:

“I will bow 27 times daily.”

Gurudev says genuine reverence becomes:

natural.

Why?

Because:

I want Self-realisation.

And in front of me stands:

someone Self-realised.

So naturally:

  • respect,
  • love,
  • reverence,
  • surrender

arise.

The seeker sees his destination embodied in another Being and wants to fall at His Feet.

21. Dandavat — surrender like a falling stick

Gurudev explains:

Dand = stick.

When a stick falls, it doesn't carefully bend at the waist.

It simply:

falls completely.

That's the feeling behind:

Sashtang Dandavat.

Full prostration.

It represents reverence reaching the level of:

surrender.

Again, the physical posture isn't the entire point.

The inner feeling is:

I surrender at the Feet of the One who has attained what I seek.

22. The progression of the seeker

Gurudev gives a beautiful sequence:

First:

He venerates the Jnani.

↓

Then:

He worships Him.

↓

Then:

He bows at His Feet.

↓

Then:

He follows His Ajna.

↓ Finally:

He abides in the Self.

That's the Guru-disciple relationship in one progression.

Guru-bhakti isn't meant to terminate in:

“I love my Guru.”

It ultimately leads to:

the state Guru wants me to attain.

23. The final word: AGANIT

And now we arrive at the last word of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra:

Aganit — innumerable.

How many salutations?

1?

3?

11?

27?

108?

142—one for each verse?

1008?

One million?

Gurudev says:

Don't even bring Ganit — mathematics — into it.

😂 The heart isn't counting anymore.

Ho Vandan Aganit.

24. Why “innumerable”?

Because:

the Upkar was immeasurable.

Remember:

“E Upkar Amaap.”

How do you numerically repay:

immeasurable benevolence?

You don't.

So the disciple's devotion also refuses measurement:

I won't count my salutations.

That is why Gurudev says only:

a heart overflowing with devotion

can produce the word Aganit. Pasted text(20261119-001539)

25. Lalluji Muni — several salutations after every verse

Gurudev asks us to imagine Lalluji Muni going into the jungle with the newly composed Atmasiddhi.

It wasn't yet memorized.

He would read a verse…

then offer several salutations.

Then read the next… and bow again.

Not mechanically:

“One completed.”

But from the feeling:

How can I possibly bow enough?

That is the spirit of:

Ho Vandan Aganit.

26. Gurudev refused to write anything after those words

This is one of the most beautiful personal parts.

When Gurudev was finishing his commentary, he wondered:

How do I end something this extraordinary?

He could have written:

  • another paragraph,
  • another devotional reflection,
  • another conclusion.

Instead, he ended with:

“Ho Vandan Aganit.”

Full stop.

He specifically asked for those words to be bold.

Why?

“If my Bhagwan ends with ‘Ho Vandan Aganit,’ who am I to say anything beyond that?”

That really captures the feeling of the entire ending.

27. Atmasiddhi is the “wonder of wonders”

Gurudev calls Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra:

the wonder of wonders.

And gives a playful analogy:

Salt in an extraordinary dish.

You can have every expensive ingredient.

But without salt:

something fundamental is missing.

Likewise, Gurudev says Atmasiddhi gives taste and dynamism to our spiritual life.

Why?

Because it gives us:

the goal
the path

and:

enthusiasm to walk the path.

That's a great summary of what this entire study has done.

28. “Much beyond Harvard” — managing YOURSELF

Gurudev jokes that this knowledge is:

much beyond Harvard.

Not because worldly education is bad—he explicitly says he's not against it.

Harvard can teach:

  • management,
  • finance,
  • resources,
  • worldly skills.

But Atmasiddhi teaches:

how to manage yourself.

How to discover:

a happiness independent of worldly objects and occurrences.

How to become:

master of your inner state.

That's why Gurudev says its importance should become deeply engraved within us.

29. Surrender unlocks potential

Gurudev beautifully connects the final verse back to Upadan and Nimit.

You possess:

immense spiritual potential.

But who made you understand that?

Who revealed:

“Sarva jeev chhe Siddh sam”?

Who showed you the path?

Who keeps redirecting you?

Sadguru.

So Gurudev says:

Surrendering to the One who has transcended the body can unlock your immense, priceless potential.

Again:

Upadan = your potential.

Nimit = His guidance.

The final verse brings both together in devotion.

30. The SHEPHERD and the lost sheep

Gurudev ends with a beautiful Biblical analogy.

A shepherd has a flock of around 100 sheep.

One disappears.

He doesn't say:

“99 are enough.”

He goes looking for the lost one.

When he finally finds it, he:

  • lifts it,
  • places it on his shoulders,
  • brings it back joyfully.

Gurudev says:

That's us.

We're the ones:

  • wandering,
  • making mistakes,
  • doing stupid things,
  • getting distracted,
  • going astray.

And the reason to trust and love Sadguru is:

No matter how much we wander, He doesn't want to let us remain lost.

Gurudev's line is beautiful:

“He won't let us meander.”

He will keep trying to keep us:

hooked to the Higher.

31. Param Krupalu Dev didn't merely give information — He gave a VISION

Near the end, Gurudev describes Param Krupalu Dev as:

  • Yugpurush,
  • Yugpravartak — epoch-maker,
  • Yugpradhan — leader of the era,
  • visionary for generations.

Why?

Because He didn't merely address individual symptoms:

anger,

sadness,

attachment,

particular problems.

He showed the:

root cause.

Atmabhranti.

And then gave:

a long-term spiritual vision.

That vision becomes our:

mission.

32. “Self-realisation is not a luxury”

This is one of Gurudev's final statements of the entire Yajna:

“Self-realisation is not a choice. Not a luxury. It's imperative.”

Why?

Because once Atmasiddhi has shown:

  • who I am,
  • why I suffer,
  • what bondage is,
  • that Moksh exists,
  • that Moksh Marg exists,
  • how the path works,

then spiritual pursuit isn't merely:

“something nice to do.”

The path is now clearly in front of us.

So:

Walk.

This connects directly to Verse 141.

13. And finally…

Look at the One who has actually reached the state you seek:

“Te Jnanina charanma, Ho Vandan Aganit.”

The deepest takeaway from the final satsang

I think Gurudev deliberately leaves us with two things, not one:

Self-awareness

and

Guru-bhakti.

And importantly, they aren't competing.

The entire purpose of Guru-bhakti is not:

“Keep looking at Guru and forget yourself.”

Sadguru keeps pointing:

Look within.

You are the soul.

Become aware.

Don't identify with the body.

Reduce attachment and aversion.

Experience what I am showing you.

And yet the deeper that realization becomes, the more the disciple looks back at Guru and thinks:

Who showed me this?

So:

Self-awareness deepens Guru-bhakti.

And:

Guru-bhakti deepens Self-awareness.

That's why Atmasiddhi can begin by bowing to Sadguru, spend its entire middle explaining the Self, and then end by bowing to Sadguru again.

The circle is complete.

And Gurudev gives us one beautifully practical place to begin right now:

When something happens, ask:

Did this affect my body?
Did this affect my mind?
Or have I allowed it to become “me”?

Can I observe:

“There is happiness.”

“There is sadness.”

“There is physical discomfort.”

“There is praise.”

“There is criticism.”

while retaining even a tiny awareness:

“I am not this. This is not my pure nature.”

That is the seed of Dehatit Gurudev wants us to cultivate.

We don't have to pretend to possess the Jnani's state.

But now we know the direction.

We know the goal.

We know the path.

And we have seen, through Sadguru, what the destination looks like embodied.

So the appropriate ending isn't sadness.

It isn't even:

“What a great satsang series.”

It is gratitude for receiving the map—and then actually walking.

Which makes it hard to improve upon Param Krupalu Dev's final words:

Ho Vandan Aganit.

Innumerable salutations.
