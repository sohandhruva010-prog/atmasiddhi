Topic: God is not necessary as “fruit-giver”; karmas fructify by their own nature and then get exhausted

1) Where Verse 85 fits in the argument (the “link”)

The disciple had two arguments (from earlier verses):

1. Karma is lifeless, so how can it “know” what result to give and deliver fruits to the soul?

2. If God gives fruits, then God must be involved in worldly dispensing/justice → Godliness gets compromised.

What Guru says at the start of this video

  • If you truly understood Verses 82–84, you should almost stop needing the “God as fruit-giver” hypothesis.
  • Because Verse 83’s logic already shows:
    • Poison/nectar don’t know anything
    • but the consumer experiences the result
    • therefore a conscious dispenser isn’t required for effects to occur.

Yet, Param Krupalu Dev doesn’t leave things incomplete, so Verse 85 addresses the second argument anyway—but within limits:

  • The topic is NOT: proving God’s existence, nature, creatorhood, God’s will, etc.
  • The topic is only: Is the soul the receiver of fruits of actions?
  • So Verse 85’s scope is: God as “fruit-giver” is not necessary.

2) Verse 85 — Key line + word meanings (as taught in the transcript)

Verse core (as explained)

“Faldata Ishwartani ema nathi jaroor.”
  • Faldata = fruit-giver
  • Ishwartani = God
  • ema nathi jaroor = not necessary here / no need in this matter

Then: “Karma swabhave pariname, thay bhogthi dur.”

  • karma swabhave pariname = karmas fructify by their own nature
  • thay bhogthi dur = once their fruits are experienced (bhog), they become exhausted / shed / removed

Short meaning (Guru’s direct conclusion)

  • There is no necessity to consider God as the dispenser of karmic fruits.
  • Karmas ripen by their own nature, give their effect at the right time,
  • and after the soul experiences the effect, karmas get exhausted and shed away, returning to neutrality.

3) The main doctrine of Verse 85 (in one clean sentence)

Karma is a self-operating cause–effect system

It has:

  • its own mechanics
  • its own momentum
  • its own justice / balance
  • its own cause–effect linkage

So you don’t need God to step in and “deliver results.”

4) The full karmic “cycle” (he repeats this like a loop)

The Guru lays out a very clear cycle of karmic matter. This is the backbone of Verse 85.

Karma particle cycle (step-by-step)

1. Neutral state

  • karmic matter exists as karman vargana (neutral karmic particles in the “atmosphere/space”)

2. Bondage occurs due to bhaavkarma

  • when the soul modifies into bhaavkarma (impure feelings), karmic matter gets bound

3. Programming / stamping happens at bondage

  • time, place, intensity, instrumental cause, etc. get fixed
  • he compares this to programming an alarm clock

4. Dormant period

  • karmas remain present but not operative yet
  • they must “ripen”

5. Operative / ripe / effective stage

  • karmas become uday / operative and start giving effect

6. Fruition / experience by the soul

  • the soul experiences the effect (pleasant or painful)

7. Exhaustion and shedding

  • thay bhogthi dur
  • after giving results, karmas lose that potency and get shed

8. Return to neutrality

  • karmas go back into the atmosphere as neutral karman vargana again

And then:

9) If bhaavkarma erupts again → cycle restarts

  • this has been happening “since time immemorial”

✅ His one-line loop formula:

Impure feelings → bondage → karmas fructify by nature → after experience they exhaust → again impure feelings → …

5) Why God isn’t required: the “common-sense consequences” argument

The disciple’s hidden assumption was:

  • “For an effect to happen correctly, a conscious being must supervise it.”

Guru rejects this as inconsistent, because:

If you demand God for karmic fruits…

…then logically you must demand God for every single effect in the universe.

So he says:

  • If God must make karma give fruit,
  • then God must also make chilli spicy, water cool, fire burn, etc.

That becomes absurd—God would have to manage:

  • all chillies of the world,
  • all salt/pepper/asafoetida,
  • every object’s every property,
  • every consequence of every contact.

So the correct understanding is:

  • Effects follow from nature + cause–effect law.
  • No dispenser is required for each result.

6) Every example he uses (and what each proves)

A) Alarm clock example (program → effect → neutral)

He repeats the alarm clock theme in Verse 85 too:

  • You program for 5:30
  • it rings accurately
  • it doesn’t “know” anything
  • the ringing lasts a bit (5:30 to 5:35)
  • then it “unwinds” / stops → returns to neutrality

What it proves: A lifeless system can deliver a result precisely without consciousness—because it was programmed, and it operates by its nature.

B) Mango ripening example (dormancy → ripening → effect)

  • Mango plucked from tree may not be ripe.
  • It needs time (ripens in a heap/hay etc.)
  • Only when ripe is it “effective” to eat.

What it proves: Karmas can be bound and yet not immediately effective; they can stay dormant until the right time.

C) “Seed becomes tree and bears fruits” example

  • You have seen a seed grow into a tree and then produce fruits.
  • You don’t require God’s permission for each fruit.

What it proves: Natural processes mature into results over time without a conscious dispenser standing there “allocating” outcomes.

D) “Railway engines are programmed and carry loads” example

  • Engines / systems run according to programming and mechanics.
  • You don’t insert “God” as the driver for each trip/load.

What it proves: A complete mechanism can be sufficient; it doesn’t require an extra “fruit-giver.”

E) Poison / sugar / fire / snow (Letter of Six Fundamentals linkage)

He says: if you need God for karmas, then you must need God for:

  • Poison → death
  • Sugar → effect (he corrects the audience: not “sweet” only; it gives energy/activity)
  • Fire → burn
  • Snow → cool

He explicitly says:

  • this is written in Param Krupalu Dev’s framework and links beautifully with the Letter of Six Fundamental Truths.

What it proves: Cause–effect is built into substances and interactions; results arise automatically.

F) “Switch on fan” example

  • If God were required for every effect,
  • you would need God’s approval before turning a switch and having the fan start.

What it proves: The “God must dispense outcomes” logic becomes unworkable when applied consistently.

G) Cake / dhokla baking example (matter doesn’t know)

He uses a vivid illustration:

  • Dough is unpleasant/you wouldn’t even want to touch/smell it.
  • After baking, it becomes tasty.
  • The cake/dhokla doesn’t know the result of baking.

What it proves: Unconscious matter can transform and produce outcomes without “knowing.” The difference is: the soul knows and experiences; matter does not.

H) “Clock vs book tension” (knowing vs non-knowing)

He jokes:

  • It is 10:35.
  • Will the clock have tension? Will the book have tension? No.
  • The point is: not all things share the same capacity.

What it proves: Outcomes can occur without awareness; awareness belongs to the soul.

I) Flour vs rice vs calories (potency to become karma)

He gives a very important “potency” example:

  • Bread is made from maida/wheat flour, not rice.
  • Not all grains have the same potential for that transformation.
  • Similarly:
    • Calories in food do not have the potency to bind as karmas.
    • Karman vargana specifically has the potency to become karmas.

What it proves: Karmic particles have a specific natural capacity to become bound karmas and yield fruits. Not “any matter” can do it.

J) “Shedding vs acquiring” (5 grams shed, 5 kilos bound)

He makes a powerful point:

  • karmas don’t remain like an “album” of hardened stuff
  • they shed and return to particles (like ashes dispersing)
  • but the tragedy is:
    • while some shed, we bind more, often far more

What it proves: The problem isn’t that karmas can’t be exhausted; it’s that we keep creating new bondage during fruition.

K) Lifespan “10:45 is over” example (moment-by-moment exhaustion)

He says:

  • at 10:45, that “slice” of lifespan is exhausted
  • it has shown its result; now it becomes discharged/neutral

What it proves: He’s illustrating exhaustion after experience in an easy time-based way.

L) Snake venom removed → big worm (nirmalya analogy)

He uses a memorable potency-removal illustration:

  • Remove venom from a snake → it becomes like a big worm (no fear)
  • “Nirmalya” = powerless (he connects to temple décor removal: once decorations are removed, that substance is no longer “active for worship”; it is called nirmalya)

What it proves: After karma has given its fruit, its “effective potency” is gone. It becomes neutral karman vargana again—nothing to fear.

M) Atom bomb Hiroshima/Nagasaki example (lifeless substance power)

He emphasizes:

  • lifeless substances can have enormous power
  • he cites atomic bomb devastation as “within seconds you can see what lifeless substance can do”
  • many people die in a moment

What it proves: “Lifeless can’t do anything” is false. Lifeless matter can have immense impact by its nature and configuration.

7) The Indianapolis “ant accident” question (very important section)

A question is raised:

  • If bhaavkarma = impure feelings, and bondage occurs because of bhaavkarma…
  • then if you accidentally step on an ant (no anger/revenge), is there bondage?

He ties it to practice:

  • In Micchami Dukkadam, we explicitly say forgiveness for:
    • knowingly (with impurity)
    • unknowingly (without conscious intent) So why ask forgiveness for “unknowing” if it had no karmic consequence?

His answer (he says he answered in 2–3 ways)

Answer 1: Unawareness is also impurity
  • Ignorance (ajnan) is an impure state.
  • Unawareness (not paying attention) is also impurity.
  • Otherwise, “I didn’t remember” would pardon everything in life.

He explicitly names:

  • pramad (indolence / heedlessness) is also an impure state.
Answer 2: Yog (mind–speech–body activity) is primary; bhaav adds intensity

He says:

  • the activity of mann–vachan–kaya (mind, speech, body) is a major causal channel
  • bhaavkarma contributes especially to:
    • intensity and duration
  • yog contributes to:
    • quantity and nature aspects (he says you don’t need to learn all that detail right now, but he signals the structure)

✅ Practical takeaway: Not only “obvious” impurities like anger count—ignorance + heedlessness also count, and bodily activity can generate consequence even when intention isn’t malicious.

8) “Karma has its own judiciary” (core philosophy statement)

This is one of the most central passages of the video.

He says karma:

  • creates its own law of cause and effect
  • maintains its own balance
  • maintains its own justice
  • has its own “judiciary” (his word/analogy)

Then he intensifies it:

Cause-effect linkage is:

  • inexorable
  • unstoppable
  • irrevocable
  • cannot be altered

He explains the moving chain:

  • Bandh (bondage) → Uday (manifestation)
  • Manifestation becomes instrumental for new bondage
  • Effects become causes for new effects

✅ This is why God isn’t required as a manager: The system itself has momentum and completeness.

9) Operative karma vs dormant karma (very clear teaching section)

He says:

  • every situation is due to operative karma (karma uday), not dormant karma.
  • you can have many karmas attached, but you experience the ones currently operating.

Vedniya karma example (pleasant vs painful)

He uses:

  • shata vedaniya = pleasure-producing karmas → comforts/luxuries/facilities
  • ashata vedaniya = pain-producing karmas → sickness/discomfort

He gives a “now vs later” illustration:

  • at 10:35, if ashata is operative → you feel fever, foot pain, head pain, etc.
  • later, when shata is operative → you relax comfortably (AC, pillows, lemonade, etc.—he says he personally doesn’t do it, but it illustrates the point)

Dormant vs operative vocabulary analogy

He jokes:

  • he knows many English words
  • but only some are “operative” in the moment
  • the rest are dormant (not currently in use)

✅ Practical takeaway: Don’t say “I have karma” in a vague way; understand:

  • bonded vs operating
  • you experience uday, not the entire stock at once.

10) The Jnani’s vision (sees both present fruit and future fall)

He says:

  • A Self-realised One can see:
    • what is operating now
    • and what consequences are coming later
  • So when someone is arrogant during pleasure-uday, the Jnani can say:
    • “I am seeing his downfall after two years.”
  • Then the Jnani gives ajnas (commands) to prepare you for difficult udays.

What it proves: Karma law is stable and readable to higher knowledge; it isn’t random or dispensed ad hoc.

11) “Keep God for better jobs” — the maturity line

He says:

  • Holding God responsible for what subtle karmic particles can do is “not mature”
  • A mature thinker says:
    • “Let’s keep God for better jobs.”
    • This (karma-fruition) can be handled by the karmic mechanism itself.

✅ Meaning: Don’t downgrade the karmic science by inserting God as a routine clerk delivering every small outcome.

12) What is God’s role then? (He answers carefully)

He makes a precise claim:

God:

  • knows all souls
  • knows all karmic bondages
  • knows which karmas are operative
  • knows who is deluded, angry, lustful, etc.
  • knows liberation progress

But:

  • does not involve Himself in occurrences
  • God is the knower (omniscient), not the distributor.

Meditation analogy (very important practical bridge)

He says:

  • In meditation, what should you do?
    • Know everything (thoughts arising, weaving, going)
    • but do nothing (don’t interfere)
  • “Just don’t fall asleep.”
  • True meditation:
    • consciousness at peak
    • but without likes/dislikes (without impure bhaav)

✅ This is his final synthesis:

  • Godliness = pure knowing without intervening
  • spiritual practice = move toward that same purity of knowing.

13) What Verse 85 conclusively establishes (the “exam answer”)

Verse 85 seals two things at once:

1. The soul is the receiver of karmic fruits (already proven in 82–84, now reinforced)

2. God is not needed as the fruit-giver Because karmas:

  • have inherent potency,
  • are programmed during bondage,
  • fructify by their own nature,
  • then exhaust and shed after being experienced.
