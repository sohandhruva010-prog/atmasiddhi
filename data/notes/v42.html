He situates us:

  • From verse 34 onwards, Param Krupalu Dev has been describing:
    • The attributes of an ātmārthī – a true seeker.
  • We’ve gone through:
    • 34–36: Inner mindset and convictions before Sadguru.
    • 37: What he does once he finds a Sadguru.
    • 38: His worthiness (the four virtues).
    • 39: What happens if worthiness is absent.
    • 40: What happens if worthiness is present (Sadgurubodh suhay → suvicharna → sukhday).
    • 41: The entire path: from right thinking → Self-realisation → destruction of delusion → Nirvan.

Now:

Verse 42 is the last verse of this section. It concludes the atmārthī section and introduces the main philosophical section.

He summarises:

  • Beginning of a true seeker:
    • Right mindset / worthiness.
  • End of a true seeker:
    • Liberation.

Verse 42 answers:

“What should right thinking be about? What should the seeker think on to make his thinking right enough to lead to Self-realisation?”

Param Krupalu Dev’s answer:

You must know the six fundamentals (shatpad) of the soul/truth.

2. Why six fundamentals? From “what not to think” → “what to think”

He puts it very simply:

  • You’ve realised:
    • “Thinking of worldly topics (people, food, status, pleasures etc.) makes me:
      • impure,
      • agitated,
      • infatuated,
      • prone to anger, selfishness, jealousy, pride.”
  • So you conclude: “I shouldn’t keep thinking about all this.”

But:

“If not this, then what? You know what not to think – but what should you think about?”

His point:

  • Right thinking is not just absence of wrong topics.
  • It is: “Thinking about the nature of the Self / truth.”

To think about that, you must know:

  • Who am I?
  • Am I really the body?
  • Do I die when body dies?
  • What is soul?
  • What is its nature?
  • Is liberation possible? etc.

So Param Krupalu Dev says:

“You are quite ignorant about the Self. I must now tell you the six fundamentals which are the actual subject of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra.”

Verse 42 is the bridge from:

  • “Here is the ātmārthī.” →
  • To: “Here is what an ātmārthī needs to contemplate on.”

3. The verse (42) – sense and structure

The verse in essence:

Upje te suvicharna, mokshmarg samjāy;
Gurushishyasamvādthi, bhakhu shatpad āhi.

3.1 “Upje te suvicharna…”

  • Upje te – “That which gives rise to…”
  • Suvicharna – right thinking:
    • Thinking that:
      • Purifies,
      • Leads to Self-realisation,
      • Flows from Sadguru’s guidance.

Thus:

“That by which right thinking arises…”

3.2 “Mokshmarg samjāy…”

  • Mokshmarg – path of liberation.
  • Samjāy – is understood, grasped, seen clearly.

So together:

“That by which right thinking arises and the path of liberation is understood clearly…”

3.3 “Gurushishyasamvādthi…”

  • Gurushishya – Guru and disciple.
  • Samvād – dialogue, discussion.

So: “Through a dialogue between Guru and disciple…”

He’s telling us:

  • The form of the next section:
    • It won’t be a monologue or dry lecture.
    • It will be Sadguru–disciple conversation.

3.4 “Bhakhu shatpad āhi…”

  • Bhakhu – “I shall state / I will explain.”
  • Shatpad:
    • Shat = six,
    • Pad = positions / aphorisms / fundamentals.
    • So six fundamentals (six “funda”s).
  • Āhi – here/now.

So:

“I will now state these six fundamentals here and now, in the form of this Guru–disciple dialogue.”

4. Short meaning (as he gives it)

In order to generate right thinking and to understand the path of liberation clearly, one needs to know the six fundamental truths. Param Krupalu Dev says, “I now state these six aphorisms in the form of a dialogue between Enlightened Mentor and disciple.”

He highlights two purposes:

1. Upje te suvicharna – material/content for right thinking.

2. Mokshmarg samjāy – clear, correct understanding of the path.

5. “Is all the Guru–disciple sweetness over now?”

He anticipates a wrong assumption:

  • Now the “Guru–disciple romance part” is done.
  • From next section:
    • “Dry philosophy”,
    • “Only Jain metaphysics and cross-philosophical critique,”
    • “Existence of soul, eternity of soul, doer/receiver, possibilities of liberation etc.”

He plays with this fear:

“So now the beautiful divine romance, Guru loving the disciple, rescuing him when stuck in the rot of his thinking – all that is over, right?”

And answers:

“Actually, no. The entire next section is also a Guru–disciple dialogue.”

Previously:

  • We saw the Guru & disciple as a concept (Guru helping, disciple transforming).
  • Now:
    • We will actually see the disciple asking questions,
    • And the Guru answering – line by line.

He makes it vivid:

  • From now on, every day Guru will speak and disciple will speak.
  • It will be a living conversation, not just philosophical bullet points.

6. Who is the Guru? Who is the disciple?

He shares a very intimate insight:

  • He asked Param Krupalu Dev: “Who is this disciple? Who is this Guru in the dialogue?”
  • The response (as he presents it):

“When I am asking, I am the disciple. When I am answering, I am the Guru. And sometimes, I am just Truth – not as Sadguru or disciple, simply the voice of Truth.”

He compares it playfully:

  • Like Amitabh Bachchan doing a triple role in a movie.
  • Here:
    • One Being appears as:
      • Guru,
      • Disciple,
      • Pure truth.

So, the samvād is:

Shrimad Rajchandra playing:

  • the seeking mind,
  • the guiding Master,
  • and the impersonal Truth.

7. Why Guru is irreplaceable (beyond scriptures)

He says:

  • A seeker can get knowledge from scriptures.
  • But: “There is no substitute for a living Guru.”

Why?

He explains brilliantly:

  • Scriptures:
    • Know the truth they carry,
    • But do not know the reader.
  • Sadguru:
    • Knows the truth (by experience),
    • Knows the scriptures (their essence),
    • And knows the seeker.

So:

“Do you want to study a text perfectly? Don’t go only to some academic professor. Go to an Enlightened Master.”

Because:

  • An academic:
    • Explains textual meanings, grammar, historical context.
  • An Enlightened Master:
    • Will change the emphasis according to what you need,
    • Sometimes bends word-level meaning to convey what your soul needs now.

He jokes:

“If you’re dry, for you every ‘jnān’ will mean devotion: I’ll say, ‘Here “jnān” means deep bhakti.’”

That is: Guru is like a mother, not an aunt:

  • Aunt may know the medicines,
  • Mother knows the child.

He gives the Nemi–sweet example:

  • After lunch, Nemi says:
    • “I’m full, no more chapati.”
  • Five minutes later:
    • “Do you feel like something sweet, Bapa?”
  • Bapa:
    • Goes to drawer,
    • Looks at Nemi’s eyes,
    • Guesses fudge / chikki / chocolate,
    • Picks exactly what he wants.
  • Nemi: “That’s exactly what I wanted, Bapa!”

Point:

A Guru knows:

  • The truth,
  • The shastra,
  • The student.

8. Why dialogue instead of lecture

He says:

  • Param Krupalu Dev could have easily used:
    • Vyakhyān – formal exposition,
    • Pravachan – discourse,
    • Bhāshan – speech,
    • One-way lecture style.

But for the six fundamentals, He chooses Guru–shishya samvād (dialogue).

Why? He gives four reasons:

1. Interesting – Dialogue holds attention more than monologue.

2. Easy – Questions raised are exactly the doubt patterns of real seekers.

3. Graspable – Concepts land better when framed as:

  • Objection → clarification,
  • Doubt → reasoned answer,
  • “I don’t believe in soul” → demonstration.

4. Comprehensive – We see truth from:

  • Multiple angles,
  • Response to different confusions,
  • Comparison with other philosophies.

He says:

“This method is best for lovers to understand deep philosophy.”

He calls it:

  • A “lover’s way” to get Vedānta / Jain metaphysics:
    • Full of logic,
    • But also full of respect, affection, trust.

9. Connection to Mahavira–Gautam samvād

He notes:

  • This style is not new.
  • It matches: Bhagwan Mahavira and Gautamswami’s dialogues.
  • Gautam asked: “Kim tattvam, Hey Bhante?” – “Lord, what is Truth?”
  • Mahavira answered.
  • Those answers:
    • Formed Dwadashāng – the 12 primary scriptures.
    • Their structure:
      • Gandharvāda – questions asked by chief disciples, answers by the Lord.

He highlights: “This is the same style: the lover’s style to understand deep philosophy.”

So:

  • Param Krupalu Dev is echoing Tirthankar style:
    • Q & A,
    • Doubt & resolution,
    • Not just proclamations.

10. Worthy disciple, willing Guru – the dynamic

He stresses:

“Guru will only start this deep dialogue if the seeker is genuine.”

If the seeker is:

  • Half-hearted,
  • Casual,
  • Curiosity-driven only,

Sadguru:

  • Might go into silence,
  • Just like:
    • Mahavira, who (according to traditions):
      • Stayed silent until a worthy Gautam arrived:
        • Digambar: 66 days,
        • Shvetāmbara: 1 day.

He says: “When the seeker is deserving, Sadguru is willing.”

  • “Bhakhu shatpad āhi”:
    • “I will state (bhakhu) the six fundamentals here” is His way of saying:
  • “I see you as deserving. Now I’m ready to talk.”

He also says something beautiful and less expected:

  • Everyone knows: “Master’s words foster the disciple’s growth.”
  • But also: “A worthy disciple’s presence fuels the Master’s thoughts.”

Example:

  • Saubhag requests → Shri Atmasiddhi arrives.
  • One worthy seeker in the audience:
    • Guru blooms,
    • Guru’s own mastery expresses.

So:

“Guru energises seeker;
seeker’s worthiness energises Guru’s expression.”

11. The purpose of Shatpad in Atmasiddhi

He ends by tying it to the core aim of the whole text:

“The purpose of Atmasiddhi Shastra is: How do I attain Self-realisation?”

And:

  • Root of Self-realisation: Reflection on the Self (atma-chintan, atambhāvanā).
  • But: “How do I reflect on Self if I don’t even know what it is?”

So we need:

1. To know:

  • Six fundamentals about the soul:
    • Soul exists,
    • It is eternal,
    • It is doer,
    • It is enjoyer,
    • Liberation exists,
    • There is a path.

2. Then:

  • To reflect on these,
  • Turn reflection into right thinking (suvicharna),
  • Which leads to Self-realisation,
  • Which in turn leads to liberation.

He also hints at a coming question:

  • As a Jain, he learned:
    • The nine fundamentals (nav tattva):
      • Soul, non-soul, merit, demerit, influx, bondage, stopping, shedding, liberation.
    • Taught as “basis of Jainism”.
  • Here, Param Krupalu Dev speaks of six fundamentals.

So he leaves a cliffhanger:

“Where did He get these shatpad from, when the tradition stresses nav tattva? To know that, you have to come to the next Yajna…”

12. Final condensation of Verse 42

  • The atmārthī section is now complete.
  • Verse 42 says in spirit:

“Right thinking is essential. To generate it and to see the path of liberation clearly, you must know the six fundamentals of the soul. I will now reveal these six truths through a Guru–disciple dialogue, so your intellect is satisfied by reasoning and your heart stays soaked in reverence and love.”

From the next section on:

  • We will watch:
    • Disciple:
      • Bringing doubts, logic, counter-arguments,
    • Guru:
      • Responding with:
        • Reason,
        • Experience,
        • Compassion.

And through that samvād, the seeker gradually receives:

  • Right thinking,
  • Self-realisation,
  • Liberation – the very goal of Atmasiddhi Shastra.
