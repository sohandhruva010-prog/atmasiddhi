Video 93 Systematic Notes (Verse 80 Focus)

Fourth Fundamental Truth begins: Soul is the receiver (bhokta) of fruits of karmas

1) Why we’re studying this (0:00–0:20)

  • The sole purpose: Self-realisation.
  • Therefore, we study the nature of the soul through the Six Fundamental Truths.

2) Quick recap of the first three Fundamentals (0:20–2:24)

1st Fundamental

  • Soul exists.

2nd Fundamental

  • Soul is eternal (everlasting).

3rd Fundamental: “Soul is the doer” (Atma karta chhe)

  • Important clarification:
    • Param Krupalu Dev writes: “Atma karta chhe” (Soul is a doer)
    • He does NOT write: “Soul is doer of karma always.”
  • Why?
    • Doership remains even in liberation (Siddhahood) — because it is the soul’s nature.
    • But doership of karma does NOT remain in liberation.

Key distinction Guru emphasizes

  • In liberation:
    • Doership remains as: doership of manifestation of true nature (pure manifestation).
    • Not: doership of karma.

Example (book example) (1:08–1:35)

  • Like a plain book: every second it remains the same, yet there is still continuous “doership” (activity) in the soul.
  • So: Soul is active even in Siddhahood.

Clarifying a common confusion (1:46–2:16)

  • Some people asked: “Will soul remain doer after liberation?”
  • Answer: Yes (doership is soul’s nature).
  • But: karma-doership is gone (even from early spiritual stages when one abides in the Self).

✅ Conclusion of recap: Third Fundamental = Doership of the soul (not necessarily doership of karma forever).

3) Introducing the 4th Fundamental Truth (2:24–3:10)

4th Fundamental Truth

  • Soul is the receiver (bhokta).

Disciple’s doubt

  • The disciple doubts:
    • Soul is NOT the receiver of fruits of karmas (karma-fal = fruits/results of karmas bound to the soul)

Structure

  • Disciple has two arguments for this doubt:

1. Verse 79 argument (already discussed in previous session)

2. Verse 80–81 argument (this video begins moving into this)

4) Recap of Disciple’s 1st argument (Verse 79) (3:10–6:29)

Main question raised

  • “Soul may be receiver… but who gives the fruits?”

First option (rejected)

  • If karmas are the giver:
    • karmas are inanimate (jada) → no consciousness
    • so karmas cannot “know”:
      • what to give
      • whom to give
      • when to give
      • how much to give, etc.

Important logical point Guru stresses

  • It does NOT logically follow that:
    • If you are the doer, you must be the receiver.

Everyday examples (3:50–4:41)

  • Cook cooks your food (doer), but may not eat it (not receiver).
  • Mother makes cheese toast (doer), but may not eat it herself (not receiver).

Very important “4 combinations” point (5:04–5:16)

Guru says all four are possible:

1. Doer + Receiver

2. Doer + Non-receiver

3. Non-doer + Receiver

4. Non-doer + Non-receiver

So the disciple’s doubt has room logically.

✅ So disciple says:

  • “I may accept soul as doer… but I still don’t see how soul becomes receiver of fruits.”

5) Moving to Disciple’s 2nd argument (Verses 80–81) (6:29–8:05)

Why a second argument is needed

  • Since karmas are inanimate, they can’t perform the conscious “giving fruit” function.

Disciple’s reasoning

  • The “giving-receiving fruits” process looks like a conscious activity.
  • Therefore:
    • there must be some conscious entity behind it.

Disciple’s next conclusion

  • That conscious entity can be none other than:
    • God (Ishwar)

So disciple proposes:

  • God dispenses justice and gives rewards/punishment → souls receive.

6) The twist: even this “God as fruit-giver” option creates a problem (8:05–9:15)

Very important: The disciple is honest and subtle here.

  • He proposes:
    • “If karma can’t give fruits, then maybe God gives fruits.”
  • But he immediately admits:
    • “Even this doesn’t fully feel reasonable… I see difficulties… but I don’t know how to rule it out.”

So disciple becomes more confused, not more confident.

7) Verse 80 explained word-by-word (9:15–11:39)

Verse idea (as explained)

Faldata Ishwar ganye, bhoktapanu sadhay;
Em kahye Ishwartanu, Ishwarpanu j jaay.

Key terms
  • Faldata = fruit-giver (fal = fruit/result, data = giver)
  • Ishwar ganye = “if we consider God as…”
  • Bhoktapanu = the soul’s receivership
  • Sadhay = can be established/proved
  • Em kahye = if you say like this / if you claim this
  • Ishwarpanu j jaay = God’s “Godliness” goes away (Godhood collapses)

Disciple’s statement (exact logical structure)

1. If we accept God as the fruit-giver, then:

  • the proposition “soul is receiver” becomes easy to establish.

2. But if we say this:

  • God loses Godliness.
  • His “Ishwarpanu” collapses.

✅ Short meaning (as Guru states)

  • God-as-fruit-giver solves the “receiver” problem…
  • but creates a bigger problem:
    • God is no longer truly God.

8) Why “God loses Godliness” (11:52–15:01)

Disciple’s concern

If God must give fruits:

  • God becomes like an accountant / judge managing infinite karmic accounts.

Why this is degrading

  • Infinite souls (anant jivo) are receiving fruits:
    • every moment (kshane-kshane).
  • That means:
    • God has no break, no “March ending,” no pause—constant workload.

So:

  • God becomes worldly-entangled, not divine.

The “mess” the disciple sees

  • If we accept God as fruit-giver → Godliness collapses.
  • If we don’t accept God as fruit-giver → soul’s receivership becomes hard to prove (in disciple’s mind).

So disciple feels trapped.

9) Legal / court analogy: why “fruits” imply a judge (15:01–19:31)

Why disciple thinks a “judge” is needed

  • Like a thief committing robbery:
    • punishment doesn’t happen automatically.
    • you need:
      • judge + law system + process

IPC example

  • IPC 420 (cheating/forgery) is referenced culturally (“420”).

Big takeaway Guru highlights

  • Even in worldly life:
    • the act itself doesn’t instantly produce its consequence.
    • a system must decide, dispense, deliver.

✅ The “3 D’s” (19:05–19:11)

1. Decide

2. Dispense

3. Deliver

Setup for next verse

  • Guru hints:
    • “designated places” like jail exist in worldly punishment
    • similarly, concepts like heaven/hell come in next verse discussion

10) Emotional reason people want God as justice-dispenser (20:11–24:47)

Guru explains the psychology (very important tone):

Bollywood-style belief

  • “Incomplete justice of court is completed by God.”

Human emotional need

  • When facing injustice, people feel:
    • “God is watching”
    • “God will deliver justice”
  • Otherwise people wonder:
    • What’s the point of temples, statues, prayers, devotion—if God only knows but never acts?

Employer analogy

  • Like an employer:
    • checks work done
    • pays wages accordingly
  • People want God similarly:
    • reward good deeds
    • forgive sins

11) The “Justice vs Compassion” contradiction (24:55–37:07)

Guru shares a Theology exam insight:

Key statement

  • God cannot be perfectly just and perfectly compassionate at the same time (in the way people emotionally demand).

Why?

  • If God is just:
    • He must punish wrongdoing → feels harsh.
  • If God is compassionate:
    • He forgives → then victims feel injustice → God appears partial.

The human double-demand

  • When we are right:
    • we want God to punish the wrongdoer (justice).
  • When we are wrong:
    • we want God to forgive us (compassion).

So our expectations themselves are contradictory.

12) Workload problem: God becomes a “busy manager” (37:07–43:43)

What people want in God (37:07 onwards)

  • Almighty
  • Omniscient (all-knowing)
  • Omnipotent (all-powerful)
  • Compassionate

But if God must dispense fruits every moment…

  • Infinite souls, day and night
  • constant checking + accounting + delivering consequences

Guru uses humor:

  • “Can God go to Maldives for 3 days?”
  • Even if He does, the work continues there too.

Strong point: God is not even “free”

Guru pushes the logic further:

  • Even as “judge,” God must act according to karmic record.
  • So God is more like a:
    • manager implementing rules,
    • not an independent divine will.

So calling Him “God” becomes meaningless—He’s reduced to administration.

13) Godliness = absolute purity; fruit-giving compromises purity (43:55–45:35)

Godliness definition in this talk

  • Godliness means:
    • unadulterated purity
    • no iota of impurity

But fruit-giving requires:

  • involvement with everyone’s good/bad deeds every moment
  • constant engagement with worldly functioning

So:

  • God’s purity gets “compromised” (in disciple’s view).

Guru references:

  • “Bhagwan” relates to “bhag” (Godliness)
  • Godliness expressed in 16 ways (he doesn’t list them here)

14) Practical-life analogies: Why burden God with administration? (47:51–53:42)

Housing / allotment example

  • If you chose 1-bedroom, you get 1-bedroom—why should God interfere?

“Let God enjoy bliss” idea

  • Guru says (in effect):
    • don’t remove Godliness by making God do files, lists, allocations, administration

Guru-compassion analogy

  • Devotees don’t want Guru entangled in administration:
    • “You remain in bliss; we will manage.”
  • Then Guru asks:
    • if you can think this way for Guru, why not for God?
