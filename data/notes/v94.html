Video 109 — Verse 94 (Third Argument of the Disciple)

1) Where we are in the scripture (big picture)

  • The disciple’s first five doubts (about the first five Fundamental Truths) have been resolved:
    • Soul exists
    • Soul is eternal
    • Soul is doer of karma
    • Soul is receiver/sufferer of fruits
    • Liberation (moksh) exists / is possible
  • Now begins the last and most “seminal” doubt:
    • Moksh upay = the means/path to liberation (Sixth Fundamental Truth).
  • The disciple has no doubt about moksh being possible now, but he has doubt about:
    • Is there one “undisputed / unfailing” path?
    • Or is everything disputed, contradictory, confusing?

2) The disciple’s worthiness (Guru highlights this strongly)

Your Guru repeatedly shows that this disciple is not ordinary. He has:

A) Unflinching faith in Sadguru

  • Even while presenting difficult doubts, he is not afraid that the doubt will remain unresolved.
  • He trusts: “Once my Guru speaks, not even an iota of confusion will remain.”

B) Intelligence + humility

  • He presents confusion clearly, not emotionally, not defensively.
  • He admits his limitation without ego.

C) Child learning to walk → now wants to run (beautiful metaphor)

  • The disciple is like a child who has just learned to stand and walk…
  • Now he wants to run, fearless of falling, because:
    • “His mother has held his hands.”
    • Meaning: Sadguru is holding him.

3) “Noise of doubts” becomes “silence of devotion” (Airport analogy)

Your Guru uses a powerful modern analogy:

Earlier airports: noisy

  • Announcements, commotion, loud calling for flights.

Now: “silent airports”

  • No announcements.
  • Only signboards.
  • Airports are calm and quiet.

✅ Spiritual parallel:

  • In the beginning: lots of noise → many questions, many doubts.
  • Gradually, as doubts are cleared one by one:
    • the inner noise reduces,
    • devotion becomes quieter,
    • a stillness arises.

He even adds a personal memory:

  • At a silent airport, due to jetlag they fell asleep because there were no announcements—so you had to stay alert.

Key point: clarity creates silence; confusion creates noise.

4) The core doubt again (what exactly is the disciple doubting?)

  • Truth (Sixth Fundamental): There is a path of liberation.
  • Disciple’s doubt: There seems to be no path that is:
    • Avirodh = undisputed / universally acceptable
    • Achuk = unfailing (will not fail you)

Your Guru notes: Param Krupalu Dev uses these Gujarati terms:

  • Achuk upay → unfailing method (won’t fail)
  • Avirodh upay → undisputed method (universally acceptable)

And he emphasizes:

  • What will be taught here is so universal that any enlightened person, of any faith, time, place would agree.

5) The disciple presents the doubt using 3 arguments (92, 93, 94)

Your Guru organizes it clearly:

Argument 1 (Verse 92)

  • Infinite karmas from infinite past + still adding new karmas
  • How can they be destroyed in one short human life?

Argument 2 (Verse 93)

  • Too many paths; multiple and diverse opinions
  • Hard to decide which is true.

Argument 3 (Verse 94) — THIS VIDEO

  • Confusion is intensified by external identity markers:
    • caste / gender
    • attire / dress / garb
  • “If liberation depends on these… then which one is correct?”

6) Verse 94: Word-by-word meaning (as Guru explains)

Main phrase 1: “Kai jatima moksh chhe?”

  • Kai = which
  • Jati = two meanings:

1. caste

2. gender

  • Moksh chhe = leads to liberation / is eligible for liberation

So the disciple is asking:

  • “Which caste (or gender) is required for moksh?”

Main phrase 2: “Kaya veshma moksh?”

  • Vesh = garb / attire / apparel / clothing
  • “Which dress/garb is required for moksh?”

Main phrase 3: “Eno nishchay na baney”

  • Nishchay = firm decision / determination
  • Na baney = cannot be done / extremely difficult Meaning:
  • “I can’t decide this.”

Main phrase 4: “Ghana bhed e dosh chhe”

  • Ghana bhed = many differences, divisions, conflicting variations
  • Dosh = fault/problem Meaning:
  • “The biggest problem is the enormous differences of opinion.”

✅ Guru’s memory technique:

  • He calls this the “two C’s” issue:
    • Caste and Clothing (C–C)

7) The Guru “leaks” the answer early (important)

Your Guru openly says:

  • The disciple’s confusion exists because he is caught in the external.
  • The Guru’s reply will be:
    • Don’t get caught in the external.
    • Focus on inner purity.
    • Inner purity is what leads to liberation.

Balloon example (very powerful)

He asks (especially in USA discourses):

  • What makes balloons rise high?
    • the outer color/design/shape?
    • or the inner gas?

Answer: internal.

✅ Message:

  • Inner purity lifts you, not your caste or clothing.

8) Why the disciple’s confusion is “justifiable” (Guru is sympathetic)

Your Guru says: this confusion is understandable because:

  • The disciple has studied many religions and scriptures.
  • He has read many “do’s and don’ts.”
  • And he finds:
    • not only multiple rules,
    • but opposite rules.

Example:

  • Some say: wear white.
  • Some say: be unclad.

So he’s confused—but the root cause is external obsession.

9) External vs internal: faith needs a “wrapper” but don’t worship the wrapper

Your Guru explains balance using simple examples:

A) Gift wrapper example

  • Every gift has a wrapper or bag.
  • It’s natural.
  • But the wrapper is not the real gift.

B) Rules in black and white

  • In any organized group, you need external rules written clearly:
    • e.g., “can you wear shorts or not?”
  • But “be a nice guy” can’t be written so neatly.

C) Driving-side example (Canada)

  • You can’t allow people to drive left or right however they want.
  • External discipline is necessary for society.

✅ Key warning:

  • If external becomes over-emphasized and internal is forgotten:
    • organization will thrive
    • but humanity/spirituality will decay and die

This line is a major takeaway.

10) Why “external questions” still arise at the beginning (practical spiritual psychology)

Your Guru says:

  • People ask:
    • which mantra?
    • which daily prayer?
    • which book?
    • how to meditate?
  • These are not ultimate questions, but they are common at the start because:
    • mind-body-speech needs to be engaged first,
    • then gradually you withdraw inward.

He even comments:

  • Earlier people asked him “which book should I read?”
  • Now nobody asks (either because they became spiritual… or lazy)
  • He chooses optimism: “you’ve become spiritual.”

✅ Important nuance:

  • Guru does not deny externals.
  • He puts them in their proper place:
    • start there if needed
    • but don’t get stuck there.

11) Deep dive: “Jati” as caste (why this confuses the disciple)

Your Guru explains caste historically and psychologically (as context for the disciple’s confusion):

Origin (as an arrangement, not spiritual truth)

  • Society divided into four for practical functioning:

1. Brahmin — religion, rituals, teaching, guidance

2. Kshatriya — ruling/protection; legislature/executive/judiciary

3. Vaishya — trade/commerce, finances

4. Shudra — labor/workforce

Originally it was meant to be harmonious.

Later corruption

  • Values declined; ego/pride increased.
  • Discrimination began:
    • Shudras called “low,” “untouchable,” etc.
    • not allowed into temples
    • not allowed to chant Vedas

He mentions that if one reads lines like “Shudras should be beaten” without Sadguru’s proper interpretation, one can be misled—showing how texts can be misunderstood without right guidance.

The disciple’s problem

He has studied conflicting claims like:

  • “Only Brahmins can attain liberation” vs
  • “Anyone with the right virtues can attain liberation”

He cites Uttaradhyayan Sutra as an example your Guru references:

  • Virtues matter, not caste.
  • Example given: Harikesh muni (from scheduled caste) attained omniscience/liberation.

So the disciple asks:

  • “Before I put effort… tell me: is liberation even available to someone like me?”

12) “Jati” as gender (second meaning)

Your Guru says gender (in old classifications) is:

1. male

2. female

3. neuter Some traditions restrict liberation eligibility, claiming:

  • only males can attain liberation

He mentions disputed examples:

  • Marudevi mata
  • Chandanbala
  • Mrugavati
  • Mallinath as female Tirthankar

Then he clarifies the real question:

  • “Historical arguments aside—what is the principle?”
  • “If females put right effort, can they attain liberation?”

So again: multiple claims → confusion.

13) “Vesh” as attire/garb (why attire becomes a battleground)

Your Guru explains:

A) Uniforms exist everywhere (for identification)

He lists:

  • jails (uniforms)
  • hospitals (nurse/doctor uniforms)
  • police (uniform creates instant authority)
  • taxis (identifiable colors/shapes by city: Kolkata, Mumbai, London, NY)
  • schools (we had uniforms; we didn’t oppose)

✅ Point:

  • Uniforms are natural for organizations.
  • They are not inherently “wrong.”
  • But they are not the cause of liberation.

B) Strong statement: both extremes are foolish

Your Guru says:

  • If you say: “Only THIS attire gives moksh” → foolish.
  • If you say: “I reject all attire norms” → bigger fool.

Why?

  • If you are truly detached from body,
    • what difference does clothing make?
    • A spiritual person can follow rules easily because ego doesn’t get hurt.

This is a key “inner spiritual maturity” diagnostic.

14) Sectarian confusion becomes intense (Shwetambar vs Digambar etc.)

Your Guru shows how it escalates:

  • Hindu monks: saffron/ochre
  • Buddhists: yellow
  • Shwetambar: white required
  • Digambar: unclad required

Then more layers:

  • Some say householders can attain liberation.
  • Some say without monk attire (and our sect’s accessories like muhapatti/rajoharan), you cannot even be considered eligible.

He critiques the mentality:

  • loyalty to sect/attire becomes mandatory,
  • loyalty to soul/purity becomes optional.

He uses a sharp analogy:

  • liberation becomes a commodity,
  • sects become advertising houses:
    • “Use OUR washing powder only!”

✅ This is how he frames the disciple’s confusion as very real and very modern.

15) Final shape of the disciple’s third argument (Verse 94)

So the disciple’s argument becomes:

  • If moksh-upay is real, why do:
    • caste rules differ?
    • gender eligibility differ?
    • attire requirements differ?
    • sects insist “only ours”?

Even if not every priest agrees… at least enlightened masters should agree—yet he sees differences.

So he concludes emotionally + logically:

  • “Because there are so many differences and contradictions, I cannot decide.”
  • “Therefore I feel there is no undisputed path.”

He even illustrates the seeker’s chaotic mind:

  • “Maybe I should’ve worn yellow.”
  • “No progress—maybe remove clothes completely.”
  • “Still nothing—maybe wear red.”
  • Going helter-skelter.

16) The central takeaway your Guru wants you to carry (before the formal answer)

Even before Guru’s final reply verses begin, the direction is crystal:

✅ Inner purity is the engine of liberation. ❌ Caste and clothing are not the cause of moksh.

But:

  • external structures exist in religions for organization/identity,
  • and beginners may need external engagement first,
  • yet the core is always internal transformation.

Verse 94 — Third argument: Because of many conflicting opinions about:

  • Jati (caste or gender) and
  • Vesh (attire/garb), the disciple says he cannot determine the universally accepted, undisputed path of liberation.

Memory hook: C–C = Caste & Clothing. Guru’s leaked answer: like balloons rise due to inner gas, liberation depends on inner purity, not externals.
