Section: 2 — matārthīnā lakṣhaṇ (attributes of a religious bigot) Earlier (v.24–26): the bigot’s false beliefs about Satdev (true God) and Satguru (true Guru). Now (v.27–28): false beliefs about Satdharma (true religion). Today’s focus (v.27): two mistakes a bigot makes about religion:

1. What counts as scriptural study (shrutjnan).

2. What counts as religious attire (external dress/symbols).

The verse (key lines & sense)

  • “Devadi gati bhangma je samje shrutjnan” Meaning: He thinks that knowing and repeating the classifications (bhang) of the four gatis (forms of existence—human, animal, celestial, hellish) is the scriptural knowledge needed for liberation.
  • “Maney nij mat veshno agrah muktinidan” Meaning: He believes (maney) that insisting (agrah) on the attire (vesh) of his own sect (nij mat) is the cause (nidan) of liberation.

Bottom line: The bigot mistakes cataloguing facts for spirituality and external dress for inner virtue.

Before the line-by-line: “Closed mind syndrome”

  • “Having an idea is a dangerous thing in itself if you don’t have any other idea.”
  • “More dangerous is when you insist that that is the best for everyone.”
  • “This is called closed mind syndrome… keyhole perception: knows little, thinks it is complete, and insists—agrah.”
  • Social effect: Insistence creates rebels; “both are not truth seekers.”
  • Guiding spirit: “Live and let live… I’m saying—grow and let live.”

These set the tone for why the two mistakes below are spiritually harmful.

Mistake #1 — What counts as “scriptural knowledge” (shrutjnan)

What the bigot believes

  • If he studies, memorises, and recites the classifications of living beings and realms, he has the scriptural knowledge needed for liberation.

“If you study it, if you memorise it, you have done your work.”

  • He focuses on names, numbers, sizes, lifespans, subcategories, etc., e.g.,
    • The four gatis: manushya (human), tiryanch (animal), dev (celestial—“don’t call them Gods”), narak (hellish).
    • Body sizes, lifespans (e.g., “python… 700–800 years,” “mosquito… few minutes or few hours”).
    • Birth types: sexual vs asexual (sammurchhim).
    • Plant categories: “anantkaay and pratyek vanaspatikaay—green plants, non-green plants like algae, bacteria, fungus, mushrooms.”
  • He can rattle off counts (e.g., “563 subcategories”) and labels (e.g., “5th stage… Desh virti [Deshvirati]”) yet remains unchanged within.

“There is no Self in it… and Self-realisation means knowledge of the Self.”

The teacher’s clarification

  • These are right scriptures (e.g., Gommatsaar by Nemichandra Siddhānt-Chakravarti, Shatkhandagam, Karmagranth, Lokprakash, Jivavichar Prakaran, Tattvarth Sutra, the 4 Prakaran, 3 Bhashya—Devvandan, Guruvandan, Pratyakhyan—Dandak, Sangrahani, Jambudweep Sangrahani).

“He is introduced to right scriptures… still he does not know the spiritual purpose of studying.”

  • Spiritual purpose of such detailed descriptions:

1. Awareness of transmigration — “how much you have wandered in different forms of existence since time immemorial.”

2. Cultivate detachment — loosen fascination with worldly objects, people, events.

3. Retreat from impure feelings — e.g., “too lustful, then you become a pig;
deceit, you become a snake.”

The point is to withdraw from passions, not to collect trivia.

  • Two criteria to know your study is working:

1. “Decreasing of attachment [to worldly people/objects/events].”

2. “Increasing of inward focus [inner stillness].”

  • “This is liberation: ‘My attachment to my son is loosening.’ Not that Siddhashila.”
  • Memorisation without these two effects brings “no spiritual benefit.”

“It is the virtues which is important and not just memory.”

Illustrative asides (kept in his words)

  • Reverence for life: the “deadly cockroach” and “lizard, mosquito may be my devotee who wanted to stay near me” → handle gently.
  • “No admission without permission” sign for the “pair of Jerry” (rats) outside the room—humour used to train non-violence and detachment.
  • Study discipline praised: “Maulikji… 5:30 am studying Tattvarth Sutra—beautiful sight.”
  • On insistence in little things:

“If the same bhajan is sung in a different tune, you’ll be upset… room for no other way but this way.”

Mistake #2 — What counts as “religious attire” (vesh)

What the bigot believes

  • “Maney nij mat veshno agrah muktinidan”: He insists (agrah) that his sect’s dress/symbols are the cause of liberation.

“Till you don’t adopt this specific external attire, you cannot attain liberation.”

  • He attaches outsized importance to robes, colors, badges, and sect markers:
    • White vs saffron/orange/ochre: “The moment he is in saffron… he is not on the right path.”
    • Mouth-cover (mask) styles: “Terapanthis have a long mouth cover this way;
      Sthanakvasis this way… a little change of size/shape and you’ll call that person deluded.”

“In COVID, everyone is a Sthanakvasi.”

  • Tilak marks: “Vaishnavs have standing ‘U’; Shaivites have sleeping (horizontal).”
  • Implements/symbols: “Mouth cover, broom, peacock feathers, kamandu/kamandal, rudraksh…”
  • Orders/robes: “If I’m Sthanakvasi, Digambar, idol-worshipper…” “Ji’s have a specific dress, Atmarpits have a specific dress.”
  • He equates samyak charitra (right conduct) with dress, not with virtues.

The teacher’s clarification

  • Dress is of the body; liberation is of the Soul.

“There is no discrimination based on caste or attire… In any caste, in any attire, if you follow the said path.”

  • What is that said path?

“Love, hate and ignorance are the main causes of bondage; that by which they are removed—that alone is the path of liberation.”

  • Adopt external aids if they help you, but don’t insist on them for everyone.

“He does not negate anything… He just says don’t insist.”

  • True monk = inner state, not outfit:

“The true sign… is freedom from love and hate—raag and dwesh. If you see someone in shirt-pant but free within, your head should bow.”

  • Scriptural support for non-sectarian liberation:

“‘Siddha panras bhayo’ (pander bhede Siddha)—15 categories of Siddhas—even non-Jain traditions and householders are included.” Meaning: attire/affiliation are not the decisive factors; freedom from passions is.

Illustrative asides (kept in his words)

  • On sectarian sensitivity: a devotee joyfully sings “I’m in love with Your Countenance, O dear Krishna,” but stops at “Bai Meera’s Lord Krishna.”

“If I say Allahu Akbar, then I don’t know what would happen! And I don’t care!” (Point: Truth over sect-labels.)

  • On food-rules and petty identity wars: “In our Sthanakvasi, we can eat an a-ple [apple] on the 5, 8, 14th day… Do you eat papaya?” (Point: stop weaponising customs; see their purpose or let them be.)

Practical diagnostics for study & practice

Use these to check whether your scripture-study and practice are on track:

1. Is attachment decreasing?

  • “Worldly people, worldly objects, worldly events, worldly achievements”—is your grip loosening?
  • Everyday signs: less possessiveness, softer reactions, fewer demands.

2. Is inward focus increasing?

  • More inner stillness, quicker self-awareness when passions arise, easier retreat from unwholesome feelings.

If these two are present, your study is “perfect”—even if you don’t remember all the technicalities.

Core definitions (as used)

  • Matārthī: religious bigot; stubborn, insistent (agrah), closed mind syndrome.
  • Atmārthī: true seeker of the Soul; focuses on inner state.
  • Shrutjnan: scriptural knowledge; purpose is inner transformation, not information.
  • Gati: form of existence—human, animal, celestial, hellish.
  • Bhang: classification.
  • Samyak charitra: right conduct; freedom from raag-dwesh (love-hate).
  • Muktinidan: cause of liberation.
  • Agrah: insistence/rigid adherence.

“Do / Don’t” at a glance

Do
  • Read scriptures to see through transmigration, cultivate detachment, and retreat from impure feelings.
  • Measure progress by the two criteria: less attachment, more inwardness.
  • Use attire/symbols as optional aids, never as yardsticks of others’ worth.
Don’t
  • Reduce study to cataloguing names, numbers, and lifespans.
  • Insist on your sect’s dress/symbols as necessary for liberation.
  • Confuse memory with virtue or uniform with freedom from passions.

Short, smooth recap (one page)

  • Context: We’re in matārthīna lakṣhaṇ—exposing how a religious bigot thinks.
  • Verse 27 targets two misbeliefs about religion:

1. Mistaking classification-learning for liberation-knowledge.

2. Mistaking sect attire for the cause of liberation.

  • Why wrong? Because both are external: they don’t touch the Self.
  • Right use of scriptures: To awaken detachment, inwardness, and withdrawal from impure feelings.
  • Right view of attire: Wear what helps, but liberation is by ending raag-dwesh, not uniforms.
  • Checks: Am I seeing less attachment and more inward stillness? If yes, the study is working.
  • Spirit: Trade insistence for openness—from matārthī to Atmārthī.
