Śrī Atmasiddhi Śāstra — Verse 7 Renunciation & Detachment (Tyāg–Virāg) — Necessity, Balance, and Aim

Verse (Gujarati & Transliteration)

Tyāg virāg na chittmā, thāy na tene jñān;
Atke tyāg virāgmā, to bhule nij-bhān. 7

Transliteration: Tyāg virāg na chittmā, thāy na tene jñān;
Atke tyāg virāgmā, to bhule nij-bhān.

Word-to-Word Meaning
  • Tyāg virāg — renunciation & detachment
  • Na chittmā — not present in one’s heart/mind (inner feeling absent)
  • Thāy na tene jñān — he does not attain Self-realisation/enlightenment
  • Atke — gets stuck / is satisfied / halts there
  • Tyāg virāgmā — in the practices/feelings of renunciation & detachment
  • To bhule nij-bhān — then he forgets Self-awareness (awareness of the pure Self)

Short Meaning One cannot attain Self-realisation without inner feelings of renunciation and detachment. Conversely, if one gets stuck merely in renunciation/detachment practices, he forgets Self-awareness and misses the goal.

1) Where Verse 7 sits in the flow (Verses 3–8)

  • 3: Names two deluded types — kriyājada (mechanical ritualist) & śuṣka-jñānī (dry intellectualist).
  • 4–5: Exposes their traits.
  • 6: Shows the place of auxiliaries (detachment etc.) before & after Self-realisation.
  • 7 (this verse): Reinforces necessity and warns against mistaking means for the end.
  • 8: Portrait of a true seeker (atmārthī): prudence — adopting what is fitting, where fitting.

Verse 7 is a double-edged instruction: (a) cultivate inner tyāg–virāg or SR won’t dawn; (b) don’t stall in tyāg–virāg as a goal, or you’ll forget nij-bhān.

2) Two lines, two audiences

Line 1 — Message to the śuṣka-jñānī (dry intellectualist): Tyāg virāg na chittmā, thāy na tene jñān. If renunciation and detachment are not alive within, Self-realisation won’t dawn. Knowledge that doesn’t soften the heart into letting-go remains dry — information without transformation.

Line 2 — Message to the kriyājada (mechanical ritualist): Atke tyāg virāgmā, to bhule nij-bhān. If one gets stuck in the practices/identity of renunciation/detachment — counting fasts, hours of silence, rituals — he forgets Self-awareness, mistaking means (sādhana) for end (sādhya).

3) Why inner tyāg–virāg is necessary (Line 1 unpacked)

As long as the world is important to me: 1) I derive joy from it.

2) Desires flare for it.

3) Detachment does not arise.

4) I do not turn inward (antarmukhīta doesn’t stabilise).

Therefore, without inner renunciation/detachment, Self-vision cannot settle; scriptural knowledge remains śuṣka (dry), with no “taste of the Self.”

It is not how much you can do, but how much you truly feel. The chitta must value the inward over the outward.

4) The “getting stuck” hazard (Line 2 unpacked)

Symptoms of ‘atke’ — halting in auxiliaries: - Counting replaces consciousness: “I finished 1000 samayik, 8-day fasts, 12-hour silences…” but Why did I do them?

  • Means become the goal: The day ends when the checklist is done, not when awareness deepens.
  • Identity-building: “I am austere/devotional” → subtle pride; insistence (agraha) and condemnation (nindā) of others.
  • Result: Nij-bhān is forgotten; effort becomes busy piety instead of inner purity.

Touchstone question: After any practice, can I point to more Self-awareness, more equanimity, less reactivity? If not, I’ve likely stalled in form.

5) Verse 6 vs Verse 7 — the reinforcement

  • Verse 6 established: Auxiliaries (detachment etc.) are fruitful with SR (post-SR) and are valid means when aimed solely at SR (pre-SR).
  • Verse 7 reinforces: (i) Without inner tyāg–virāg, SR will not dawn; (ii) With attachment to tyāg–virāg, SR will be forgotten.

Thus: Don’t call auxiliaries useless (dry-intellectual error), and don’t call them the goal (mechanical-ritual error). They are tools to be felt and used, not worshipped as ends.

6) Practical diagnostics

A. For the dry intellectualist (Line 1):
  • Do I still seek both — inner awakening and undiminished worldly importance?
  • Do I excuse attachment with “The soul is pure anyway”?
  • Action: adopt a small, steady auxiliary (e.g., measured speech, mindful giving, one daily simplification) stamped with the intention: “May this prepare me for Self-realisation.”
B. For the mechanical ritualist (Line 2):
  • After ritual/fast/silence, can I report one inner shift?
  • Do I feel satisfied by the performance itself?
  • Action: attach a clarity clause to every practice: “If this doesn’t raise awareness/equanimity, I will re-design the practice tomorrow.”

7) Means vs End — everyday examples

  • Samayik: not “48 minutes performed,” but “48 minutes of equanimity training; how much samatva rose today?”
  • Fasts: not “day 8 achieved,” but “did fasting loosen craving, refine attention, open compassion?”
  • Silence: not “12 hours checked,” but “did silence thin mental loops, brighten awareness?”

Swap “What did I do?” with “What did I become?”

8) Four-by-Four recap you can memorise

If the world is important: (i) joy from it, (ii) desires arise, (iii) detachment absent, (iv) no inwardness. If practice becomes the goal: (i) counting replaces awareness, (ii) ego of austerity, (iii) insistence/ condemnation, (iv) Self-awareness forgotten. If inner tyāg–virāg grows: (i) value turns inward, (ii) desires subside, (iii) equanimity rises, (iv) SR becomes plausible. If means serve the end: (i) awareness deepens, (ii) reactivity drops, (iii) humility grows, (iv) love for the Self intensifies.

9) A gentle correction to both camps

  • To the śuṣka-jñānī: “Cultivate feelings of renunciation/detachment; don’t only speak of them.”
  • To the kriyājada: “Practise — but don’t stop at practice; carry it into nij-bhān every time.”

Verse 7 is the art of aiming: hold tools lightly, aim firmly at the Self.

10) A mini practice plan (this week)

1) Intention stamp (10s): Before any auxiliary, say: “Let this serve nij-bhān.”

2) One inner renunciation: Identify an inner giving-up (e.g., “renounce irritation with X”), not just an outer one.

3) Outcome log (1–2 lines) after each practice: note 1 change in awareness/equanimity.

4) World-importance check: List today’s top 3 pulls; rehearse their transitoriness for 60s each.

11) Memorisation & punctuation cue

Tyāg virāg na chittmā — (pause) thāy na tene jñān;
Atke tyāg virāgmā — (fresh start) to bhule nij-bhān.

One-breath Summary

Self-realisation requires inner renunciation/detachment; mere talk won’t do. Yet if one halts in renunciation/detachment as a finish line, Self-awareness is forgotten. Let every auxiliary be a living means to nij-bhān — never the substitute for it.

End of Notes (Verse 7)
