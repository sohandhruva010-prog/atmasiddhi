1) Link between Verse 68 → Verse 69

Verse 68 established:

  • Every substance has both:
    • permanence (as substance/dravya)
    • impermanence (as states/paryay) ✅ So “change happens” is accepted.

The remaining confusion:

Some people accept soul exists, but say it is absolutely momentary:

“Created + destroyed every moment.”

Guru’s key point:

  • Saying “it changes” is TRUE ✅
  • Saying “it is ONLY momentary” is FALSE ❌

📌 The entire mistake is the word: “ONLY”

2) What Guru does in Verse 69

He does not say:

  • “Jain philosophy says…”

He does: ✅ Logic-only proof that “only momentary” cannot stand.

The target belief:

  • “Soul exists for one moment only.”

3) The Verse (central statement)

Main line:

“Athvā jñān kṣhaṇiknu je jānī, vadanāro kṣhaṇik nahī;
Kar anubhav nirdhār.”

Meaning in one sentence: ✅ The one who knows and speaks (narrates) momentariness cannot himself be momentary.

4) Word-by-word meanings (very exam-friendly)

Athvā

= “Or / alternatively” (Guru gives one more argument)

Jñān

= knowledge

Kṣhaṇik

= momentary (existing only for one moment)

Je jānī

= the one who knows

Vadanāro / Vadnaro

= the speaker / narrator / describer (he says: vadnar = kehnar, so vadnaro = the one who speaks)

Kṣhaṇik nahī

= is not momentary

Kar anubhav nirdhār

= decide by your own experience / observation

5) The core logic (the killer proof)

If someone says:

“Everything exists for only ONE moment.”

Then the person must:

1. Know it (moment 1)

2. Say it (moment 2)

✅ That requires at least 2 moments of existence.

So the speaker/narrator cannot be “one-moment only.”

📌 Even if they claim:

  • “I understood it instantly in 1 moment” They still need another moment to describe it.

So at minimum: ✅ the knower + narrator is not momentary.

6) What exactly gets proven (important nuance)

Guru is very precise here:

He does NOT claim yet:

  • “Soul is eternal” (infinite-time)

He only proves: ✅ “Soul is not momentary” (not one-moment only)

Because technically, someone could exist for:

  • 10 moments, 100 moments, then die So eternity isn’t proved here.

But the disciple’s doubt was:

“Soul is momentary.”

So Guru only needs to defeat: ✅ “momentary” → refuted

7) Why this is so powerful

This argument is a classic “self-defeating statement” logic:

Like Guru’s examples:

  • “The moment you say I cannot speak, you prove you CAN speak.”
  • “The moment you say I don’t have a tongue, your speech proves you do.”

Same idea:

  • The moment you argue “soul is only momentary,” your continuity of knowing + speaking disproves it.

8) Connection to Jain scholarship (background he gives)

He says: Acharyas gave 5 refutations of “only momentary” (ekānt kṣhaṇik):

1. Kṛt-nāsh = what you do gets destroyed → no fruit of actions

2. Akṛta-ābhyāgam = you experience something you didn’t do (new soul suffering)

3. Sansār-nāsh = no heaven/hell makes sense (doer is destroyed)

4. Mokṣha-asambhāvnā = liberation becomes meaningless (it’s just permanent death)

5. Smaran-asambhav ✅ (the one he uses here) = recollection / memory / continuity becomes impossible

📌 Param Krupalu Dev chooses #5 because it hits directly:

  • knowing → remembering → narrating

9) How Verse 69 fits the “Second Fundamental Truth”

Second Fundamental Truth = Soul is eternal

Flow of proof:

  • Verse 68: substance eternal, states change
  • Verse 69: “only momentary” is illogical → soul not momentary
  • Verse 70: will bring in the law of conservation style argument (no destruction of substance)

So 68–70 together become a complete multi-angle answer to Verse 61.

10) One-line takeaway (perfect to memorize)

✅ States may change moment-to-moment, but the knower of change cannot be one-moment only. Therefore the soul is not “only momentary.”
