Video 129 Notes: Verse 114 — The Dream of Beginningless Delusion Ends on Awakening

1) Context: the third group begins

After completing the explanation of the stages of spiritual progress from verses 108–113, the Guru now begins the final group of verses, 114–118.

These verses are described as inspirational because the factual map of the path has already been given. The Guru has already explained:

Verse Stage

108 Preparation: four virtues of a true aspirant

109 Vyavhar samkit — right faith based on Sadguru’s teachings

110 Shuddh samkit — experiential faith

111 Parmarth samkit — highest right faith

112 Right conduct and complete dispassion

113 Kevaljnan — Omniscience

Now the seeker’s natural question is:

If my impurity has existed since beginningless time, how can it be removed? And how long will it take?

Verse 114 answers both questions with one simple example.

2) Verse 114

Koti varshnu swapna pan, jagrut thata shamay;
Tem vibhav anadino, jnan thata dur thay.

Simple meaning

Even a dream lasting millions of years vanishes the moment one awakens. Similarly, delusion existing since beginningless time vanishes when right knowledge or Self-realisation arises.

3) Word-by-word meaning

Koti

Literally means a crore, but here it is explained as millions for an international audience.

Varshnu

Of years.

Swapna

Dream.

Pan

Even.

So:

Koti varshnu swapna pan Even a dream of millions of years.

Jagrut thata

On awakening.

Shamay

Disappears, vanishes, ends.

So:

Even a dream of millions of years disappears on awakening.

Tem

Similarly.

Vibhav

Impurity, delusion, or feelings contrary to the soul’s true nature.

It includes both:

  • belief delusion
  • conduct delusion

In simple language: impurity.

Anadino

Beginningless, from time immemorial, since infinity.

Jnan thata

When right knowledge arises, when enlightenment or Self-realisation occurs.

Dur thay

Goes away, disappears, vanishes.

4) The two questions answered

The verse answers two major questions:

1. How can beginningless impurity be removed?

By jnan — awakening to the true nature of the Self.

2. How long will it take?

Like a dream disappearing on awakening, it can vanish instantly when true awakening occurs.

The key point:

Beginningless impurity does not require endless future time to be removed.

5) Beginningless impurity does not mean endless effort

The soul has remained in impurity since beginningless time. There is no first point from which ignorance began.

But the Guru clarifies:

Infinite past does not mean infinite future.

Just because delusion has existed for beginningless time does not mean it will require beginningless time to end.

A million-year dream can disappear in one moment. Similarly, beginningless delusion can disappear with awakening.

6) Does “jnan” mean Self-realisation or Omniscience?

Because verse 114 comes immediately after verse 113, one may interpret “jnan” as Kevaljnan, Omniscience.

But the speaker explains that Pujyashri Ambalalbhai interprets it as Atmajnan, Self-realisation.

This is important.

At Omniscience, all delusion is completely destroyed. But even at Self-realisation, the deepest faith-delusion is removed, and the soul is considered awakened.

So here “jnan” is best understood as:

Awakening to the true nature of the Self.

When the soul attains Self-realisation, the anantanubandhi passions and fundamental delusion are destroyed. The soul has awakened.

7) Factory machine example

The speaker gives a worldly example.

A factory machine may run continuously for twelve years. It may work day and night without stopping.

But how long does it take to stop the machine?

Not twelve years.

It stops instantly when the switch is turned off.

The point:

The duration for which something has been operating does not determine the time required to stop it.

Similarly, karmic bondage may have continued since beginningless time, but the process can be stopped immediately through the right awakening.

8) Spacecraft example

A spacecraft may have taken billions of dollars, years of planning, and immense technical skill to launch and operate.

But if there is a serious technical issue, it can be stopped or destroyed instantly by the correct command.

Again, the point is:

Long operation does not imply long termination.

Karmic bondage has been continuing for countless lives, but the switch can be turned off through right knowledge.

9) Even a child can switch off the machine

The speaker adds a striking point.

Setting up a factory requires intelligence, resources, and expertise. But switching it off can be done by a child, if the child knows:

  • where the switch is
  • how to operate it
  • when to operate it

This is applied spiritually.

Even an eight-year-old can attain Omniscience if he or she knows the path and applies it rightly.

The emphasis is not on age, scholarship, or worldly intelligence. The emphasis is on knowing how and when to turn inward.

10) Inanimate machinery and conscious machinery The speaker distinguishes between two kinds of machinery.

1. Jad machinery — inanimate machinery

Examples:

  • factory machines
  • spacecraft
  • mechanical systems

These produce only what they are designed to produce.

If a machine is designed to make iron spare parts, it cannot suddenly weave cloth.

It has a fixed output.

2. Chetan machinery — conscious machinery

The soul is conscious machinery.

Unlike inanimate machinery, it can produce opposite outputs.

It can produce:

Impure modification Pure modification

anger forgiveness

pride humility

deceit sincerity

greed contentment

hatred compassion

attachment detachment

This is the power of consciousness.

The soul can choose what to produce.

11) First difference: stopping karmic bondage cannot be delegated A machine can be switched off by someone else. You can ask another person to turn off a light or stop a factory machine.

But karmic bondage cannot be stopped by delegation.

You cannot say:

“Nemi, please stop my karmic bondage.”

Not even the Sadguru can do your inner work for you.

The Guru can guide, inspire, command, and bless. But the effort must be yours.

The first major difference between inanimate and conscious machinery:

In spiritual life, stopping bondage requires self-effort.

12) Second difference: conscious machinery can change its output

A physical machine produces only one kind of output.

But the conscious soul can change its inner production.

The same situation can produce anger or forgiveness depending on the soul’s awareness.

For example:

  • Without awareness, insult produces anger.
  • With Guru’s teaching, the same insult can produce forgiveness.
  • Without awareness, success produces pride.
  • With reflection, the same success can produce humility and gratitude.

The situation does not need to change. The inner production can change.

This is one of the most practical teachings of the video.

13) “Challenges” become “choices”

The speaker recalls telling someone in America:

Do not say, “I have many challenges.” Say, “I have many choices.”

Every situation gives a choice:

  • Will I react or respond?
  • Will I blame or introspect?
  • Will I produce anger or forgiveness?
  • Will I strengthen delusion or move toward Omniscience?

The same situation can become either bondage or liberation.

The deciding factor is the soul’s choice.

14) Two big sins in daily life

The speaker identifies two major sins:

1. Blaming others

This strengthens victimhood, complaint, and attachment-aversion.

2. Taking credit for everything

This strengthens pride and ego.

Even if an idea comes from someone else, one may present it as one’s own. The speaker says this is spiritually harmful.

The right response is gratitude or at least honest acknowledgement.

These two tendencies keep the soul trapped:

  • blaming others
  • claiming credit

Both arise from false ego.

15) Negative thoughts produce negative feelings

The conscious machinery works according to the input.

If one feeds negative thoughts, negative feelings will arise.

The speaker’s formula:

Negative thoughts produce negative feelings.

If the mind constantly thinks in a distorted way, one cannot expect calmness or happiness.

To change the emotional output, one must change the inner input.

16) Delusion can transform into Omniscience

The speaker makes a powerful statement:

If you put in right effort, you can transform the strength of delusion into the strength of Omniscience.

The energy itself need not be destroyed. It must be redirected.

Through:

  • right faith
  • right knowledge
  • right conduct

the soul can transform its direction.

This is the path of samyak darshan, samyak jnan, and samyak charitra.

17) Dream state: neither awake nor deeply asleep

The speaker explains the dream state.

A dream occurs when one is:

  • not awake
  • not in deep sleep

In that state, the subconscious mind is active.

The conscious mind is not functioning fully, but the subconscious mind weaves dreams from impressions, desires, fears, and unresolved experiences.

In the dream, everything feels real because the dreamer becomes part of the dream.

Only on awakening does the dream become unreal.

18) Why dreams feel real

While dreaming, the dreamer participates in the dream.

The dreamer does not think:

“This is only a dream.”

The dream feels real because the dreamer is inside it.

Similarly, in ignorance, worldly life feels absolutely real.

The soul identifies with:

  • body
  • family
  • possessions
  • status
  • pleasure
  • pain
  • relationships
  • roles

Only after awakening does one see the dream-like nature of worldly identification.

19) Dreams can be meaningful or meaningless

The speaker says dreams can be of many kinds.

Some dreams make sense, like the fourteen dreams of Trishla Mata.

Others make no sense because they are combinations of unresolved impressions in the subconscious.

The humorous example used is an elephant wearing a red dress with white polka dots.

This kind of strange dream comes from mixed impressions stored in the subconscious.

The point:

What is not purified consciously sinks into the subconscious and reappears in strange ways.

20) Importance of pratikraman

The speaker connects strange dreams to lack of purification.

When impressions are not erased through introspection and pratikraman, they move from the conscious mind into the subconscious.

Later, they arise as dreams, impulses, or strange inner movements.

So pratikraman is not merely a ritual. It is inner cleansing.

It prevents mental and emotional garbage from accumulating.

21) Short dreams and long dreams

Dreams may last:

  • a few seconds
  • several minutes
  • seemingly the whole night
  • symbolically, even millions of years

Param Krupalu Dev deliberately gives the example of the longest possible dream:

A dream of millions of years.

Even such a long dream disappears instantly when the dreamer wakes up.

The length of the dream does not matter once awakening occurs.

22) Transmigration as a dream

The speaker then expands the analogy to transmigration.

The soul has wandered through countless forms:

  • human
  • animal
  • celestial
  • hellish
  • male
  • female
  • rich
  • poor
  • learned
  • ignorant

In each birth, the same attachment takes a different form.

A person who loves his son in one life may become a dog and love puppies in the same attached way.

The object changes, but the underlying inclination continues.

This is the tragedy of transmigration.

23) The inclination remains, expression changes

One of the most striking insights:

The inclination does not change. The way it is expressed changes.

Attachment remains attachment.

It may express toward:

  • a child
  • a spouse
  • a queen
  • a celestial companion
  • a puppy
  • a female pig

The outer form changes from birth to birth, but the inner attachment continues unless one meets a Sadguru and awakens.

24) The role of Sadguru

Only the association of an Enlightened Master can awaken the soul from this long dream.

The speaker recalls the virtues prayed for in Jay Viyaray:

  • grief for transmigration
  • following the path shown by the Lords
  • attainment of the desired fruit
  • giving up socially and spiritually improper actions
  • worshipping elders
  • benevolence
  • contact with a true Guru
  • following the Guru’s teachings until the end of life

These qualities help the seeker awaken from the dream of worldly existence.

25) The dream of “I” and “mine”

The speaker says we are in a deep dream of ignorance.

In this dream, our “I-ness” and “my-ness” are distorted.

We think:

  • this body is I
  • this family is mine
  • this house is mine
  • this car is mine
  • this status is mine
  • this role is mine

This is the dream state.

It is not merely a dream of millions of years. It is a dream since beginningless time.

Awakening means the false “I” and “mine” disappear.

26) The car example

The speaker gives a simple everyday example.

If someone places a glass of tea on “my car,” agitation arises.

Why?

Because of the false identification:

This is my car.

The object itself is not the cause of suffering. The “my-ness” attached to it is the cause.

This is part of the dream state.

27) Dark room and light example

Another powerful example:

A room may have been dark for many years. But one ray of light can brighten it instantly.

The darkness does not say:

“I have been here for years, so I will need years to leave.”

The moment light appears, darkness disappears.

Similarly, ignorance has existed since beginningless time, but the light of Self-realisation can remove it instantly.

28) The pall of ignorance

The speaker says ignorance grips us like a pall.

A pall is a cloth covering a coffin.

Ignorance descends on the mind like a covering. It:

  • clouds intelligence
  • masks awareness
  • hides the truth
  • distorts perception

When truth strikes, the veil of ignorance is torn.

Then one realises:

What I really am.

And the entire outlook changes.

29) Life as acting: the role and the Self

After awakening, life is seen like a stage.

The soul recognises:

I am an actor with entrances and exits.

Roles arise and end.

For example, one becomes an uncle only when a niece or nephew is born. Before that, there was no “uncle” role.

The role enters the stage at a certain time and exits at another time.

The awakened view is:

I am a pure soul with a specific role.

The role is temporary. The Self is eternal.

30) The verse’s core insight

The whole verse teaches:

Time spent in ignorance does not determine the time needed for awakening.

A beginningless dream ends in awakening.

Beginningless delusion ends in Self-realisation.

The solution is not to calculate time, but to awaken.

31) Practical takeaways

1. Do not be discouraged by beginningless delusion

The impurity may be ancient, but awakening can still be immediate.

2. Self-effort is essential

No one else can stop your karmic bondage for you.

3. Every situation is a choice

The same situation can produce bondage or purity.

4. Change the inner production

The conscious machinery can produce anger or forgiveness, pride or humility.

5. Stop blaming and taking credit

Both strengthen ego.

6. Clean the subconscious

Pratikraman and introspection prevent unresolved impressions from becoming inner garbage.

7. See worldly life as dream-like

Until awakening, false “I” and “mine” seem real.

8. Remember the role-Self distinction

The role belongs to worldly drama. The Self remains pure.
