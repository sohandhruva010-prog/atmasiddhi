Verse 134 of the Upsamhar, and it is essentially the conclusion of the Nishchay–Vyavhar discussion from verses 131–133.

The previous verses established:

  • Verse 131: Don't hear about the pure Self and abandon spiritual practices.
  • Verse 132: Nishchay and Vyavhar must remain together; neither should be adopted one-sidedly.
  • Verse 133: Right Vyavhar reduces attachment–aversion; right Nishchay increases awareness of the Self.

Now Verse 134 gives the conclusion:

“Agal Jnani thai gaya, vartamanma hoy;
Thashe kaal bhavishyama, margbhed nahi koy.”

Meaning:

Enlightened Ones have arisen in the past, exist in the present, and will arise in the future. Regardless of time, there is no difference in the fundamental path by which enlightenment is attained. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 134: The Path Is One — Past, Present and Future

The central message is beautifully simple:

External methods may differ.

The essential path to liberation does not.

And what is that essential path?

Awareness/realisation of the pure Self + the right conduct that reduces attachment, aversion and ignorance.

1. The connection with Verse 133 is crucial

At first, Verse 134 can sound like a completely new devotional verse:

“There were Enlightened Ones in the past, there are Enlightened Ones now, and there will be Enlightened Ones in the future.”

Beautiful.

But Gurudev says:

That's not the main point.

Param Krupalu Dev is still answering the question from verses 131–133:

What is the correct path when neither one-sided Nishchay nor one-sided Vyavhar is sufficient?

Answer:

Their correct integration.

And Param Krupalu Dev now says:

Every Enlightened One—past, present or future—attains through this same fundamental path.

There is “margbhed nahi koy” — no difference in the path. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

2. What exactly is this unchanging path?

Gurudev summarizes what we've learned across the previous three verses.

First, there must be correct understanding of the Self:

The Self is pure consciousness.

The Self is blissful.

The Self is distinct from the body.

That conviction must become firmly established.

Why?

Because without it, even our supposedly religious activity can become another playground for:

  • ego,
  • attachment,
  • comparison,
  • pride,
  • identity.

So Nishchay gives the correct target. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

But that alone isn't enough.

3. Religious activity by itself does NOT end transmigration

This is an important clarification.

Gurudev says:

Fasting itself doesn't end transmigration.

Bhakti itself doesn't end transmigration.

Scriptural study itself doesn't end transmigration.

Seva itself doesn't end transmigration.

These are all means.

The deeper spiritual movement is:

Attachment ↓
Aversion ↓
Self-awareness ↑

That's what actually moves the seeker toward liberation. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

So again, don't confuse:

the instrument

with:

the result the instrument is supposed to produce.

4. But don't therefore reject the instruments!

And this is where Gurudev preserves the balance.

Someone hears:

“Fasting isn't liberation.”

and concludes:

“Great, no fasting.”

Or:

“Ritual isn't the ultimate goal.”

therefore:

“No ritual.”

That's exactly the one-sided mistake Verse 131 warned against.

Gurudev says these practices are means that help us move forward.

So the proper question isn't:

“Is this activity itself Moksh?”

Obviously not.

The question is:

“Is this activity helping reduce attachment/aversion and turn my awareness toward the Self?”

If yes, use it. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

5. Gurudev deliberately DEFENDS the word “ritual”

This is interesting.

Modern spiritual seekers often dislike the word:

ritual.

It sounds:

  • mechanical,
  • old-fashioned,
  • external,
  • unspiritual.

Gurudev deliberately says:

I'm going to use the word anyway.

Why?

Because every serious pursuit has:

  • discipline,
  • method,
  • regimen,
  • repeated practice.

Sports has it.

Bodybuilding has it.

Business has it.

Finance has it.

So why should spirituality somehow mean:

“Give me the destination but don't give me a method.” Pasted text(20261112-232320)

That's a great point.

6. Ritual is not the problem — MECHANICAL ritual is

Gurudev has seen people perform countless rituals without developing virtues.

But he says:

Don't hate the ritual because someone performed it badly.

A spiritual discipline has value when it helps increase:

Self-awareness.

So again:

Empty ritual → not enough.

No practice whatsoever → also not enough.

Meaningful practice directed toward the Self → useful.

That's the Nishchay–Vyavhar balance.

7. “Spirituality is a science — experiment and experience”

Gurudev says something nice here:

“Spirituality is a science. You should experiment and experience.”

Don't merely debate whether the discipline works.

Practice.

Observe what happens internally.

Does it:

  • quieten you?
  • reduce desires?
  • reduce attachment?
  • reduce aversion?
  • increase Self-awareness?
  • create greater inner stability?

The path is meant to become experiential, not merely theoretical. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

8. Now Verse 134: Enlightened Ones exist in ALL three times

Param Krupalu Dev says:

Past

Enlightened Ones have existed.

Present

Enlightened Ones exist.

Future

Enlightened Ones will exist.

Gurudev specifically challenges the idea:

“All the real Jnanis were in the past. There aren't any anymore.”

Param Krupalu Dev explicitly says:

“Vartamanma hoy” — They exist in the present. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

9. “The problem is in your eyes”

Gurudev says people glorify earlier eras:

“Fourth era!”

“Back then there were Enlightened Ones!”

“Now it's the fifth era.”

But Param Krupalu Dev says Enlightened Ones exist in the present too.

The problem may simply be:

We cannot recognize Them.

Gurudev gives the analogy:

The sun is shining in broad daylight.

You close your eyes.

Then declare:

“There is no sun.”

The absence isn't necessarily outside.

The problem may be in your vision. Pasted text(20261112-232320) And Gurudev points to Param Krupalu Dev Himself:

He was an Enlightened Being.

Yet how many people living around Him actually recognized Him?

10. “There were only these three Jnanis” — also wrong

Gurudev humorously rejects the idea that enlightenment somehow ended after a fixed number of Enlightened Ones.

As though:

“There were these three.”

“Finished.”

“No more stock.” 😂

Param Krupalu Dev's verse explicitly rejects that.

Whenever the appropriate spiritual realization manifests:

There is a Jnani.

Past.

Present.

Future.

11. Can I worship MY Guru as the only One?

This becomes a subtle and beautiful point.

Gurudev says:

YES.

You can worship your Sadguru:

as if there is no other.

Your heart can say:

“For me, it is Him and only Him.”

Gurudev himself expresses:

“My world is He; my heart, my breath, my soul.”

Once I have found my Enlightened One, I can remain completely surrendered at His Lotus Feet.

That is single-pointed bhakti. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

But there is a crucial distinction.

12. “Only Him FOR ME” ≠ “Only He is Enlightened”

These are completely different statements.

Devotion says:
“For me, only my Gurudev.”

Beautiful.

Sectarian ignorance says:
“Only my Gurudev can be Enlightened. Everyone else is false.”

Wrong.

Gurudev says:

Worship your Sadguru as if there is none other, but don't deny the existence of other Enlightened Ones.

That is a very refined balance between:

exclusive devotion

and

non-sectarian understanding. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

This connects directly to Verse 133.

13. Worshipping ONE Jnani can be worshipping ALL Jnanis

Gurudev explains why.

Why do I worship my Sadguru?

Because of:

His Enlightenment.

So fundamentally, what am I revering?

Enlightenment itself.

Therefore, in sincerely worshipping one Enlightened Being for His Enlightenment, I am respecting that same spiritual state wherever it manifests:

  • past,
  • present,
  • future.

Gurudev says:

In worshipping one Enlightened One, you worship all Enlightened Ones.

But:

By denying another genuine Enlightened One, you deny Enlightenment. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

That's a really important distinction.

14. Different Jnanis, SAME essential path

Gurudev gives examples:

  • Adinath Bhagwan,
  • Mahavir Bhagwan,

Different:

  • eras,
  • circumstances,

  • external settings.

Yet Gurudev says the fundamental path is the same:

“Margbhed nahi koy.”

There is no difference in the essential path. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

15. But don't different Gurus teach different things?

Yes—externally.

Gurudev says Enlightened Ones may prescribe different remedies according to:

  • the seeker,
  • their potential,
  • their flaws,
  • place,
  • time,
  • circumstances.

One Guru may prescribe one practice.

Another may emphasize something else.

Even forms of worship may differ.

That's allowed.

Because:

Form may differ. Spirit does not. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

This is one of the main points of the satsang.

16. Different REMEDIES, same CURE

Think back to Verse 129:

Self-delusion = disease.

Sadguru = physician.

Different patients don't necessarily receive identical prescriptions.

One may need:

  • more bhakti,
  • more meditation,
  • more restraint,
  • more seva,
  • more contemplation,
  • more renunciation.

But what is the ultimate cure?

Removal of Self-delusion.

What is the result?

Realisation and abidance in the Self.

So external prescriptions can differ while the fundamental spiritual movement remains identical.

17. Wrong paths are many. Right path is ONE.

Gurudev asks:

How many wrong paths can there be?

Many.

How many right paths?

One.

His reasoning is that if two paths genuinely lead to opposite spiritual destinations, they cannot both be the same ultimate path.

But remember what he means by one path:

He does not mean:

one sect.

one ritual.

one institutional tradition.

He means the essential movement:

Realize the Self and abide in the Self. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

That's a very important distinction.

18. Gurudev connects this back to Verses 100–101

He summarizes the essential path using earlier Atmasiddhi teachings.

What must disappear?

Attachment
Aversion
Ignorance

What must manifest?

Awareness/abidance in the soul's eternal existence and consciousness.

That's the path.

Everything else is a means supporting that movement. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

So now Verse 134 becomes very clear:

Every Jnani, irrespective of era, must ultimately have undergone:

ignorance → Self-knowledge
attachment/aversion → dispassion identification with non-Self → abidance in Self

That essential movement cannot change.

Gurudev describes the path as:

Definite
Undisputable
Eternally constant
Changeless across past, present and future

Why?

Because the fundamental spiritual problem doesn't change.

The disease is still:

Self-delusion.

And the liberated state is still:

complete purity / abidance in Self.

So the essential bridge between them cannot suddenly become something fundamentally opposite in another century. Pasted text(20261112-232320) Pasted text(20261112-232320)

That's why Dharma may look different in different times and places.

But:

“In looks it's different; in spirit it is the same.” Pasted text(20261112-232320)

How Verses 131–134 fit together

These four verses now form one very coherent unit.

Verse 131

Don't abandon Vyavhar because you've heard Nishchay.

Keep the pure Self as your goal and continue the means.

↓

Verse 132

Neither viewpoint alone is complete.

Nishchay + Vyavhar must remain in harmony.

↓

Verse 133
How do I know whether I'm using them correctly?

Right Vyavhar:

Raag-dwesh decreases.

Right Nishchay:

Self-awareness increases.

↓

Verse 134
Is this balance merely Param Krupalu Dev's personal method?

No.

This is the essential path through which:

Jnani thai gaya — Enlightened Ones attained in the past,

Vartamanma hoy — attain/exist in the present,

Thashe kaal bhavishyama — will attain in the future.

Because:

Margbhed nahi koy.

There is no difference in the fundamental path.

The deepest practical takeaway

I think this satsang clears up a really important confusion:

One path does NOT mean one external form.

If we misunderstand “the path is one” as:

“Therefore everyone must do exactly what my group does,”

we've immediately contradicted Verse 133.

The forms can vary enormously.

One seeker may need more bhakti.

Another more meditation.

Another restraint.

Another seva.

Another contemplation.

Different Gurus may prescribe different disciplines according to the seeker, place and time.

But underneath those differences, Gurudev says the movement must be the same:

Attachment ↓
Aversion ↓
Ignorance ↓
Self-awareness ↑
Abidance in Self ↑

That's Margbhed nahi koy.

And I think the other beautiful takeaway is the distinction Gurudev makes regarding BAPA:

I can genuinely feel:

“For me, BAPA is everything. I need no other refuge.”

That is single-pointed devotion.

But that should make me more reverential toward enlightenment wherever it manifests, not more sectarian.

Because what am I actually worshipping in BAPA?

Not merely:

  • a personality,
  • a name,
  • an organization,
  • “my group.”

I am bowing to:

the Enlightened state.

So Gurudev's logic is beautiful:

By worshipping one Enlightened One because of His Enlightenment, you honor all Enlightened Ones.

Whereas:

by denying another genuine Enlightened One simply because He isn't “mine,” you are denying the very Enlightenment you claim to worship. Pasted text(20261112-232320)

That ties the whole sequence together:

Don't worship the external label.

Don't get trapped in ritual.

Don't get trapped in intellectual Nishchay.

Don't get trapped in sect.

Use whatever genuine means Sadguru gives you to:

realize the Self, reduce attachment and aversion, and increasingly abide in your pure nature.

The prescription may look different.

The physician may express it differently.

The time and place may change.

But the cure is the same.

And therefore:

“Margbhed nahi koy.”

There is no difference in the essential path.
