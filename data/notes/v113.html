Video 128 Notes: Verse 113 — Omniscience and Liberation While Embodied

1) Link from the previous verse

Verse 112 explained the fruit of intensified samkit:

Right faith intensifies → conduct delusion is destroyed → right conduct arises → complete dispassion is attained.

The highest state of right conduct is vitragta, complete dispassion.

Now verse 113 asks:

What is the fruit of complete dispassion?

The answer:

Kevaljnan — Omniscience.

The sequence is:

Cause Effect

Intensified right Right conduct faith

Right conduct Complete dispassion

Complete Omniscience dispassion

So verse 113 explains the state that arises after kshinmoha, the destruction of delusion.

2) The journey from verse 108 to 113

The speaker summarises the whole journey:

VersSpiritual Stage e

108 Preparation: four virtues of a true aspirant

109 First samkit: vyavhar samkit

110 Second samkit: shuddh samkit, experiential faith

111 Third samkit: parmarth samkit

112 Right conduct and complete dispassion

113 Omniscience and liberation while embodied

The aspirant who began in verse 108 as a newcomer cultivating basic virtues has now reached a state of complete dispassion.

Yet the speaker still calls him an aspirant until verse 113, because until omniscience manifests, he is still working on himself.

3) The wrestler and brick example

The discourse begins with a powerful example.

A young man is walking near a building. A brick falls from the terrace and hits his head. He becomes angry and climbs up with the brick, ready to attack whoever threw it.

But when he reaches the terrace, he sees a strong wrestler doing push-ups. The brick had accidentally fallen because of the wrestler’s exercise.

Seeing the wrestler’s strength, the young man’s anger disappears. When the wrestler asks, “Why have you come here?” the young man replies:

“I came to return this brick to you.”

The speaker explains:

The wrestler is the soul.

If the soul becomes strong, delusion will not dare to stand before it.

Delusion is not weak. It will retaliate. Therefore, the seeker must become spiritually strong.

And where does that strength come from?

From devotion to the Dispassionate One, contemplation on the Guru’s teachings, following His commands, pure love for Sadguru, faith, surrender, and obedience.

The teaching:

Love for the Vitragi makes the soul strong.

4) The state of complete dispassion

The speaker describes the state reached in verse 112:

The soul revels in the ever-present divinity within. His character glows with:

  • selflessness
  • pure love
  • complete dispassion
  • inner power
  • freedom from worldly impact

The world no longer affects him.

5) Bamboo shadow example

The speaker gives a beautiful image:

A bamboo stands in a courtyard and casts a shadow. The shadow may sweep across the courtyard, but it does not stir even one particle of dust.

Similarly, the world may move around the Dispassionate One, but it does not affect Him even slightly.

He is still:

  • in the body
  • in the world
  • among people
  • involved in visible activities

But inwardly, He is untouched.

The world affects Him like the shadow of bamboo affects dust:

Not even a little.

This is the state of complete dispassion.

6) Verse 113

The verse explained is:

Keval nijswabhavnu, akhand varte jnan;
Kahie kevaljnan te, deh chhata nirvan.

7) Word-by-word meaning

“Keval”

Means: only, exclusively.

Here it means the soul is absorbed only in its own true nature.

“Nijswabhavnu”

Means: of one’s own nature, the true nature of the Self.

“Akhand”

Means: uninterrupted, unbroken, without interval.

“Khand” means broken or interrupted. “Akhand” means unbroken.

“Varte”

Means: prevails, exists, remains active.

“Jnan”

Technically means knowledge.

But the speaker explains that here it is not ordinary intellectual knowledge. It is not scriptural information or reading.

Here, “jnan” indicates:

absorption, direct knowledge, complete illumination of the Self.

“Kahie kevaljnan te”

This is called Kevaljnan — Omniscience.

“Deh chhata”

Means: despite the body, despite the association of the body.

“Nirvan”

Means: liberation, liberated state.

So the line means:

Even while the body remains, this state is liberation.

8) Short meaning of the verse

When there is uninterrupted, exclusive absorption in one’s own true nature, that state is called Omniscience. Though the body remains, such a soul is already liberated.

9) The key idea: Omniscience is constant Self-absorption The speaker emphasises that Param Krupalu Dev defines Omniscience in a very direct and spiritual way.

Usually, people understand Omniscience as:

knowing everything in the universe — past, present, future, every soul, every particle.

But this verse defines Omniscience as:

uninterrupted absorption in one’s own true nature.

This is the heart of the discourse.

Param Krupalu Dev does not deny that the Omniscient One knows everything. But He shifts the focus from knowing the universe to being fully absorbed in the Self.

10) Traditional definition of Omniscience

The speaker explains the common Jain understanding of Kevaljnan:

The Omniscient One knows:

  • all three times: past, present, future
  • all three worlds
  • every soul
  • every particle of matter
  • all living and non-living substances
  • all modifications of all substances

This is the traditional definition:

Complete knowledge of all dravya, kshetra, kaal, and bhaav.

Meaning:

Term Meaning

Dravya substances Kshetrplaces / space a

Kaal time

Bhaav modes / modifications

This is not wrong.

But the speaker says:

It is not the complete spiritual understanding needed by a seeker.

Why? Because if the seeker only hears that Omniscience means knowing the universe, he may become fascinated by supernatural knowledge rather than Self-absorption.

11) Param Krupalu Dev’s spiritual definition

Param Krupalu Dev says:

Omniscience is exclusive, uninterrupted knowledge of one’s own true nature.

This is the seeker’s definition. This is practical for one who wants to attain Omniscience, not merely worship an Omniscient Being.

The speaker says:

For common people, the idea that the Omniscient knows everything may inspire faith. But for a true aspirant, that definition is not enough.

A seeker needs to know:

What did the Omniscient One actually strive for?

The answer:

He strove for complete absorption in the Self.

Because of that complete purity, everything else becomes known naturally.

12) Acharang Sutra: “The One who knows the Self knows everything”

The speaker refers to a statement from the Jain canon:

Egam janai, so savvam janai The One who knows one, knows all.

Param Krupalu Dev loved this statement deeply.

When He published Mokshmala, He did not want an image or decorative design on the front page. He wanted one sentence:

The One who has known the Self has known everything.

This same statement was also placed in Vachanamrutji.

The teaching:

Know the Self completely, and everything is known.

This is the spiritual essence of Omniscience.

13) The mirror example from Samaysaar

The speaker uses the example of a mirror.

A mirror reflects whatever comes before it.

If fire is reflected in the mirror, the mirror does not become hot. If snow is reflected in the mirror, the mirror does not become cold.

The mirror reflects naturally because reflecting is its disposition. It does not desire to reflect.

Similarly, when the soul’s pure and complete knowledge manifests, everything is known naturally.

But the Omniscient One does not desire to know the world.

He does not think:

  • What is in this person’s mind?
  • What will happen to this boy in the future?
  • What is going on in the universe?

He remains absorbed in the Self. Because His knowledge is pure and complete, all objects are illumined.

The teaching:

The Omniscient knows everything, but not out of desire, curiosity, or concern. Everything is known naturally because of complete purity.

14) The dark room and light example

The speaker gives another example.

Suppose you want a clock from a dark room. Your purpose is to get the clock. Your effort is to switch on the light.

When the light is switched on, the clock becomes visible. But along with the clock, everything else in the room also becomes visible.

You did not switch on the light to see every object. You only wanted the clock. But once the light is on, everything is illumined.

Similarly:

The seeker’s effort is to know the Self.

His purpose is Self-realisation and Self-absorption.

When complete knowledge manifests, the whole universe is illumined.

But that was not the seeker’s desire.

The teaching:

The Omniscient knows the universe as a natural result of complete illumination, not because He wanted to know the universe.

15) Why the traditional definition attracts worldly minds

The speaker says the common definition attracts worldly people:

“He knows past and future? Then tell me whom I will marry!”

People are fascinated by prediction, supernatural knowledge, mind-reading, and future events.

But a true seeker asks:

What is my eternal nature?

The seeker is already developing detachment toward the world. So he is not interested in what will happen in the world.

He wants to know:

  • Who am I?
  • What is my true nature?
  • How can I abide in it?
  • How can I become free?

This is why Param Krupalu Dev gives the seeker a spiritual definition of Omniscience.

16) Dispassion is the precursor to Omniscience

The speaker makes a very important point:

Detachment is a precursor of Omniscience.

Without complete dispassion, Omniscience would be unbearable.

He gives a humorous example:

Suppose one had Omniscience but no dispassion. Then one would know everyone’s thoughts.

He would know:

  • who is bored
  • who is criticising
  • who is wondering when the discourse will end
  • who is pretending enthusiasm
  • what people will think next

Without dispassion, such knowledge would create endless reactions, likes, dislikes, hurt, and irritation.

Therefore:

Omniscience must come after complete dispassion.

Otherwise, the Omniscient would be the most miserable soul.

Because the Omniscient One is completely dispassionate, knowing everything does not disturb Him.

17) Why complete dispassion allows total absorption

Complete dispassion means:

  • no attachment
  • no aversion
  • no unnecessary thinking
  • no complaints
  • no reactions
  • no mental residue after activity
  • no past-future rumination

Because He is dispassionate, He is free.

Because He is free, He can remain fully absorbed in the Self.

The speaker says:

Because I am not attached, I am free. Because I am free, I can focus on the Self.

Dispassion makes constant Self-absorption possible.

18) Life without complaints

The speaker says that a dispassionate life is:

A life without complaints, only compliments.

He recalls the school prayer:

Thank you God for the world so sweet. Thank you God for the food we eat. Thank you God for the birds that sing. Thank you God for everything.

The teaching is that dispassion reduces complaint and increases gratitude.

A person with attachment complains constantly. A dispassionate person accepts, appreciates, and turns inward.

19) Omniscience as exclusive absorption

The verse says:

Keval nijswabhavnu only one’s own true nature.

This means the Omniscient One is not scattered.

He is not absorbed in:

  • people
  • situations
  • possessions
  • praise
  • insult
  • past
  • future
  • worldly success
  • others’ thoughts
  • body
  • mind

He is absorbed only in the true nature of the Self.

This is why the word keval is so important.

It means:

only, exclusively, nothing else.

20) Omniscience as uninterrupted absorption

The verse says:

Akhand varte jnan uninterrupted knowledge/absorption prevails.

The speaker jokes about movie intervals to explain “akhand.”

In Bollywood movies, there is an interval — and songs may become informal intervals too. But in Omniscience, there is no interruption.

No gap. No break. No fluctuation. No falling from the state.

The Omniscient One remains in uninterrupted absorption in the Self.

21) Verse 113 and the 13th gunasthanak

Verse 113 describes the 13th gunasthanak.

The speaker summarises:

  • verse 108: preparation
  • verse 109–111: samkit
  • verse 112: 5th to 12th gunasthanaks
  • verse 113: 13th gunasthanak

The 13th gunasthanak is the state of:

  • constant Self-absorption
  • total bliss
  • complete peace
  • irreversible spiritual attainment
  • liberation while embodied

22) What is the difference between Self-realisation and Omniscience?

The speaker gives the chocolate example.

Self-realisation

Like tasting one piece of chocolate.

Omniscience

Like having the whole bar.

The quality of taste is the same, but the fullness is different.

A Self-realised soul has truly tasted the Self. The taste is real and of the same nature.

But the Omniscient One has complete, uninterrupted, total absorption.

So:

State Example Meaning

Self-realisatioOne piece of true taste of the Self n chocolate

Omniscience Whole chocolate bar complete and uninterrupted fullness

The speaker says the quality is the same, but the extent is different.

23) Yashovijayji’s line: a miniature of the Liberated Ones The speaker refers to Yashovijayji’s Aath Drashtini Sajjhay:

Anshe hoi ahiya avinashi

Meaning:

In part, the Enlightened One here is like the Imperishable Liberated Ones.

A Self-realised soul is like a miniature of the Siddhas.

He has tasted the same nature, though not in complete fullness.

24) Shuddh samkit, parmarth samkit, and Kevaljnan

The speaker distinguishes three states:

Shuddh samkit

A glimpse of the Self. The experience is momentary but transformative.

Parmarth samkit

Constant flow toward the Self through experience, awareness, or conviction.

Kevaljnan

Complete and uninterrupted absorption in the Self.

The distinction:

State Experience

Shuddh samkit momentary glimpse

Parmarth constant orientation to Self samkit

Kevaljnan complete uninterrupted Self-absorption

25) “Deh chhata nirvan” — liberated despite the body

The second line says:

Deh chhata nirvan Though the body remains, He is liberated.

Usually, people think liberation happens only after death, when the soul leaves the body.

But this verse says:

Even with the body, the Omniscient One is liberated.

He is with the body but beyond the body.

This state is also praised in verse 142:

Though the body remains, His state is beyond the body.

The Omniscient One is:

  • beyond the body
  • beyond the mind
  • beyond the world
  • beyond pure and impure modifications
  • rooted in the eternal nature

26) Ghati and aghati karmas

The speaker explains that there are eight karmas:

Four ghati karmas — obscuring karmas

These obstruct the soul’s essential qualities.

They must be destroyed before Omniscience manifests.

Four aghati karmas — non-obscuring karmas

These do not obstruct knowledge directly, but they keep the body and worldly embodiment functioning.

After the destruction of the four ghati karmas, Omniscience arises. But the four aghati karmas remain until the end of that life.

Therefore:

  • Omniscience manifests while the body remains.
  • The soul is already liberated in state.
  • At the end of life, when the remaining aghati karmas are destroyed, the soul attains Siddhahood.

The speaker says:

It cannot be said that He will be liberated only then. He is already liberated despite the association of the body.

27) Liberation while embodied vs Siddhahood

There is a distinction:

Liberation while embodied

The Omniscient One is liberated in state even while the body remains.

This is deh chhata nirvan.

Siddhahood

When the body is finally left and the remaining non-obscuring karmas are destroyed, the soul reaches the Siddha state.

So:

State Body? Liberation?

Omniscient embodied Body Already liberated in state remains state

Siddhahood No body Final bodiless liberation

28) Why Omniscience is irreversible

Until the 12th gunasthanak, there can still be fluctuation:

  • rising
  • falling
  • good states
  • low states
  • climbing
  • slipping

But once Omniscience manifests, the state is irreversible.

The speaker uses the word:

Apratipati — no fall from this state.

He refers to the Namutthunam phrase:

Appadihay varnan dansandharanam

Meaning that this state cannot be reduced or destroyed.

Once Omniscience arises:

  • there is no fall
  • no reduction
  • no fluctuation
  • no loss
  • no decrease
  • no interruption

It is complete and permanent.

29) The speaker’s love for this verse

The speaker shares that this was one of his favourite verses of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra.

He recalls earlier days of bhakti from 1978–79, when devotees would gather in homes for rotating bhakti.

They would sing devotional songs to the Dispassionate Lords, Guru bhakti, and dhun.

This verse:

Keval nijswabhavnu, akhand varte jnan;
Kahie kevaljnan te, deh chhata nirvan.

would sometimes be chanted for two hours.

The speaker says:

  • this verse cannot be sung dullly
  • either one enters deep meditation
  • or one feels like standing and dancing
  • the verse carries love and detachment together

He emphasises the power of compositions of Enlightened Ones. They generate both:

  • love
  • dispassion

30) The power of compositions of saints

The speaker contrasts ordinary devotional songs with compositions of Enlightened Ones.

All devotional songs may create love for Guru, but the words of Enlightened Ones create something deeper:

love and detachment simultaneously.

He urges seekers to learn and sing compositions of great saints, even if the melody or style is not immediately appealing.

Their words carry spiritual power.

31) Why this verse inspires awe

This verse reveals an extraordinary state:

  • complete Self-absorption
  • complete knowledge
  • complete peace
  • total bliss
  • no interruption
  • no body-identification
  • liberation while embodied

The speaker calls it a state of:

  • ecstatic joy
  • effervescent joyousness
  • wonder

The shackles of karmas and the world are broken. The soul stands liberated.

32) The full spiritual logic of verse 113

The complete logic is:

1. The seeker cultivated virtues in verse 108.

2. He attained right faith in verse 109.

3. He experienced the Self in verse 110.

4. He became constantly oriented to the Self in verse 111.

5. Right conduct arose and passions were destroyed in verse 112.

6. Complete dispassion made uninterrupted Self-absorption possible.

7. That uninterrupted absorption is Kevaljnan.

8. Though the body remains, the soul is liberated.

33) Practical takeaways for seekers

1. Become spiritually strong

Delusion will not surrender politely. The soul must become strong through devotion, contemplation, surrender, and obedience to Sadguru.

2. Dispassion is not dullness True dispassion is powerful, glowing, selfless, loving, and free.

3. Omniscience is not curiosity about the universe

The seeker should not be fascinated by knowing others’ minds or future events. The true aim is complete absorption in the Self.

4. The Self is the real focus

Classifications, scriptures, rituals, and introspection are steps. The final focus is the unchanging Self.

5. Dispassion enables absorption

Without dispassion, the mind remains busy with people, complaints, and reactions. With dispassion, the soul is free for the Self.

6. Complete knowledge naturally illumines everything

The Omniscient knows the universe, but naturally — like a mirror reflecting or a light illuminating a room.

7. Liberation begins before leaving the body

The Omniscient One is already liberated while embodied.

8. Omniscience is irreversible

Once the state arises, there is no fall.
