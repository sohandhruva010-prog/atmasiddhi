3:57–6:41 — Core logic: if body = soul, then body-size should correlate with knowledge (directly proportional)

He gives the “short explanation first” and then expands:

The structure

1. Soul has the attribute knowledge/knowing.

2. If someone claims body and soul are one, then it becomes: body and knowledge are one.

3. If that’s true, then:

  • bigger body → more intelligence
  • smaller/lean body → less intelligence

4. But in real observation, we often see the opposite:

  • thin body + super intelligence
  • fat body + low intelligence

He repeatedly calls this a contradiction that “would not arise” if it was one substance.

He adds jokes while still holding the logic:

  • Vyom being thin + less intelligence (so “good example for body=soul”)
  • Bhavin being bulky + “feels he is very intelligent” (department thinks he feels he is)
  • Then he says both examples “don’t help” but the verse meaning will unfold—classic playful teaching style.

6:41–9:12 — Short meaning summary (he re-states cleanly)

He gives the crisp wrap:

  • Super intelligence is found in someone with thin body.
  • Less intelligence found in someone with fat body.
  • If body and soul were one, this contradiction would not happen.
  • Therefore, no relation between body and soul; soul cannot be identified with body.

17:41–20:33 — Final doctrinal conclusion: knowledge is attribute of soul; buddhi/mati are “modifications” of knowledge

He ties it back to Jain metaphysics language:

  • Knowledge is the attribute of an entity called soul:
    • formless, non-tangible, separate from body

  • Why the verse uses buddhi (intelligence)?

  • He frames it with:

    • Guna (attribute) = knowledge
    • Paryay (modification) = intelligence/memory (expressions of knowledge)
  • Therefore Verse 56: “param buddhi / mati alp” is just a way to point to knowledge.
