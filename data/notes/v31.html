1) Where this verse fits

  • v.24–28: Kriyājada matārthī — the mechanical ritualist (lots of doing, no inner change).
  • v.29–31: Śuṣk-jñānī matārthī — the dry intellectualist (lots of saying/knowing, no inner change).
  • v.30: Warning about association: if you keep the dry-intellectualist’s company, you drown too.
  • v.31 (this verse): Names them “matārthī” explicitly and gives the root cause and verdict:
    • “E pan” — that first type and this second type → both are matārthī.
    • “nij-mānādi kāj” — ego/vanity/self-importance is the engine.
    • “pāmey nahi parmārthne” — they do not attain the Supreme Truth/state.
    • Outcome: they are an-adhikārī — unworthy/inedible even for the path to Self-realisation, let alone the state.

2) Word-by-word sense

  • E pan / Te pan — That one as well as this one → both categories are included.
  • Jīva — such a soul (person).
  • Matārthmā — in bigotry (sectarian/self-opinionated manner).
  • Nij-mānādi kāj — because of one’s own pride/ego/vanity (and allied passions).
  • Pāmey nahi parmārthne — does not attain the Supreme purpose/truth/state.
  • An-adhikārī — not qualified / not eligible / not deserving for ātmā-jñān or even its path.
Essence:

Both—the doer-without-awareness and the knower-without-practice—are matārthī. Driven by ego, they remain unworthy for the Supreme path and state.

3) What makes them “matārthī”

  • Mechanical ritualist: cherishes ego through practices (fasts, vows, grand “parṇā,” visible austerity).
  • Dry intellectualist: cherishes ego through knowledge (lofty quotes, “absolute viewpoint” talk, lip-adoption).
  • Shared flaw: no inner transformation; both run on attention, status, and insistence.

“He does all for vanity—everything becomes a ladder for mān (pride).”

4) The core culprit — EGO

  • Handy definition shared in the talk: E-G-O = Edging God Out — crowding out the Divine/Truth with me–mine.
  • Ego hides in three A’s (check yourself here):

1. Ability — “I can do it better than anyone.”

2. Activity — “Look at how much I do.”

3. Achievement — “I accomplished this.”

Practice cue: Remove ego from Ability–Activity–Achievement. Let outcomes be Guru’s grace, not personal credit.

  • Pride → Arrogance (uddhatā): turns you hard-hearted, people feel unsafe around you.
  • Scriptural sting: “Mān na hot to ahij mokṣa hot.” If pride were absent, liberation would be here and now.
Why pride is tricky:
  • Anger feels like poison, you want out;
  • Pride feels like sweet poison—pleasant to receive, deadly to keep.

5) Why they become an-adhikārī (unworthy)

  • To accept a Sadguru’s correction means admitting “I’ve been wrong,” which hurts ego.
  • So the matārthī shuns the right path to protect face: “I don’t agree,” “This isn’t necessary.”
  • Result: Misses the highway to liberation; remains ineligible even for the path, not just the goal.

Shunning the right path is easier than apologising or changing—that is the matārthī reflex.

6) Positive contrast implied by the verse’s flow

This verse triggers the question: “Then who is worthy (adhikārī)?”

  • The text will answer by pivoting to the attributes of the true seeker (ātmārthī)—the positive list.
  • Here the negative filter is clear: ego-driven insistence disqualifies; humility + discipline qualify.

7) Diagnostics you can use (quick self-audit)

1. Language test: Do I state views as if omniscient, or do I say “in my view,” remaining open?

2. Motive test: In practice (fasting, service, singing, teaching), am I nourishing ego or purifying passions?

3. Three-A test: After ability/activity/achievement, do I credit grace or collect credit?

4. Correction test: When a mentor points out my error, do I welcome it or defend myself?

5. State vs. show: Is there softening of anger/pride/deceit/greed? Is my “sorry” frequency decreasing because passions subside (not because ego dodges apology)?

8) Practices that answer the verse

  • Daily introspection > mere routine: The talk highlights a monastic rule: missing hymns may be excused; missing introspection is grave.
    • Bring strict, session-by-session introspection: Every time you serve/sing/lead, check for ego-tickle.
  • Balance Absolute & Relative: Hold “I am pure Self” inwardly, but outwardly keep discipline, devotion, repentance, restraint—your runway.
  • Prefer humility over brilliance: Let skill and study reduce ego, not inflate it.
  • Choose safe association: If company feeds pride or negates discipline, step back (v.30 logic still applies).

9) Red flags of a matārthī (both types)

  • Insistence: “This is the only way; others are wrong.”
  • Show: visible austerity / visible erudition used to attract attention.
  • Negation: dismisses devotion, vows, or Sadguru’s role as “unnecessary.”
  • Face-saving: refuses to admit error; prefers to deny than repent.
  • No change: years of doing/knowing, but passions don’t soften.

10) One-page takeaway

  • “E pan” ties both bigots under one label: matārthī.
  • Root cause: “nij-mānādi kāj”—ego and its crew.
  • Verdict: “pāmey nahi parmārthne”—they do not attain the Supreme; indeed they’re an-adhikārī for the path itself.
  • Your move:
    • Strip ego from Ability–Activity–Achievement.
    • Keep Absolute conviction inward, Relative sādhana outward.
    • Practise relentless introspection.
    • Welcome correction more than being right.
    • Measure progress by quieter ego, softer passions, readier apology, truer simplicity.

When ego stops “edging God out,” worthiness begins—and the path to parmārth opens.
