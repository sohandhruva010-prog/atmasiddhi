• Verse 38: Gave the ID card of a true seeker – the four virtues that make the heart an ātmārth nivās (house fit for Self-realisation).
  • Verse 39 (negative side): “If that state is not there, then…”
    • You don’t benefit from Sadguru’s association,
    • You don’t attain the mokshmarg (path of liberation),
    • Your inner disease (delusion, mann-rog) is not cured.

Now Verse 40 flips it:

“When such a state does arise, then what naturally unfolds?”

This verse shows the positive chain:

Worthiness → Guru’s teaching becomes effective → Right thinking arises → Bliss awakens.

2. The verse – broken down simply

Verse 40 (in essence):

Āve jyā evi dasha, Sadgurubodh suhay;
Te bodhe suvicharna pragate, tya pragate sukhday.

Plain meaning:

  • Āve jyā evi dasha → When that state of worthiness (from verse 38) arises,
  • Sadgurubodh suhay → The teachings of the living Enlightened Mentor “suhay” – they become effective, fruitful, fragrant, shining within.
  • Te bodhe suvicharna pragate → From that very teaching, right thinking (suvicharna) arises.
  • Tya pragate sukhday → Through that right thinking, there arises bliss – both joy in the process and the path to ultimate bliss of the soul.

3. “Āve jyā evi dasha…” – When worthiness finally comes

The “dasha” referred to is:

  • Kashayni upshānttā – passions calmed,
  • Matra moksh abhilāsh – only desire for liberation,
  • Bhavey khed – tiredness of endless rebirths & their causes (faults),
  • Pranidayā – compassion for all beings.

Verse 39 said:

  • Without this, you don’t even enter the path; inner disease remains.

Verse 40 now says:

“When this state does come, the same Sadguru, same satsang, suddenly begins to work very differently inside you.”

4. “Sadgurubodh suhay” – When the words start working inside

This is the heart of the verse.

What is “suhay”?

He invites everyone to fill this in with their own adjective:

  • It:
    • Bears fruit,
    • Takes effect,
    • Penetrates deeply,
    • Shines forth,
    • Feels nectar-like, fragrant, touching, alive, transforming.

Not that: “Yesterday Guru spoke badly, today He finally spoke well.”

But: Your receptivity has changed.

  • The same teaching now:
    • Enters,
    • Stirs,
    • Melts,
    • Re-shapes you.

She jokes about Amit Sheth:

  • After almost every satsang he messages: “Bapa, today’s satsang was the best of my life 🙏👏”
  • 30–60 messages of enthusiasm.
  • It shows his inner experience of “suhay” – it did something to me.

So Sadgurubodh suhay =

“The Master’s teachings are rightly grasped, allowed to penetrate, and start producing inner transformation.”

5. The inner shift: from “worldly mind” to “sv-swaroop contemplation”

She gives the short meaning:

When the soul attains such a state of worthiness, the preachings of the Enlightened Mentor bear fruit, and that leads to the state of true bliss because the disciple turns his mind from worldly thoughts to contemplation and meditation on the nature of the true Self (svaswaroop).

So “suhay” is visible in a simple test:

  • Earlier:
    • Same situation → you reacted with:
      • Anger, hurt, ego, tit-for-tat.
  • Now, with worthiness:
    • Same situation → You find:
      • Forgiveness arises faster,
      • Love arises even for the one hurting you most,
      • You almost surprise yourself.

He says:

“Previously, I’d want to thrash this person. Now I can forgive and still count him as favourite. That is Sadgurubodh suhay – teachings working within.”

6. “Te bodhe suvicharna pragate” – What is suvicharna?

From this effective teaching, “suvicharna” arises.

Not just “good thoughts”, but a full spectrum of inner work.

She expands suvicharna to include all the “-ing” verbs of spiritual thinking:

  • Reflecting, contemplating, ruminating, pondering,
  • Remembering, recollecting, revising,
  • Discriminating (bhed-jnān: Self vs non-self),
  • Chanting with understanding,
  • Introspecting, examining, questioning your ego,
  • Meditating – eyes open or closed.

From first introspection all the way to deep meditation, everything that purifies, brings you closer to:

  • Your Sadguru, and
  • Your pure Self,

is included in “suvicharna”.

So:

  • Making heartfelt notes → suvicharna
  • Sitting in quiet bhed-jnān → suvicharna
  • Even going beyond thoughts into silence → still born from suvicharna.

In classical language:

श्रवण → मनन → न दध्यासन (shravan → manan → nididhyāsan): Listening → Contemplation → Meditation – this whole ladder is “suvicharna pragate”.

7. “Tya pragate sukhday” – Bliss begins & deepens

“Tya pragate sukhday” =

“There arises bliss from that right thinking.”

And she stresses:

  • Two levels:

1. While thinking, reflecting, meditating rightly, you already feel a sweet joy – a sense of “Ah, this is right, this is wholesome.”

2. The same suvicharna leads you to the supreme, unobstructed bliss of your pure soul – mokshānand.

So “sukhday” is both:

  • The joy in the process, and
  • The doorway to the highest bliss.

“Imported bliss” vs local sensual bliss

She gives a lovely image:

  • Worldly pleasures → “local” products:
    • From senses,
    • From situations,
    • Always temporary, dependent.
  • Now, glimpses begin from another world:
    • Bliss beyond senses,
    • Arising from within.

She jokes:

  • Someone told her a new chocolate shop opened in Mumbai:
    • “All imported chocolates!”
  • She says: “The moment you said ‘all imported’, I thought: Even my bliss is imported now! Not local from senses, but from the world beyond them.”

That is sukhday: Bliss not manufactured by situations, but imported from the soul.

8. When the soil is ready, virtues come up on their own

She uses the soil image again:

  • When soil is prepared (worthiness cultivated),
  • Then virtues start emerging spontaneously:
    • More calmness,
    • More forgiveness,
    • More softness,
    • More joy in others’ joy,
    • More natural humility.

This is transformation:

  • It shows up in:
    • Your thinking,
    • Your feeling,
    • Your responding.

She says:

“What was once tit-for-tat, now becomes forget-and-forgive.”

That feeling:

  • “Something is changing in me. I’m not how I used to be.” → That is the inner proof of Sadgurubodh suhay.

9. When even the Sadguru’s silence becomes bodh

Once worthiness is ripe:

“Not only the Master’s words, even His silence becomes a source of inspiration for radical changes within.”

Meaning:

  • He doesn’t need many lectures for you.
  • A single look,
  • A small gesture,
  • Or simply sitting in His presence,

can shake you awake, reorganise your inner world. He says: If you still need a lot of words to move, that itself shows your worthiness is still low.

Example:

  • Story of Chandkaushik, the snake:
    • Just two words from Mahavira – “Buj buj” (“Calm down, calm down”),
    • And his centuries-old rage melted.
  • That is the power of suhay when worthiness meets Sadguru’s grace.

10. Calm in chaos – mind ready for contemplation

With this worthiness, a new quality appears:

  • A calmness which:
    • Keeps your mind quiet in a chaotic world,
    • Clears out:
      • Prejudices,
      • Preconceived notions,
      • Old narratives.

You become: Able to drop incidents quickly:

  • Something happens,
  • You respond appropriately,
  • Within minutes, mind is back to the main work:
    • “I am the soul.”

This is: Mind becoming ready for suvicharna – ready for contemplation and meditation any time.

Then: Without worthiness: 10 minutes in meditation feels like 16 hours.

  • With worthiness: Even 10 hours with Guru or in meditation can feel like a moment.

Effort reduces, ease increases.

11. Understanding the Guru’s āshay – intent behind the words

She uses verse 40 to highlight another mark of worthiness:

You start understanding the Sadguru’s āshay – His inner intent.

Example phrases:

  • He says:
    • “Don’t come back.” → It may mean: “Stay there, don’t run away.”
  • He says:
    • “Do as you wish.” → It’s not license for self-will.

When He doesn’t give a direct “yes/no”:

  • It’s a higher test:
    • “Have you studied Me enough?”
    • “Can you read My eyes?”
    • “Can you feel what My heart prefers?”

She says:

“If I give you a direct answer, it’s a smaller test. If I don’t, it’s a bigger test.”

Worthiness includes:

  • Not just listening to His words,
  • But feeling His intent and aligning with it.

That too is Sadgurubodh suhay.

12. Signs that Sadgurubodh is really “suhay” for you

She lists lived signs:

1. Listening changes:

  • You listen with:
    • More enthusiasm,
    • More reverence,
    • More devotion,
    • Deep interest.

2. Inner transformation & “spiritual blooming”:

  • You experience:
    • More prem (love for Guru),
    • More vairāgya (detachment from worldly pulls).

3. Teachings feel unparalleled & unique:

  • You feel:
    • “No one speaks like this.”
    • “Even You have never spoken like this before, Bapa!”
  • You almost complain:
    • “What were You doing for 30 years before this? Nursery rhymes?” (The point isn’t that He changed – your worthiness did.)
  • This is āpūrva vāṇi – unique speech, newly felt.

4. Right interpretation of situations:

  • Te bodhe suvicharna pragate:
    • You interpret situations as Enlightened Ones would.
    • You stop arguing, complaining.
    • You say: “This too is my karmaphal, this too is for my spiritual welfare.”

Then:

“Thinking like an Enlightened One is already stepping on the path towards Enlightenment.”

You are freeing yourself from ignorance, step by step.

13. The full ladder in this verse

She strings the whole journey together:

1. Āve jyā evi dasha – Worthiness arises (the four virtues).

2. Sadgurubodh suhay – Teachings become effective:

  • Enter,
  • Stir,
  • Transform.

3. Te bodhe suvicharna pragate – Inner work (listening → contemplation → meditation) awakens:

  • Right thinking,
  • Right remembering,
  • Right discriminating,
  • And withdrawing from worldly obsessions.

4. Absorption in the pure Self:

  • From bhed-jnān (distinguishing Self and non-self),
  • You move to abhed-jnān:
    • You simply ignore non-self,
    • Rest in pure “I am” without body-identification.

5. Self-realisation dawns:

  • The “sunrise” of the soul:
    • Delusion breaks,
    • You taste your own nature.

6. Tya pragate sukhday – Fountains of bliss emerge:

  • First as glimpses,
  • Then more stable,
  • Then ultimately as the constant bliss of moksh.

All this begins with:

“Āve jyā evi dasha…” – When that inner state is there

14. One-line summary of Verse 40

When the inward state of worthiness (verse 38) arises, the Sadguru’s teachings start truly working within. From those teachings, deep inner work – suvicharna – awakens, and through that, the seeker begins to experience the real, imported-from-the-soul bliss: sukhday.
