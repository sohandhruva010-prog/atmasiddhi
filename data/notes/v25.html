Verse 25 — False beliefs about Dev (Satdev)
The mistake

The matārthi confines his intelligence (rokī rahe nij buddhi) to external glories and treats those as the essence of the Omniscient Lord:

  • Jindeh pramāṇ – body size/form/complexion of the Tīrthaṅkara
  • Samavasaraṇādi – the gorgeous assembly hall of a Tīrthaṅkara, its jewels/floors/zones, celestial arrangements
  • Siddhi – other external manifestations: golden lotuses where He steps, divine fanfare, etc.

He does worship a true God (e.g., Mahāvīra) — yet his reason and focus are wrong, so he remains a matārthi.

What he overlooks (the real nature)
  • Sarvajñatā – Omniscience
  • Vītarāgatā – total detachment/dispassion
  • The inner state: stillness, purity, equanimity — this is why God is worship-worthy.

Line of thought: “If I praise bodies, crowns, halls… am I really seeking inner freedom, or just outer spectacle?”

Not “against” descriptions — but against limiting yourself to them
  • Names, symbols (lāñchhan), lineage, dates, samavasaraṇ details show up in Āgamas and Purāṇas; they’re not wrong.
  • They’re fine as early education (kids learn 24 Tīrthaṅkar names, symbols, mothers/fathers, heights, complexions, dates).
  • Error = treating these by-products of karma as the essence and the reason for worship.
Key doctrinal clarification (powerful)
  • The fanfare (samavasaraṇ, eight excellences, golden lotuses…) arises from auspicious karma (puṇya), not from the inner purity itself.
  • When auspicious karma is exhausted (e.g., post-liberation on Siddhaśilā), the fanfare ceases — but omniscience and detachment remain.
  • Therefore, if you worship mainly for the fanfare, you’re really praising karma, not the Lord’s inner glory.

Classic support (Aptamīmāṁsā via Vachanāmṛt paraphrase): “O Lord, I do not worship You for external grandeur but for Your inner glory.”

Vivid examples & analogies used
  • Newspaper headline trap: “Master addresses 1 million; miracles!” → crowds equate scale/spectacle with spiritual greatness.
  • Salesman/magician analogy: A clever charlatan can pull crowds, sell an image.
  • Lao Tzu (paraphrased): “In the fanfare of decorations and celebrations, God is lost.” (Focus drifts from inner stillness.)
  • Childhood learning: Knowing every Tīrthaṅkara’s height/complexion/mother/father/symbol and being able to draw the samavasaraṇ — yet knowing nothing of omniscience and detachment until guided by a Sadguru.
  • Capital city analogy: Describing New Delhi is not the same as describing the Prime Minister. Likewise, samavasaraṇ ≠ the Lord’s inner state.
What this means for practice
Your worship focus (Dev-darśan)
  • Shift from outer marvels → inner virtues: omniscience, detachment, fearlessness, compassion, impartiality.
  • During festivals (Pañcha-Kalyāṇak, pratiṣṭhā), enjoy beauty but anchor attention in the Lord’s state.
  • In bhajans/stavans, prefer/include songs that praise inner qualities (vītarāgatā, dr̥ ṣṭā-bhāva, subsidence of passions), not only ornaments and scenes.
Discernment checklist (Dev & Guru)
  • ☐ Do I admire spectacle more than stillness?
  • ☐ Do I know the inner marks (sarvajñatā/vītarāgatā) and bring them to mind while worshipping?
  • ☐ Do I conflate popularity/miracles with realization?
  • ☐ Am I limiting my intelligence to safe, story-like details instead of stretching toward the invisible essence?

Mini-glossary
  • Kriyajāda matārthi: one stuck in doing/ritual, not inner change
  • Bahya-tyāg: outer renunciation (robes, vows, austerities)
  • Ātma-jñān: experiential self-realisation
  • Samavasaraṇ: divine assembly of a Tīrthaṅkara (celestial construction)
  • Siddhi: external manifestations of glory
  • Sarvajñatā / Vītarāgatā: omniscience / total detachment
  • Āgama / Purāṇa: philosophical canon / mythic-narrative texts
One-screen takeaway
  • Don’t confuse the Lord’s karma-byproducts (halls, lotuses, radiance) with His inner essence (omniscience, detachment).
  • It’s fine to learn and enjoy descriptions; just don’t stop there.
  • Let every darśan pivot to: “May that vītarāgatā & sarvajñatā be the state I adore and cultivate.”

Additional notes on this verse
Verse 25

Romanized text Je jindeh pramāṇe samavasaraṇādi siddhi;
varṇan samaje jinanuṁ rokī rahe nij buddhi.

Plain meaning One who takes descriptions of the Jina’s body, the Samavasaran, and other outer glories as the very nature of the Jina, and then stops his own understanding there, remains a bigot.

Word by word
  • Je = that person, the one being described
  • Jindeh = of the Jina’s body
  • Pramāṇe = by measures and features like size, form, colour
  • Samavasaraṇ-ādi = the Samavasaran and similar arrangements and pageantry
  • Siddhi = outer marvels and manifestations such as golden lotuses and celestial grandeur
  • Varṇan = descriptions and narratives of these things
  • Samaje = understands or takes as
  • Jinanuṁ = belonging to the Jina
  • Rokī rahe = halts, confines, stops short
  • Nij buddhi = one’s own intellect or discernment

Where this verse fits Param Krupalu Dev divides Atmasiddhi into twelve parts. We are in the section on the attributes of the bigot.

  • Verse 24 showed the bigot’s wrong view about the Guru
  • Verse 25 shows the bigot’s wrong view about God Here God is true, the understanding is wrong.
The mistake being exposed

The person worships a true Tirthankar but equates greatness with externals. He gets stuck on:

  • Body details: height, complexion, symbols, lineage, dates
  • Samavasaran details: multi tiered halls, jeweled floors, zones, music, crowds
  • Siddhi or marvels: golden lotuses where the Lord steps, celestial processions

He then concludes: this is why the Jina is great, and this is what should be worshiped. Because he halts his understanding at descriptions, he remains a mechanical ritualist.

What he should be seeing instead

Two inner realities are the touchstones:

  • Sarvajñatā: omniscience, the unobstructed knowing
  • Vītrāgatā: total non attachment, the steady freedom from likes and dislikes

These are the Jina’s real nature, the reason for reverence, and the compass for practice.

Why externals are the wrong yardstick
  • The Samavasaran and such glories are effects of auspicious karmas, not the expression of inner purity.
  • After liberation, auspicious karmas are exhausted. The Samavasaran no longer appears, but omniscience and dispassion abide.
  • Therefore, to take the pageantry as the essence is to praise karma, not the Jina.
Signature lines and images the teacher uses
  • Fanfare warning: in decorations and celebrations, God is often lost. God is recognised in inner stillness.
  • Capital city example: describing the capital does not describe the king. Beautiful streets are not the king’s intelligence and courage.
  • Crowd and miracle example: numbers, stagecraft, or marvel stories can mislead. Salesmanship is not sanctity.
What the verse is not saying
  • Names, symbols, histories, festivals, and decoration are not false and not forbidden. They help beginners and children identify and connect.
  • The warning is only this: do not stop there. Use externals as doors to the inner virtues.
The role of ego

Ignorance begins the error. Ego preserves it. Once we invest pride in these outer measures, we defend them and refuse to move inward.

Short meaning you can memorise

A person who limits devotion to body features, divine halls, and marvels, taking those as the Jina’s nature, has stopped his intellect at the surface and remains a bigot, even while worshiping a true God.

Practice cues to correct the mistake

Use these exactly during darshan or prayer:

1. Inner praise statement I bow to Your omniscience and Your dispassion. May a drop of that calm arise in me now.

2. Focus shift during rituals As the eye sees ornament and hall, the mind repeats: The essence is knowing and non attachment.

3. Daily reflection Today did I admire the setting or did I emulate the state

4. Sankalp before festival Let celebration point attention to inner stillness. Let music and colour serve silence.

5. One line filter If it can vanish when merit ends, it is not the essence.

One page recap for quick study
  • Verse: Je jindeh pramāṇe … rokī rahe nij buddhi
  • Error: Taking body, Samavasaran, marvels as the Jina’s nature
  • Essence: Sarvajñatā and Vītrāgatā
  • Reasoning: Externals are karmic by products and do not persist after liberation
  • Warning: Fanfare can hide God if focus is outward
  • Allowance: Externals are fine as aids, never as the goal
  • Action: Praise virtues, imitate inner state, keep the mind on essence
