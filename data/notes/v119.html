Expression of the Disciple’s Enlightenment & the Beginning of the Path of Devotion

This lecture begins the actual expression of the disciple after Self-realisation. The dialogue is over. The Guru has become silent and absorbed in the Self. The disciple has turned within, experienced the Self, and now speaks again.

But what is striking is what he says first.

He does not begin with:

“I realised the Soul.”

He begins with:

“Sadguruna updeshthi…” — By the teachings of my Sadguru…

His first response to Self-realisation is gratitude toward the Guru. That becomes the heart of Verse 119. Pasted text

1. Verse 119 — The Core Meaning

The verse can be understood through four key phrases:

Sadguruna updeshthi — By the teachings of the True Guru Avyu apurva bhan — I attained unprecedented realisation Nijpada nijmahi lahyu — I realised my true Self within myself Dur thayu ajnan — My self-delusive ignorance was destroyed

Short meaning

Through the teachings of the Sadguru, I attained a realisation I had never experienced before. I realised my true Self within myself, and my self-delusive ignorance was destroyed. Pasted text

There are therefore three things that happened to the disciple:

1. Apurva bhan — unprecedented realisation arose.

2. Nijpada nijmahi lahyu — he realised the true Self within.

3. Dur thayu ajnan — beginningless self-delusive ignorance was destroyed.

And the disciple attributes the entire transformation to:

Sadguruna updeshthi — the teachings and grace of the Sadguru.

2. Apurva Bhan — Not Knowledge, but Realisation

Bapa makes a very important distinction between jnan and bhan here.

The verse does not merely say:

“I gained knowledge.”

It says:

“Avyu apurva bhan.”

Apurva

Never before. Unprecedented.

This is something the soul had never experienced before, despite countless lifetimes.

The disciple has experienced countless things before:

  • possessions
  • relationships
  • status
  • achievements
  • pleasure
  • pain
  • different bodies
  • different circumstances

All of these have come and gone innumerable times.

But this?

This had never happened before.

For the first time, he has awakened to who he actually is. Pasted text

Bhan

Bhan is deeper than intellectual knowledge.

It is realisation.

Previously, the Guru explained:

“You are the Soul.”

The disciple understood.

Now:

The disciple experiences it.

So the progression is:

Information → Understanding → Contemplation → Experience → Realisation

The disciple essentially tells the Guru:

You explained everything beautifully before, but I am no longer merely saying that I understood You. Something has actually happened within me.

Bapa calls this the difference between information and transformation. Pasted text

3. The “Seeing Eye” Has Opened — Atmadrashti

Bapa gives a beautiful description:

The Guru has opened the disciple’s “seeing eye.”

Not the physical eyes.

The physical eyes have been functioning for countless births.

But through those eyes, the disciple always saw:

  • body
  • people
  • objects
  • circumstances
  • relationships
  • likes and dislikes

He never saw the Self.

He was always identified with:

body + senses + intellect

Now, through the Guru's grace, atmadrashti — the vision of the Self — has opened.

Suddenly:

“I can see the Soul within.”

The change is not that a new Soul was created.

The Soul was always there.

What changed was the vision.

This is why the transformation is so radical. The disciple had been living with the Self forever, yet had never recognised it. Pasted text

4. The Great Shift: “Up Till Now” vs. “Now”

Bapa beautifully calls Verse 119 an autobiography.

It is the story of:

Up till now…

versus

Now…

Up till now:

“I” meant the body.

“I am hungry.”

“I am hurt.”

“I am happy.”

“I am insulted.”

“I like this.”

“I dislike this.”

The pure Self was identified with the non-self.

The disciple was living in wrong identification — body, senses and intellect were taken to be “I.” Pasted text

Now:

“I” has shifted.

“Now I can identify ‘I’ with the pure Soul.”

He realises:

I am an eternal conscious substance. I am knowingness. I am not this body. I am not even these likes and dislikes.

That is the revolution of Self-realisation.

The external world may look exactly the same.

The body may be the same.

The circumstances may be the same.

But the meaning of “I” has completely changed. Pasted text

5. Nijpada Nijmahi Lahyu — I Found Myself Within Myself

This phrase is extremely deep.

Nijpada

My true state / true Self.

Nijmahi

Within myself.

Lahyu

I obtained / realised.

So:

“I realised my true Self within myself.”

Notice what was not attained.

The disciple did not receive the Soul from the Guru.

The Guru did not put purity into him.

The Guru did not give him something external.

The Self was already within.

This connects beautifully with the earlier verses:

Tu chho moksh swaroop — You are already of the nature of liberation.

The disciple finally experiences what the Guru had been explaining.

6. “I Attained Myself Through Myself Within Myself”

Bapa distils the philosophy beautifully:

“Although I attained myself, through myself, within myself…”

From the path of knowledge, this is completely correct.

The Self was:

  • already there
  • within oneself
  • realised through one's own experience

No one else can literally experience your Soul for you.

No one can hand Self-realisation to another soul like an object.

So from the philosophical standpoint:

I attained myself through myself within myself.

But Bapa then says:

Add one word before this:

“Although…”

Although I attained myself through myself within myself… had You not shown me the way, Gurudev, I would never have found it.

That “although” beautifully connects jnan and bhakti. Pasted text

7. Path of Knowledge vs. Path of Devotion

This is one of the deepest parts of the lecture.

From the path of knowledge, we could explain the Guru as:

Nimitta — instrumental cause

and the disciple/Self as:

Upadan — material/substantial cause

Philosophically, this is precise.

The Guru did not create the Soul.

The Guru did not manufacture Self-realisation inside the disciple.

The disciple realised the Self within himself.

But bhakti speaks differently.

Bhakti does not stand before the Guru after Self-realisation and say:

“Technically, You were merely my instrumental cause.”

That may be philosophically accurate.

But the heart says:

“You are the cause.”

That is why the verse simply says:

Sadguruna updeshthi By the teachings of the Sadguru.

It does not qualify it with philosophical terminology.

Bapa's distinction is beautiful:

Jn an says: “I attained myself through myself within myself; You were the instrumental cause.”

Bhakti says: “Gurudev, this happened because of You.”

Both are true from their respective perspectives.

But Verse 119 is now beginning the language of the heart. Pasted text

8. Self-Realisation Does NOT Reduce Devotion to the Guru

This is probably the most important devotional teaching of the lecture.

One might think:

“Once I realise the Self, why do I need the Guru?”

After all, the disciple has reached within.

His attention is now directed toward the pure Self.

Surely Guru-bhakti should become less important?

Bapa says:

No. It becomes more.

After Self-realisation:

  • attention turns more toward the Self
  • atmadrashti awakens
  • ignorance is destroyed

But love, respect and devotion toward the Guru increase.

Why?

Because now the disciple finally understands what the Guru actually gave him.

Before realisation, he had faith that the Guru was benefitting him.

After realisation, he knows the magnitude of that benefaction. Pasted text

That is such an important progression:

Before experience:

“I trust You.”

After experience:

“Now I know what You did for me.”

Therefore, Self-realisation does not produce:

“Now I am also a Jnani.”

It produces:

“How can I ever repay You?”

9. His First Instinct Is to Go to the Guru

The disciple has just attained something unprecedented.

He has experienced the Soul.

He has discovered the source of peace and joy within.

What should he do?

Celebrate?

Remain absorbed within?

Enjoy the bliss?

No.

His immediate response is:

First, let me go to my Guru's Lotus Feet.

He wants to:

  • bow down
  • express gratitude
  • offer respect
  • offer devotion
  • offer salutations

He will not even remain absorbed in his own newfound joy before acknowledging the One who showed him the way. Pasted text

This tells us something very deep about true bhakti:

The greater the attainment, the greater the gratitude.

10. The Devotion of a Self-Realised Disciple

Bapa specifically asks us to notice that this is not ordinary Guru devotion.

Many people bow to the Guru.

Many people perform puja.

Many people sing bhakti.

Many people say:

“I am Your servant.”

But here we are being shown:

The devotion of a Self-realised Soul toward his Guru.

And surprisingly, the higher the disciple becomes, the smaller he feels before his Guru.

Bapa gives examples of great Acharyas and elevated beings expressing themselves as:

  • a speck at the Lord's Feet
  • a humble servant
  • the servant of the Guru's servant
  • filled with faults and lacking virtues

Meanwhile, an ordinary person gives one good speech or wins one quiz and ego begins to rise.

Bapa jokes that we become like thermocol balls — immediately floating upward. Pasted text

Spiritual elevation therefore produces humility, not superiority.

The disciple does not think:

“You are Self-realised and now I am Self-realised too, so we're equal.”

Instead:

“Let me remain the most humble servant of Your servant.”

That is the devotion being presented here.

11. “From Now On, Let This Body Behave According to Your Ajna”

This is another powerful implication.

After realising:

I am not the body

the disciple does not become careless about the body.

Instead, he essentially offers it to the Guru:

Everything other than the Soul is inferior to the Soul — and even my discovery of the Soul was bestowed through You. Therefore, let this body now function according to Your command.

This is beautiful because surrender becomes deeper after Self-realisation, not weaker.

Before:

“I follow Your ajna because I trust You.”

After:

“I follow Your ajna because I have directly experienced where Your ajna leads.”

That is a much deeper surrender. Pasted text

12. Reconstructing the Scene: Guru in Samadhi, Disciple Watching

Bapa asks us to mentally return to the scene.

The Guru had just been speaking to the disciple.

He was:

  • answering doubts
  • using words
  • reasoning
  • recalling teachings
  • explaining subtle truths

And suddenly...

He becomes silent.

He withdraws.

He becomes absorbed within Himself.

The disciple is watching this transition happen before him:

speech → silence activity → absorption teaching → samadhi

The Guru who was completely present with the disciple moments ago is suddenly completely immersed within the Self. Pasted text

And watching this activates something within the disciple.

13. The Guru’s State Awakens the Disciple’s State

The disciple sees the transcendental state of his Guru.

His own mumukshuta — yearning for liberation — intensifies.

He begins turning inward.

Bapa identifies several virtues that have already been cultivated throughout Atmasiddhi:

  • exclusive desire for liberation
  • grief of transmigration
  • compassion
  • subsidence of passions
  • love
  • faith
  • surrender

Until now, these may have appeared as individual qualities.

But now:

They all come together.

And when vairagya (dispassion) and prem (love) come together, the disciple naturally turns within.

The world loses its pull.

The Guru has closed His eyes and withdrawn within — so, as Bapa puts it:

What is left outside to see?

The disciple becomes introverted and experiences the Soul through the light of his own direct experience. Pasted text

This is an important point:

The Guru cannot replace the disciple's experience.

The Guru inspires.

The Guru prepares.

The Guru explains.

The Guru removes confusion.

The Guru creates worthiness.

The Guru's presence activates the disciple.

But finally:

The disciple experiences the Self himself.

14. The Ambalalbhai Example — What Must It Be Like to Be Near Such a State?

Bapa reflects on Ambalalbhai holding the lantern while Param Krupalu Dev composed Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra.

Ambalalbhai apparently did not know exactly what was being written as it happened. He had simply been told to hold the lantern.

Yet Bapa wonders:

What must have been happening inside Ambalalbhai during that time?

If we can sense someone's:

  • anger
  • love
  • hunger
  • mood

without them explicitly saying anything, then what must it have been like to stand beside Param Krupalu Dev during such an extraordinary spiritual event?

Perhaps Ambalalbhai did not intellectually understand everything occurring, but Bapa suggests he must have sensed that something divine was happening. Pasted text

This connects back to the previous lecture:

The Guru teaches not only through words.

His state, presence and inner absorption can itself affect a worthy disciple.

15. What Exactly Has Been Destroyed?

The disciple says:

“Dur thayu ajnan.”

Ignorance has been destroyed.

This is not simply:

“I learned something I didn't know before.”

The ignorance here is self-delusive ignorance.

For beginningless time:

Self → mistaken for non-self

The body was “I.”

The body's relations were “mine.”

Likes and dislikes became “my feelings.”

The karmic personality became “me.”

Now that basic error has broken.

The disciple can distinguish:

Pure Self

from

Karmic personality

Bapa gives the example that instead of saying:

“I am hungry,”

the realised perspective would see:

“Rakesh is feeling hungry.”

The body and karmic personality have not disappeared.

But they are experienced as separate from the true “I.” Pasted text

16. Darkness Did Not Need to Be Removed Piece by Piece

Bapa uses the analogy of someone who has spent years imprisoned in darkness.

Suddenly bright sunlight enters.

Would he need to ask:

“Has the darkness gone?”

No.

Light itself proves the disappearance of darkness.

Likewise:

Self-realisation itself proves the destruction of self-delusive ignorance.

There is no need to individually fight every piece of darkness.

When the correct vision appears, the fundamental ignorance disappears.

This strongly connects Verse 119 back to Verse 114:

A beginningless condition need not require an equally long period to end.

Just as darkness disappears when light appears, ignorance disappears with awakening. Pasted text

17. The Joy That Comes With Self-Realisation

The disciple doesn't merely discover:

“Oh, I am the Soul.”

Along with that recognition comes something experiential:

Peace and joy arise from within.

Bapa describes it almost like a fountain of joy bursting from the depths of the Soul.

And importantly:

The joy was not newly created.

It was always within.

But it had never been experienced because attention had always remained outward.

Now, with Self-realisation:

The Self is experienced → the inherent peace is experienced → the inherent joy is experienced.

That is why this experience is apurva — unprecedented. Pasted text

18. The Greatest Paradox of Verse 119

This is perhaps the most beautiful philosophical-devotional tension in the verse:

Philosophically:

The Self was already mine.

Experientially:

I experienced it within myself.

Spiritually:

I had to turn within myself.

Yet devotionally:

“Gurudev, You gave it to me.”

The disciple knows perfectly well:

Nijpada nijmahi lahyu I found my Self within myself.

Yet his heart still says:

Sadguruna updeshthi It happened through You.

That is not philosophical confusion.

That is gratitude.

Bapa therefore says the disciple is associating his entire experience of Self-realisation with the Sadguru and acknowledging the immeasurable benefaction of the Guru. Pasted text

19. Amaap — Immeasurable Is the Guru’s Contribution

Bapa uses three beautiful words for the Guru's benefaction:

Anhad — limitless Anant — unending/infinite Amaap — immeasurable

The disciple cannot calculate:

“The Guru contributed 60%; I contributed 40%.”

Bhakti does not operate like that.

The disciple looks at everything that happened:

  • the yearning for liberation
  • the correct understanding
  • the Six Fundamental Truths
  • the destruction of misconceptions
  • the inward turn
  • the Guru's presence
  • the Guru's teachings
  • the Guru's blessings
  • finally, Self-realisation

and says:

“This happened because of You.”

That is the heartfelt devotion being introduced in these verses. Pasted text

20. This Begins the Path of Devotion

Bapa makes an important structural observation about Atmasiddhi.

From approximately verses 43–118, we have largely travelled through the path of knowledge:

  • doubts
  • answers
  • logic
  • Six Fundamental Truths
  • nature of the Self
  • path of liberation
  • contemplation
  • Self-realisation

But these nine verses beginning with 119 reveal something else:

The path of devotion.

And importantly, it is not devotion born from ignorance.

It is the devotion of someone who has realised the Self.

That makes it extraordinarily powerful.

The disciple knows the truth and yet bows.

He has realised his greatness as the Soul and yet becomes more humble before the Guru.

He has attained something within himself and yet gives the credit to his Guru.

Jnan and bhakti are not opposing paths here.

At their highest point, they beautifully meet. Pasted text

21. The Real Test of Studying Atmasiddhi

Bapa makes a striking statement:

“If we learn devotion, then Atmasiddhi is a success.”

It is possible to study all the philosophy and become:

  • knowledgeable
  • articulate
  • good at quizzes
  • good at explaining verses

But if knowledge produces ego rather than humility, something essential has been missed.

The realised disciple demonstrates the opposite.

More realisation → more humility

More experience → more gratitude

More closeness to the Self → more devotion to the Sadguru

So one sign that the teaching is penetrating deeply is not merely:

“I understand more.”

It is also:

“I bow more deeply.”

Pasted text

22. The Final Conclusion of the Lecture

Bapa ends with the central conclusion of Verse 119:

The disciple has attained Self-realisation.

Technically, the Self was within him.

Technically, he experienced it himself.

Technically, his own Soul is the substantive cause of the experience.

Yet his heart refuses to reduce the Guru to merely an “instrumental cause.”

Instead:

“Sadguruna updeshthi…”

Through You.

The disciple recognises his debt, feels deeply obliged, and expresses heartfelt devotion toward the Sadguru. And Bapa concludes that this verse establishes the refuge of a benevolent, compassionate, Enlightened, Self-realised Guru as the easiest and best way to Self-realisation. Pasted text

The Full Inner Journey of Verse 119

I would remember the lecture through this sequence:

Guru's teachings → worthiness develops → Guru enters samadhi → disciple observes → mumukshuta intensifies → love + dispassion unite → disciple turns within → Self is experienced → atmadrashti opens → ignorance disappears → inner peace and joy arise → disciple turns back toward Guru → gratitude explodes → devotion deepens.

And that last part matters enormously:

Self-realisation does not make the Guru less important. It finally allows the disciple to understand how important the Guru truly was.

Key Lines to Remember

Sadguruna updeshthi — Through the teachings of my Sadguru.

Avyu apurva bhan — I attained unprecedented realisation.

Nijpada nijmahi lahyu — I realised my true Self within myself.

Dur thayu ajnan — My self-delusive ignorance was destroyed.

Before: “I” = body, senses, intellect, karmic personality. Now: “I” = eternal conscious Self.

Path of knowledge: “I attained myself through myself within myself.”

Path of devotion: “Gurudev, it happened because of You.”

The greater the realisation, the greater the humility and gratitude.

The disciple does not say, “Now I am also a Jnani.” He wants to remain the humble servant of the Guru.

The Self was always within; the Guru opened the ‘seeing eye’ through which it could finally be recognised.

One-line essence

Verse 119 is the moment the disciple opens his eyes to the Self and, instead of looking at himself with pride, immediately looks toward his Guru with gratitude: “I found the Self within me, but Gurudev, I found it because of You.”
