Where this sits in the flow

  • v.24–28: the mechanical ritualist bigot (kriyājada) → action and show, no inner change.
  • v.29–31: the dry intellectualist bigot (śuṣk-jñānī) → words and concepts, no inner change.
  • v.29: he negates practices and becomes “sādhana-rahit” (devoid of means).
  • v.30 (today): not only he sinks; those who associate with him sink too. The verse is a teaching on the power of association.

The verse’s core idea (plain sense)

  • The dry intellectualist:
    • has not attained Self-realisation (“jñān-daśā pāmey nahi”),
    • nor is he in right discipline/practice (“sādhan-daśā”).
  • Association with such a person → the associate also “bude bhav māhi” — drowns in the ocean of transmigration (cycles of birth and death).

“He is not established in Self-realisation and not established in right discipline… and the person who stays in his company also sinks.”

Key terms (as used)

  • Jñān-daśā: the state of Self-realisation / Self-knowledge (“pāmey nahi” = not obtained).
  • Sādhan-daśā: the state of right discipline (vows, restraint, devotion, daily practice).
  • Bude / bhav māhi: drowns in the ocean of worldly existence (saṁsāra).
  • Sādhana-rahit: devoid of means (no workable runway to lift off spiritually).

What makes the dry intellectualist dangerous

1. Zero attainment + zero discipline

  • “Not Enlightened” (no effortless Self-state) and “not disciplined” (no consistent practice).
  • He has neither the end (state) nor the means (sādhana).

2. Strong influence despite inner hollowness

  • He may speak well (“apparently true logic,” “lofty words”), attract crowds, and induce beliefs subtly—without any inner substance.
  • This is “lip adoption” continued: language of purity without purification.

3. Association transfers beliefs

  • “Wrong person you associate with and your thinking also changes.”
  • The transfer path he gives: (1) sharing of stuff → (2) sharing of thinking → (3) sharing of beliefs—often without noticing.

4. Contagion metaphor

  • The belief-infection is “more than even COVID-19”: mere association can make you start thinking, believing, doing the same.

Why the associate drowns too

  • If your guide has no swimming skill (Self-state) and no flotation (means/practice), he cannot save you:
    • “How can someone save someone from drowning if he himself does not know how to swim and has no means?”
    • If he at least had a float/boat, he could help; here there is neither.
  • Result: doob-doob-doob—“music of drowning.”

What this verse is not saying

  • Scriptural study is not wrong. “Acquiring scriptural knowledge is not wrong—it is the beginning.”
    • The mistake is making it the end: finishing books but remaining the same.

Practical diagnostics (for yourself)

1. Status check

  • Jñān-daśā? Have I attained an effortless inner stability (even amid praise/blame, gain/loss)?
  • Sādhan-daśā? Do I have a disciplined routine (devotion, meditation, vows/restraints, daily repentance, study)?

2. Association audit

  • Who are my 3 closest influences?
  • Do they soften passions (anger, pride, deceit, greed) — or feed them?
  • After time with them, am I more humble, restrained, devotional — or more reactive, self-willed, proud?

3. Outcome lens

  • Is my “sorry / Micchāmi Dukkaḍaṁ” decreasing because passions are subsiding (not because ego avoids apology)?
  • Are my words outrunning my state (beautiful talk, same reactions)?

Do / Don’t (association)

Do
  • Choose company that raises you: Sadguru, true seekers, those living discipline + humility.
  • Prefer being alone to being in bad company: “It is better to be alone than be in a bad company—even if a little loneliness comes.”
  • Keep scripture → reflection → application as a single pipeline: study, then use the knowledge to restrain tendencies.
Don’t
  • Don’t mistake eloquence for enlightenment.
  • Don’t follow someone who neither lives the state nor the means.
  • Don’t let “I understand so much” become the shield for unchanged behaviour (“I still got angry”). Use the understanding to prevent or shorten anger.

Red flags of the drowning mentor

  • Negates vows, devotion, discipline as “unnecessary,” because “I am already pure.”
  • No timetable, no restraints, no repentance; yet talks big on the Absolute.
  • Generates attention and status, not subtlety and simplicity.
  • Leaves followers with more cleverness, no contrition; more debates, no detachment.

Positive counter-model

  • If you truly accept “I am the pure Self” (Absolute), you’ll quietly keep Relative sādhana as your runway:
    • Daily devotion, study, meditation, restraint, repentance.
    • Steady softening of passions and shorter recovery when you slip.
    • Language that matches state; less show, more gravity.

Memorable lines (as spoken)

  • “Not established in Self-realisation and not established in right discipline.”
  • “The person who stays in his company also sinks in the ocean of infinite cycles of birth and death.”
  • “Wrong person you associate with and your thinking also changes.”
  • “It is better to be alone than be in a bad company.”
  • “Acquiring scriptural knowledge is not wrong—It is the beginning. Your problem is it’s your end.”

One-page recap

  • Verse 30 warns: the dry intellectualist has neither the state (jñān-daśā) nor the means (sādhan-daśā).
  • His influence is contagious: eloquence without substance lures; beliefs transfer; the associate also drowns (“bude bhav māhi”).
  • Study is good—but only as a beginning. The goal is inner transformation, evidenced by subsiding passions and steady discipline.
  • Choose company that gives you a runway (sādhana) and points to the sky (Self-state). If forced to choose, pick solitude over bad company.
  • Measure progress not by pages finished or phrases mastered, but by quicker humility, fewer flare-ups, and deeper stillness.
