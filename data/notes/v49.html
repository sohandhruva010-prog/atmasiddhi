34:14–39:48 — Verse 49 begins: root cause is “dehadhyas” (false identification)

He introduces the Sadguru’s first strike:

Verse line (as spoken)

  • “Bhasyo dehadhyasthi, atma deh saman;
    Pan te banne bhinn chhe, pragat lakshane bhan.”

Word-by-word (his flow)

  • Bhasyo = appears (he says it can also carry “illusory” sense, but keep it simple as “appears”)
  • Dehadhyasthi
    • break it: deh + adhyas
    • adhyas = habit (he simplifies as “wrong habit”)
    • dehadhyas = habitual false identification with the body (“I am the body”)
  • Because of this, what happens?
    • Atma deh saman = soul and body appear the same / like one thing / two words for one reality
  • Reality check:
    • Pan… te banne bhinn chhe = but in reality they are separate entities
  • How can you know?
    • Pragat lakshane bhan
    • pragat = obvious/evident
    • lakshan = characteristics/signs/traits
    • bhan = clear knowing/recognition

Short meaning he gives

  • Due to false identification, soul and body appear the same.
  • But they are separate and can be distinguished
  • by their evident characteristics.

53:03–55:22 — Key correction: “Not knowable by senses” ≠ “not knowable”

He tackles a crucial logical mistake:

  • “Soul cannot be known by senses” does not mean:
    • “soul cannot be known”
  • Like:
    • “I don’t know French” ≠ “I don’t know anything.”

Even the Enlightened One:

  • does not “see soul with eyes” like a clock/time reading.
  • So your observation (can’t be seen by senses) may be right,
  • but your conclusion (“therefore it doesn’t exist”) is wrong.

He links it back:

  • disciple observed correctly: “not perceived by senses”
  • but concluded wrongly: “therefore it doesn’t exist.”

Sadguru’s method:

  • not just “telling you you’re wrong”,

Quick “cheat sheet” (the verse vocabulary he teaches)

  • Bhasyo: appears (can imply illusory appearance)
  • Dehadhyas: body-identification habit (“I am body”)
  • Atma deh saman: soul/body seem like one reality
  • Bhinn chhe: separate entities
  • Pragat: evident/obvious
  • Lakshan: characteristics/signs
  • Bhan: clear recognition/knowing
  • Bhedjnan: discriminative knowledge (separating knower from known)
