Sixth Fundamental: Moksh Upay (Path/Means of Liberation)
Doubt 2 — “Too many paths… which one is true?”

1) Quick context (where we are)

  • Common doubt (verses 92–96): “Even if moksh exists, I don’t see an undisputed (avirodh) path to attain it.”
  • Argument 1 (Verse 92): Infinite karmas + short human lifespan → “How can all karmas be destroyed?”
  • Now this video = Argument 2 (Verse 93):
    • Not only is the task huge…
    • the paths themselves are too many and contradictory, so the disciple can’t even decide where to begin.

2) Verse 93 key words (as your Guru explains)

Opening word: “Athva”
  • Athva = “or / alternatively”
  • Meaning: “Even if we keep aside the first issue (infinite karmas vs short life)… here is my second argument.”
Main line (disciple’s argument)
“Mat darshan ghana… kahey upay anek.”
  • Mat = opinions, viewpoints (“this is my opinion”)
  • Darshan = philosophies / schools of thought / religions
  • Ghana = many
  • Kahey upay anek = they describe the means/path in many different ways

✅ Simple meaning:

  • “There are many opinions and many schools of thought, and they all teach different methods for liberation.”

3) Why “many” is not the only problem — the real problem is “diverse & conflicting”

Your Guru makes an important point:

  • If there were many paths but they were saying the same thing, it would be manageable.
  • But here, paths are:
    • different, and often
    • mutually critical / denouncing each other, and
    • each claims: “mine is the best / only / flawless.”

4) Example set 1: Knowledge-path vs Devotion-path (they criticize each other)

A) Knowledge-path person says:
  • “What bhakti are you doing?”
  • “Study, contemplate, meditate!”
B) Devotion-path person says:
  • “My Guru has already done all knowledge.”
  • “I only need bhakti / surrender—just listen to Him!”

✅ Result:

  • Both sound confident.
  • Both look “right” to their followers.
  • Disciple gets stuck: “Then which one is truly the moksh-upay?”

5) Example set 2: Introvert vs Seva-heavy path (personality conflict)

Your Guru voices the disciple’s inner struggle:

  • “I am an introvert.”
  • Some tell me: do selfless service 10–12 hours daily
  • But I feel drawn to: sit quietly, close eyes, meditate on Self

So the confusion becomes practical:

  • “Should I do seva?”
  • “Or scriptural study + meditation?”
  • “Or some mix?”

6) The decisive line: “Which one is true?”

“Tema mat sacho kayo?”
  • “Among these, which view/path is true?”
“Baney na eh vivek.”
  • “It is not possible (for me) to decide.”
  • He admits humbly:
    • “It’s beyond my intelligence.”
    • “I’ll waste my whole life just deciding… and then I’ll die.”

✅ This is a major “worthiness” point:

  • The disciple is not pretending.
  • He’s honestly admitting: “I cannot determine the true path on my own.”

7) The “everyone says ONLY” problem (the real confusion trigger)

Your Guru highlights the dangerous word:

The problem word: “ONLY”
  • Some say: ONLY true knowledge
  • Some say: ONLY pure devotion
  • Some say: ONLY selfless service
  • Some say: ONLY harsh austerity (example: varshitapa)

Even worse:

  • They don’t just offer a method.
  • They claim:
    • superiority (“mine is best”)
    • infallibility (“mine cannot mislead you”)

So the disciple’s cry becomes:

  • “Guruji… every teacher says the same thing: only my way is right. Then how do I decide?”

8) Example set 3: “Different opinions in mango juice are fine… but not in moksh!”

Your Guru gives a simple contrast:

  • People can disagree about:
    • mango juice being sour vs sweet
    • tasting like “Mangola”
    • more pulp vs less pulp
  • That’s normal.

But:

  • Different opinions about the path of liberation?
  • And they all call themselves enlightened?
  • That is terrifying for a seeker.

✅ The disciple’s logic:

  • “If enlightened people differ so much… what will happen to a simple person like me?”

9) “As many heads, that many opinions” + YouTube effect

Your Guru makes it modern and sharp:

  • “There are as many paths as there are brains.”
  • Earlier, people just followed what they were taught (example: Navkar routine).
  • Now:
    • You can watch every saint on YouTube
    • one says: “no need for meditation”
    • another says: “wake up at 4am”
    • one says: “awareness itself is meditation”
    • another teaches detailed techniques

✅ Outcome:

  • The more you study without a final compass,
  • the more you enter a maze.

10) Example set 4: Meditation techniques & posture confusion (very important)

Your Guru lists how even one topic becomes countless:

  • Half-lotus vs full-lotus
  • Then new poses appear:
    • Baddha Konasana (butterfly)
    • Supta Baddha Konasana (reclining butterfly)

And the seeker feels:

  • “I barely learned the last one, now a new one comes.”
  • “Teacher announces the name, doesn’t demonstrate.”
  • “For a simple person, I need to SEE it every time.”

✅ This is not just yoga humor — it proves:

  • “If even postures are endless, imagine paths of liberation!”

11) Example set 5: Sects and sub-sects (even inside one religion)

Your Guru shows how “one faith” contains layers:

  • Jain → Shwetambar or Digambar
  • Shwetambar → Murtipujak or Sthanakvasi
  • Murtipujak → Tapagacch / Khartargacch / Achalgacch, etc.
  • Even basic disagreements exist:
    • “Should one worship idols or not?”

✅ Disciple’s conclusion:

  • “If even one religion contains many branches… how can I decide across all religions?”

12) Example set 6: “Instant liberation promises” + Vivekananda reference

Your Guru mentions:

  • Many paths are full of verbal promises:
    • “Do this and you will definitely attain Self-realisation.”
    • “Touch toe between eyebrows and your work is done.”
  • He references the story-type idea of Vivekananda (kick/grace):
    • But points out: even that required real surrender, not opposition.

✅ Message:

  • Promises are cheap.
  • The seeker needs a path that can’t mislead.

13) Why THIS disciple is extra-confused: he is well-read

Your Guru says the disciple has studied many:

  • Philosophies: Sankhya, Vedanta, Jain, Buddhism…
  • Faiths: Sikhism…
  • Masters: J. Krishnamurti, Osho, etc.
  • Even meditation slogans:
    • Gorakhnath: “Hasiba-Kheliba-Dhyanam” (laugh/play as meditation)
    • Others: “2–2.5 hours serious meditation; don’t laugh”

✅ So:

  • Study increased his exposure,
  • but without a final key, it became confusion.

14) A powerful hint of what Param Krupalu Dev will do (Guru gives preview)

Even though the formal answer comes later, your Guru gives the direction:

The undisputed core is not an external technique

He says the essence is:

  • Attachment + aversion + ignorance are the principal knots of karma.
  • Receding from these is the path of liberation.

Then:

  • Whether you choose worship or meditation in the morning is secondary.
  • First, this inner condition must be addressed.

✅ He explains this beautifully with two analogies:

Analogy 1: Marriage choice (Anushka example)
  • “Choose your partner as you like.”
  • But the real point is:
    • “How will you live?”
    • “What will be your attitude?”
  • Same here:
    • Choose external practices,
    • but the inner transformation is the undisputed path.
Analogy 2: River and banks
  • “Let the river flow… but with banks.”
  • Meaning:
    • Guru gives freedom,
    • but within the discipline of what truly removes bondage.

15) Final output of Doubt 2 (what disciple is asking the Guru)

The disciple’s final position is:

  • “I accept moksh as possible.”
  • “But too many paths exist.”
  • They are diverse and each claims “ONLY.”
  • I do not have vivek to decide correctly.
  • So I request:
    • “Guruji, please show me the undisputed, unfailing, universally acceptable path.”

16) One-screen summary (Revision-ready)

Doubt 2 (Verse 93): Because there are many religions, philosophies, sects, and methods, and they are diverse and conflicting, each claiming “ONLY my way is true,” the disciple says:

  • “Tema mat sacho kayo?” → Which path is true?
  • “Baney na eh vivek.” → I cannot decide with my discrimination. So he begs Sadguru to reveal the one undisputed path.
