Detailed Notes — Verse 121: “Karta Bhokta Karmano…”
The Disciple Realises the True Nature of Doership and Enjoyership

This lecture moves to Verse 121 of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra. Verse 120 was the disciple’s direct experience of the First and Second Fundamental Truths: I am the Soul, and I am eternal — pure consciousness, ageless, immortal, imperishable and distinct from the body.

Now, in Verses 121 and 122, the disciple expresses his experiential understanding of the Third and Fourth Fundamental Truths:

  • The Soul is the doer
  • The Soul is the enjoyer/endurer

But Self-realisation completely transforms what these statements mean.

The central insight of Verse 121 is:

The Soul is the doer and enjoyer of karma only while delusion prevails. When the tendency turns inward toward the Self, the Soul becomes a non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.

And there is one crucial qualification:

The Soul does not become inactive. It becomes a non-doer of karma. Its activity continues as pure manifestations of its own nature.

1. Where We Are in the Disciple’s Experience

The previous verse was:

Verse 120
“What am I?”

The disciple experienced:

I am pure consciousness — ageless, immortal, imperishable and separate from the body.

That was an experiential confirmation of:

1. The Soul exists

2. The Soul is eternal

Now comes the next question:

If I am the Soul, what exactly is my relationship with karma?

Earlier, the disciple had intellectually learned:

The Soul is the doer of karma. The Soul is the enjoyer/endurer of karma.

But now he has actually experienced the Self.

So he discovers that the statement requires a much deeper understanding.

The Soul is not eternally a doer of karma.

It is a doer of karma only in a particular state:

Vibhav varte jyay — as long as delusion/impurity prevails.

When the attention turns inward:

Vrutti vahi nij bhaavma — my tendency flowed toward my own Self.

Then:

Thayo akarta tyay — I became a non-doer of karma.

2. Short Meaning of Verse 121

The verse can be understood as:

“The Soul is the doer and enjoyer of karma as long as delusion prevails. When my attentiveness flowed toward my own Self, I became the non-doer of karma.”

The non-enjoyership is implied here and is developed further through the explanation and the following verse.

The verse is also autobiographical.

The disciple is not merely making a philosophical statement:

“When one's attention turns inward…”

He is saying:

“My attention turned inward.”
“I experienced myself as a non-doer of karma.”

That distinction becomes extremely important later in the lecture.

3. The Big Question: Is the Soul a Doer or a Non-Doer?

This is the central philosophical question of the lecture.

If the Third Fundamental Truth says:

The Soul is the doer

how can the disciple now say:

“I became a non-doer”?

Is that a contradiction?

No.

Bapa makes a very precise distinction:

The Soul always remains a doer.

But it does not always remain a doer of karma.

The Soul's natural disposition is active — sakriya.

It does not become an inactive substance after Self-realisation.

So the question is not:

Does the Soul act?

The question is:

What is the Soul the doer of?

4. Two Forms of Doership

This gives us one of the most important distinctions in the lecture.

In ignorance:
Soul = doer of karma
In Self-awareness:
Soul = doer of its pure manifestations

So doership itself does not disappear.

The object of doership changes.

Before enlightenment:

The Soul's activity is associated with impure feelings, which become instrumental in karmic bondage.

After enlightenment:

The Soul abides in its own nature and its activity manifests as purity.

Therefore:

Non-doer does NOT mean inactive.

It means:

Non-doer of karma.

Bapa repeats this distinction because without it, one could wrongly conclude that liberation means the Soul becomes inert or actionless.

5. The Blank-Page Example — Pure Manifestation After Pure Manifestation

Bapa gives a very simple visual.

Imagine turning the pages of a completely blank book:

blank page blank page blank page blank page…

Similarly, after the Soul is established in its pure nature:

pure manifestation pure manifestation pure manifestation pure manifestation…

Activity continues.

Doership continues.

But now:

There is no impure activity producing karmic bondage.

So the Soul remains active eternally, even in liberation, but the activity is aligned with its pure nature.

This anticipates the next verse, which explains more clearly:

If the Soul is not the doer of karma, what is it the doer of?

Answer:

Its own pure modifications.

6. From Intellectual Knowledge to Samyak Darshan

Bapa then returns to the transformation happening inside the disciple.

When true understanding awakens:

False/perverse beliefs begin to change.

Truth does not merely remain something intellectually understood.

“The truth percolates as faith.”

It becomes:

Samyak drashti / samyak darshan — right faith.

The disciple is no longer merely saying:

“My Guru has taught that I am the Soul.”

He has experienced it.

And now that experience changes:

  • his identity,
  • his understanding of karma,
  • his doership,
  • his enjoyership,
  • his relationship with external circumstances.

7. Why Is the Disciple Expressing His Experience?

This continues a beautiful point from Verse 120.

The disciple has realised the Self.

The Guru already knows what has happened.

So why does the disciple need to tell Him?

Because:

Expression enhances the continuity of experience.

Bapa explains the loop:

What you speak, you contemplate upon. What you contemplate upon, you speak. And what you repeatedly contemplate upon impacts your inner state.

So the disciple is almost reliving his Self-realisation while expressing it before the Guru.

And emotionally, he wants to tell his Guru everything:

“Gurudev, by Your blessings, I have rightly understood and experienced the true doership and enjoyership of the Soul.”

8. “But Gurudev Already Knows”

Bapa beautifully explores the Guru-disciple relationship here.

The disciple knows:

My Guru is Enlightened. He already knows exactly what I experienced.

Yet the disciple still feels:

“Gurudev, I want to tell You.”

That is not because the Guru requires information.

It is because the disciple wants to share his heart.

This is the nature of the relationship:

He knows — yet I still want to tell Him.

There is joy simply in expressing before the Guru what His grace has done within you.

9. Guru Purnima Is Really the Disciple’s Day

This leads Bapa into a beautiful reflection.

Guru Purnima may be described as a celebration of the Guru.

But Bapa says:

It is actually the disciple's celebration.

The Guru does not need celebration.

The disciple celebrates:

“I have a Guru in my life.”

The Guru may almost be a spectator to the celebration.

The disciple is the one overflowing with gratitude because he realises what he has received.

10. What Makes the Disciple Truly Rich?

Bapa makes a powerful comparison.

Someone may possess:

  • immense wealth,
  • buildings,
  • businesses,
  • status,
  • material comforts.

But the disciple feels:

“I possess something far rarer — I have a Sadguru.”

Therefore:

“What I have makes me the richest person on earth.”

Not because of external possessions.

But because:

I have a Sadguru, His teachings, His blessings and His grace.

11. The Sadguru Becomes the Disciple’s Ray of Hope

Bapa then expresses a very personal devotional feeling.

A disciple may see his own weaknesses and feel:

“I am deeply flawed.”

“I have so much masking.”

“I am unworthy.”

Yet there remains one unshakeable hope:

“I have a compassionate Guru.”

A forgiving Guru.

A benevolent Guru.

Therefore:

No matter how much I fall, that ray of hope remains.

And Bapa beautifully says:

There is now even a limit to how far I can fall — because He will catch me.

The disciple's confidence is not:

“I am strong enough that I will never fall.”

It is:

“He is there.”

12. Now the Disciple Tells His Story

With this devotional background, Verse 121 becomes much more alive.

The disciple essentially says:

“Gurudev, let me tell You what I have understood.”

“As long as I remained in ignorance and delusion, I was the doer and enjoyer of karma.”

“But the moment my attention turned inward, everything changed.”

This is not a dry discussion of karma theory.

It is the disciple describing:

Before You entered my life vs. after Your teachings bore fruit within me.

13. The Story of the Soul Since Time Immemorial

Bapa then explains the entire karmic process in a simple sequence.

Since time immemorial:

Step 1 — I forgot my true nature.

My actual nature is:

  • pure
  • peaceful
  • blissful
  • conscious
  • knowing

But I forgot this.

Step 2 — Because I forgot myself, impure feelings arose.

Such as:

  • attachment
  • aversion
  • likes
  • dislikes
  • love/hate in the deluded sense
  • auspicious and inauspicious feelings

Step 3 — Those impure feelings attracted karmic matter.

The impure inner state became instrumental in material karmas becoming associated with the Soul.

Step 4 — Therefore, I became the doer of karma.

This is the karmic mechanism the disciple had learned earlier and now understands experientially. Pasted text(5)

14. The Chain of Bondage

The sequence can therefore be remembered as:

Forgetfulness of Self

↓

Impure feelings

↓

Attraction/acquisition of karmic matter

↓

Bondage

↓

Doership of karma

↓

Enjoyership/endurance of its fruits

The root is not merely external action.

The deeper root is:

Forgetting who I am.

Once the Self is forgotten, identification shifts outward.

From there:

attachment → aversion → impure feelings → karma.

15. Bhaav Karma and Dravya Karma

Bapa briefly distinguishes two aspects.

Bhaav karma The impure feelings arising within the Soul.

Dravya karma

The material karmic particles acquired through those impure feelings.

Therefore, in the ignorant state, the Soul can be described as the doer of both:

Doer of bhaav karma — impure feelings

and, instrumentally,

Doer of dravya karma — material karmic bondage.

16. Then Something Completely Changes

Now comes Self-realisation.

Bapa wants us to enter the disciple's excitement.

This Soul has been in what he calls a:

“metaphysical slumber”

for lifetimes.

And suddenly:

He wakes up.

The disciple looks back at his entire past and says:

“Gurudev, that was my story until now.”

I lived in ignorance.

I did not know my Self.

I did not have the refuge of the Sadguru.

I remained in impure feelings.

I acquired karma.

I endured karma.

Again and again.

But now:

Sadguru bodh suhay — the teachings of the True Teacher have borne fruit.

17. “It Has Borne Fruit, Gurudev!”

This is one of the most beautiful devotional ways to understand the verse.

The Guru had:

  • taught,
  • explained,
  • answered,
  • corrected,
  • guided,
  • inspired,
  • blessed.

The disciple had listened.

He had contemplated.

He had tried to turn within.

And now finally:

The teaching has become experience.

The disciple essentially says:

“Gurudev, Your teachings have borne fruit.”

The seed planted by the Guru has finally become direct realisation.

18. The Moment the Attention Turns Within

The crucial phrase is:

Vrutti vahi nij bhaavma

Meaning:

My tendency / attentiveness / focus flowed toward my own Self.

Earlier, attention flowed outward toward:

  • body,
  • people,
  • objects,
  • situations,
  • likes,
  • dislikes,
  • karmic manifestations.

Now it turns:

inward.

And the moment it turns inward, the disciple experiences something astonishing:

“I am neither the doer nor the enjoyer of karma.”

19. What Did He Actually Experience?

This is where the lecture becomes especially deep.

The disciple sees karmic manifestations occurring.

Things still happen.

But internally:

He remains aloof.

He sees himself as:

  • spectator
  • knower
  • observer
  • witness

A karmic manifestation may occur.

But he experiences:

“I can know it.”

“I can observe it.”

“But I am not becoming it.”

There is no compulsive:

  • like
  • dislike
  • love
  • hatred
  • reaction

He remains the knower.

20. Knowing the Karma vs. Becoming the Karma

This distinction is essential.

Suppose an unpleasant karmic manifestation arises.

Before Self-realisation:

Something happens → I dislike it → I react → new impure feelings arise → new karma is bound.

After Self-realisation:

Something happens → I know it → I observe it → I remain distinct.

The event is known.

The karmic manifestation is known.

But the Soul does not lose itself in it.

So the disciple says:

“Gurudev, I could know the manifestation of karma, but I did not get affected by it.”

That is why he experiences himself as:

non-enjoyer of karma

and because he does not generate the same impure reaction:

non-doer of karma.

21. The Connection Between Non-Enjoyer and Non-Doer

There is a beautiful circular relationship here.

If a karmic manifestation arises and I become affected:

pleasure → attachment pain → aversion

Then I generate new impurity.

That impurity creates new karmic bondage.

So:

affected by old karma

↓

attachment/aversion

↓

new impure feeling

↓

new karma ↓

new doership

But if I remain the knower:

karmic manifestation arises

↓

I know it

↓

I do not identify with it

↓

no new attachment/aversion

↓

no new karmic doership

So non-enjoyership helps explain non-doership.

22. Doership Does Not Disappear

Bapa again makes the qualification:

Doership is the nature of the Soul.

So Self-realisation does not turn consciousness into an inactive blank.

The distinction is:

Doer of karma

versus

Doer of pure manifestations

Therefore:

“Doer” and “non-doer” are not contradictory when we specify of what.

The ignorant Soul:

doer of karma

The Enlightened Soul:

non-doer of karma, but doer of its pure manifestations

23. What Exactly Is Vibhav?

This is one of the most important technical clarifications in the lecture.

We may casually translate:

Swabhav = nature

and therefore:

Vibhav = opposite to nature

But Bapa says this is not sufficiently accurate here.

Why?

Suppose the nature of the Soul is:

consciousness

What would the literal opposite be?

lifelessness / unconscious matter

But the Soul can never stop being conscious and become lifeless matter.

Therefore, vibhav cannot literally mean:

“The Soul becomes the opposite of its nature.”

That would imply the Soul ceases to be what it fundamentally is, which is impossible within the framework being explained.

24. The Deeper Meaning of Vibhav — Vishesh Bhaav

Bapa gives the deeper meaning attributed to Param Krupalu Dev's explanation:

Vibhav = Vishesh Bhaav

Going beyond one's nature.

The Soul's natural function is:

know and observe.

But instead of simply knowing an event, it goes beyond.

It adds:

“I like this.”

“I dislike this.”

“This should happen.”

“This should not happen.”

“Why did he do this?”

“I want more.”

“I hate this.”

That extra involvement is vibhav.

So vibhav does not mean that consciousness becomes non-consciousness.

It means:

The Soul goes beyond simple knowing and observing and becomes involved through attachment, aversion and identification.

25. The Pav Bhaji Example

Bapa gives a very ordinary example.

Pav bhaji arrives.

The Soul's natural function:

Know it.

See it.

Taste occurs.

Know the taste.

But then the mind goes:

“This is amazing!”

“This is terrible!”

“This is like that restaurant!”

“Why didn't they make it like that?”

“I need more.”

That additional involvement is the movement beyond simple knowing.

Bapa jokingly calls it:

being oversmart.

The teaching is:

Remain the knower and observer.

26. Swabhav vs. Vibhav

A simple way to remember the distinction:

Swabhav Vibhav Knowing Getting involved

Observing Reacting

Witnessing Identifying

Knower “I like / I dislike”

Awareness Attachment-aversion

Remaining within Going beyond

Pure manifestation Impure manifestation

So:

Vibhav is not the destruction of the Soul's nature.

It is the Soul going beyond the simplicity of its knowing nature into involvement.

27. What Self-Realisation Does to Vibhav

With enlightenment, the disciple experiences:

“I could stabilise within myself.”

And from that stability:

  • external objects do not pull him in the same way,
  • external occurrences do not create the same internal reactions,
  • karmic manifestations can occur without the Soul losing itself in them.

Therefore:

“I experienced myself as non-doer and non-enjoyer of karmas.”

28. “The Impurity Is Dead”

Bapa uses a strong phrase.

After Self-realisation, in the state he is describing, an external event may occur but internally there is no corresponding impure response.

He says:

What is dead? Not the Soul — the impurity is dead.

The Soul is intensely alive as consciousness.

What loses its force is the automatic chain of:

object → reaction → attachment/aversion → bondage.

So the Enlightened Soul is not numb.

It is not unconscious.

It is not lifeless.

Rather:

Consciousness remains.

Impure involvement falls away.

29. This Is Exactly What the Guru Taught Earlier

Bapa connects Verse 121 back to Verse 78.

The Guru had already taught:

If the Soul is aware of its true nature, it is the doer of its pure nature.

If it is not aware of its true nature, it becomes the doer of karma.

At that time, the disciple heard it.

Now:

He experiences it.

That is the beauty of this section.

Earlier verses contained the Guru's teaching.

These verses contain the disciple saying:

“Gurudev, what You taught me — I can now see for myself.”

30. Verse 78 vs. Verse 121

This connection is worth making explicit.

Verse 78 — Guru teaches
Awareness of Self → doer of pure nature
Unawareness of Self → doer of karma
Verse 121 — Disciple experiences
“My tendency turned inward → I experienced myself as non-doer of karma.”

So the path moves:

Guru's words

↓

Disciple's faith

↓

Contemplation

↓

Turning inward

↓

Experience

↓ Disciple confirms what Guru had taught

That is the entire Guru-disciple journey being shown in these verses.

31. The Significance of the Word “Vahi” — Flowed

Just as Verse 120 had one word that proved the disciple was speaking from experience, Verse 121 has one too.

Verse 120:
Bhasyu — experienced

That showed:

“I experienced myself as pure consciousness.”

Verse 121:
Vahi — flowed

Not:

“A person's tendency should flow inward.”

Not:

“The scriptures say the tendency flows inward.”

But:

“My tendency flowed inward.”

This is autobiographical.

It carries the touch of direct experience.

Bapa specifically points this out as something worth remembering:

Verse 120 → Bhasyu = experience
Verse 121 → Vahi = experience

32. A Very Important Progression: Verse 111 vs. Verse 121

Bapa makes another subtle distinction.

In Verse 111:

“Vrutti vahey nijbhaavma”

The tendency flows inward.

That was a description of the state of the Enlightened One in general.

But here:

“Vrutti vahi…”

My tendency flowed inward.

The difference is subtle but beautiful.

Verse 111 describes the state.

Verse 121 is the disciple saying:

“That happened to me.”

The teaching has become autobiography.

33. The Third Fundamental Truth Is Now Alive

Originally:

The Soul is the doer.

That can easily remain a memorised philosophical statement.

Bapa even has the disciple essentially admit:

“I had mugged it up.”

I knew:

  • Soul is doer,
  • Soul acquires eight karmas,
  • Soul creates auspicious and inauspicious feelings.

But now he understands something completely different:

Doership depends upon the state of the Soul.

In ignorance:

I am the doer of karma.

In Self-awareness:

I am the doer of my pure manifestation and therefore the non-doer of karma.

This is the experiential Third Fundamental Truth.

34. The Fourth Fundamental Truth Is Also Beginning to Transform

Similarly:

The Soul is the enjoyer/endurer.

Earlier this meant:

I bind karma → karma matures → I experience its fruit.

That remains part of karmic law.

But spiritually, after Self-realisation:

The karmic manifestation may occur without penetrating the inner identity of the Soul.

The Enlightened One knows:

“This is a karmic manifestation.”

But remains:

  • witness
  • observer
  • knower
  • spectator

So Bapa says:

If I am not inwardly affected by it, in that spiritual sense, how can I call myself its enjoyer?

This sets up the next verse's deeper treatment of enjoyership.

35. A Simple Example of the Difference

Imagine someone insults you.

In vibhav:

“He insulted me.”

↓ “How dare he?”

↓ anger

↓ aversion

↓ replaying the incident

↓ new impure feelings

↓ new karma

You were:

enjoyer/endurer → reactor → doer

Now imagine the knower-state described in the lecture:

“An insult occurred.”

↓ “I know the karmic manifestation.”

↓ “I know the body's response.”

↓ “I know the thoughts arising.”

↓ “I remain distinct.”

↓ no compulsive identification

↓ no fresh attachment/aversion

This is movement toward:

non-enjoyer → non-doer of karma

The external event may be identical.

The inner relationship to it is completely different.

36. This Is Why Self-Realisation Changes Karma Without Changing the World

One of the deepest implications of this lecture is:

The world does not necessarily need to change.

The karmic situation may not change.

The body may not change.

Other people may not change.

What changes is:

Where the tendency flows.

Outward:

object → identification → reaction → karma

Inward:

Self → knowing → witnessing → purity

That is why:

Vrutti vahi nij bhaavma

is such an important phrase.

37. The Real Shift Is From Reaction to Knowing

Before:

Something happens → I become it.

After:

Something happens → I know it.

Before:

Anger arises → I am angry.

After:

Anger/modification is known → I am the knower.

Before:

Pain arises → I am suffering.

After:

Pain is known → I remain aware of the Self.

Before:

Praise comes → I become inflated.

After:

Praise is known.

This is not emotional suppression.

According to the lecture, it is a change in identity and attentiveness.

38. “Just a Spectator”

The disciple repeatedly describes himself as:

Spectator
Observer
Knower
Witness

These are important because they express what has happened to doership.

The karmic movie may continue.

The body is still on the screen.

Circumstances continue.

Past karma continues to manifest.

But the Soul no longer jumps into the movie and says:

“This is me.”

It knows.

It observes.

And because it knows without becoming involved:

The old cycle of karma begins to break.

39. The Deepest Cause of Karma Is Therefore Not “Doing Things”

This is a major takeaway.

The lecture does not reduce karma to:

physical activity = karma.

The same person may:

  • eat,
  • speak,
  • walk,
  • work,
  • serve,
  • interact,
  • experience bodily pain.

Yet the spiritual question is:

What is happening within?

Is there:

  • identification?
  • attachment?
  • aversion?
  • impurity?
  • vibhav?

Or:

  • awareness?
  • witnessing?
  • inwardness?
  • stability in the Self?

That inner distinction determines the deeper form of doership being discussed.

When false identification ceases, you are no longer the doer of karma nor its endurer — that is the essence of religion.

42. Connection With Verse 120

Verse 120:

I experienced what I am.

Verse 121:

I experienced what happens when I abide as what I am.

That is a very useful distinction.

Verse 120 — Identity

I am pure consciousness.

Verse 121 — Function

When I remain in that pure consciousness, I cease to be the doer and enjoyer of karma.

So Self-realisation is not merely discovering a metaphysical fact.

It changes the entire mechanism through which the Soul relates to karma.

“Your teachings bore fruit.”

“Your grace awakened me.”

philosophy + experience + devotion.

45. The Most Important Distinction to Remember

Do not write:

“After enlightenment, the Soul becomes a non-doer.”

That is incomplete.

The correct understanding from the lecture is:

After enlightenment, the Soul becomes a non-doer of karma.

The Soul remains active.

It remains the doer of its own pure manifestations.

That is exactly what the next verse develops further.

46. Three Levels of Understanding This Teaching

You can see the progression beautifully:

Level 1 — Memorised knowledge

“The Soul is the doer and enjoyer.”

Level 2 — Intellectual understanding

“The Soul is the doer of karma under ignorance and the doer of pure manifestations in awareness.”

Level 3 — Experience “My tendency turned inward, and I experienced myself as non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.”

The disciple has reached Level 3.

That is why these verses feel so different from the earlier philosophical arguments.

He is no longer asking:

“Is this true?”

He is saying:

“Gurudev, now I see.”

47. Practical Reflection — Catch the Moment of Vibhav

A very practical application from this lecture is to notice the exact point where knowing turns into involvement.

Something happens.

At first:

You simply know it.

Then comes:

“I don't like this.”

“Why did they do this?”

“This shouldn't have happened.”

“What will people think?”

“I need this.”

“I deserve this.”

That transition is worth observing.

Because in the terminology of this lecture:

The problem begins when the Soul goes beyond knowing and starts becoming entangled.

So a useful inner question is:

“Can I know this without becoming it?”

Anger is present.

Can I know anger without becoming “I am angry”?

Pain is present.

Can I know pain without turning it into “Why me?”

Praise is present.

Can I know praise without constructing an identity from it?

An unpleasant person is present.

Can I know their behaviour without immediately producing attachment or aversion?

That points toward the knower-observer orientation Bapa repeatedly emphasizes.

That is Verse 121.

Key Lines to Remember

The Soul is the doer and enjoyer of karma only as long as delusion prevails.

Vrutti vahi nij bhaavma — my tendency flowed toward my own Self.

Non-doer does not mean inactive; it means non-doer of karma.

Doership remains because activity is the nature of the Soul.

In ignorance: doer of karma.

In Self-awareness: doer of pure manifestations.

Vibhav does not mean that the Soul literally becomes opposite to its nature.

Vibhav = going beyond the simplicity of knowing and observing into involvement, attachment and aversion.

The natural position is knower-observer.

“Gurudev, I could know the karmic manifestation, but I was not affected by it.”
Verse 120: Bhasyu — “I experienced.”
Verse 121: Vahi — “My tendency flowed inward.”

Earlier, the Guru taught it. Now the disciple experiences it.

One-Line Essence

Verse 121 is the disciple telling his Sadguru: “Gurudev, I finally experienced what You had taught me — while I lived in delusion, I became entangled in likes and dislikes and therefore remained the doer and enjoyer of karma; but when, through Your grace, my attention turned inward toward my true Self, I remained simply the knower and observer. The karmic manifestations could be known without becoming ‘me,’ and I experienced myself as the non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.”
