He recaps the flow:

  • Verse 34 – Ātmārthī’s conviction about a true Guru:
    • Sadguru must be Self-realised; saintliness flows from Enlightenment.
  • Verse 35 – Ātmārthī’s devotion to a living Sadguru:
    • Pratyaksh Sadguru is a supreme benefit.
    • With mind–speech–body united, he lives in ajna-dhār (Sadguru’s command).
  • Verse 36 – Now:

The ātmārthī’s conviction about the path of liberation itself.

Why is verse 36 placed here?

  • Because:
    • Verse 35 said: “I will follow whatever ajna my living Sadguru gives.”
    • Verse 36 now gives the inner foundation needed to:
      • Understand Sadguru’s ajna,
      • Trust His intent (ashay),
      • Especially when His instructions are:
        • Non-traditional,
        • Non-conventional,
        • Even seemingly “absurd” from a ritual/traditional viewpoint.

In other words:

Verse 36 builds the ātmārthī’s readiness and broadness so that any ajna the Sadguru gives (even if different from “how it’s always done”) can be recognised as a possible means to the one supreme path.

2. The verse: “Ek hoy tran kālma, parmarathno panth…”

He splits it line by line.

Line 1 – “Ek hoy tran kālma, parmarathno panth”

  • Ek hoy – “There is one” / “It is the same.”
  • Tran kālma – in all three periods of time:
    • Past, present, future.
  • Parmarathno panth – the path of:
    • Supreme truth, supreme aim,
    • Mokṣamarg – path to liberation.

So:

“The path of supreme truth / liberation is one and the same in all three periods of time – past, present, and future.”

Implications:

  • It was not one path for Mahavira, another for Śreṇik, another for Param Krupalu Dev, and yet another for us.
  • The essential path is identical for:
    • All Enlightened Ones,
    • All aspirants,
    • All eras.

He then links this with Tattvārth Sūtra, verse 1:

samyag-darśan-jñān-charitrāṇi mokṣamārgaḥ Right faith, right knowledge, right conduct = path of liberation.

So:

  • Mokṣamarg / parmarathno panth = the 3 jewels:

1. Right faith (samyak darśan),

2. Right knowledge (samyak jñān),

3. Right conduct (samyak charitra).

And this does not change.

To make it clearer, he uses four categories:

2.1 The path is one by dravya (substance)

  • Same for all beings:
    • Not one path for Tirthankars, another for other Omniscients.
    • Not one for men and another for women.
  • Example note: “It’s the same path for everyone – the soul’s nature doesn’t differ by gender or role.”

2.2 The path is one by kṣetra (place)

  • Same in all locations:
    • India or America,
    • Bharat Kṣetra or Mahāvideh Kṣetra.
  • You can’t say: “In Mahāvideh it’s different, here it’s different.” The pure path is unchanged by geography.

2.3 The path is one by kāl (time)

  • Same in all eras:
    • 4th era, 5th era, etc.
    • Same for Ṛṣabhadev, Mahavira, Param Krupalu Dev, future Omniscients.
  • This is directly what “ek hoy tran kālma” says.

2.4 The path is one by bhāv (inner feeling)

  • Many external bhāv / expressions exist, but:
    • The essential inner state required for liberation is one:
      • śuddha vītarāg bhāv – pure, attachment-free, aversion-free feeling.
  • You may come to that inner state by:
    • Devotion,
    • Meditation,
    • Study,
    • Service, etc.
  • But:

Ultimately the path = pure, still, vītarāg feeling, however you reach it.

He sums this up beautifully:

The path of liberation does not depend on place or time. It depends on the pure Self. Since the pure Self is one and same, the essential path must also be one and same.

3. Line 2 – “Prere te parmarathne, te vyavhār samant”

Now the crucial second line:

  • Prere te parmarathne – Whatever inspires, propels, leads to that supreme path (parmarathno panth).
  • Te vyavhār samant – That practice / behaviour / conduct is:
    • Right,
    • To be accepted / respected.

He translates the sense:

“Whichever practices lead one to this one supreme path should be accepted (or at least respected).”

This is the explosion point of the verse:

  • Path = one.
  • Means / practices = many.
  • If a practice genuinely:
    • Inspires vītarāg bhāv,
    • Helps still the mind,
    • Leads to right faith–knowledge–conduct, then that practice is vyavhār samant – noble conduct.

He says:

  • The young generation will hear this and say:

“Isn’t this just common sense?”

  • The elderly (he jokingly says “50+”) often struggle:
    • Prejudices about:
      • “This is the only proper way to do ārtī,”
      • “This is how bhakti must be done,”
      • “Only this method is Jain,” etc.

So he teases:

“This verse is mainly for the 50+ people who have insistence and prejudices about rituals and methods.”

Examples:

  • Different styles of:
    • Bhakti,
    • Study,
    • Austerities,
    • Arti (old “Jai Jai Arti” vs new tunes),
  • If these bring you into the same feeling of devotion and vītarāg bhāv:
    • Then they are valid vyavhār.
    • They must be respected, potentially even adopted if helpful to you.

He connects this back to the Guru:

If you digest verse 36, all your “Why? Why like this? Why not the old way?” about Guru’s ajnas will drop.

Because you realise:

  • Your Guru is not serving a tradition.
  • He is serving the parmarathno panth (supreme path).
  • So whatever ajna genuinely leads to that is legitimate, even if:
    • Not in scriptures,
    • Not heard before,
    • Not done before.

He quotes Param Krupalu Dev’s Letter 76 (sense):

A method may not be in the scriptures, may never have been heard before, and yet can be experienced as spiritual truth.

So:

  • Scripture is not a ceiling for methods.
  • Experience of inner transformation is the test.

4. Why this verse became his favourite: the hunt for the “link”

He shares very personally:

  • In the mid-90s, he wrote 40–50 pages on this verse.
  • The meaning of each word was clear.
  • But for almost a month, he couldn’t see:
    • How verse 36 connects with:
      • Verse 35 before it (supreme benefit, surrender), and
      • Verse 37 after it.
  • Without this “flow,” he felt: “I have not yet touched His heart.”

He uses an image:

  • A shower with just dripping drops is not satisfying.
  • You want a steady flow of water for a good bath.
  • Similarly, in Shrimad’s composition:
    • Verses are not random beads,
    • They form a continuous flow of insight.

He says:

  • For three weeks he refused to proceed to verse 37:
    • “Until I see the flow, all these 40–50 pages are useless.”
  • When the link finally became clear:
    • They literally danced.
    • He wanted to celebrate with:
      • Good food,
      • Good music,
      • Both sensory and non-sensory joy.
  • Since then, verse 36 became: “My most favourite verse,” because he had to dig so deeply into Param Krupalu Dev’s heart.

And what is that flow?

  • Verse 35:
    • “I will follow a living Guru’s ajna with all three energies.”
  • Verse 36:
    • “The path is one, and anything that really leads to it is acceptable.”
  • Together:
    • Prepare the ātmārthī to:
      • Accept even very vague, non-traditional ajnas,
      • Not be blocked by ritual habits and tradition blindness.

5. Purpose revealed: making the ātmārthī worthy to follow any ajna

He summarises the purpose of verse 36:

To show the worthiness of a true seeker, whose subtle inner convictions about the path naturally arise and help him:

1. Obey the Sadguru’s ajnas, and

2. Understand the intent (ashay) behind them.

Convictions like:

  • “The path is one, not two.”
  • “Methods may differ; what counts is whether they lead to that one path.”
  • “If an ajna leads me toward:
    • Right faith,
    • Right knowledge,
    • Right conduct,
    • Vītarāg bhāv and stillness, then I must accept it, even if I personally don’t ‘like’ it.”

This conviction unlocks obedience to strange-looking ajnas.

6. His PhD story: An example of a non-traditional ajna

To illustrate, he shares his own life:

  • His natural temperament:
    • Emotional, devotional.
    • Loved bhakti and meditation, not study.
    • He left academics after 10th standard.
    • In the 80s, his identity was:

“Rakeshbhai – the one who makes everyone do long devotional sessions.”

  • But in 1989, on a trip to Nepal:
    • He went longing for “vibrations” of Shrimad’s past life.
    • No particular vibrations in Kathmandu,
    • Sat in meditation and received a clear inner command:
      • In English: “PhD.”

He laughs:

  • At that time:
    • He didn’t even know what “PhD” stood for.
    • No Google then.
    • His family also had limited academic exposure.
  • Gradually, he discovered:
    • PhD = Doctorate of Philosophy,
    • Needs post-graduation, etc.

Timeline:

  • Command received in May 1989.
  • June 18: he enrolled in B.A. (since he wasn’t even 12th pass).
  • Had to complete:
    • 11th, 12th, 3 years of graduation,
    • Then M.A., then M.Phil, then PhD.
  • He studied up to 16 hours a day at times.

Emotionally:

  • Initially:
    • These were the worst days for his heart:
      • He constantly craved bhakti.
      • One devotional song would make him cry: “I’m missing all the joy of life – only study, study…”
  • Slowly:
    • He learned to find devotion within study itself.
    • He understood:
      • For Western audiences,
      • For rational minds,
      • He’d need logical exposition, not just emotion.
  • He interprets: Guru knew: “You will need logic, structure, the ability to explain systematically, without demanding blind faith. So I’ll train you through PhD.”

This is exactly what verse 36 is about:

  • An ajna that:
    • Was not his taste,
    • Was not his temperament,
    • Was non-traditional for a bhakta.
  • But because he knew:
    • Path is one,
    • Anything that truly leads to it is acceptable,
  • He could say: “Whatever is His command, if it leads me to the supreme path, I must follow.”

7. The “antarmukh = absent-minded” misunderstanding

He then moves to a belief-correction example, showing why right convictions are so vital:

  • Nemi had a subtle wrong concept:
    • Antarmukh (turned within) = so absorbed in the Self that:
      • You become absent-minded,
      • Not aware of day, night, surroundings,
      • Careless with worldly dealings.
  • He compares it to the cliché:
    • “Absent-minded professor” who:
      • Forgets what he ate,
      • Forgets if he’s even eaten.

He corrects it strongly:

  • If a person cannot lead himself properly in the world:
    • How could he lead a society, a civilisation?
  • True antarmukhta:
    • Is non-attached, not non-aware.
    • Awareness is:
      • Sharper, more subtle, more pure.

He gives a real incident:

  • Once, a sound came from a window.
  • He asked Maulik:
    • “Where is this sound coming from?”
    • Maulik misidentified the source – simple unawareness.
  • Nemi, jokingly, said:
    • “Oh, Maulikji, you’ve become very antarmukh.”
  • At that, Bapa physically slapped Nemi.

Why so strong?

  • He says:
    • “His fault (Maulik’s) was simple unawareness.”
    • “Your fault (Nemi’s) was much bigger:
      • You misused “antarmukh” to mean stupidity/unawareness.”
  • Danger:
    • If you label carelessness as antarmukhta, subconsciously people will think: “To be spiritual, I must be dull, unaware, impractical.”
    • Then no one will genuinely want antarmukhta.

So he insists:

Never use spiritual words like antarmukh as a taunt. Antarmukhta must mean purity and full awareness, not stupidity.

He stresses:

  • He is not trying to make people scared and stiff.
  • He just wants them cautious, not self-conscious.
  • These subtle conviction-corrections are part of the personal training of an ātmārthī.

And again, this ties back to:

  • Verse 36: Right convictions about the path must be clean and precise, else you won’t want to follow it wholeheartedly.

8. The practical takeaway: Path is one, methods many

He gives a short meaning:

1. “There is only one path” for achieving supreme truth:

  • Not 4–5 separate paths:
    • Knowledge, devotion, meditation, rituals, karma… At the core, the essence is one.

2. One should accept / respect whichever practices:

  • Truly help lead to this one path of liberation.

He clarifies:

  • To “accept” doesn’t mean:
    • You must do exactly what others do.
  • It means:
    • At least respect it if it leads them to vītarāg bhāv and stillness.

He uses down-to-earth analogies:

  • Flatbread vs pizza:
    • Purpose of eating flatbread (bhakrī) = make blood, nourish body.
    • Purpose of eating pizza = same.
    • So: “Mummy, the purpose is blood;
      let me eat pizza in peace.”
  • Similarly:
    • Whether someone:
      • Does bhaav pūjā (mental worship),
      • Dances in kirtan,
      • Sits in padmāsan in silent meditation,
    • If they reach:
      • Forgetfulness of body and world,
      • Attachment-free, aversion-free state,
    • Then the specific method is secondary.

Thus:

Be convinced, not confused. Be open, not insistent. Don’t insist your method is the only one. Don’t criticise others’ methods.

He sums it sharply:

  • There is one path – parmarathno panth.
  • The role of rituals/practices is:
    • Not to be the path,
    • But to lead you to the path.
  • Here you may have variety and choice.
  • There, at the level of inner state (śuddha vītarāg bhāv), there is no choice:
    • It must be pure.

9. Final picture of the ātmārthī from verse 36

So, what is the trait revealed here?

Trait of ātmārthī #3 (after Guru-understanding & devotion): He is deeply convinced that:

  • The path of liberation is one and universal,
  • And any means that genuinely leads to that:
    • Is worth accepting, or at least respecting.

Because of this:

  • He doesn’t get stuck in:
    • Tradition-worship,
    • Ritual insistence,
    • “We’ve always done it this way.”
  • He can fearlessly follow:
    • A living Sadguru’s ajnas,
    • Even if they are:
      • New,
      • Unheard of,
      • Unwritten,
      • Vague to the conventional mind.
  • His inner readiness is such that:
    • Whatever truly leads him to:
      • Right faith,
      • Right knowledge,
      • Right conduct,
      • Śuddha vītarāg bhāv,
    • He embraces it with gratitude.

That is the living spirit of verse 36:

“Ek hoy tran kālma parmarathno panth, prere te parmarathne, te vyavhār samant.”
