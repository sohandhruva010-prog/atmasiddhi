Session Frame & Plan

Series Aim. Continuing Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra Jñān Yajña — to understand the inspiration, composition, and transmission of this timeless text.

Today’s focus: 1. The inspiration behind Atmasiddhi Shastra

2. The composition and occasion of its creation

3. The first worthy receivers

Historical context: In this session, we discover how Saubhagbhai’s devotion, Ambalalbhai’s service, and Lalluji Muni’s discipleship inspired Param Krupalu Dev to compose His magnum opus — Atmasiddhi Shastra — in 1896 (V.S. 1952) at Nadiad.

1) The Inspiration

The inspiration behind the composition of Atmasiddhi Shastra was Shri Saubhagbhai of Sayla, a deeply devoted seeker and spiritual friend of Param Krupalu Dev.

The Humble Request

Saubhagbhai had previously received the Chha Padano Patra (Letter on Six Fundamentals), written in 1894 (V.S. 1950). While he found the letter profoundly enlightening, he struggled to memorise its prose form due to old age.

Out of humility and compassion for other seekers, he requested Param Krupalu Dev:

“If this letter were rendered in verse, it would be easier to memorise and beneficial for all.”

This pure-hearted request became the spark that led to the composition of Atmasiddhi Shastra.

“As a result of Shri Saubhagbhai’s humble request, from Param Krupalu Dev’s supreme inner purity manifested Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra — an invaluable jewel-like text incorporating the six fundamentals in poetic form.”

A Tradition of Compassionate Composition

Throughout history, Enlightened Masters have composed scriptures at the requests of others: - Acharya Nemichandra composed Gommatsaar and Labdhisaar for Chamundraya. - Acharya Hemchandrasuri wrote Yogshastra and Vitragstav for King Kumarpal. - Upadhyaya Yashovijayji and Gani Devchandraji composed several texts through similar inspiration. - Even Param Krupalu Dev, at age 16, wrote Mokshmala for Shri Popatbhai Daftary, to make spiritual truth accessible to all ages.

Thus, Atmasiddhi Shastra stands in this sacred lineage — a scripture born from a devotee’s devotion and a Master’s compassion.

2) The Background — “Chha Padano Patra”

Before Atmasiddhi Shastra, in Surat (V.S. 1950), Shri Lalluji Muni was suffering from a long illness. Believing his body might not survive, he wrote several letters to Param Krupalu Dev, pleading for Self-realisation before death.

Moved by His disciple’s sincerity, Param Krupalu Dev replied with the Chha Padano Patra (Letter on Six Fundamentals – letter no. 493). This letter explained the six truths of the soul and served as the foundation for the Atmasiddhi Shastra.

He instructed:

“Memorise and reflect upon it again and again.”

Later, the same letter was sent to Saubhagbhai for memorisation and contemplation.

Vartman aa kaalma, mokshmarg bahu lop;
Vicharva atmarthine, bhakhyo atra agopya. In this era, the path of liberation has nearly disappeared. For the true seeker, it is revealed here without concealment. — Atmasiddhi, Verse 2

This verse mirrors the essence of that letter — the revival of the path of liberation for genuine seekers.

3) The Composition and Occasion

Setting the Scene

In Shravan month (V.S. 1952), Param Krupalu Dev travelled through Kavitha, Ralaj, and other villages during His retreat, remaining absorbed in the Self. Later, through Anand, He reached Nadiad in the month of Aso.

Ambalalbhai, a devoted disciple, was serving Him during this period.

The Evening of Divine Outpouring

On Aso Vad Ekam, the first day of the waning moon, the day after Sharad Purnima — Param Krupalu Dev returned from a walk in the evening. He asked Ambalalbhai to bring a lantern, and began writing.

“With devout humility, Ambalalbhai stood holding the lantern like a lamp post, while Param Krupalu Dev’s pen flowed ceaselessly.”

In a single sitting of about 1½ to 2 hours, He composed the entire 142-verse Atmasiddhi Shastra — each verse compact, profound, and radiant with truth.

This moment, witnessed only by Ambalalbhai, marked the descent of a spiritual Ganga — the eternal wisdom of the Self flowing from the heart of the Enlightened One.

The Place and Date

  • Date: Aso Vad Ekam, V.S. 1952 → 22 October 1896 (Thursday)
  • Place: A room within Nana Kumbhnath Mahadev Temple, Nadiad, Charotar District, Gujarat.

“The scriptural Ganges descended on earth from the heart of the Self-realised Master in an uninterrupted flow to purify the world.”

4) Symbolism of the Occasion

Like a raindrop falling into an oyster during the Swati constellation, turning into a pearl, the request of Saubhagbhai, combined with the auspicious moment and the Master’s compassion, produced the pearl of Atmasiddhi Shastra.

This text contains the quintessence of the 14 Purvas (ancient Jain scriptures) — the distilled essence of truth expressed in simple, heartfelt Gujarati verse.

5) The Worthy Receivers

Atmasiddhi Shastra is not an ordinary text. It demands spiritual eligibility. Param Krupalu Dev gave only four copies, handwritten by Ambalalbhai, to those He deemed worthy to study and reflect upon it.

The Four Worthy Receivers

ReceiverLocationRelationship

Shri SaubhagbhaiSaylaDevotee whose request inspired the Shastra

Shri Lalluji Muni (Laghuraj Swami)KhambhatMonk, seeker of Self-realisation

Shri Maneklal Ghelabhai JhaveriRangoonBusiness associate, devotee

Shri AmbalalbhaiKhambhatDisciple and scribe present during composition

Each copy was given with instruction not to publicise or share the scripture with those unready for its depth. Param Krupalu Dev’s reason: the unprepared mind might misunderstand and commit irreverence, causing spiritual harm.

“As an Ayurvedic doctor does not give diamond powder to the unworthy, so the Master shared this jewel only with the qualified.”

Even devotees close to Him were denied copies. The text was never published during His lifetime. Only through these four copies did it quietly reach sincere seekers.

Letters of Instruction

In a letter (Aso Vad Dasam, V.S. 1952) accompanying the copy to Lalluji Muni, Param Krupalu Dev wrote:

“I am sending herewith Atmasiddhi Shastra to reflect upon in solitude. It should be reflected upon by Lalluji at present. If Shri Devkaranji has an intense desire, he may reflect upon it only in Lalluji’s company, with the resolve that no one has been as benevolent to me as the living Master.”

Such precision and compassion in instruction show His vigilance to protect both the sanctity of the text and the seekers’ spiritual safety.

6) The Passing of Saubhagbhai and Preservation of the Text

After Saubhagbhai’s passing (V.S. 1953), his sons requested permission to study Atmasiddhi Shastra, but were not granted it. Param Krupalu Dev ensured that the text remained with the worthy only. Even Ambalalbhai handled it with utmost caution.

Over time, its limited circulation prevented misunderstanding and preserved its spiritual purity until the world was ready.

“His care was not secrecy but compassion — to prevent irreverence and protect seekers from harm.”

7) The Fruit of Worthy Study

The four original receivers studied the Shastra and experienced transformative results: - Saubhagbhai, Lalluji Muni, and Ambalalbhai attained Samyak Darshan (Self-realisation).

  • Maneklal Ghelabhai too was deeply benefited and inspired.

Thus, those who studied Atmasiddhi Shastra with reverence attained the first awakening of right faith and direct experience of the Self.

Je swaroop samjya vina, pamyo dukh anant;
Samjavyu te pada namun, Shri Sadguru Bhagwant. Without understanding the true Self, one suffers endlessly; I bow to the Enlightened Master who revealed that truth. — Atmasiddhi, Verse 1

8) Lessons from Param Krupalu Dev’s Vigilance

  • Eligibility matters in spirituality.
  • Profound truths require inner preparedness to be truly grasped.
  • The Guru’s restrictions are expressions of compassion, not exclusivity.
  • True seekers are those who protect purity, not those who demand access.

Reflection Prompts

1. What inspires me about Saubhagbhai’s humility and Param Krupalu Dev’s compassion?

2. How does this story redefine my understanding of eligibility in spirituality?

3. What does divine vigilance teach me about respecting sacred knowledge?

4. How can I approach Atmasiddhi Shastra with the same devotion and responsibility as the worthy receivers?

5. What are my current “lantern moments” — small acts of service that keep the Master’s light shining?
