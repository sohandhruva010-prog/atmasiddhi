What the disciple has already accepted (by Guru’s grace)

He admits a firm conviction that:

  • The soul is the doer (karta) of karmas
  • The soul is the receiver/endurer (bhokta) of karma-fruits

Why a doubt about liberation arises (the link with “eternity”)

The disciple’s thinking goes back to Truth #2:

  • The soul is beginningless and endless (eternal)

So he reasons:

  • If the soul is eternal → the cycle of bondage + fruit + reaction + new bondage feels:
    • beginningless (no start)
    • and therefore endless (no end)

That’s why, to him:

  • A karmaless state is never seen at any point in time.
  • “Liberation” (dictionary/philosophical sense) = absolute freedom from karmas/flaws
  • Therefore he says: “I can’t even visualize liberation.”

2) Quick recap of the disciple’s two arguments (87 & 88)

Verse 87 (first argument)

  • Infinite time has passed, and yet impurity still continues (“vartaman chhe dosh” — the causes of bondage still exist)
  • Therefore the karta–bhokta cycle seems eternal → moksh seems impossible.

Verse 88 (second argument)

  • There’s a choice only between:
    • good (shubh / meritorious) karmas → “heaven etc.”
    • bad/evil (ashubh) karmas → “hell etc.”
  • In all four gatis (human, animals/plants, heavenly, infernal), he never sees a karmaless soul.
  • Since moksh = karmaless state, he concludes: “there is no moksh.”

3) A “detour” the speaker takes here (still important)

He brings up a practical question that often confuses people:

3.1 “Supreme state of existence” — which gati is supreme?

He asks:

  • Among the four worldly gatis (not counting moksh yet), which is “supreme”?

He connects it to Param Krupalu Dev’s final statement about someone:

  • “This soul will certainly attain the supreme state.”
  • Also: “Your brother’s death will be in samadhi… and he will attain the supreme state.”

Two answers depending on viewpoint (very important distinction)

  • Worldly (comfort) viewpoint: Heavenly/celestial birth is supreme because it is described as least suffering / most comfort.
  • Spiritual (liberation-effort) viewpoint: Human birth is supreme because it is most suitable for moksh effort (right understanding + practice).

He adds details (as he remembers them):

  • Human life is a package of comforts + discomforts
    • Example: even small discomfort like constipation becomes a repeated complaint.
  • Scriptural generalization he quotes:
    • Souls have gone twice as much time to heaven than hell
    • Human birth has occurred the least number of times
    • Births as plants/animals are innumerable

3.2 Seeing heavenly beings in earlier eras (a vivid example)

He says:

  • In the 4th era, celestial beings and their “planes” could be seen.
  • Not in the 5th era (our time).
  • He compares it with modern “air taxis” beginning now.

Why this matters: it supports why the disciple speaks from what is “seen/known,” and why there’s uncertainty about gati-based conclusions.

4) He reframes the topic: “Liberation is a state, not a place”

He explicitly says he prefers not saying “existence of liberation” like a geography. Instead:

  • Liberation = a state of the soul:
    • No dravya karma (no bound karmic matter)
    • No bhaavkarma (no impure feelings)
    • In short: karmaless state

So the disciple’s conclusion is stronger than “not attained yet”:

  • He concludes: “There is no liberation.”

5) Why the disciple is stuck: influence of many philosophies

He revisits (briefly) the “many views” the disciple is exposed to:

Examples of “ultimate goal” concepts (as stated here):

  • Some: Heaven is the ultimate goal
  • Some: Proximity with God is liberation (no separation/obstruction)
  • Some: “Merging” / loss of individuality (various Vedanta interpretations)
  • Buddhism (as he states here): extinction of the soul
  • Jain view (hinted here, will be established later): individuality remains even in liberation

He says the disciple:

  • sees “something right” in some angles
  • but can’t reconcile/integrate everything
  • so he humbly requests Guru to clarify.

6) The roadmap of solutions (very clear)

He states:

  • The Guru clarifies in 3 verses (89, 90, 91)
  • Specifically:
    • 89 & 90 answer the first argument (verse 87)
    • The third verse answers the second argument (verse 88)

And he emphasizes:

  • This video = Verse 89
  • Verse 89: proves existence/possibility of liberation
  • Verse 90: explains why liberation hasn’t happened yet and what to do (a glimpse of moksh-upay)

7) Verse 89 — the Guru’s statement (core of the video)

7.1 The verse (as explained)

“Jem shubhashubh karmapada, janya safal praman;
Tem nivrutti safalta; matey moksh sujaan.”

He unpacks it in a clean logic:

(A) “Jem…” — Just as:

  • Shubh-ashubh karmapada = auspicious & inauspicious karmas/activities/feelings
  • Janya safal praman = you have understood with sufficient proofs that they are “successful/fruitful,” i.e., they definitely produce results.

He reminds: you were convinced using many proofs and examples, e.g.

  • “Ek raank ne ek nrup” (beggar vs king)
  • “Poison and nectar don’t know their effects, but effects happen”
  • and other earlier examples from the karma section

(B) “Tem…” — Similarly:

  • Nivrutti (cessation/withdrawal from shubh-ashubh) is also safal (fruitful).

(C) “Matey moksh” — Therefore:

  • Because withdrawal/cessation is also fruitful,
  • Liberation exists / is possible.

(D) “Sujaan” — the Guru’s tone

He notes the Guru’s affectionate address:

  • calling the disciple “Sujaan” = wise/intelligent/knowledgeable
  • or “su-jaan” can be read as: “understand properly.”

8) Key concept: “Every activity is fruitful” — and nivrutti is also an activity

This is the Guru’s clever move (as the speaker emphasizes):

  • Earlier (3rd & 4th Truths), you learned: doing shubh/ashubh leads to fruits.
  • Now the Guru extends the same principle: withdrawing / ceasing is also a “verb” (an action) → it also has a fruit.

He explicitly says:

  • “Retiring is also activity… it’s a verb, ‘-ing’.”
  • “Abiding in the soul is also activity.” So:
  • The indulging activity had results, the withdrawing activity also has results.

This is how the Guru proves moksh without needing a long metaphysical explanation in this verse.

9) The famous “Nivrutti” list (he stresses this hard)

He insists nivrutti should be understood with multiple words. He gives five (and says these will be asked in quiz):

Nivrutti =

1. retiring

2. refraining

3. retreating

4. withdrawing

5. eliminating (He repeatedly says: write all five.)

He adds a caution:

  • Don’t answer like a parrot (“1, 2, 3…” mechanically).
  • Answer from understanding.

10) Very important clarification: Moksh is NOT a “karma-fruit”

He makes this crystal clear:

  • He is not saying:
    • “a special kind of karma gives moksh”
    • like punya gives heaven, paap gives hell, and “moksha-karma” gives moksh

Instead:

  • Moksh is the result of elimination of karmas, not the product of a karma.

Why he still uses “fruitful” language:

  • Because even withdrawing has a consequence.
  • That consequence culminates as liberation.

11) Why the verse uses “shubhashubh” (not just “ashubh”)

He says he’ll explain more later, but he gives the key reason now:

Society’s mistaken belief

  • Many people believe: punya (merit) leads to moksh.
  • Because of this myth, Param Krupalu Dev uses shubhashubh explicitly.

The sharp teaching coming next

He foreshadows:

  • In the next verse (90), he will speak in the language of cutting/destroying both shubh and ashubh (he hints: “te shubhashubh chhedta…”).

Dharma vs Punya (big distinction he introduces)

He says:

  • Be excited for dharma, not for “collecting punya.”
  • Both can look “good,” but they’re not the same.
His example:
  • Charity feeling = “good”
  • Abiding as Witness / in true Self = also “good” But:
  • punya-good ≠ dharma-good One binds (as good karma), the other leads toward freedom.

12) The one-line mapping you should remember

He gives an ultra-clean summary:

  • Shubhashubh (engaging in good/bad) → Transmigration (birth–rebirth)
  • Nivrutti (withdrawal from both) → Liberation

He even says:

  • “What do you get in one word from shubhashubh? Transmigration.”
  • “What do you get in one word from nivrutti? Liberation.”

13) Practical meaning of “real nivrutti” (very applied teaching)

He warns against a common misuse:

Wrong meaning people take

  • Nivrutti = quitting worldly duties (like retiring from office) He says:
  • Fine, do it if helpful…
  • but it is not guaranteed to lead to liberation (it may even lead to laziness).

Right meaning (as Sadguru teaches)

  • Retire from:
    • ego
    • expectations
    • attachments
    • aversions This is the nivrutti that leads to moksh.

14) He starts bridging to the “method” (without fully teaching it yet)

He says:

  • As long as you have mind, speech, body, activity is inevitable.
  • You can’t escape activity. But:
  • There is a manner to do activity:
    • without ego
    • without expectations
    • without attachment-desire

Key line:

  • “Activities are there, attachment is not there.”

This is the direction the Sixth Fundamental Truth will expand (moksh-upay).

15) Examples used to deepen the karmic interpretation (important for equanimity)

15.1 “You only receive what you have done”

He says:

  • Soul receives fruits of its own karmas only, not “someone else’s decision.”
Example (very direct):
  • If someone slaps you, it can only happen if your karma is operative.
  • The other person isn’t “free” to harm you outside your karma.
  • Seeing life this way supports equanimity.

15.2 Honeymoon / “seven births” example (humorous but sharp)

He says:

  • People say during romance: “For seven births, may I get only you.”
  • He calls that an “ignorant state.”
  • Later you realize:
    • marriage adds responsibilities, expectations, duty
    • you “gave up your freedom” (not a children’s ring game)
    • you don’t have a choice now; it becomes your dharma/duty

Purpose:

  • To show how desire itself programs future bondage, even when it feels sweet.

16) “Sujaan” and the idea of gradual progress (not instant moksh)

He explains a subtle point using the verse’s “jem jem / as” style:

  • Liberation won’t happen instantly after understanding.
  • Because the soul has lived in mithyatva (delusion) since infinite time.

Habit example

He compares it to changing a long habit:

  • Even 30–35 years’ habit takes time to change → so infinite-time conditioning won’t vanish instantly.

The “causes of bondage” are removed gradually

He lists a sequence (very important):

1. Mithyatva — delusion (wrong belief)

2. Avirati — absence of restraint

3. Pramad — indolence/carelessness

4. Kashay — passions

5. Yoga activity — mind/body/speech activity (as cause for bondage in the technical chain)

As these causes reduce:

  • involvement in shubhashubh becomes less and less
  • progress becomes steady.

“Partial liberation” concept

He gives a very practical framing:

  • Don’t think “liberation” only means reaching Siddhashila immediately.
  • First comes partial freedom, e.g. freedom from delusion.

He explains why saints may be called “muktatma” in a partial sense:

  • free from delusion (even if body remains)

Technical stages (he briefly touches)

  • Passionless state: 13th gunasthanak
  • Karmaless state: end of 14th gunasthanak So moksh is:
  • complete/passionless and finally karmaless — but progression is gradual.

17) Guru’s role (another strong detour, but meaningful)

He says:

  • Guru is not just for information.
  • If it were only knowledge, one session would be enough (like instrument lessons: once basics are learned, teacher isn’t needed).

But here:

  • You need Guru till absolute liberation:
    • for inspiration
    • for strengthening awareness
    • for daily grace/blessings

He gives a very “real-world” inner process:

  • Guru shows your flaws:
    • first general, then personal hints
  • you first defend
  • then you see your contribution (even 0.01%)
  • you feel humility (“going down”)
  • then Guru lifts you back with enthusiasm

He adds a striking image:

  • “Catching your ears” (a gesture of humility) kills ego “in kgs.”

18) The final distilled meaning of Verse 89 (what you should carry)

What the Guru proves here

  • You already accepted: doing (shubh/ashubh) produces results.
  • Therefore accept: withdrawing/cessation (nivrutti) also produces a result.
  • That result is moksh.

One-line essence

If engagement in shubhashubh creates transmigration, then nivrutti from shubhashubh makes liberation possible.
