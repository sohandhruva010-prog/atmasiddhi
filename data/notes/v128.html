This is the first actual verse of the Upsamhar — Verse 128. After the introduction to Section 12, Param Krupalu Dev begins the conclusion by looking back at the Six Fundamental Truths and the different philosophical positions that appeared throughout the Guru–disciple dialogue.

But Gurudev's main point is that this verse is much more than a recap. Hidden inside it is a method for how a spiritual seeker should relate to different viewpoints:

Don't reject. Don't blindly accept. Understand the viewpoint from which something is correct.

That is the spirit of Anekantvad — multiplicity of viewpoints / non-absolutism.

And if we contemplate the Six Fundamental Truths properly in this way, the result is:

“Sanshay rahey na kai” — no doubt remains.

That doubtless conviction ultimately becomes Samkit — Right Faith. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 128: The Six Philosophies Within the Six Fundamental Truths

The verse essentially says:

All six schools of philosophy are encompassed within these Six Fundamental Truths. If they are contemplated deeply and in detail, no doubt remains. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

1. Gurudev first takes us BACK to verses 42 and 44

To understand verse 128, Gurudev says we need to remember something Param Krupalu Dev promised much earlier.

Verse 42

Param Krupalu Dev said He would explain the Six Fundamental Truths through a dialogue between Guru and disciple, so that the path to liberation could be understood.

In other words:

Guru–disciple dialogue → Six Fundamental Truths → understanding Moksh Marg.

Verse 44

Then Param Krupalu Dev said that within these Six Fundamental Truths are encompassed the purposeful spiritual discussions of the six major schools of Indian philosophy.

So Gurudev links:

Verse 42 → the format was announced.

Verse 44 → the subject was announced.

Then verses 45 onward actually fulfilled that promise.

Now, in verse 128, Param Krupalu Dev looks back and essentially says:

What I promised you—I have now done. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

2. What are the Six Fundamental Truths again?

The Six Fundamental Truths we've studied are:

1. The soul exists.

2. The soul is eternal.

3. The soul is the doer.

4. The soul is the enjoyer of the fruits.

5. Liberation exists.

6. There is a path to liberation.

Throughout the dialogue, the disciple raised different doubts and objections regarding each one.

And through answering those doubts, Param Krupalu Dev was simultaneously addressing the major philosophical positions found across Indian thought. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

3. Why did so many philosophies arise in the first place?

Gurudev then zooms out.

He says the root problem of human suffering is ignorance of the Self.

Because I don't understand who I actually am:

Ignorance of Self ↓

Desires

↓

Wanting things

↓

Desires aren't fulfilled

↓

Agitation / misery

So philosophers across different:

  • times,
  • places,
  • traditions,

all tried to answer essentially the same question:

Who am I really, and how do I remove ignorance of the Self?

Different answers produced different philosophical systems. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

4. The six major philosophical categories

Gurudev says the major Indian philosophical traditions can broadly be categorized into six groups.

Three non-Vedic traditions

1. Charvak

2. Jainism

3. Buddhism

Three Vedic groupings

4. Nyaya–Vaisheshika

5. Sankhya–Yoga

6. Purva Mimamsa–Uttar Mimamsa Gurudev deliberately doesn't go into their detailed histories or individual doctrines here because that's not the purpose of this verse.

His point is simply:

These traditions contain an enormous variety of sophisticated philosophical positions.

Studying each independently could take an extraordinary amount of time. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

5. Param Krupalu Dev compresses all of that into SIX truths

This is what amazes Gurudev.

These philosophical systems are:

  • diverse,
  • complex,
  • extremely deep.

Yet Param Krupalu Dev manages to encompass their relevant spiritual questions within:

Six Fundamental Truths.

For example:

Is the soul eternal or non-eternal?

Is there one soul or many?

Is the soul the doer or non-doer?

Is liberation possible?

What causes liberation?

These philosophical positions appeared throughout the disciple's objections.

And Sadguru answered them while explaining the Six Fundamental Truths. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

6. But Gurudev says CIRCLE one word: “SAMAY”

This is probably the most important part of the entire satsang.

The verse doesn't say:

The Six Fundamental Truths REJECT the six philosophies.

It says:

“Samay chhe” — they are INCLUDED / encompassed.

Gurudev says:

Circle the word “Samay.” Don't even merely underline it.

Why?

Because that one word reveals the mindset of the Enlightened One. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

7. Param Krupalu Dev is NOT saying: “Everyone else is wrong; Jainism wins.”

Gurudev explicitly warns us against leaving with that interpretation.

Don't think:

“Param Krupalu Dev destroyed all the other philosophies and raised the flag of Jainism.”

If that's what we understand, Gurudev says we have missed His intent.

Param Krupalu Dev isn't saying:

Rejected.

He's saying:

Included.

And Gurudev says:

“Enlightened Ones are all accommodative. We are not. We want to be unique.” Pasted text(20261109-181335)

That's a very important distinction.

8. The danger is ONE-SIDEDNESS

Different philosophies may emphasize:

The soul is eternal.

or:

The soul is non-eternal.

One soul.

Many souls.

Doer.

Non-doer.

And Gurudev says the problem arises when someone takes a particular viewpoint and says:

“ONLY this.”

That only creates one-sidedness.

Once someone becomes fixed in a one-sided belief, their understanding becomes flawed.

Then when they explain the spiritual path, their exposition of the path also becomes flawed. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

This directly connects with the introduction to the Upsamhar:

The danger isn't always complete falsehood.

Sometimes the danger is:

Taking one aspect of truth and turning it into the whole truth.

9. Param Krupalu Dev does something incredibly skillful

Throughout Atmasiddhi, there were many philosophical objections.

Gurudev says there were roughly 17 different doubts/arguments across the Six Fundamental Truths.

But Param Krupalu Dev doesn't say:

“This is Buddhism's mistake.”

“This is Vedanta's mistake.”

“This is Sankhya's mistake.”

Instead:

He puts all the one-sided positions into the mouth of the disciple.

Then:

The Guru gives the supreme truth.

So the structure becomes:

Disciple → expresses the various doubts / one-sided positions
Guru → resolves them through the complete viewpoint

No philosophy needs to be attacked by name. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

10. Why doesn't Param Krupalu Dev NAME the philosophies?

This becomes a beautiful teaching from Gurudev.

Param Krupalu Dev could easily have written:

“This philosophy is wrong.”

“That tradition is mistaken.”

But He doesn't.

Gurudev says:

If He doesn't even want to NAME them unnecessarily, would He want us to CONDEMN them?

No.

Because naming and attacking another tradition could create:

  • dislike,
  • negativity,
  • prejudice,
  • aversion.

And those are themselves spiritually harmful.

Instead, Param Krupalu Dev simply states the truth without condemning anyone. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

11. Gurudev's advertising example 😂

Gurudev compares this with advertisements.

To sell your detergent, you show:

Their detergent:

dirty, dull clothing.

Our detergent:

perfectly bright white clothing.

Sometimes the comparison itself is unfair—the person advertising their own product may even be wearing a brand-new white sari while the “competitor” gets an old one 😂.

The point:

Marketing often requires making the OTHER product look bad so YOUR product looks better.

But Param Krupalu Dev doesn't do that.

He doesn't need to say:

“Look how terrible their philosophy is.”

to establish:

“This is the truth.”

Truth can stand on its own. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

12. But being accommodative does NOT mean saying everyone is equally right This distinction is crucial.

Gurudev doesn't want us to misunderstand “acceptance” as:

“Everything is true!”

“Everyone is correct!”

“Whatever you believe is fine!”

He jokingly calls that making a:

“fruit salad.”

That's not Anekantvad.

That's confusion.

Param Krupalu Dev is very clear about what He has experienced as truth.

But He doesn't need hatred or condemnation to express it.

So:

Don't compromise TRUTH to avoid dislike.

But also:

Don't create DISLIKE while expressing truth. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

That balance is one of the deepest points in this satsang.

13. Gurudev connects this to the three internal knots

Remember from the previous sections:

Attachment + Aversion + Ignorance

are the fundamental knots of karma.

Now apply that here.

One extreme:

“Everyone is right! Everything is true!”

Why?

Perhaps because I don't want conflict.

But then I may compromise the truth.

That's ignorance.

Other extreme:

“They're wrong! Reject them!”

Now I may preserve my intellectual position…

but generate:

  • attachment to my view,
  • aversion toward theirs.

That's raag-dwesh.

The Enlightened One has neither.

No ignorance.

No likes/dislikes.

He knows truth clearly without needing to hate the person holding another viewpoint. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

14. Gurudev's practical rule: replace “REJECT” with “CORRECT”

Gurudev gives a fantastic practical technique:

Don't say “reject.”
Say “correct—from this viewpoint.”

Instead of:

“You're wrong.”

Try understanding:

“From which viewpoint is what you're saying correct?”

Then clearly state that viewpoint.

This doesn't mean falsely flattering them.

It means identifying the partial truth contained within their position without mistaking that partial truth for the whole truth.

Gurudev says:

“Take a vow to use correct instead of reject.” Pasted text(20261109-181335)

I think this is probably the main practical takeaway of the satsang.

15. Neither REJECT nor FLATTER

Gurudev says both extremes are easy.

Reject:

“You're completely wrong.”

Easy.

Flatter:

“You're fantastic! You're completely right!”

Also easy.

The difficult spiritual position is:

Neither reject nor flatter.

Remain so firmly rooted in truth that you can say:

“From this particular viewpoint, this is correct.”

while remaining clear about the complete truth.

That requires:

clarity + non-attachment + absence of aversion. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

16. This is Anekantvad

Gurudev then gives the philosophical name:

Anekantvad

He translates it more accessibly as:

Theory of multiplicity of viewpoints.

Rather than absolutizing one perspective:

“This alone is true.”

Anekantvad recognizes that reality can be understood through multiple valid viewpoints, while maintaining clarity about the context and limits of each viewpoint. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

17. The OCEAN and RIVERS example

This is Gurudev's central example for Anekantvad.

Imagine many rivers:

  • one coming from one direction,
  • another from somewhere else,
  • another following another path.

Eventually:

All empty into the ocean.

The ocean doesn't reject the rivers.

It absorbs them.

Gurudev says:

River = one-sided viewpoint.

Ocean = supreme truth.

The supreme truth can absorb the various one-sided positions by understanding:

From which viewpoint is this statement valid?

So Anekantvad doesn't destroy partial viewpoints.

It gives them their correct place within a larger truth. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

18. This is why “Samay” matters so much

Now Gurudev's obsession with that one word makes sense.

Samay = included.

The ocean doesn't say:

“River, you don't exist.”

It says:

“Come. I understand where you fit.”

Likewise, Param Krupalu Dev doesn't need to destroy different philosophical viewpoints.

The Six Fundamental Truths are broad enough to encompass them by assigning each its appropriate viewpoint.

That's why Gurudev sees this verse not merely as philosophy but as a methodology for living.

19. Gurudev says: “This is MY methodology”

This part becomes personal.

Gurudev says this principle has touched him deeply.

He doesn't claim he practices it perfectly.

But he says:

This is my ideal.

This is what my mindset should be.

This is my methodology.

Meaning:

Clear about truth.

No compromise due to ignorance.

No attachment to “my” position.

No aversion toward someone else's position.

That's the balance he wants to cultivate. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

20. “Sanshay rahey na kai” — NO doubt remains

Now Gurudev comes to the second major part of the verse.

Param Krupalu Dev says:

If you contemplate these Six Fundamental Truths deeply and in detail, no doubt remains.

Important:

Not:

Memorize them.

But:

Contemplate them.

Gurudev emphasizes that simply remembering:

soul exists, soul is eternal, soul is doer…

isn't enough.

Think deeply.

Contemplate peacefully.

Understand them through their proper viewpoints.

Then doubts regarding the nature of the soul begin to disappear. Pasted text(20261109-181335) Pasted text(20261109-181335)

21. Doubtless faith = SAMKIT

Then Gurudev asks:

What do we call faith without doubt?

Answer:

Samkit — Right Faith.

So Gurudev says:

“This is the verse for Right Faith.”

When there is no doubt regarding:

  • God,
  • Guru,
  • the Six Fundamental Truths explained by God and Guru,
  • the true nature of the Self,

faith becomes doubtless.

And that is Samkit / Right Faith. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

This makes verse 128 much deeper than it initially appears.

22. Why verse 128 is NOT simply verse 44 repeated

At first glance, you could say:

Verse 44 already told us that the Six Fundamental Truths encompass the six philosophies. Why repeat it?

Gurudev says:

No.

Verse 44 was primarily factual.

It announced:

These philosophical discussions are contained within the Six Fundamental Truths.

But verse 128 is in the Upsamhar.

Now Param Krupalu Dev is giving us a technique:

Learn to be inclusive without losing clarity.

Understand viewpoints rather than rejecting them.

Contemplate deeply until doubt disappears.

So verse 128 isn't merely saying:

“Six philosophies are included.”

It's teaching:

how an Enlightened One relates to differing viewpoints. Pasted text(20261109-181335)
The deepest message of Verse 128

I think Gurudev is giving us two enormous teachings from what initially looks like a simple connecting verse.

First: How to relate to other viewpoints

Don't approach spirituality with:

“Mine is right, yours is wrong.”

But also don't become vague:

“Everything is equally true.”

Instead:

What viewpoint makes this statement correct?

Understand it.

Accept the truth contained within it.

Place it correctly.

But don't absolutize it.

That's:

Anekantvad.

And Gurudev's practical formula is beautifully simple:

CORRECT, not REJECT.

Second: What should happen to MY faith?

After studying all these different philosophical possibilities, I shouldn't become more confused.

If I have properly contemplated the Six Fundamental Truths:

doubts decrease

↓

conviction increases

↓

faith becomes doubtless

↓

Samkit.

So interestingly, being open to multiple viewpoints does not mean becoming uncertain about everything.

Gurudev presents almost the opposite:

True openness + proper contemplation → stronger conviction.

Because now my faith isn't:

“I believe this because somebody told me.”

I've seen the alternatives.

I've understood their viewpoints.

I've contemplated them.

I've understood where each fits.

And therefore:

“Sanshay rahey na kai.”

No doubt remains.

How this fits the Upsamhar

This is a perfect first verse for Section 12 because the introduction warned us:

Verse 128 begins training the opposite mindset:

And I think Gurudev's river–ocean analogy captures it perfectly:

A one-sided view is like a river.

The Enlightened One's vision is the ocean.

It has the capacity to receive all the rivers without itself becoming limited to any one river. Pasted text(20261109-181335)

That is the mindset Param Krupalu Dev begins the Upsamhar by teaching us.
