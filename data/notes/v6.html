Śrī Atmasiddhi Śāstra — Verse 6 Detachment, Etc. (Vairāgyādi) — Purpose & Place on the Path

Verse (Gujarati & Transliteration)

Vairāgyādi safal to, jo sah ātam-jñān;
Tem j ātam-jñān-nī, prāptī tanā nidān. 6

Transliteration: Vairāgyādi safal to, jo sah ātam-jñān;
Tem j ātam-jñānni, prāptī tanā nidān.

Word-to-Word Meaning
  • Vairāgyādi — detachment etc. (all noble auxiliaries: renunciation, vows, austerities, charity, compassion, devotion, worship, humility, reverence…) - Safal to — are fruitful/meaningful only if - Jo sah ātam-jñān — accompanied with Self-realisation (experiential knowledge of the soul) - Tem j — and / or (both readings are used; intent covers both) - Ātam-jñān-nī prāptī tanā nidān — the sole reason/goal (nidān) is attainment of Self-realisation

Short Meaning Detachment and other pure feelings are fruitful if together with Self-realisation; and / or they are the means to Self-realisation when practised for that sole purpose.

1) Link & Purpose (Verses 3–8)

  • Verse 3 named two deluded types: kriyājada (mechanical ritualist) & śuṣka-jñānī (dry intellectualist).
  • Verses 4–5 described their traits.
  • Verses 6–7–8 now invite both groups onto the true path: adopt renunciation/detachment & allied virtues in their right relation to Self-realisation.

Key idea: External practices matter, but only in right orientation to ātam-jñān. This verse answers, “What is the place of our practices—bhakti, tapas, vrata, dāna, sevā, etc.—in spiritual effort?”

2) What “Vairāgyādi” Includes (the “ādi” matters)

Not only “detachment.” Ādi (“etc.”) explicitly widens the scope to all spiritual auxiliaries:

  • Inner: detachment (vairāgya), renunciation (tyāga), humility (vinaya), reverence, compassion (karuṇā), truthfulness, forgiveness…
  • Devotional: worship (pūjā), devotion (bhakti), satsang.
  • Disciplinary: vows (vrata), austerities/penance (tapas), seva, simplicity, solitude, silence… The verse is not exalting detachment alone; it is locating every tool in its proper alignment with Self-realisation.

3) The Two Lines — “After” & “Before” Self-Realisation

Line 1 — After Self-Realisation: Vairāgyādi safal to, jo sah ātam-jñān. Even for a realised one, auxiliaries with Self-abidance complete the work of annihilating residual fluctuations.

Four benefits (post-realisation):

1) Ātma-sthiratā — steadier abidance in the Self.

2) Saṁvara — stoppage of new karmic influx.

3) Nirjarā (vipul) — shedding of bound karmas in large measure.

4) Tvarita-pragati — quicker growth to the goal.

Line 2 — Before Self-Realisation: Tem j ātam-jñān-nī prāptī tanā nidān. The same auxiliaries, if practised with the sole purpose of Self-realisation, become authentic means/ ladder to it.

Four benefits (pre-realisation):

1) Kṣaya of passions — reduction of viṣaya-vāsanā / kaṣāya.

2) Citta-śuddhi — purification of mind-stuff.

3) Ruchi/vātsalya for the Self — intensified love for satsang & the inner quest.

4) Avakāśa for awareness — more time/opportunity for inner work and self-awareness.

“Sole reason” is decisive. The same practice, if done for fashion, praise, fear, or heaven, misses its transforming power.

4) What Detachment Is Not

We sometimes feel “detached” during physical pain, emotional stress, or bereavement. That is duḥkhāgarbhit vairāgya—detachment born of sorrow, not of insight. It fades when pleasure returns. True detachment arises from seeing the transitoriness (anityatā) and insufficiency of worldly enjoyments; it stays even amid favourable conditions.

Willpower caveat: Mere abstinence can become suppression or just “self-control” as a capability. Willpower is spiritually valuable only when yoked to the right aim.

5) The Orientation Test — “1 and 0” Analogy

All auxiliaries are zeros; ātam-jñān is the 1.

  • Zeros without the “1” = no value or even side-effects.
  • Zeros after the “1” = value multiplies. Place the 1 (Self-realisation) first/central; then the zeros of practice become powerful.

6) Mini-Anecdote — “Renounce Hate”

A monk boasts: “I have renounced wine, (other) women, wealth; coloured clothes; I eat less, sleep less; I hate laziness and colour.” The Master, smiling warmly: “Has it occurred to you to renounce hate?” Point: External renunciations without uprooting inner hostility miss the essence of renunciation.

7) If the Sole Aim Is Missing — Pitfalls

Detachment → loneliness/depression: silence/retreat, done as trend or escape, can isolate you within your mental loops. Well-done practices → celestial gains: Sincere yet mis-aimed effort can result in heavenly merit, not liberation.

  • Identity-building: “I’m austere/spiritual” subtly feeds ego, hindering Self-vision.

9) Practice Planner — Before & After

Before Self-realisation (sole aim = ātam-jñān):
  • Daily 2 anchors: (i) reflective reading (10–15 min), (ii) seated awareness (10–15 min).
  • One auxiliary at a time (e.g., forgiveness drill, measured speech, mindful giving).
  • Sole-aim reminder: brief intention at start: “Let this support Self-realisation.”
After glimpses/abidance strengthen:
  • Continue auxiliaries to stabilise abidance (sthiratā), saṁvara–nirjarā, and safeguard humility.
  • Let devotion expand; let disciplines become effortless expressions rather than ego projects.

10) Reflection Prompts

  • Which auxiliary in my current routine is most alive, and why am I actually doing it?
  • Where do I notice duḥkhāgarbhit vairāgya in me? What understanding would make it steady?
  • How will I stamp today’s practice with the sole reason of ātam-jñān?
  • Which inner renunciation (e.g., hate, resentment, pride) is most urgent?

12) Memorisation & Punctuation Cue

Recite with the turn between the two clauses:

Vairāgyādi safal to — (pause) jo sah ātam-jñān;
Tem j — (fresh start) ātam-jñān-nī, prāptī tanā nidān.

One-breath Summary

All practices (the whole vairāgyādi family) are potent only as satellites of Self-realisation. With SR, they complete the journey; aimed at SR, they carry us to its door. Without that aim, they become either fashion, fatigue, or future heaven—not freedom.
