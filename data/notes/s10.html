2) The “ordering” question: Why is liberation 5th and path 6th?

Your Guru raises a very logical doubt (and answers it immediately):

The question
  • If liberation is possible only by treading the path,
  • then path seems like a prerequisite for liberation.
  • So shouldn’t the order be:
    • 5th = Path of liberation
    • 6th = Liberation exists

He says: This question may not have arisen in you, but it arose in me.

The answer (the key principle)
Objective must be clear before effort begins
  • Unless a person is clear about the goal, he won’t feel inspired to pursue the means.

He repeats the core logic:

  • First establish the objective clearly (liberation is real).
  • Then reveal the method (path of liberation).

3) Examples used to prove this “objective-first” logic

Your Guru gives two very practical examples:

Example 1: Crude oil under the ground
  • If you know there is crude oil below the surface:
    • people will drill
    • people will invest effort
    • people will endure difficulty

But if you are not sure oil exists:

  • why would anyone drill?
Example 2: Gold mine in an area
  • If you are sure a gold mine exists in some region:
    • you will travel even from far away
    • you will attempt the possibility seriously
    • you will put full effort
Lesson

You pursue a path intensely only when you are convinced the goal is real.

So:

  • Fifth Fundamental = establish that liberation is real/possible
  • Sixth Fundamental = then naturally the seeker asks: how do I attain it?

4) Why the first 5 Fundamentals were necessary before teaching the path

Your Guru explains a step-by-step necessity:

Before “path,” the disciple must be convinced about reality of the soul and bondage To pursue moksh sincerely, the disciple first had to understand:

1. Soul exists

2. Soul is everlasting / eternal

3. Soul is the doer (karta) of karma

4. Soul is the receiver/endurer (bhokta) of karma-fruits

5. Liberation is possible and is the only true end of misery/transmigration

He emphasizes: this isn’t just philosophy — the disciple must clearly see:

The cause of bondage
  • How karmas are acquired (through impure reactions, etc.)
The impact of karma on a worldly soul
  • When karmas give results as situations,
  • the worldly soul is conditioned to react,
  • and through reaction, the cycle continues.

He briefly names karmas just as examples:

  • Naam Karma (body-determining karma)
  • Mohaniya Karma (deluding karma)

And he clarifies an important boundary:

  • Here we are talking about worldly souls affected by karmas
  • not liberated beings, not omniscient beings, etc.

5) A quick clarity on “worldly soul vs higher beings” (as he mentions)

Your Guru lists terms you’re now familiar with after the Fifth Fundamental:

  • Liberated/Emancipated Beings (Siddha)
    • state is eternal
    • liberation is karmaless
  • Omniscient / Enlightened beings
    • he hints at karmic differences (e.g., “four karmas… eight karmas… impact differs”)
Main point he wants here (in the intro):
  • The disciple must first understand how karmas affect a worldly soul (because the path is about ending that condition).

What has happened inside the disciple?

A burning desire to end transmigration

So this knowledge kindles desire:

Therefore the disciple asks:
“Show me the correct method—so I can proceed as quickly as possible.”

7) Definition: Moksh Upay

Your Guru defines the phrase plainly:

  • Moksh = liberation
  • Upay = means / method / path

So:

Moksh Upay = the correct method to become liberated

He also describes the disciple’s mood:

  • intense eagerness
  • indomitable enthusiasm
  • urgency (not laziness, not casual interest)
