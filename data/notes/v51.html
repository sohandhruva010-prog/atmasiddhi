• He begins by reminding: Verse 49–50 exposed the root flaw behind the disciple’s doubts about the soul.
  • The flaw: false identification with the body (deh-adhyas).
    • Because of that, soul + body appear as one, so we start:
      • believing like a body
      • behaving like a body
  • He uses the “name” example to make it personal:
    • You think “I am Dr. Mansi” because a body got a name.
    • But 20–30 years earlier, “Dr. Mansi” as an identity didn’t even exist—parents could’ve chosen a different name (Deepti).
    • So “I = name/body identity” is clearly something you picked up, not the deepest truth.
Key pivot he makes:
  • Don’t keep it abstract (“body and soul”).
  • Make it real: “I and Dr. Mansi are separate entities.”
  • He says: an Enlightened Master sees you and your body-identity as separate.

3:20–6:54 — The tool the Guru wants to give: bhedjnan (discriminative knowledge)

He introduces the method (before the verse):

  • Bhedjnan = discriminative knowledge (knowing the difference).
  • Step 1 is not “grab the soul directly.”
  • Step 1 is: discriminate between knower and known.

He gives the simplest training rule:

  • Every time you notice: “I knew something”
    • Immediately split it into:
      • known (object) — pencil, phone, person, sound, etc.
      • knower (knowingness itself) — the constant “I” behind it

He keeps stressing:

  • The object can change (this pencil → that object → that sound),
  • but the fact of knowing stays present.
  • Then:
    • contemplate on the chief attribute (consciousness/knowingness)
    • meditate
    • get absorbed
    • experience the soul

He even says (in essence):

The whole sadhana path is already hidden in this: notice knowing → separate knower/known → rest in knowingness.

6:54–9:08 — “Wait for the full first fundamental” + don’t turn questions into fashion

  • He says: the Guru will now answer doubts one-by-one.
  • But he also warns:
    • Don’t start firing personal doubts mid-way.
    • First, hear the full First Fundamental Truth—many doubts dissolve naturally.
    • If something still remains, reflect first; then ask.

He’s blunt here: asking questions doesn’t impress him if you haven’t understood the verses.

9:08–10:39 — Verse 51 begins: this verse answers the disciple’s first argument (Verse 45)

He frames it clearly:

Disciple’s first argument (Verse 45):
  • Soul can’t be seen by eyes
  • form/shape isn’t known
  • other senses don’t catch it either
  • therefore it doesn’t exist

Guru’s response will come in two verses: 51 and 52. Now he begins Verse 51.

11:10–14:36 — Verse 51 line-by-line (simple meaning first)

He gives the core words:

  • drashta = seer
  • drashti = sight

Verse idea (as he explains it):

  • “Je drashta chhe drashtino” = that which is the seer of sight
  • “je janey chhe roop” = that which knows forms
  • “abadhya anubhav je rahe” = that which remains as a continuous, unobstructed experience (of knowingness)
  • “te chhe jivswaroop” = that is the nature/sign of the soul
His main reversal:
  • You think “eyes see” and “brain knows.”
  • Guru says: actually, the seer/knower is the soul, operating through eyes/brain.

And he adds a crucial nuance:

  • “Not seen by senses” does not mean “cannot be known.”
  • It means: your conclusion is wrong, not your observation.

14:36–22:59 — The thesis he’s setting up: knowingness is independent of objects

He starts building the bigger point:

  • Even when you say “I know nothing,” notice:
    • you still know that you know nothing.
  • So knowingness doesn’t depend on objects.
  • It remains even in sleep/dream (you later say “I knew my dream”).

He distinguishes:

  • He’s not describing the current limited state only.
  • He’s pointing to the pure nature of the soul:
    • it doesn’t need objects to validate itself
    • it doesn’t need physical contact to know (he hints at omniscience)

Then he makes it clear:

  • Guru is not forcing belief.
  • He’s guiding the disciple to examine:
    • Who really sees?
    • Who really knows?
    • Don’t just stop at eyes/brain.

He says this needs “math-class” attention:

  • miss one step, the next won’t land.

23:06–27:58 — Kohinoor diamond story: praise the seer, not the seen

He shares a personal memory:

  • London, 1979, standing long in rain to see the Kohinoor.
  • People are amazed at the diamond.
  • He says: the bigger wonder is the seer.
    • Without the seer, the object has no “meaning” for anyone.
  • This ties directly into Samaysaar-like teaching:
    • we value the known
    • we forget the knower
  • And he points out:
    • if focus shifts to the knower, attachment outside weakens naturally.

28:06–39:28 — The disciple’s “eyes = existence” logic gets dismantled (step by step)

He returns to Verse 45 mindset:

“If it exists, it must be visible to eyes.”

Then he playfully shows the limitations of visibility by forcing the disciple to keep adding conditions:

1) “It exists, but it’s too far”

  • He asks: Los Angeles exists—can you see it while facing west?
  • No.
  • So visibility now requires: not too far.

2) “It exists, but it’s too near”

  • He brings the eyeliner/very-near example:
  • You can’t see something extremely near your eyes.
  • So add: not too near.

3) “It exists, but it’s behind you”

  • Something is positioned behind—can’t be seen.
  • Add: not behind.

4) “It exists, but it’s covered”

  • Object is present but concealed.
  • Add: not covered.

5) “It exists, but there’s no light”

  • Pitch dark → can’t see.
  • Add: must have light.

6) “It exists, but it’s too small”

  • Bacteria: it’s visible in principle (not “ghost-invisible”), but still you can’t see it unaided.
  • Add: must be big enough.
His punchline here:
  • Your “seer” (eyes) has tons of limits.
  • So don’t jump from:
    • “I can’t see it” to:
    • “It doesn’t exist.”

He even says:

At most, say “I can’t see.” Don’t declare non-existence.

39:40–47:20 — The dead body argument: open eyes still don’t see → eyes aren’t the seer

Now he shifts to a stronger proof:

  • Eyes can “see” these objects (TV, chair, iPad…).
  • Then he asks everyone to imagine:
    • a dead body, eyes open,
    • pencil placed in front.
  • Does it see? No.

So what follows?

  • If open eyes were truly the seer, they would see.
  • Since they don’t, eyes are instruments, not the real seer.

Then he adds the eyeball donation story:

  • An eyeball removed and placed somewhere still doesn’t “see.”
  • The missing condition isn’t distance/light/position—
  • The missing condition is connection with the actual seer.

He doesn’t demand you accept “soul” instantly. He says: at least accept this much:

  • there is an actual seer behind the eyes.

47:43–50:57 — “Why can’t the soul be seen by eyes?” becomes an absurd question

Now he flips the disciple’s question elegantly:

  • If eyes are the seer (as you assumed), can eyes see eyes?
    • No—because the seer cannot be the seen.
  • Therefore, when we say soul is the seer, asking:
    • “why can’t eyes see the soul?” becomes like asking:
    • “why can’t eyes see eyes?”
  • You never doubt your eyes exist just because you can’t see them directly.
    • You know you have eyes because you can see.

So similarly:

  • If soul is the seer, it won’t be “seen as an object” by the eyes.

51:11–57:36 — Window analogy: house/body, seer/soul, window/eyes, hills/sights

He gives a clean 4-part model:

  • House = body
  • Person inside (Shiv) = soul / seer
  • Window = eyes (medium/instrument)
  • Hills = sights (objects)

Key point:

  • The window doesn’t “enjoy the view.”
  • The seer enjoys/knows the view using the window.

So:

  • eyes are a medium,
  • soul is the seer/knower,
  • objects are sights/forms.

58:27–1:03:26 — Memory + control: the seer is more than eyes

He points out:

  • Even when eyes are not currently seeing, you still remember what you saw.
  • That integrating, recollecting capacity is not “in the eyeball.”
  • There’s an “entity” that knows, remembers, connects experience (he avoids overusing “analysis” to not trigger intellect debates yet).

He also adds:

  • the “seer” even guides/controls the eyes:
    • “look here”
    • “don’t look”
  • So at least accept: consciousness is separate from the eyes.
  • He phrases it as:
    • soul = ruler
    • senses = ruled

1:03:26–1:06:51 — “abadhya anubhav”: the constant, unobstructed “I am”

Now he defines the difficult phrase:

  • abadhya = continuous / constant / unobstructed
  • anubhav = experience So: abadhya anubhav = the always-present experience of knowingness / “I-am-ness”

He uses a helpful distinction:

  • don’t define soul only in relation to objects (seer of sights, knower of forms)
  • the soul has an independent nature:
    • constant consciousness even when objects change

He gives the paternal-uncle analogy:

  • “maternal/paternal uncle” is a relational identity
  • but “I am” existed even before those relationships existed
  • so don’t reduce soul to “I am knower of this object” only— know it as I am (constant).

1:07:07–1:11:50 — The “subtraction” method: remove body-parts, the “I” remains

He demonstrates how to detect the soul through elimination:

  • If “I” was legs because “I walk,” and legs are amputated → I still remain (and I remember I used to walk).
  • Finger cut → finger gone → I still remain.
  • Eyesight lost → eyes subtracted → I still remain.

So what is this constant remainder?

  • the uninterrupted “I am”
  • that is consciousness
  • that is the chief sign of soul

He ties it back:

  • Verse 51 gives soul’s key positive characteristic:
    • not just “formless” (negative definition)
    • but knowingness (positive definition)

Final wrap-up of Verse 51 (as he concludes)

Verse 51 is establishing this:

1. Soul is the seer of sights

2. Soul is the knower of forms

3. Soul is the constant, unobstructed experience of knowingness (“I am”) ➡ That is the jivswaroop (nature/sign of the soul)

Then he says they’ll sing the verse again.

Quick cheat sheet: terms + the argument in one chain

Key terms
  • drashta: seer
  • drashti: sight
  • roop: form
  • abadhya anubhav: continuous/unobstructed experience
  • jivswaroop: nature/sign of the soul
  • bhedjnan: discriminative knowledge (knower vs known)
Argument chain
  • Eyes have many limits → “not seen” never proves “not existing”
  • Dead body eyes open still don’t see → eyes are instruments
  • Seer can’t be “seen as an object” by its own instrument (eyes can’t see eyes)
  • Therefore soul-as-seer won’t be “seen” by eyes
  • Soul is known through abadhya anubhav: constant “I am/knowingness” that remains even when parts/objects are subtracted
