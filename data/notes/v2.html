Talk focus: Anubandha chatuṣṭaya continued—Subject (abhidheya/viṣaya) and Purpose (prayojana) made explicit in Verse 2; clarity on “mokṣa-mārga” today, why it feels lost, and how Param Krupālu Dev states it clearly (agopya) for true seekers to contemplate (vichār).

0) Quick Summary (1-Page)

Verse (transliterated): Vartamān ā kāl mā mokṣamārg bahu lop;
Vichārvā ātmārthine bhākhyo atra agopya.

Very short meaning: In the present times, the path of liberation is almost forgotten. For true seekers to contemplate, it is stated here, clearly—without concealment.

Arc of the talk:
  • Links Verse 1→2: Verse 1 covered Maṅgal (obeisance) & Sambandh (source-authority). Verse 2 covers Subject (mokṣa-mārga) & Purpose (why this text: to enable seekers’ contemplation).
  • Diagnoses our era: path exists but is obscured (“bahu lop” ≠ zero).
  • Clarifies immediate vs ultimate goals for an aspirant.
  • Insists on a living Sadguru to avoid confusion and enable transformation.
  • Agopya: nothing new is invented; the original Tīrthaṅkara path is revived, not remade—stated without hiding, bias, or motive.

Practice takeaway: Become the kind of ātmārthī who reflects daily on the Sadguru’s articulation of the path and implements one concrete step—today.

1) Link from Verse 1 → Verse 2 (Sambandh)

  • Verse 1 (recap): Endless suffering arises from non-realization of the Self; the remedy enters via the Sadguru who reveals the Self—hence obeisance.
  • Verse 2 (advance): Having bowed and established source-authority, Param Krupālu Dev now announces the subject (mokṣa-mārga) and the purpose (to make seekers contemplate and walk it).

2) Word-to-Word Meanings (Śabdārth)

  • Vartamān ā kāl mā — in the present times; the era we live in.
  • Mokṣamārg — the path of liberation.
  • Bahu lop — almost disappeared / largely lost, forgotten, ignored (not fully extinct).
  • Vichārvā — to contemplate/reflect.
  • Ātmārthine — for the true seeker aiming at Self-realization.
  • Bhākhyo — stated / explained / shown.
  • Atra — here, i.e., in this scripture (Atmasiddhi Śāstra).
  • Agopya — without concealment, no hedging or reservations; explicit.

3) Short Meaning (Sankṣiptārth)

In our times the path to liberation is widely forgotten (though present). For sincere seekers to contemplate and adopt, it is stated here plainly and without hiding.

4) Explanation — The Thought Expands

4.1 Subject & Purpose made explicit

  • Subject (first line): Mokṣamārg — the authentic path to liberation.
  • Purpose (second line): Vichārvā ātmārthine — to give seekers a clear basis for contemplation leading to practice.
  • The text’s function is not novelty but lucidity: bhākhyo atra agopya.

4.2 “Bahu lop” — what has (and hasn’t) happened

  • Not total extinction: the path exists, but is obscured—misunderstood, forgotten, marginalized.
  • You may feel like the odd one out if you speak of Self-realization and inner transformation; popular religion may reward show, donation, function, and fame over sādhanā.
  • Anecdotes used:
  • Dubai cousin: enjoyed the talk yet remarked “You speak about the Soul a lot”—illustrating how unusual true interiority sounds in public religious spaces.
  • “Beacon in a tunnel”: In darkness, the Sadguru becomes the lamp; what’s needed from us is willingness to be led.

4.3 Where are we in time? Two complementary frames

A) Jain cosmology — Eras in Bhārat-kṣetra
  • 4th era: Liberation possible; path present.
  • 5th era (now): Liberation not possible here, but path to liberation is present; Self-realization is possible and brings you ever nearer to Godhood/omniscience.
  • 6th era: Neither liberation nor path—religion absent.

Hence “bahu lop”: not gone, but fading/forgotten; every word is precise.

B) Hindu yugas — “legs of dharma” image
  • Satyug: Dharma on four legs; thoughts & deeds divine.
  • Tretā: Dharma loses one leg; adharma enters; the soul is often pulled into sin.
  • Dvāpar: Dharma on two legs; adharma center-stage; social fabric decays; vices seeded among the elite.
  • Kaliyug: Dharma hobbling on one leg; deceit and materialism dominate everyone, even children; virtues crippled.

Corollary: “Bahu lop” fits Kaliyug—one leg remains; learn how to progress with one leg.

4.4 Why a living Sadguru is necessary

  • Religion is subtle (sūkṣma); seekers can be confused by sectarian prescriptions (Digambar/ Śvetāmbara, Sthānakvāsi/Derāvāsi, ritual details).
  • Scriptures can inspire/instruct; a living Sadguru can also inspect—correct you in real time.
  • Without such guidance, one gains information yet lacks inner transformation.

4.5 “Agopya” — nothing to hide, nothing to sell

  • What is disclosed: the original Jina-propounded path, carried by Ācāryas—revived, not invented.
  • Why some hide: ignorance, self-interest, sectarian bias, fear of losing patronage, preference for a more “marketable” religion.
  • Why it’s open here: no prejudice, no selfish motive, only the true seeker in mind—hence plain-spoken clarity.

4.6 Immediate vs Ultimate Goals for an Ātmārthī

  • Ultimate: Self-realization (ending ignorance).
  • Immediate: Vichār on what the Sadguru states today; analyze, plan, and execute one step now— not merely making fair notes.

5) Examples & Images Used by the Speaker

  • Odd-person-out dynamic when speaking of the soul and inner change.
  • “Beacon in a tunnel”: hope through the lamp of a Sadguru.
  • Legs of Dharma: from four (Satyug) to one (Kaliyug)—you must learn to advance even when dharma “hops.”
  • Sectarian maze examples (ritual water/pujā rules) showing how easily one is trapped without a guide.
  • Revive vs establish: uddhār of satya-dharma (“revival”); the path is shown, not fabricated.

6) Practical Application — Becoming an Ātmārthī Today

1) Daily Vichār (10–15 min): Pick one statement from the talk (e.g., “Path exists though obscured”). Reflect: Where is it obscured in my life?

2) One concrete step: Remove one obstacle that keeps the path “obscured” (e.g., swap 15 min of social scroll for silence; set a fixed slot for svādhyāya).

3) Sadguru-centric alignment: Ask for inspection this week on one habit; accept correction gratefully.

4) Attachment audit: When conflict arises over ritual/sect rules, ask: Is this helping realization or fuelling identity? Choose the essence.

5) Revival mindset: When you feel “alone,” remember “bahu lop ≠ zero”—seek the living lineage and stay loyal to it.
