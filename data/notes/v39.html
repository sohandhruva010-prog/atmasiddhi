• Verse 38 showed:
    • The state of worthiness / eligibility / identity of an ātmārthī:
      • Kashayni upshānttā (pacified passions)
      • Matra moksh abhilāsh (only desire for liberation)
      • Bhavey khed (grief of rebirths)
      • Pranidayā (compassion for all beings)
    • This is the “ātmārth nivās” – the house where Self-realisation can stay.

Now in verses 39 & 40 Param Krupalu Dev answers two big questions:

1. Verse 39:

What happens as long as that state of worthiness has not arisen? – “Dasha na evi jya sudhi…”

2. Verse 40:

What happens when such a state does arise? – “Aave jya evi dasha…”

He hints that in these two verses He’s basically sketching the entire spiritual journey, up to moksh / nirvan, in seed form — like the 14 guṇasthānaks compressed.

2. The main idea of verse 39

He frames verse 39 as negative assertion:

  • Negative style (verse 39): “As long as such a state is not there, this will not happen.”
  • Positive style (verse 40): “When such a state is there, this will naturally happen.”

The core message:

As long as the worthiness of verse 38 has not arisen,

1. The jīva cannot take true advantage of Sadguru association,

2. He does not attain the mokshmarg,

3. His inner disease (antar rog) of delusion is not cured.

So if someone:

  • Studies a lot,
  • Does rituals,
  • Practises meditation,

…but still feels stuck, no experience of the pure Self, no inner breakthrough—

Param Krupalu Dev says: “The method is not the main issue;
worthiness is missing.”

3. Parsing the verse lines

(A) “Dasha na evi jya sudhi…” – Till such a state is absent

  • “Dasha na evi” → “Such a state is not…”
    • Which state? The state of verse 38:

1. Subsided passions

2. Only desire for moksh

3. Weariness of transmigration

4. Compassion for all beings

  • “Jya sudhi” → “So long as…”

So, first part:

“As long as the state shown in verse 38 has not arisen within…”

(B) “Jīva lahey nahi jog…” – He cannot benefit from Sadguru

Here he makes a fine distinction between:

  • Yog – association.
  • Jog – benefiting from that association.

He could have used “yog” (for rhyming with “rog”), but he deliberately uses jog:

  • Yog: being around the Sadguru, having closeness.
  • Jog: actually taking advantage of that closeness.

So:

“As long as such a state is not there, the jīva cannot benefit from the association of a Sadguru.”

Even if:

  • You have Sadguru,
  • You have satsang,
  • You have seva,

…but the inner state of worthiness is not there → you don’t really gain.

He warns:

“So don’t only ask for ‘more satsang, more seva’. Ask for worthiness.”

He gives classic examples:

  • Lalluji Muni – even in separation (virah) he attained Self-realisation, because he had inner worthiness.
  • Popat Manji – had the privilege to:
    • Sleep on the same khatla (bed),
    • Share the same plate as Param Krupalu Dev.
    • But Param Krupalu Dev still had to tell him: “You did not benefit as much as you could have.”
    • Why? Insufficient inner worthiness. He could have been the fifth jewel among the four jewel-like devotees.

So:

You may have exclusive outer access, but without worthiness, jīva lahey nahi jog – you gain very little.

(C) “Mokshmarg pamey nahi…” – The path is not attained

Because he doesn’t benefit from Sadguru properly:

“He does not attain the path of liberation.”

Meaning:

  • He doesn’t recognise the true path,
  • He doesn’t enter it rightly,
  • Or he cannot proceed steadily.

He feels:

  • Stuck, dull, not progressing.
  • That’s not because:
    • Satsang is lacking,
    • Techniques are wrong,
    • Or Guru is partial.
  • It’s because: Worthiness is incomplete.

(D) “Matey na antar rog…” – Inner disease remains uncured

  • Rog – disease.
  • Antar rog – inner disease.

Which disease?

  • Disease of delusion / wrong belief,
  • Ignorance of the true nature of the Self,
  • The mental cravings, agitations, up-down fluctuations.

As long as verse 38’s state is absent:

“The inner disease is not cured.”

Hence:

  • No Self-realisation,
  • And so no liberation.

He summarises the chain:

  • Liberation (14th guṇasthān) is not possible without Self-realisation (4th guṇasthān),
  • Self-realisation is not possible without the four virtues (worthiness),
  • The four virtues are not possible without deep yearning: “Kaam ek ātmārthnu.”

4. Short meaning (in his style)

So long as the soul has not attained the state of worthiness described in verse 38, it cannot take advantage of Sadguru association and other favourable circumstances. It does not attain the path of liberation, and its inner disease of self-delusion (ignorance of the Self) remains uncured.

5. Why are these virtues so hard?

  • Attaining these virtues is not easy.
  • You must battle with:

1. Inner confusions

2. Outer temptations Earlier (in verse 37 talk) he said:

  • Life is a battle between:
    • Internal clarity and
    • External pulls.

Here he deepens it:

  • Earlier stage:
    • Confusions inside: “What is right? What do I want?”
  • With satsang:
    • Confusion reduces; clarity increases.
  • Next battle:
    • Temptations outside: even with clarity, we may choose wrongly.

Plus, hidden danger:

  • We do not know our subconscious.
  • We know:
    • Face, body, conscious mind – somewhat.
  • But:
    • Past impressions & instincts lie in the subconscious.
    • When a situation fidgets those impressions:
      • They erupt, drag us: “I’m not in my hands… I’m just getting pulled.”

That’s why:

  • Renunciation, detachment, restraints were given:
    • Not to bind us,
    • But to protect us from our own deep tendencies.

He says:

“You thought rules were bondage. They were for freedom.”

But because of:

  • Ignorance,
  • Ego,
  • Intense desires,

…we misread them as restrictions, and then fall back from worthiness.

6. Worthiness vs piling on more practices

He is very clear:

“The solution is: increase your worthiness, not: increase more rituals, more meditation, more external practice.”

He explains:

  • How your meditation goes depends more on:
    • Before and after meditation,
    • Than on what happens during meditation.

If you don’t live with:

  • Friendliness (maitri),
  • Appreciation (pramod),
  • Compassion (karunā),
  • Neutrality (madhyasthata),
  • Calmness, detachment, etc.

…then even if the meditation session feels good, soon:

  • You’ll feel stuck,
  • Inner disease of delusion will remain.

He shares the email example:

  • Someone wrote: “I want to start my meditative journey, what is your ajna?”
  • His inner response first: “I wanted to say – don’t go for meditation yet!”
  • Because:
    • They might feel they’re doing something great,
    • While missing the vital inner work on worthiness.
  • Finally he replies:
    • Begin with only 10 minutes.
    • Even if you feel great, do not exceed 10 minutes.
    • Why? “I don’t want your focus to shift from worthiness to meditation performance.”

So meditation is offered, but tightly contained, so that:

  • Priority stays: inner virtues,
  • Not: chasing experiences.

7. Punya vs worthiness – what comes from where?

He makes a very subtle, important distinction:

  • Outer conditions you enjoy:
    • Human birth,
    • Sadguru association,
    • Ashram life,
    • Conducive atmosphere,
    • Health, food, climate, devotional environment…
    • → All these come from past punya (auspicious karmas).
  • What you feel within:
    • Inner joy,
    • Peace,
    • Security,
    • Lightness,
    • → Comes from worthiness + Sadguru’s grace.

He states:

“All this you have attained not because of your worthiness. You have attained it by your past merits. But what you are feeling within is because of your worthiness (and grace), not because of past merits.”

So:

  • Staying 24x7 with the Sadguru = fruits of punya.
  • Feeling deep inner joy & transformation = grace reacting to worthiness.

For a devotee, he says:

“Don’t dissect it too much as karmaphal;
just see it as His grace.”

But metaphysically:

  • Outside = punya; inside = worthiness + grace.

8. When you feel stuck – where to look?

He says:

“Don’t ask me, ‘Why am I stuck?’ Ask yourself: ‘Which of the four virtues have I missed?’”

  • Check:

1. Have my passions been pacified?

2. Is liberation my only desire?

3. Am I genuinely tired of this samsār?

4. Has compassion blossomed?

He highlights especially compassion:

  • We always knew “pranidayā = compassion”.
  • But now we see: “Compassion is a must for worthiness.”
  • If you become too inward, too self-focused:
    • You can unintentionally lose compassion.
  • Then
    • Worthiness becomes incomplete,
    • Self-realisation doesn’t flower.

So:

“Miss out on compassion, you miss out on Self-realisation.”

Hence importance of:

  • Seva and love for others:
    • They keep the heart soft,
    • Complete the 4th wall of ātmārth nivās.

9. The right process – from Sadguru to Self-realisation

He outlines the ideal pipeline:

1. Sadguru association (yog):

  • You get:
    • Darshan
    • Listening (satsang),
    • Seva opportunities.

2. Listening with interest:

  • You are eager, receptive.

3. Grasping & retention:

  • Not just parrot-memory of words,
  • But:
    • Thoughts in the mind,
    • Feelings in the heart.
  • Revision (parāvartan) is: “When the same feelings keep coming back again and again.”

4. Right conclusions:

  • Equations in your mind change,
  • You start seeing life differently,
  • You arrive at right decisions.

5. Experiments:

  • After every satsang:
    • You give yourself at least one experiment.
  • You test the teachings in your life.

6. Self-realisation:

  • Through this process, direct experience arises.

He notes:

This whole process works only if worthiness is there.

Otherwise:

  • Punya can bring:
    • Association, satsang, seva,
  • But the journey stops there.

10. Blotting paper vs stone – receptivity image

He uses a strong metaphor for receptivity:

  • If worthiness is present:
    • The heart is like blotting paper.
    • Guru’s teachings: “Penetrate into your heart.”
    • Feelings change,
    • Thoughts change,
    • Words & actions naturally follow.
  • If worthiness is absent:
    • Heart is like hard, rigid surface.
    • Teachings fall like drops on stone:
      • Nothing is absorbed.
    • You may even:
      • Repeat the words,
      • Advise others,
      • But inside no transformation.

So:

“The inner state is either a true ātmārth nivās (blotting paper), or a closed stone floor.”

11. The Guru and your receptivity

He says something very sharp:

  • Because of absence of the four attributes:
    • You can’t develop true receptivity.
  • Receptivity is not:
    • Just the ears hearing,
    • That’s only sound.
  • True receptivity:
    • Hearing the exact message meant for you.

He adds:

“The Guru knows what He gives, and He knows whether you have picked it up or not.”

When He sees someone not picking up:

  • He recognises:
    • “This person is undeserving as of now.”
  • Then:
    • He will not waste energy:
      • Not in the sense of withdrawing compassion,
      • But He will not keep pouring extra chances where there is no receptivity.
  • He waits:
    • For the disciple to build worthiness.

Yet:

  • Even in public satsang:
    • You often feel: “He is talking only to me.”
    • And the person next to you feels the same.
  • That is: “Private time in public” – the unique way a Sadguru reaches hearts.

12. No shortcuts – in any of the three times

He ties it back to verse 36’s “ek hoy tran kālmā, parmarathno panth”:

  • Just as:
    • The path of liberation is one in all three times (past, present, future),
  • So also:
    • Without these four attributes, there is no possibility of liberation in any of the three times.

He says:

We love shortcuts – even in queues. The seeker is no exception – he, too, looks for shortcuts. But here, there is none.

  • It has never happened in the past,
  • It will never happen in the future,
  • And it will not happen with you:

No worthiness → no curing of inner disease → no Self-realisation → no moksh.

Until:

You build an inner state that is conducive to Self-realisation – the abode where it can gracefully descend.

13. One line summary (his emphasis)

Without cultivating the four virtues of verse 38, your satsang, seva, renunciation, rituals, austerities – all may remain outwardly impressive, but inwardly, the disease of delusion stays, and the path of liberation does not open.

Verse 39 is simply:

A loving but uncompromising “Not yet.” “Till you build this inner state, don’t expect the real fruit. Work on worthiness first.”
