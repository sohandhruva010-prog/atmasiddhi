What’s already established (earlier verses)

The disciple is already convinced that:

  • the soul exists
  • it is eternal
  • it is the doer of karma
  • it is the receiver (enjoyer/sufferer) of karma fruits

What starts now

Now the disciple challenges the next truth:

✅ 5th Fundamental Truth: Moksh chhe (Liberation exists)

❌ Disciple’s doubt: Even if all the earlier truths are accepted, liberation is not logically possible.

2) The Sadguru’s method (how the Guru will answer)

Before even unpacking the verse, the speaker sets the tone:

2.1 Sadguru is not “anti-logic”

  • The Guru does not dismiss logic.
  • He will counter logic with logic.

2.2 “Thorn vs thorn” method (major analogy)

Analogy:

Use one thorn to remove another thorn.

Meaning:

  • The disciple’s reasoning is like a thorn stuck inside.
  • The Guru uses a sharper / truer reasoning to remove it.

2.3 Why this is “compassion”

The speaker highlights a very important spiritual point:

  • Our thinking is partial and limited
  • Yet we feel very intelligent
  • Sadguru broadens the territory of thinking and redirects it properly (through compassion + the disciple’s love/faith/surrender)

4) The verse being explained (Disciple’s argument) — VERSE 87

The speaker quotes/explains the Gujarati lines:

Core line 1: Acceptance + “but…”

“Karta bhokta jiva ho…”
  • karta = doer
  • bhokta = receiver / endurer (enjoyer-sufferer)
  • jiva = soul
  • ho = “may be / yes it is” (here the meaning is: I am convinced about this now)

So the disciple is saying: ✅ “I accept the soul is the doer and receiver of karmas…”

Core line 2: The conclusion (denying moksh)

“…pan teno nahi moksh”
  • pan = but
  • teno = from that (from this cycle of doing + receiving)
  • nahi moksh = there is no liberation

Meaning: ❌ “But liberation from this cycle does not exist / is not possible.”

Core line 3: The big premise

“Vityo kaal anant…”
  • vityo = has passed
  • kaal = time
  • anant = infinite

Meaning:

“Infinite time has passed.”

Core line 4: The killer support point

“…pan vartaman chhe dosh.”
  • vartaman = present
  • chhe = is
  • dosh = flaw / impurity / fault (the inner defect causing bondage)

Meaning:

“Yet even now, impurity still continues.”

5) The disciple’s logic — step-by-step (very important)

This is the disciple’s reasoning chain (the transcript repeats it in different ways):

Step 1: The cycle has been going on since infinite time

  • Bondage → fruits → reaction → new bondage has continued ceaselessly (no break “even 5 seconds”)

Step 2: The root cause (dosh/impurity) is still present today

Even now:

  • reactions exist
  • likes-dislikes exist
  • inner “faults” exist

Step 3: If moksh was possible, it would have happened already

He argues:

  • If liberation was truly possible,
  • then in infinite time, at least some final end should have occurred “by now.”

Step 4: “Was → Is → Will be” inference

He forms a strong predictive conclusion:

  • What was there,
  • is still there,
  • therefore it will remain in the future also.

Final conclusion:

✅ Soul may be doer/receiver, ❌ but absolute freedom from bondage (moksh) is not possible.

The speaker even says:

  • “See the logic—wonderful, right?”
  • The disciple’s argument feels very convincing until the Guru dismantles it.

6) Clarification: “Freedom from one karma” vs “Freedom from the cycle”

The speaker adds a key distinction:

6.1 One karma can end

He references the earlier principle:

  • “Thay bhogthi dur” = karma sheds after giving fruit

So yes:

  • you can become free of that particular karma

6.2 But disciple denies “absolute freedom”

The disciple says:

  • the cycle of bondage will still continue
  • so “complete liberation” is impossible

7) The disciple’s experience-based evidence (reactions are automatic)

A major part of the verse explanation is psychological realism:

7.1 Reaction seems built-in, like “automatic gears”

He says:

  • sometimes he didn’t even plan the reaction
  • it happens reflexively
  • then sorrow and guilt come:
    • “Why did I say like this?”
    • “I never meant to hurt…”

This supports his doubt:

If reacting is automatic, how can bondage ever stop?

7.2 Time and situations don’t cure the flaw

He says:

  • Passage of time didn’t remove it
  • Changing situations didn’t remove it

8) Big example set: “Hell → Heaven” still doesn’t end bondage

The disciple gives a seemingly strong argument:

Argument: If favourable situations come, desire should end, right?

He reasons:

  • In hell, reaction is understandable.
  • But in heaven, desires are fulfilled.
  • So desire should end → bondage should end → fruits should stop → cycle should finish.

Speaker’s reply (within disciple’s logic):

Even in favourable/heavenly conditions:

  • jealousy still exists
  • comparison still exists
  • “dosh” still exists
Example: jealousy/comparison with Shebani

He says:

  • “You got what you wanted… then why jealousy?”
  • “Why bothered that Shebani got more?”
  • “She got a ‘Dr’ in front of her name…”

So: ✅ desires fulfilled does not automatically mean faults vanish.

9) Everyday-life version of the same excuse (very important)

He gives a very common pattern:

“Once this problem is solved, I’ll practice dharma peacefully.” Examples he lists:

  • once my daughter gets married
  • once debt is paid
  • once I’m healthy
  • once this problem ends…

Then he challenges:

  • even if he changes your situation…
  • do you guarantee: “no desires, no anger, no reactions”?

And he repeats: ✅ “Vartaman chhe dosh.” Even when situations improve, impurity continues.

10) “Heaven description” example (used to expose hidden jealousy)

He uses vivid, humorous imagery to show: Even in ultra-comfort, faults don’t necessarily end.

10.1 “We have already reached heaven”

He jokes:

  • no earning
  • no cooking

Then he jokes further:

  • “only 12 yojans away from Siddha abode”
  • but “tremendous effort is required” to actually cross that last spiritual distance

10.2 “In heaven they think of a bundi laddoo and directly burp”

Meaning:

  • effortless enjoyment
  • no obstructions

Then he says: Even then, jealousy can remain.

Purpose:

  • shows how deep “dosh” is—it’s not solved just by comfort.

11) Detour: Introspection (the speaker’s big spiritual intervention)

This video contains a long, important teaching on introspection—because this verse is about the disciple observing faults.

11.1 “Don’t tell me: ‘Please show me my faults’”

He says strongly:

  • Discourses themselves are mirrors
  • If you still can’t see your faults:
    • it’s either ignorance
    • or more often ego

He even jokes:

  • “I’ll blacklist you”
  • “I won’t even wish you on your birthday” (to shock the listener into self-responsibility)

11.2 Different listeners = different inner levels

Even if:

  • both wear white clothes
  • both hear the same discourse

Still:

  • faith / love / surrender differ

11.3 “Satsang-proof” concept (very memorable)

He says:

  • some people become “incident-proof”
  • then “satsang-proof” Meaning:
  • no impact on the soul Like:
  • “water has no impact on a watch” (he uses this as the comparison)

So:

  • hearing isn’t enough
  • introspection must occur

11.4 Confusion vs frustration

He says:

  • With Sadguru satsang, confusion should go to zero
  • Frustration may increase (because you know what to do but still don’t do it)
  • If someone says “I’m confused,” it shows lack of introspective virtue

11.5 “Inner Search” reference

He hints:

  • later, Verse 109 (“Inner Search”) will come in the “path of liberation” section

Main message:

Without introspection, spiritual talent has “zero value.”

12) Destiny vs choice (major corrective teaching inside the talk)

He insists repeatedly:

12.1 “There is freedom of will—don’t blame destiny”

He says:

  • there is something like destiny only in the sense that you created causes earlier
  • but not “someone else wrote your destiny”

So instead of saying:

  • “destiny” Say:
  • “I chose this” (through past causes / present choices)

12.2 Quarantine email example

Someone wrote:

  • “You called everyone into quarantine, I’m alone…”

He replies:

  • “I didn’t put you there—you chose it”
  • (they delayed coming because of renovation etc.)
  • “Don’t sympathise with yourself”
  • “Don’t say God’s will—this is your will”

He says: Using satsang words to justify wrong thinking is:

  • “Satan speaking” (his blunt phrase)

12.3 Responsibility model example (nails in ears)

He says:

  • Bhagwan didn’t say “poor me”
  • he would say: if not in this birth, then some past birth cause exists

12.4 Memory example (15th August food)

He says:

  • you don’t remember what you ate on a date
  • but it still happened Similarly:
  • you may not remember karmic causes
  • but effects prove some cause existed

He emphasizes three inner attitudes that cure mental suffering:

2. Forgiveness

3. Gratitude

Key point:

15) Big story example: the lizard in his room (VERY important example set)

He tells a long story that actually ties back to bondage + status + proximity-without-transformation.

15.1 The “same lizard” continuity

He says:

  • the lizard’s pattern is so consistent it must be the same one
  • Nemi is scared it will fall on him
  • now it runs on the ground and jumps on the speaker’s asana (sitting mat)

15.2 “It might be a soul like Bhavin”

He jokes:

  • maybe it’s a soul like Bhavin that wanted to clean his asana
  • but got bound in inauspicious state and came into reptile form

15.3 Proximity vs oneness (big lesson)

He says to Nemi:

  • don’t aim for proximity
  • aim for oneness

Because:

  • the lizard is in the room “listening” too
  • but what use is satsang to it?

Purpose:

  • shows: just being near dharma is not the same as inner transformation

15.4 Peacock vs lizard/cockroach (status hierarchy example)

He says:

  • peacock: people feel delight, take photos, gather crowds
  • lizard/cockroach/spider: people feel disgust, want it removed

Lesson: Even within animal births, there is “upper/lower status,” reflecting karmic causes.

15.5 “Good and bad don’t cancel each other”

He says clearly:

  • 50 good + 20 bad doesn’t become “net 30”
  • it remains 50 good fruits and 20 bad fruits
  • unless you do nirjara (shedding)

This supports why:

  • one can have comforts + still face ugly births/sufferings too.

16) Compassion + “detached sensitivity” + warning about premature witnessing

This is one of the most “new-section” teachings of the talk.

16.1 Upsham (subsidence) and reaction-control

He says:

  • your reaction intensity may reduce outwardly
  • you may not shout anymore
  • but internally reaction can still exist
  • if you don’t work on this, Six Fundamentals study becomes useless

16.2 “Bhaare karmi” definition (heavy karma-laden)

He says: If someone can keep going while others are unhappy because of them, that soul is very “bhaare karmi” from Enlightened perspective.

16.3 Param Krupalu Dev compassion example

He says:

  • Krupalu Dev’s eyes welled up just seeing vegetables being cut (to show extreme sensitivity/compassion)

16.4 Detached sensitivity (key idea)

  • help outwardly
  • remain inwardly detached Otherwise the Enlightened would be endlessly sad.

16.5 Warning: witnessing meditation without compassion

He says: Doing “witnessing mode” prematurely (without compassion) can make you selfish:

  • when you suffer, you expect help
  • when others suffer, you “watch-watch-watch”

So: Witnessing should come after compassion (pranidaya) and virtues of worthiness.

17) The disciple’s final stance at the end of this video

After all this, the verse’s conclusion remains:

The disciple says:

  • The cycle was beginningless
  • therefore he concludes it will be endless
  • moksh is a “chimera” (illusion)
  • not because he is casual, but because:
    • he observes reactions are automatic
    • flaws remain despite time + situation changes

Speaker closes the setup:

The disciple cannot even visualize an end to reactivity, so moksh appears impossible.

And the speaker ends with:

“Let’s see how the Guru answers.”

18) Quick “exam-ready” summary you can revise fast

Verse 87 in one line

Even if the soul is doer/receiver, moksh seems impossible because infinite time has passed and impurity still remains.

The disciple’s logical formula

Infinite past + same flaw today ⇒ same flaw in future ⇒ no moksh

Key phrase to remember

✅ “Vityo kaal anant, pan vartaman chhe dosh.” Infinite time has passed, yet the flaw remains in the present.
