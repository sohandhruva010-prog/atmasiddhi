Verse 21 — the warning: “Asadguru e vinayno… mahamohniyā karmathī buḍe bhavjal māhī”
One-line essence

Humility is supremely powerful—but it must be placed at the right feet. If an untrue teacher (asadguru) exploits a disciple’s reverence, he binds massive deluding karma and drowns in saṁsāra’s ocean. This verse warns seekers to discriminate and avoid the trap.

1) Where we are in the flow (9–20 → 21)

  • Verses 9–18: greatness of the living Sadguru; inner enemies fall “with little effort.”
  • Verse 19: zenith of humility—even a disciple who reaches omniscience keeps revering his Guru (who may still be chhadmasth, pre-omniscient).
  • Verse 20: the path of humility is the Vītrāgs’ own path; only a few grasp its real purpose (to melt swachhand).
  • Verse 21 (today): a stern caution: humility must be intelligent (vivek). Misplaced vinay toward an asadguru harms both him and you.

2) The bridge: “vinay” needs vivek (discernment)

Humility is praiseworthy—even capable of carrying a disciple to omniscience—but you must know:

  • To whom to offer it (the Sadguru, not merely a nice person),
  • Why, when, where, how, and how much to offer it (protocol + inner meaning),
  • How to keep love + respect together:
    • Love draws you near (but can indulge ego: “come to my house…”).
    • Respect is a direct blow to the ego (“Come whenever you wish.”).

Etiquette note he gave: except for scriptures (hand them to the Guru), offer everything at the feet. The point isn’t ritualism; it’s cultivating inner deference guided by vivek.

3) The five marks of a Sadguru (the right recipient of vinay)

Many teachers are kind, eloquent, charitable—good—but not for that reason a Sadguru. The teacher reiterated the five must-haves:

1. Self-realisation (direct knowledge of the Self),

2. Equanimity,

3. Life aligned to past karma (acceptance; no egoic meddling),

4. Unprecedented, soul-stirring speech (that awakens),

5. Highest scriptural insight (not bookishness—living understanding).

Without these, one may still help worldly matters (influence, timing, fixes), but cannot help spiritually. Vinay belongs to the Sadguru, not to anyone wearing the garb.

4) Why verse 21 “turns” to the asadguru

It looks like he starts speaking to the asadguru—but the aim is to protect the seeker:

  • “If this is the fate of an asadguru who misuses your reverence, imagine the damage to you if you follow him.”
  • Two audiences, one warning: a jolt for the imposter, a shield for the seeker.

5) Who is an asadguru? (three traits)

1. Lacks the Sadguru qualities above.

2. Desires honor/worship (hunger for being adored, being “Guru”).

3. Uses disciples to satisfy desires—comforts, wealth, name, fame, senses.

He knows he is not Self-realised yet poses as if he were; that’s not naïve ignorance—it’s impurity.

Typical tells (ranging from obvious to subtle):
  • Obvious: astrologising your life, promising worldly boons, beads/rings/quick fixes; scaring or flattering to keep you hooked.
  • Subtle (harder): speaks of ātma, quotes scriptures, does austerities, conducts bhakti—outwardly identical to a true Guru. Only nearness and reading the eyes/state reveals the truth.

Your task is to be a true seeker (not an attention-seeker). Don’t pick a “guru” because he always gives you time or praises you. Seek Self-realisation—even if he never meets you, the right one is right.

6) The verse’s two lines (sense and impact)

Line 1 — “Asadguru e vinayno… lābh lahey.” An untrue teacher takes advantage (undue) of a disciple’s humility/devotion.

Line 2 — “Mahamohniyā karmathī buḍe bhavjal māhī.” By generating enormous deluding karma (mahā-mohaniya), he drowns in the ocean of rebirth.

What is mahā-mohaniya?
  • Not just a flare of anger or a passing attachment.
  • Deep delusion (mūḍhatā)—a willful falsity that misleads others from the right path.
  • Hence the “mahā”: the magnitude of karmic bondage surges.
How heavy is the fall?

He illustrated it with traditional measures: vast stretches of time (think multiple time-cycles; dozens upon dozens of Tīrthaṅkaras coming and going) while the impostor wanders, even risking unimaginably low forms of life. The point isn’t the math; it’s the gravity.

7) Why the bondage is so severe (four reasons he implied)

1. Posing as Sadguru—root falsehood.

2. Stealing disciples’ vinay meant for the Vītrāg-path.

3. Derailing seekers (double harm: to himself and to them).

4. Feeding greed/ego with sacred offerings (prostituting devotion).

8) What this means for you (the seeker)

  • Devotion is for your benefit, not the Guru’s. The Enlightened don’t desire worship; but without devotion, teachings often don’t bear fruit in the aspirant.
  • Therefore, separate chaff from grain—like the elders sorting pulses: discriminate patiently, humbly, intelligently.
  • If you’re in a trap unknowingly, show compassion to yourself (and others) and step out. Don’t let pious effort be wasted.

9) Clear, quick checklists

Red flags (asadguru tilt)
  • Craves titles, display, and being worshipped.
  • Trades in astrology/amulets/quick boons, past-life gossip, fear or flattery.
  • Demands money/comforts/personal service to feed lifestyle.
  • Keeps you busy with outer shows, little to no inner transformation.
  • Your swachhand is unchallenged (or even stoked).
Green lights (Sadguru tilt)
  • Centers you on the Self; challenges ego & self-will.
  • Equanimous under praise/blame; no hunger for honor.
  • Speech pierces and clarifies; life models acceptance.
  • Steers you away from dependency on him toward direct seeing.
  • Your humility grows clean (love + respect), your swachhand thins.

10) Practical vivek protocols (today)

1. Before bowing, remember why: “This vinay is to dissolve my ego and align me to ājñā.”

2. Scriptures → to the hands; everything else to the feet (outer protocol as inner training).

3. Ask for inner help, not favors: “Please show me my blind self-will; command me.”

4. Audit after satsang: “Did swachhand relent? Did I obey even a small ajñā?”

11) Pocket recap (one breath) Humility can carry you all the way—but only when given to a true Sadguru. Verse 21 warns: an imposter who exploits your reverence binds colossal delusion and sinks; and if you follow him, you squander your life. Use vivek: place your vinay wisely, let it break your ego, and keep walking toward direct Self-experience.
