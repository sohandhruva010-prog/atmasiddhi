Hospital example

  • In hospitals, usually there are more visitors than patients.
  • Not talking about COVID restrictions—normally:
    • 1 patient
    • many visitors

Spiritual application

  • Many people have enough punya/merit to “come to Sadguru.”
  • But the Guru asks implicitly:
    • Have you come as a visitor or a patient?
Visitor mindset
  • Enjoys darshan, moments, atmosphere
  • But “antar bhed na kai” → no inner change / no increase in worthiness
Patient mindset
  • Comes to be treated
  • Treatment = correction + inner transformation
  • Worthiness increases (faith, subsidence, detachment, devotion)

“Scanning” analogy (CT Scan)

  • Enlightened One doesn’t need incidents to know your level.
  • Like a CT scan, He can “scan”:
    • worthiness of faith
    • worthiness of upsham (subsidence)
    • worthiness of vairagya (detachment)
    • worthiness of bhakti/devotion
  • He can see:
    • how much association is needed
    • how much seva / conversation
    • how much ajna
    • how much correction is needed
Key line
  • “He may give His body to the lucky, but He will give His heart only to the worthy.”

How we realize our unworthiness

  • We realize only when an incident happens → we react → we see our lack.
  • But Sadguru knows without needing an incident.

2) Why the disciple is “worthy” (and what happens at the end of Sixth Truth)

  • The disciple’s worthiness is visible because:

1. doubts are presented properly

2. Sadguru’s replies flow beautifully

  • After the Sixth Fundamental Truth ends (verse 118):

1. disciple doesn’t bring new doubts

2. he expresses joy / attainment, not confusion

  • Guru’s prayer:

1. May all beings get Param Krupalu Dev’s refuge

2. Those who already have it—may it become falibhut (fruitful)

Word-note: “falibhut”

  • “fal” = fruit
  • “fal + ee + bhut” → “falibhut” = becoming fruitful
  • Similar example: “sheetalibhut” = becoming cooled
  • He links “fruitful refuge” with:
    • increasing worthiness
    • self-realisation
    • progressing on path of liberation
    • “janma tehna alp” = very few births left (he says maybe 1–3, max 15)

3) Core recap from Verse 89–90: the principle being built

The big principle (from verse 89–90)

  • Every activity is fruitful.
  • This includes:

1. auspicious activity (shubh)

2. inauspicious activity (ashubh)

3. withdrawal from shubhashubh (nivrutti) — this also bears fruit

Fruit of shubh/ashubh → He condenses it into one word: Transmigration (leave one body, take another)

Fruit of withdrawing / abiding in Self → Liberation (eventually absolute liberation)

Activity never disappears (important nuance)

  • He says: activity is seen in all substances (3rd Fundamental Truth idea).
  • Even in moksh, there is still a kind of “activity”:
    • abiding in the Self
    • some “-ing” verb remains
    • some kriya remains

So the real choice is not “activity vs no activity.” The real choice is:

  • shubhashubh kriya (mixed activity: punya/paap mode) vs
  • Self-abidance (withdrawal from shubhashubh)

4) “Can shubhashubh tendency be destroyed?” Yes — 3 methods

He states: this tendency is reducible and destroyable.

The three words he highlights (very important)

1. Anabhyasthi – discontinuance of practice

  • Stop rehearsing the old reaction-pattern.

2. Aparichay thi – avoiding contact / staying away

  • Like staying away from the “stove” (causal trigger).

3. Upsham – calming down / subsiding

  • Pacifying the inner turbulence.

He emphasizes gradual training:

  • gradually intensity reduces
  • duration reduces
  • frequency reduces
  • and eventually → absolute liberation

5) Self-awareness clarification (not just sitting with eyes closed)

He corrects a common misunderstanding:

  • “Abiding in Self” does not mean:
    • eyes closed
    • lotus posture
    • sitting in a corner all day

Instead:

  • Self-awareness must remain even during activity
  • He stresses the word awareness, not “experience”:
    • because “no-mind experience” doesn’t last long
    • but the connection/after-effect lingers

Practical fruit of Self-awareness

  • No new bondage (no new earning of punya/paap)
  • Old karmas shed faster
  • Technical words:
    • samvar = stop influx
    • nirjara = shed old

6) Big discussion: Why Navkar says “destroy paap” but Atmasiddhi says “destroy shubhashubh”

He returns to the homework-type question:

  • Navkar mantra: “savva pavappanasano” (destroys sins/paap)
  • Atmasiddhi: “teh shubhashubh chhedta” (destroy both good & bad karmas)

Group answers in the transcript (he praises them)

1. Navkar is general: If it explicitly said “destroy punya also,” some people might misuse it and stop even basic auspicious discipline.

2. Navkar is salutations to the five supreme beings: Its fruit is destruction of sins from that devotional angle.

3. A deeper answer (important):

  • Bowing/salutation becomes meaningful when it turns inward
  • As you go deeper in chanting, you connect to the Self
  • That Self-abidance ends both punya and paap eventually

Key correction he repeats strongly

  • Merits cannot destroy demerits.
    • If you gain punya, you still keep paap as paap.
    • Only nirjara sheds karmas (merit or demerit).

7) Are merits helpful or not? “Helpful initially, but not final.”

He gives a balanced, staged teaching:

Moksh requires destruction of both punya + paap

Because:

  • Shubh is not your true nature
  • Ashubh is not your true nature
  • Moksh is attained only by going beyond both

But merits can still help early-stage seekers

He says you cannot declare “punya is useless,” because there are two kinds of punya:

1. Merit that begets demerit

  • gives material associations, luxuries, distractions

2. Merit that begets merit

  • gives supportive conditions:
    • human birth
    • Sadguru’s association
    • right scriptures
    • prolonged stay in Dharampur, etc.

So:

  • in the beginning, punya can be a support-tool
  • but later, even that must be left

Warning about wrong order

Some people may misuse “destroy punya also” to skip discipline:

  • “I can’t stop night-eating, but I can stop temple.”
  • “I can stop ayambil easily.” So they drop helpful supports too early.

8) Two major analogies for “use punya, then leave it”

A) Thieves and Police analogy

  • Thieves entered your house → you call police.
  • Police help catch thieves.
  • But after thieves go:
    • police cannot stay forever either.
  • Even police must leave.

Meaning:

  • Use auspicious supports to remove paap tendencies,
  • but eventually you must transcend even that support.

He jokes:

  • “Thank you” sometimes really means: now go.

B) Dharampur → Mumbai via Vapi analogy (very clear)

  • Goal: Mumbai = moksh
  • Vapi = punya / auspicious practices
  • You must make effort to reach Vapi (you need it on the way)
  • But once you reach Vapi:
    • you must leave Vapi to reach Mumbai
  • Don’t treat Vapi like the destination.

He links this directly:

  • Chanting Navkar is punya (auspicious)
  • It helps stop paap (downward pull)
  • If done deeply, it turns inward → Self-awareness
  • That ends even punya and fulfills: “teh shubhashubh chhedta, upje moksh swabhav.”

9) Now the verse focus: Verse 91 — “outer/physical liberation”

He says:

  • Verse 90 explained inner / non-physical liberation:
    • no shubhashubh feelings inside
  • Verse 91 explains outer / physical liberation:
    • no body, no karma, no material associations

Verse 91 (as explained)

“Dehadik sanyogno, atyantik viyog;
Siddha moksh shashwat padey, nij anant sukhbhog.”
Key terms
  • Dehadik sanyog = associations starting with:
    • body (deh)
    • karma
    • family & objects (house, car, chair, table, etc.)
  • Atyantik viyog = absolute/total separation
    • separation that will never reappear
  • Siddha moksh = accomplished liberation (nothing left to do)
  • Shashwat pad = permanent state (he prefers “permanent” more than “eternal” wording)
  • Nij anant sukh bhog = enjoying one’s own infinite bliss

Short meaning (what he wants you to write)

  • With permanent disassociation from body + karmas + all associations,
  • the soul remains permanently liberated
  • and experiences its own infinite bliss

10) Crucial distinction: Death vs Liberation (and why liberation means “no reassociation”)

He explains:

  • Separation of soul from body is called death, not liberation.
  • In the endless past, the soul separated from bodies many times, but immediately got another body (reassociation).

So what makes moksh unique?

  • Separation that happens such that reassociation never happens again. That is atyantik viyog.

He briefly touches a “campfire” discussion:

  • brain-dead vs heart functioning, artificial support
  • he says spirituality defines death simply:
    • when soul leaves, death occurs
    • decay indicates soul has left
  • He mentions he will bring Deyaan’s case later for discussion.

(He’s not fully teaching that here—just showing the difference between theories.)

11) Why bodiless = karmaless (they go together)

He makes a clean causal chain:

  • To have a body, you need karma operative.
  • To have material karma, you need karma-binding feelings (likes-dislikes).
  • In liberation:
    • karma-binding feelings are destroyed
    • so new karmic matter isn’t acquired
    • nothing remains to ripen → no body formation

Therefore:

  • bodiless and karmaless go hand in hand

He clarifies:

  • If someone is “liberated” but still has body/karma untouched:
    • you may call that jivanmukta
  • But Siddha state requires:
    • no body
    • no karma at all (past exhausted + no new formed)

12) “No Avatar” point (Siddha never returns)

He states firmly:

  • A Siddha will never come back.
  • No compassion, no “avatar” idea can make them return. Because return needs:

1. body

2. karma

3. karma-binding feelings

And he insists:

  • these are destroyed, not merely suppressed
  • “ashes are not even left”

So:

  • state never ends
  • never lost
  • never return to worldly existence

13) Substance vs state: Milk → Curd (Yogurt) analogy

He addresses other schools (extinction/merging ideas) and gives Jain framing:

  • Some say individuality ends / soul extinguishes (he mentions Buddhism lamp/oil example).
  • Jain view:
    • the substance is not destroyed
    • the worldly state is destroyed

Milk-curd example:

  • Milk becomes curd/yogurt:
    • “end of milk-state forever”
    • “beginning of curd-state forever”
  • Substance remains; form/state changes permanently.

So:

  • individuality is not “killed”
  • it becomes permanently established in pure state

14) “Don’t depict Siddha with a body” (formless point)

He criticizes a common depiction:

  • God with 4 hands / 10 hands, lotus, disc, etc.

He says:

  • If you speak of Siddha, you cannot show a body.
  • Siddha = bodiless → formless.

He adds:

  • if people visualize a form, it’s usually the last body’s form (for devotional convenience),
  • but truth is: Siddha is formless, karmaless.

15) “Won’t we get bored in moksh?” — kids + video games example

He says kids often ask:

  • “What will we do there forever?”
  • “What if we get bored with that happiness?”

He uses worldly boredom examples:

  • kids get the best video games → bored after hours
  • bored of one restaurant → go to another
  • bored of one friend group → switch groups

But in moksh:

  • there is no “switching”
  • yet no boredom, because it is nij anant sukh:
    • bliss of the Self
    • infinite intensity
    • permanent duration

16) Why bliss is infinite (two reasons)

1. Bliss is the nature of the soul

2. The karmas obstructing bliss are destroyed

  • and no new karmas can arise because no karma-binding feelings exist

17) Three types of happiness (important structured teaching)

He lists 3, and also gives a helpful English mapping:

1. Bhautik sukh (material/worldly “happiness”)

  • He calls it more accurately joy
  • because it includes duality and sorrow mixed in

2. Upsham sukh (happiness from subsidence/pacification)

  • peace because passions have calmed down
  • example phrases people say:
    • “Thank God, no lust for 10 days.”
    • “For 25 days, no anger/ego/comparison.”
    • “I feel much better; there is peace.”
  • this is happiness from upsham (subsidence), not yet moksh

3. Atmik sukh (bliss of the soul)

  • he calls this Bliss
  • this is what Siddha experiences: nij anant sukh bhog

18) Final “3-point conclusion” (he summarizes liberation very cleanly)

He concludes Siddhahood / physical liberation has 3 features:

1. Bodiless + Karmaless

  • ultimate state cannot be rightly described with a body

2. Eternal/Permanent state

  • substance remains; worldly state is ended forever
  • no extinction, no merging that deletes individuality

3. Infinite bliss

  • forever, without getting tired

“Amazon deal” joke

  • Work hard properly for one life (say 80 years)
  • get eternal bliss
  • “You won’t get such a deal on Amazon.”
