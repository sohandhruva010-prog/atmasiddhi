Topic: How non-sentient (jada) karmic matter can still give results

Where this verse fits

  • We are in the 4th Fundamental Truth: The soul is the receiver (bhoktā) of the fruits of karmas.
  • Disciple’s main objection (Verse 79): “Karma is lifeless → it can’t know/plan → so how can it give fruits?”
  • Guru’s resolution of this objection is in 3 verses: 82–84
    • Verse 82: explains bondage + programming (bhaavkarma programs dravya karma)
    • Verse 83: explains HOW lifeless matter can show effects (knowledge is not required for effect)
    • Verse 84: completes/extends the explanation further (next step)

✅ So Verse 83 is the “execution logic”: Even if something is lifeless, it can still produce effects by its nature, and the experiencer is the conscious soul.

1) What the Guru says is “incomplete” so far (why Verse 83 is needed)

He begins by reminding:

  • Up to now (Verse 82): we understood only bonding + programming/stamping.
  • The remaining question is: “Okay, programming happened… but HOW do those particles actually show fruits later?”

So Verse 83 is to answer:

How do inanimate karmas give results without knowledge/intention?

2) Side-point: “Programming details” exist in scripture (Gautamswami → Mahavira)

Before coming to the verse, he briefly shows that:

  • The “what fruit comes from what feeling/action” is extremely detailed in Jain Agamas.

Examples he gives (keep these in notes; he uses them to show depth of karmic science)

  • Gautamswami asking Bhagwan Mahavira: “If someone harbours such feelings… what must he endure/enjoy/suffer?”
  • Those answers are collected across scriptures, and systematised in texts like:
    • Tattvarth Sutra (he mentions it as a compilation/systematic form)
    • Bhagvati Sutra: he says Mahavira answered 36,000 questions of Gautamswami.
    • A smaller scripture formed from this: Gautam Pruchha (“Pruchha = asks”)

Personal childhood example (why he mentions this)

  • He says his mother read Gautam Pruchha to him during Samayik when he was 6–7.
  • At that age he could already connect:
    • “If I do this → I will bear such fruit.”
    • “If I don’t want the consequence → I must stop here.”

✅ Purpose of this whole opening:

  • To show: Jain philosophy doesn’t say “fruits are random.”
  • It says there is a precise causal system, but Verse 83 is about the mechanism of effects from lifeless matter.

3) Bridge from daily life: “Programming exists even in ordinary objects”

He connects Verse 82 to everyday experience:

Examples

  • Clock: you program it now → it rings later exactly as set
  • Oven: you program it → after 20 minutes it beeps and the cake is baked

✅ Purpose: We already accept “program now → result later” in daily life. Now the remaining puzzle is: how does lifeless matter deliver the effect?

4) Verse 83 — The verse + word-by-word meaning

From the lecture, the verse idea is:

Core illustration

“Jher sudha samje nahin; jiva khay fal thay;
em shubhashubh karmanu bhoktapanu janay.”

Word meanings (as Guru explains)

  • Jher = poison
  • Sudha = amrit / nectar
  • Samje nahin = do not know / have no knowledge
  • Jiva khay = the living being/person consumes
  • Fal thay = result happens / effect is experienced
  • Em = similarly
  • Shubh–ashubh karma = auspicious and inauspicious karmas
  • Bhoktapanu = being receiver (enjoyer/sufferer)
  • Janay = understand/know (i.e., “know this principle”)

✅ Main meaning (straight): Poison and nectar do not “know” their power, but the one who consumes them experiences effects. Similarly, karmas (even if insentient) show effects, and the soul experiences them.

5) The disciple’s mistake that Verse 83 destroys

The disciple is mixing up two ideas:

1. Inanimate cannot know ✅ (true)

2. Inanimate cannot do anything ❌ (false)

Verse 83 is mainly the correction of (2).

The Guru keeps repeating:

Lifeless cannot know — agreed. But lifeless “cannot do” — that is a mistake.

6) Main illustration: Poison & Nectar

What the Guru wants you to see

  • Poison does not know:
    • “I can kill.”
  • Nectar does not know:
    • “I can rejuvenate / give benefit / immortality association.”

Yet:

  • If someone consumes poison → harmful effect occurs
  • If someone consumes nectar → beneficial effect occurs

✅ The crucial principle: Knowledge is not required for effect. Effect happens due to objective nature/property of the substance.

7) Kitchen-spices extension (he deliberately expands the example)

He says: if you start insisting “lifeless must know,” then you must question everything in the kitchen.

Examples he lists

  • Salt
  • Chilli / chilli powder / green chillies
  • Pepper
  • Wine / alcohol (intoxication effect)
  • Cinnamon
  • Clove
  • Cardamom
  • Black salt
  • Mustard
  • Asafoetida (hing)

The way he dramatizes it

He asks:

  • Do chillies have a plan:
    • “We will enter Maulikji’s body and change his facial expressions”?
  • Do they understand what happens in:
    • tongue, intestine, oesophagus, throat/food pipe?
  • Do they intend anything?
  • Do they “know the consumer”?

Answer: No. Yet the consumer experiences the effect.

✅ What this proves for the karma question: Even if karmas are lifeless, demanding “knowledge” is unnecessary.

8) Alcohol example (special case)

He adds:

  • Wine doesn’t know it must intoxicate Soham.
  • Still intoxication happens because that is its nature.

✅ This is used to strengthen: Effect is property-based, not intention-based.

9) “Nothing to question here” (why the disciple’s question becomes unnecessary)

He states: Once you accept poison/spices/alcohol can create effects without knowledge, then:

  • the disciple’s question (“how can lifeless karma know?”) loses force.

10) The philosophical punch-line (very high-yield)

He quotes/recalls a line from the Letter of Six Fundamentals:

“Sarva padarth arthakriyasampanna chhe.” Every object is endowed with some visible or invisible activity.

His explanation

  • If you observe any substance minutely:
    • it has activity (even if not visible to eyes)
  • This applies to:
    • animate and inanimate
  • Therefore:
    • “Inanimate cannot know” ✅
    • “Inanimate cannot act / produce effects” ❌

✅ This line becomes the “theory statement” behind all his examples.

11) Strong medical example: anaesthesia/chloroform (non-living yet massive effect)

He gives a long real-life illustration to hammer the point:

A) Small pain example (stapler pin / needle)

  • He got hurt by a stapler pin
  • Because he’s on blood thinners, there was a lot of blood
  • The wound wasn’t big, but pain was real
  • He distinguishes:
    • pain happens (physical)
    • equanimity/non-equanimity is about reaction (inner state)

✅ Purpose: Even tiny non-living causes can create real effects.

B) Surgery example (anaesthesia effect)

He says: After anaesthesia, doctors can:

  • move scissors/knives over you
  • cut you open
  • do intense operations And you don’t say “ouch.”

He calls it clearly:

  • anaesthesia/chloroform is lifeless/insensate
  • it does not know its power
  • it does not know whom it is affecting
  • yet the effect is huge (knowledge veiled, pain not felt)

C) Navkar Mantra episode

  • Doctor (Dr Rashmi Raja) asked him: “Recite Navkar Mantra in your mind.”
  • He started: “Namo Arihantanam…”
  • He couldn’t even finish the full mantra
  • Next thing: he regained consciousness in ICU

✅ Purpose: This is a very powerful “you don’t question here” moment: A lifeless substance can completely alter consciousness and experience.

12) Simple thorn example (community garden)

He says:

  • Walk barefoot (no slippers)
  • Thorn pricks you
  • Thorn doesn’t know:
    • “It’s Shebani’s leg”
    • “I must inflict pain on her”
  • Yet pain happens.

✅ Purpose: A clean, everyday proof: No knowledge, still effect.

13) Key link back to Verse 82: “Who put this nature into karmic matter?”

This is crucial because it ties Verse 83 back into the “programming” framework.

He says:

  • Karmic matter in the atmosphere was just neutral matter.
  • During bondage, a specific prakruti (nature/type) gets programmed.

And he states directly:

This nature (prakruti bandh) is programmed during bondage.

Then he reminds:

  • “I said four things…” (the stamping dimensions)
    • how/why (type)
    • how long (duration)
    • intensity
    • what type of fruit, etc.

And he says:

Don’t even say “soul” casually — say bhaav karma (state/impure feelings) programmed everything.

✅ Meaning: Even if dravya karma is lifeless, it is not “random matter.” It is configured matter due to bhaavkarma at bondage-time.

14) Funny but important “container” example (lifeless in lifeless)

He notes:

  • Food products/spices are lifeless and have unique properties.
  • The container is also lifeless, so:
    • lifeless in lifeless → no impact.

Joke:

  • If container were affected like a living being:
    • the spice bottle would start dancing
    • and finish all the chilli powder

✅ Purpose: To highlight: effects manifest when the “right conditions” exist (i.e., a suitable recipient/medium).

15) Flowing river → round pebble (inanimate shaping inanimate)

He gives another strong natural example:

  • River has:
    • no plan
    • no intention
    • no knowledge
  • Stone has:
    • no consciousness
  • Yet, due to flow/current:
    • rough stone becomes round pebble

He adds childhood memory:

  • collecting pebbles, bringing home
  • mother throwing them away
  • painting them red for no reason

✅ Purpose: To show: even two inanimate things can produce transformation/effects through natural activity.

16) “Objective property” principle (very exam-friendly)

He concludes the logic in a crisp way:

  • Every substance has an objective quality (fixed nature)
    • sugar → sweet effect
    • poison → harmful effect
    • chilli → spicy (cannot complain “it’s not salty” after adding chillies)

✅ Mapping to karma:

  • Karmas don’t know:
    • “I am jnanavarniya, mohaniya, vedniya…”
  • Karmas don’t know:
    • “I must inflict this intensity on this soul”
  • Yet effects happen because:
    • the karmic matter has a programmed nature and shows results at maturity.

17) “Enjoyer/sufferer” language — and his important correction

He says he uses “enjoy/suffer” reluctantly.

Relative (low) viewpoint

  • merits (punya) = enjoy
  • demerits (paap) = suffer So we call: auspicious/inauspicious karmas.

Absolute viewpoint

He says:

Karma can only make you suffer. It cannot make you enjoy.

Then his famous metaphor:

  • merits vs demerits = golden cage vs iron cage
  • both are cages
  • liberation needs freedom from both.

✅ Purpose: Even while proving “receiver of fruits,” he ensures you don’t get attached to “good karma” as final good.

18) Final “proof is complete” + modern analogy (driverless engines)

He says: “We had to prove the soul is receiver — it’s done.”

Then he adds:

  • electric trains/engines exist
  • now driverless engines exist
  • engine doesn’t know “Mumbai to Delhi/Chennai”
  • it is programmed and runs accordingly

✅ Why he adds this: Because once you understand “programmer exists” (bhaavkarma), you stop asking: “How can lifeless do this?”

19) What Verse 83 establishes (core takeaways)

The central claim

Inanimate karmas don’t need knowledge/intention to give results — they show effects by objective nature (configured at bondage). And the conscious soul experiences those effects → hence soul is receiver (bhoktā).

The disciple’s doubt is resolved because:

  • “Lifeless cannot know” is accepted.
  • But “lifeless cannot produce effects” is proven false by many examples.
