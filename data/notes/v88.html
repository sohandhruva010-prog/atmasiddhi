Context recap (from previous video)

  • The disciple is already convinced about:

1. Soul exists

2. Soul is eternal

3. Soul is doer of karma

4. Soul is receiver of karma fruits

Now:

  • A doubt arises about Truth #5: Liberation (Moksh).

This video = the disciple’s second argument (Verse 88)

  • First argument (verse 87): “Infinite time passed + impurity still exists → moksh seems impossible.”
  • Second argument (verse 88, this video): “In all visible existences, I see only karma + karma-fruits → where is moksh?”

2) The opening teaching: “Vivek” (discriminating intellect)

The speaker begins with a key point: the disciple’s vivek has bloomed.

2.1 What “vivek” means (as defined here)

  • Vivek = you don’t need to keep questioning your Guru.
  • You understand internally, by your own discrimination.

2.2 How the speaker “measures” vivek (practical marker)

  • “The more you ask, I know he has less vivek.”
  • Less vivek = spiritual immaturity.

2.3 Strength vs vivek (important nuance)

He distinguishes:

  • Understanding (vivek) grows within you,
  • Strength (bal) may still need repeated contact.
Example/teaching:
  • “For strength, we go time and again.”
  • But strength comes when:
    • “our eyes meet His eyes”
    • or “just think of Him and you get the strength”
  • You don’t need to ask repeatedly.

3) The lens used for the disciple’s new argument: the 4 gatis

He says the disciple looks at transmigration (birth–rebirth) across the four states of existence (gati):

1. Human beings

2. Animals and plants

3. Heavenly beings (dev)

4. Infernal beings (narak / hellish)

The disciple can see these four, but:

  • “He can’t see the 5th: Moksh.”

So his reasoning is based on observable categories.

4) Verse 88 (disciple’s argument) — the

exact structure The disciple presents:

Line A: Shubh karma → fruits in heaven

  • “Shubh karey…” = if the soul does auspicious karmas (punya / shubh)
  • “Fal bhogve…” = he enjoys the fruit
  • “Devadi gatimai…” = in heavenly state, etc.
Speaker’s extra clarification/example:
  • Heaven is “devadi” (heaven, celestial states etc.)
  • And he adds: even here a person can be “multi-millionaire,” which is “like heavenly” (comfort/luxury).

Line B: Ashubh karma → fruits in hell

  • “Ashubh karey…” = if the soul does inauspicious karmas
  • “Narkadi fal…” = bears fruit in hell etc.

Line C: The main punchline

  • “Karma rahit na kyai” = “Nowhere do I find a soul without karma.”

Meaning:

  • In every gati the disciple can see, karma exists and karma-fruits are experienced.

5) The disciple’s logic, step-by-step (very clean)

This is the argument in a tight chain:

Step 1: Karmas are only of two broad kinds (as he’s using it)

  • Shubh (auspicious)
  • Ashubh (inauspicious)

Step 2: Each kind has a predictable destination/fruit

  • Shubh → heaven etc.
  • Ashubh → hell etc.

Step 3: Across the four gatis, he sees no karmaless soul

  • Humans: bondage + fruition both happening
  • Animals/plants: also under karma
  • Heavenly beings: enjoying punya-fruits
  • Infernal beings: suffering paap-fruits

So:

  • “In all four states, I don’t see any soul karmaless.”

Step 4: Therefore moksh seems false

Because moksh, in his definition, means:

  • a karmaless state (no karma at all)

So he concludes:

  • “Where is the question of moksh?”

6) Important clarification the speaker makes: “Liberated” can be confusing — disciple specifically means “karmaless”

The speaker pauses and clarifies terminology:

6.1 “Liberation” has many casual meanings people use

Examples he lists:

  • Jivanmukta = “liberated while still alive”
  • Videhi mukta / Deh chhata nirvan etc. (different liberation-phrases)
  • People may even say: “My Guru is liberated…”

6.2 But the disciple is not using the loose word “liberated”

He is specifically saying:

  • “karmaless” (karma rahit)

6.3 Example: Even Param Krupalu Dev had karmas (as per this talk)

The speaker says:

  • “No, Param Krupalu Dev also had karmas… sickness etc.”

So:

  • In the four gatis, you won’t “see” a karmaless embodied being.

This supports why the disciple feels stuck:

  • “I can’t find karmalessness anywhere.”

7) The disciple’s emotional tone: he becomes saddened / pessimistic

The speaker notes:

  • “It seems the poor disciple is saddened.”

7.1 What the disciple is grieving

He bemoans the “fate” of the soul:

  • Either heaven or hell
  • And between them: human and animal existence
  • Repeating endlessly → “no end to transmigration”

8) Core analogy: good and bad are BOTH shackles (gold vs iron)

This is one of the biggest examples in this video.

8.1 The disciple’s deeper point

Even if you choose “good”:

  • you still remain embodied (in samsar) Even if you choose “bad”:
  • you remain embodied (in suffering)

So both keep you tied.

8.2 Golden vs iron shackles (very memorable)

  • Merits (punya/shubh) = golden shackles
  • Demerits (paap/ashubh) = iron shackles

Both are still shackles.

8.3 Handcuff example

He gives a physical illustration:

  • Left hand tied with gold chain
  • Right hand tied with iron chain

Question:

  • If you remove only the iron (sins), are you “liberated”? Answer implied:
  • No. Liberation means freedom from both.

8.4 Dictionary-style definition (as he frames it)

Liberation would mean:

  • “Freedom from all shackles”
  • i.e., break both chains.

So the disciple argues:

  • “But I only ever see two choices: good or bad.”
  • Both keep you tied → “there is nothing like moksh.”

9) The “third option” joke: if you do neither, you’re called lazy

The speaker adds a practical, everyday example:

If someone says:

  • “I don’t want good karma (because heaven is still bondage)”
  • “I don’t want bad karma (because hell is suffering)”
  • “So I will just lie on the bed”

Then society says:

  • “lazy”
  • “good for nothing”
  • (polite version) “happy-go-lucky”
  • “care-free”

So the disciple’s feeling becomes:

  • There’s no workable route to moksh.

10) “Flow with the flow” example — correct mantra, wrong stage

This is a major cautionary teaching embedded here.

10.1 What he says

  • “Flow with the flow” is a good mantra…

But:

  • It depends at what stage you use it.

10.2 Two outcomes

If it pushes you into:

  • witnessing mode (detached observation) → fantastic

If it makes you:

  • lazy / unprepared → wrong stage, wrong use

10.3 Practical warning

Something unfavourable happens:

  • people don’t “flow”
  • they react

So he says:

  • plan / prepare so you don’t need to react, or be ready internally

11) Big instruction about “path steps” + “Ajna” (obedient practice)

Even though he says “this is not the verse,” he deliberately teaches discipline.

11.1 He references the “path of liberation” section later

  • He says: from 108 to 113, the path is shown stepwise.
  • People might try to “skip steps” — he warns against it.

11.2 “First I check your discipline in spiritual ‘rituals’”

He says he prefers calling them “rituals” (lower word) because:

  • “Everything is ajna” (Guru’s instruction)
  • And you love the one who gave the ajna
Key metaphor:
  • “Ajna should be like your teddy bear — cling to it.”

11.3 Emotional vs devotional

He says:

  • If you don’t cling to ajna, you’re emotional, not devotional.

11.4 Don’t let your subconscious “misuse” advanced philosophy

He warns:

  • Don’t let your subconscious mind start saying:
    • “enough of rituals”
    • “bondage-liberation is imagination”
    • “nature is pure, so nothing to do” while your actual state is still deluded.

He says:

  • People talk big under delusion,
  • then a few likes-dislikes + ego-hurts happen,
  • and they behave worse than a normal person.
Strong line:
  • “Keep all this in your faith, not on your tongue.”

12) The disciple’s cynicism: “Why rejoice? Even good ends only in heaven”

The disciple’s pessimism is expressed like this:

  • You do devotion, sermons, service…
  • And the best you get is “maximum heaven”
  • Then in heaven you “do all the mess possible”
  • And the cycle repeats

So he says:

  • “I have the right to be cynical/pessimistic.”
  • “I have no reason to rejoice.”
  • “I have lost hope even in doing good.” Because good still keeps you a worldly soul.

13) Example-set: “You get tired hearing it 3 times, but you’ve done it since infinite time”

This is another memorable illustration.

He says:

  • You’re tired of hearing “shubh/ashubh” just repeated 3 times…
  • but you’ve been doing it since infinite time and aren’t tired?

Then he links it to a virtue:

  • If you truly get tired, grief of transmigration arises (a positive spiritual virtue)

He references “four virtues” (he names a few here):

  • subsided passions
  • only desire of liberation
  • grief of transmigration (he references this set, without fully listing all four in this clip)

He even says:

  • “I’ll repeat this 50–100 times and see…”

14) Long example block: children, games, and “constructive activity”

He gives a string of examples to show repetitive, unproductive loops:

14.1 Pebbles game

Children stack pebbles:

  • place 3, 4th falls → restart
  • waste 4–6 hours

Adults say:

  • “Do something constructive.”

14.2 Video games

Parents shout:

  • “What do you get from this?”
  • No certificate, no money, no gift
  • You just get happy and restart from zero

Purpose:

  • He’s hinting: worldly cycles are like this too — repetitive loops.

15) Big personal example: Nemiji’s instruments, harmonica, art — “balance head with heart”

This section is long and very example-rich; here’s the structured capture.

15.1 Nemiji learning instruments

He says:

  • Nemiji is learning three instruments
  • He (speaker) has to keep listening — “this is my equanimity”

15.2 Harmonica example

  • Nemiji plays harmonica
  • Starts at 6 in the morning
  • Asks: “Bapa tell me what am I playing?”
  • Speaker replies: “First learn properly then I’ll understand!”

15.3 Why he encouraged this (important teaching)

He told him: To balance “head activities” (intellectual work), you need:

  • lots of meditation, or
  • lots of bhakti, or
  • art and music

He says Nemiji is doing all three.

15.4 Why balance matters (strong warning example)

If someone uses only “head”:

  • arrogance increases
  • non-emotionality develops
  • you could hurt someone and then eat sweetmeat without caring

Then he gives an extreme cautionary image:

  • head-only people may think only “smart” people deserve to live
  • and the rest should be sent to “concentration camps” (he uses this as a warning about cold intellect without heart)

15.5 “First fill heart activities, then head activities”

He says:

  • heart must be included
  • balance is mandatory or he’ll “chuck you out” (strict discipline tone)

15.6 Emotionality showing through devotion

  • when one devotional song starts, tears of gratitude roll down Nemiji’s eyes

He quotes Hiraben:

He also references:

15.7 The “one ajna” discipline point

He says:

(He admits: this detour isn’t directly about the verse, but he uses it to teach readiness for the path.)

16) Bringing it back to Verse 88: what are “heaven etc.” and “hell etc.” really?

The speaker clarifies the fruits:

16.1 Shubh fruit = comfort/luxury (not necessarily literal heaven right away)

  • “You don’t need to go to first heaven or second”
  • even here: a comfortable luxurious life can resemble “heaven etc.”
He gives a callback example:
  • “we saw that puppy” earlier (from the “soul is the receiver” section) to show how obvious karmic fruit can look as comfort/fortune.

16.2 Ashubh fruit = hell / unfavourable conditions

  • Not only “hell” as location, but:
    • “everything becomes unfavourable”
    • suffering conditions

16.3 The “correspondence principle”

He repeats the core karmic law:

  • As are your feelings/bondage, so are the fruits.
  • To receive fruits, soul must be in an appropriate gati.

He also summarises the gati mapping:

  • extreme auspicious → heaven
  • extreme inauspicious → hell
  • mixed → middle world as human/animal

So the disciple says:

  • this is so complete… why bring moksh?

17) The disciple’s final conclusion in this verse

He says:

1. I see only four gatis.

2. In none of them is a soul totally karmaless.

3. Therefore moksh doesn’t exist, because moksh = karmalessness / permanent purity.

He asks rhetorical questions:

  • Have you seen a karmaless celestial being?
  • A hellish being who becomes karmaless after suffering?
  • A karmaless human?
  • A karmaless animal?

His conclusion:

  • “Soul was never free, will never be free.”

18) Two definitions of Moksh the speaker highlights (very important)

He says moksh can be explained in two ways:

1. Totally free of karmas (karmaless)

2. Soul staying in pure state forever (permanent purity)

He uses his fingers to mark the “two.”
