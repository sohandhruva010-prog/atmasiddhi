Verse 138 of the Upsamhar, and it follows Verse 137 perfectly.

Verse 137 showed us the hypocrite / pseudo-Jnani: someone who can speak lofty spiritual knowledge while internally remaining bound by moh, ego, desires, attachment and aversion.

Param Krupalu Dev then naturally asks:

If that person isn't even worthy of being called a true seeker, what does a genuine Mumukshu actually look like?

Verse 138 gives us a remarkably practical answer—not through philosophy, but through seven observable inner virtues:

“Daya, Shanti, Samta, Kshama, Satya, Tyag, Vairagya;
Hoy Mumukshu ghat vishe, eh saday sujagya.”

In essence:

Compassion, peace, equanimity, forgiveness, truthfulness, renunciation and non-attachment remain awakened within the heart of a genuine seeker of liberation. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

Gurudev calls this the “Thermometer Verse.”

Instead of asking:

“Am I spiritual?”

measure yourself against these seven qualities.

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 138: The Seven Cardinal Virtues of a Mumukshu

The seven are:

1. Daya — compassion

2. Shanti — peace/tranquility

3. Samta — equanimity

4. Kshama — forgiveness

5. Satya — truthfulness

6. Tyag — renunciation

7. Vairagya — non-attachment Pasted text(20261116-004037)

But Gurudev doesn't treat these as seven random virtues.

There is a beautiful progression:

Daya → Shanti → Samta → Kshama → Satya → Tyag → Vairagya

One begins supporting the next.

1. Why introduce the Mumukshu NOW?

Gurudev begins by connecting Verse 138 directly to Verse 137.

Verse 137 said:

Someone may speak tremendous knowledge while internally remaining deluded.

Such a person isn't:

  • a Jnani,
  • or even a genuine Mumukshu.

So naturally:

Then who IS a Mumukshu?

Param Krupalu Dev now gives us the characteristics. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

This is important because the Upsamhar is now showing us three levels:

Verse 137 — Hypocrite
Spiritual speech without transformation

↓

Verse 138 — Mumukshu
One genuinely moving toward liberation

↓

Coming verses — Jnani
One who has actually realized

So we can compare our state honestly.

2. What does “Mumukshu” actually mean?

Gurudev breaks down the word.

Mukti / Moksh = liberation

Mumukshu = one who desires liberation.

Not someone who has already attained total desirelessness.

He still has one dominant desire:

To become free.

To become free from:

  • passions,
  • attachment,
  • aversion,
  • ignorance,
  • embodiment/transmigration.

That desire for liberation begins reorganizing the rest of his life. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

3. Jignasu, Atmarthi and Mumukshu — Param Krupalu Dev keeps repeating the same thing

Gurudev notices that Atmasiddhi has already described a genuine seeker in different ways.

Earlier we had the Jignasu.

Then the Atmarthi, with qualities such as:

  • subsiding passions,
  • exclusive desire for liberation,
  • grief regarding repeated embodiment,
  • compassion.

Now:

Mumukshu.

Why repeat it?

Because this is the Upsamhar.

These are the take-home points.

Param Krupalu Dev is essentially saying:

If you forget everything else, remember what kind of person you need to become. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

4. Gurudev's example of surrender to responsibility

Before explaining the seven virtues, Gurudev tells a story about Atmarpit Rajuji.

He had a very tight deadline for work assigned by the head of his department/team.

Rather than thinking:

“Will she even look at what I submit?”

“Will my suggestions actually be used?”

“Why should I work this hard if it might be discarded?”

his attitude was essentially:

My dharma is to complete it and surrender it to the person responsible. What she does afterward is her concern.

Gurudev loved that attitude.

Why?

Because ego usually says:

“If my work isn't appreciated or implemented, why should I do it?”

Surrender says:

“My responsibility is to sincerely do what is mine to do.”

Gurudev connects this type of dedication with the inner character required of a genuine seeker. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

5. Virtue 1 — DAYA

Compassion

Gurudev begins:

A Mumukshu cannot tolerate suffering.

Not only spiritual suffering.

Any suffering:

  • hunger,
  • physical suffering,
  • emotional suffering,
  • worldly suffering,
  • spiritual suffering.

And this compassion operates in two directions:

Toward others

I don't want you to suffer.

Therefore, at minimum:

I will not become the cause of your suffering.

And beyond that:

I want to contribute to your happiness.

Toward myself

This is equally important.

I don't want to continue making myself suffer through anger, greed, attachment and aversion.

That's antar daya — inner compassion. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

6. Daya is more than “I feel bad for you”

Gurudev's progression is practical:

First:

I don't want you to suffer.

Therefore:

Second:

At least I won't hurt you.

Then:

Third:
Can I contribute to your happiness?

He gives the example of people working extremely hard to complete the Atmasiddhi publication/commentary.

Since they were working day and night, Gurudev kept asking:

  • what facilities they needed,
  • whether they needed food,
  • whether they needed comfort,
  • whether anything could be done to make the work easier.

His point:

Compassion becomes action.

It isn't merely an emotion of:

“Poor thing.” Pasted text(20261116-004037)

7. Virtue 2 — SHANTI

Peace / tranquility

Gurudev says:

If I genuinely desire liberation, I must begin by reducing my passions.

Why?

Because:

I create my own agitation.

This is a huge point.

We usually think:

“I'll be peaceful when this situation changes.”

“I'll be peaceful when this person behaves differently.”

“I'll be peaceful somewhere else.”

Gurudev says:

Don't change the situation. Change the state.

Change places and perhaps you'll feel better for two days.

Then:

  • anger comes,
  • ego comes,
  • lust comes,
  • greed comes.

Because:

you took yourself with you. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

8. Peace comes from changing the INNER STATE

So Shanti doesn't primarily mean:

a quiet environment.

It means:

passions are subsiding.

thought patterns are becoming cleaner.

internal agitation is decreasing.

Gurudev calls it becoming:

calm and steady.

And he beautifully connects it with Daya:

Because I have compassion for myself, I don't want to keep hurting myself through uncontrolled passions.

Therefore:

Daya → I don't want to suffer

↓

I begin regulating my passions

↓

Shanti arises. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

9. Virtue 3 — SAMTA

Equanimity

Once the inner state becomes calmer, Gurudev says there can arise:

equilibrium.

The world is constantly changing.

You will encounter:

  • favorable circumstances,
  • unfavorable circumstances,
  • praise,
  • criticism,
  • success,
  • failure,
  • comfort,
  • discomfort.

The Mumukshu gradually learns not to create:

like / dislike

for every:

  • person,
  • event,
  • object,
  • situation.

Instead:

remain balanced. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

10. Stop LABELLING everything

Gurudev makes a very practical point here.

Our mind constantly labels:

Good.

Bad.

I like this.

I hate that.

This person is amazing.

This person is terrible.

Every label produces another emotional fluctuation.

Samta means gradually developing a more neutral, balanced attitude.

Not becoming emotionless.

But not being thrown around by every external change.

So:

Daya → Shanti → Samta.

11. Virtue 4 — KSHAMA

Forgiveness

Once Samta develops, forgiveness becomes more natural.

Why?

Because anger needs:

internal disturbance.

If I'm already balanced, an external trigger doesn't immediately take control of me.

Gurudev says the Mumukshu understands:

the destructive consequences of anger.

Therefore, even when triggers arise:

he tries to remain unperturbed.

And instead of cultivating:

  • hatred,
  • animosity,
  • revenge,

he cultivates:

  • love,
  • forgiveness. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

So:

Samta → Kshama.

If the mind doesn't get thrown off balance, there is much less to “fight back” against.

12. Virtue 5 — SATYA Truthfulness

Gurudev gives a deeper explanation of truthfulness than simply:

“Don't tell lies.”

As Samta grows, you're less controlled by:

  • likes,
  • dislikes,
  • emotional bias.

Therefore you begin seeing situations:

as they are.

Not:

as I want them to be.

Not:

as my ego needs them to be.

Not:

through attachment or aversion.

You perceive more objectively.

Then:

what you know,

what you think,

what you say,

what you do

begin aligning.

Gurudev calls this:

Integrity of mind, speech and body. Pasted text(20261116-004037) That's Satya.

13. Look at the progression so far

Gurudev explicitly asks us to notice the flow:

Daya

I don't want myself or others to suffer.

↓

Shanti

Therefore I begin pacifying the passions that create suffering.

↓

Samta

As passions weaken, I become more balanced amid changing circumstances.

↓

Kshama

Because I remain balanced, external triggers don't immediately become anger and hatred.

↓

Satya

Because likes/dislikes distort me less, I can perceive and express reality more truthfully.

This isn't just a list.

It's almost a psychology of spiritual development. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

14. Virtue 6 — TYAG

Renunciation Gurudev defines Tyag simply:

You possess something, but give it up because it obstructs your spiritual growth.

That “something” doesn't have to mean only physical possessions.

Tyag can involve letting go of:

  • objects,
  • comforts,
  • unnecessary pursuits,
  • worldly entanglements,
  • habits,
  • anything hindering spiritual progress. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

15. Gurudev's small-room / one-bag example

Gurudev describes staying temporarily in a room with essentially:

  • one bag,
  • books,
  • minimal necessities.

He says he actually feels comfortable with less.

He doesn't need everything unpacked into:

  • cupboards,
  • dressing tables,
  • bathroom cabinets.

If it's time to leave:

pack in 20 seconds and go.

The point isn't that everyone must literally live from one bag.

The spiritual point is:

How much do I actually need?

And:

How much of what I surround myself with is creating unnecessary entanglement? Pasted text(20261116-004037)

16. Tyag isn't only possessions — it's POSSESSIVENESS

This distinction is crucial.

You could renounce almost everything…

and remain deeply attached to the three objects you kept.

Then external quantity decreased.

Attachment didn't.

So Gurudev says:

Not only possessions, but possessiveness.

Tyag is not merely:

“How little do I own?”

It's also:

“How tightly does what I own own me?” Pasted text(20261116-004037)

17. Virtue 7 — VAIRAGYA

Non-attachment / detachment

Now the culmination.

Through Sadguru's teachings, the Mumukshu has begun understanding:

  • the nature of the Self,
  • the nature of the world,
  • the nature of material substances,
  • the transitory nature of sensual pleasure.

Therefore attachment toward:

  • world,
  • body,
  • pleasures

begins weakening.

Why?

Because he realizes:

These things aren't actually giving me lasting happiness.

Instead, dependence upon them makes me:

  • weak,
  • fragile,
  • restless,
  • sensitive,
  • agitated. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

18. Vairagya does NOT mean “I don't care”

Gurudev is very careful here.

When he says:

detachment

or:

indifference toward mundane occurrences

he does not mean:

“I don't care if you're suffering.”

That would contradict Daya, the first virtue.

So he gives a much better definition:

Vairagya = non-dependency.

If you're suffering:

it matters to me.

I still have:

  • compassion,
  • love,
  • sensitivity.

But:

My happiness does not depend on external objects or circumstances. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

That's a very important distinction.

19. The pizza example — diminishing returns 😂

Gurudev uses economics to explain worldly pleasure.

First slice of pizza:

amazing.

Second:

still good.

Third:

less.

Fourth:

maybe enough.

Eventually:

zero happiness.

The object didn't necessarily change.

Our experience did.

This is the law of diminishing returns applied to sensual pleasure.

So the Mumukshu gradually understands:

Why become dependent upon something whose pleasure is temporary and diminishing?

That understanding supports Vairagya. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

20. “The Enlightened Master seems to be the exception”

Gurudev then says something devotional.

With ordinary worldly things:

more exposure → diminishing enjoyment.

But with Sadguru:

more beholding, more listening, more association—and somehow you don't get tired.

He calls it:

Divya — divine.

The point isn't a formal philosophical claim about economic laws; it's Gurudev expressing the unique devotional experience of association with an Enlightened One. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

22. “Saday Sujagya” — constantly awake and vigilant

The verse doesn't merely say the Mumukshu occasionally experiences these seven qualities.

They are:

Saday Sujagya

constantly awake / active / vigilant within him.

Gurudev explains that these virtues themselves keep the seeker alert on the path.

When a trigger comes:

Together, they act almost like an inner protection system. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

25. The “THERMOMETER VERSE”

This is Gurudev's final and probably most useful point.

He says that decades ago he used to call Verse 138:

The Thermometer Verse.

A thermometer measures temperature.

Verse 138 measures:

Mumukshuta — my genuine desire for liberation.

Don't measure it by:

“How emotional do I become during bhakti?”

“How many satsangs have I attended?”

“How much Atmasiddhi do I know?”

“How much seva do I perform?”

Instead, examine:

How is my Daya?
How is my Shanti?
How is my Samta?
How is my Kshama?
How is my Satya?
How is my Tyag?
How is my Vairagya?

That tells me something much more real about where I actually stand. Pasted text(20261116-004037)

The deepest practical takeaway

I think this verse gives us one of the most useful self-assessments in the entire Atmasiddhi.

We've now learned a lot.

We can talk about:

  • Six Fundamental Truths,
  • Nishchay,
  • Vyavhar,
  • Upadan,
  • Nimit,
  • Satya Purusharth,
  • Atmabhranti,
  • Jindasha,
  • Moksh Marg.

But after Verse 137, Param Krupalu Dev almost says:

Enough words. Show Me your heart.

And then gives us seven measurements.

If my spiritual study is working, over time I should become:

More compassionate

Not wanting to hurt others—or myself through my own passions.

More peaceful

Less convinced that the world must change before I can be okay.

More equanimous

Less tossed around by every praise, criticism, success and inconvenience.

More forgiving

Less interested in carrying hatred and animosity.

More truthful

Seeing situations less through ego and more as they actually are; greater integrity between thought, speech and action.

More renouncing

Increasingly able to say:

“I don't need this.”

when something obstructs spiritual growth.

More detached

Able to care deeply without making my happiness dependent on the person or thing I care about.

That last distinction is especially beautiful:

Detachment doesn't mean less love.

It means less dependency.

Daya remains.

Love remains.

Sensitivity remains.

But:

“You must behave according to me for me to remain happy”

starts disappearing.

And I think Gurudev's Thermometer Verse idea is the best way to carry this forward.

Instead of asking:

“Am I a Mumukshu?”

Ask:

“Compared with one year ago, is there more Daya in me?”

“More Shanti?”

“More Samta?”

“More Kshama?”

“More Satya?”

“More Tyag?”

“More Vairagya?”

Because Mumukshuta isn't merely how strongly I say I want Moksh.

Param Krupalu Dev is saying:

If I truly want liberation, that desire will begin changing my character.

And these seven virtues are the evidence.
