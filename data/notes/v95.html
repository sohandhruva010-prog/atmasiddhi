Video 110 — Verse 95

The disciple’s “conclusion” after giving 3 arguments (92–94)

1) Where we are (context in the flow)

  • The disciple had one core doubt: “There is no universally accepted / undisputed / unfailing path to liberation.”
  • To support this doubt, he gave three arguments:
    • Verse 92: Infinite karmas, short human lifespan → how can all karmas be destroyed?
    • Verse 93: Too many views / philosophies → cannot decide which is true.
    • Verse 94: Confusion about caste/gender and attire/garb → external differences create paralysis.

✅ Now in Verse 95, he is not concluding “liberation doesn’t exist.” He is concluding:

“Because I cannot find an undisputed path, the knowledge of everything else feels useless.”

This verse is basically: “If there is no way to reach the goal, what’s the use of knowing the goal exists?”

2) Verse 95 — Key Gujarati terms (word-by-word meaning)

Your Guru highlights that in this verse, the disciple is drawing the conclusion of his arguments:

Tethi

  • “Therefore”
  • The disciple is saying: “Based on what I just argued…”

Em janay chhe

  • “It seems / It appears to me”
  • Important: he is not arrogantly declaring absolute truth; he is expressing how it appears to him from his current confusion.

Maley na

  • “Not found / cannot be obtained / cannot be accessed”
  • Meaning: the path is not available to him in a clear, reliable way.

Moksh upay

  • “Means/path to liberation”
  • The Sixth Fundamental.

Jivadi

  • “Jiva-adi” = “Soul, etc.”
  • Your Guru explains “etc.” very specifically as the Five Fundamentals already established:

1. Soul exists

2. Soul is eternal

3. Soul is doer of karma

4. Soul is receiver of karmic fruits

5. Liberation exists / is possible

Janya tano

  • “Knowledge of”
  • Knowledge of the soul and all these truths.

Sho upkar j thay

  • “What benefit is gained?”
  • The disciple’s punchline: If I can’t apply it (no path), then what’s the use of knowing it?

3) The short meaning (what the disciple is really saying)

The disciple tells the Guru:

“Therefore, it seems that the means to liberation are not obtainable. If the path cannot be found, then what benefit is there in knowing the soul, its eternity, karma-doership, karma-fruition, and even liberation?”

In simple language:

  • “If moksh is the destination but there is no road, why study the map?”

4) Emotional tone: why this verse sounds sad (and why it matters)

Your Guru very clearly points out: There is a tinge of pessimism / sadness / depression here.

Why?

Because the disciple’s main goal is liberation.

  • He didn’t ask these questions for entertainment.
  • Not for scholarship.
  • Not for debate.
  • Not for “I know philosophy.”

He wanted one thing: ✅ Moksh (ending transmigration)

So when he feels:

  • “Maybe there is no path,” he naturally feels:
  • “Then everything was pointless.”

Your Guru phrases it like:

  • “Why did we do retreats?”
  • “Why did we do discourses?”
  • “Why did we stay so long?”
  • “What was the point?”

✅ This sadness is not a “fault.” It is the natural reaction of a sincere but not-yet-enlightened seeker when the goal seems blocked.

5) The deep point: this verse actually proves his sincerity

Your Guru shows two truths revealed by this verse:

(1) He is interested only in liberation

  • His questions were never casual.
  • He is goal-oriented: “I want freedom from bondage.”

(2) He did not come for argument or ego victory

Your Guru contrasts him with people who:

  • argue habitually,
  • oppose whatever is said,
  • use partial logic + partial knowledge,
  • “defeat” weaker people,
  • and then feel like they are “victorious like Jinas.”

The disciple is NOT that kind.

He is saying:

“I didn’t ask You to win debates. I asked because I want truth and liberation.”

That makes him a worthy disciple.

6) The logic he is presenting (very important)

This is the disciple’s reasoning chain:

1. Liberation is the goal (Fifth Fundamental: moksh exists).

2. But liberation is attained only by following the path (Sixth Fundamental: moksh upay).

3. If there is no real path, then liberation cannot be attained.

4. If liberation cannot be attained, transmigration continues.

5. Therefore, knowing the soul and karmic philosophy becomes “not practically useful.”

Your Guru reinforces this with the “structure logic” of the fundamentals:

  • Understand five, then pursue the sixth, then attain the fifth.

(He even references a later verse idea: after understanding the five fundamentals, one who pursues the sixth attains moksh.)

✅ In short:

  • Knowledge without method feels futile.
  • The disciple is trapped in that feeling right now.

7) Why the disciple’s hope dips (psychology your Guru explains)

Your Guru makes this very relatable:

Worldly analogy:

  • You really want to finish a movie…
  • Electricity goes out…
  • You feel disappointed: “I wanted to complete it today!”

Spiritual parallel:

  • The disciple wants:
    • “I want to pursue the path and attain liberation in THIS birth.”
  • Your Guru even uses the phrase:
    • “Deh chhata nirvana” (liberation while still in the body — not only after death)

So the sadness comes from:

  • strong aspiration + temporary sense of impossibility.

✅ Key insight your Guru gives:

  • This low phase is common in seekers because they have not yet experienced the Self.
  • So mood fluctuates when uncertainty comes.

8) Very important transition: “This sadness won’t last”

Your Guru clearly says:

  • This pessimism is temporary.
  • In the next verse, the disciple regains optimism.

But how can optimism return before he even learns the path?

Your Guru answers: ✅ By seeing the capacity of the Master.

Meaning:

  • The disciple remembers:
    • “Earlier also, I had doubts.”
    • “Earlier also, I felt stuck.”
    • “And the Guru resolved them beautifully.” So a seed forms:
  • “Then this sixth doubt will also be resolved.”

And your Guru adds a crucial teaching:

Your enthusiasm on the spiritual journey depends directly on love + faith + surrender to the Guru.

When these slacken:

  • confusion rises
  • sadness rises
  • “feeling low” comes

So if you see yourself “low” spiritually:

  • check: has something weakened in faith/love/surrender?

9) The “Grace” detour — chili example (very powerful teaching)

Your Guru brings in a key philosophical point through an everyday example:

Chili causes burning

  • You eat chili → burning sensation.
  • Does chili have aversion toward you?
    • No.
  • Does it still play a role?
    • Yes.

Why?

  • It’s the nature of chili to be spicy.
  • Attachment/aversion isn’t required for it to produce its effect.

He adds:

  • Put sugar → you won’t get burning. So clearly:
  • substances have effects due to their nature.

✅ Then he applies this to the Lord / the Vitarag:

  • The Lord does not need to “like” you or “be pleased” like a worldly being.
  • The Lord is beyond happy/upset.
  • Grace flows because it is His nature—like chili’s heat is its nature.

This is why the devotee says:

  • “Tuha pabhaavao” = “By Your grace” Not:
  • “Because of my worthiness” Not:
  • “Because of my luck” Not:
  • “Because You are attached to me”

10) What the devotee should actually ask for (your Guru makes it practical)

Your Guru says: don’t make grace into “magic.”

True prayer is asking for inner transformation, like:

“Give me disaffection for transmigration”

  • “Make me tired of this nonsense.”
  • Not tired of others only…
  • but tired of my own repeated patterns:
    • anger
    • lust
    • jealousy
    • likes-dislikes
    • repeated foolishness across births

He even jokes (to make the point memorable):

  • “People are tired of spouse’s nature… but not tired of their own nature.”

✅ The real blessing:

  • becoming genuinely fed up of one’s own bondage-causes.

11) “Jay Viyaray” takeaway (simple but deep)

Your Guru gives a beautiful one-line meaning to remember:

  • May my desires dissolve (lai).
  • May Your wish be victorious (jay).

Because:

  • My desires create new bonds, new knots.
  • The Enlightened One’s “wish” (in teaching) is to free us from ignorance.

12) Pure love point (Morari Bapu + Rumi references)

Your Guru connects devotion to purity:

  • Morari Bapu’s idea:
    • In pure love, there is no “I” and “you.”
  • Rumi-like point:
    • Only love; no lover/beloved—just love.

He ties this back to:

  • devotion becomes pure when ego reduces.
  • Then “my demands” reduce.

13) What Verse 95 teaches YOU (application)

This verse is not just “disciple talking.” It’s a mirror.

When you feel:

  • “What’s the use?”
  • “This is all useless”
  • “No path works”
  • “Everything is contradictory” That’s Verse 95 energy.

Your Guru is showing:

  • It’s a phase that comes when you don’t yet see the path clearly.
  • The remedy is:
    • return to faith + surrender,
    • remember the Guru’s capacity,
    • don’t turn spiritual life into argument,
    • and keep seeking the undisputed core.

14) Quiz-ready recap (one-screen)

Verse 95 = Disciple’s conclusion after 3 arguments

  • Tethi em janay chhe: therefore it seems…
  • Maley na moksh upay: the means/path to liberation is not obtainable / not found clearly
  • So jivadi (the 5 fundamentals: soul exists, eternal, doer, receiver, moksh) → janya tano sho upkar j thay: what benefit is gained by knowing them?

Key mood: sadness/pessimism because the goal was liberation, not debate. Key teaching: enthusiasm rises/falls with faith + surrender to Guru. Bridge: next verse hope returns by remembering the Guru’s power.
