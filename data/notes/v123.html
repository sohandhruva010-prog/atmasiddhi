Atmasiddhi — Fifth & Sixth Fundamental Truths
Moksh and the Path to Moksh

1. The entire teaching comes down to one simple idea

The disciple says:

“Moksh kahyo nijshuddhata, te pame te panth.”

Gurudev explains:

  • Moksh = nijshuddhata → the purest state of one's own Self/soul.
  • Panth / Marg = the means of attaining that purity.
  • Therefore:
    • Goal: purity of the soul.
    • Path: whatever leads us to that purity.

Gurudev almost jokes that the 5th and 6th truths are therefore finished immediately.

Moksh is the purest state of the soul. The way you achieve that state is the path.

Yet earlier, explaining the path required extensive discourses and spiritual retreats. The beauty of this verse is that the disciple has absorbed everything so deeply that he can now summarize the entire teaching in essentially one line.

2. “Sankshepma Sakal Marg” — Concise, Yet Complete

This becomes an important theme throughout the discourse.

Sankshepma = concisely / briefly Sakal Marg = the entire / complete path

The Sadguru has explained the entire path of the Nirgranth Bhagwan — the Disentangled Lord — completely, yet concisely.

Gurudev emphasizes that these two qualities together are difficult.

Gurudev's funny comparison

He says:

  • Premarpits / older devotees: may take two hours to explain a little bit 😂
  • Yuvarpits / younger devotees: may explain something very briefly, but incompletely.

The Sadguru possesses the art of doing both:

Brief + Complete.

That is the mastery of the Enlightened Guru.

3. What is liberation actually from?

Gurudev gives another simple formulation:

Liberation = being shorn of:
  • desires,
  • passions,
  • expectations.

And whatever brings about their elimination is the path.

So Moksh isn't merely about completing religious activities. It is fundamentally about what is happening within you.

This leads to one of the strongest practical points of the lecture.

4. Your projects and seva mean nothing without inner purity

Gurudev says something very direct:

Without inner purity, your projects are useless, your sevas are useless, and your pursuits are useless.

The point isn't that seva itself is useless.

The point is:

The purpose of everything spiritual is inner transformation.

You could put in:

  • 8 hours,
  • 10 hours,
  • 12 hours,
  • even 24 hours,

but if internally you remain filled with:

attachment + aversion + ignorance,

then you have missed the actual path.

This is extremely important.

It is easy to measure spirituality externally:

“How much seva did I do?” “How much satsang did I attend?” “How much work did I complete?”

But Gurudev is shifting the measurement inward:

Did this make me purer?

If not, merely doing more activity does not automatically mean progressing further on the path. Pasted text(7)

5. The “knots” that bind us

Gurudev then plays with the meaning of Nirgranth.

Granth = entangled / knotted Nirgranth = disentangled / without knots

There are two kinds of knots:

External knots

Gurudev uses a wedding as the humorous example.

A wedding invitation might say that two people are going to “tie the marital knot.”

Gurudev jokes:

Why write their names? Just write that this “silly man” is being united with this “silly woman.” 😂 Why?

Because wherever there is a knot, there is bondage.

Even if we call marriage a beautiful or pious bondage, from this spiritual analogy it is still an external knot/bondage.

Internal knots

These matter much more spiritually.

From verse 100:

Attachment + Aversion + Ignorance = the fundamental knots of karma.

These are the knots that keep the soul bound.

So when the scriptures call Bhagwan Nirgranth, Gurudev emphasizes the beauty of that particular word.

The Lord has become Disentangled.

He has broken the knots of:

  • attachment,
  • aversion,
  • ignorance.

And because those knots disappear, there manifests:

Vitragta — complete dispassion Kevaljnan — Omniscience

So the path we are following is literally:

The path of One who became completely disentangled.

6. “Eliminate attachment to the body…”

Gurudev recalls words of Param Krupalu Dev that they used to chant repeatedly:

Eliminate attachment to the body, revel in the pure nature of the Self; such is the path of the Disentangled One, which is the remedy to end transmigration.

He says they would chant this for 2–3 hours, and he would become especially joyful when the line about the path of the Nirgranth came.

The essence is:

Turn away from identification/attachment with the body → abide in the pure Self → this is the path that ends transmigration.

That is why “Nirgranth” isn't just another poetic title for Bhagwan. It perfectly describes the goal of this teaching.

7. The path requires the 3 D's

Gurudev says this path demands:

Dedication

Not casual dedication — deepest dedication.

Devotion

Highest devotion.

Determination

Selfless determination.

And along with these comes single-minded concentration.

He makes an important distinction:

You cannot finish your morning prayers and then simply live however you want for the rest of the day and expect liberation.

The aspiration has to remain alive:

“When will I be free? When will I be free?”

Meaning liberation cannot simply be one item among hundreds of priorities.

It becomes the central orientation of life.

8. Why the disciple finally understands Moksh

The disciple says something deeper than:

“Gurudev explained Moksh to me, therefore I intellectually understand it.”

Instead:

He understands Moksh because he has now experienced purity.

Previously his soul was experienced only through impurity.

But during Self-realisation, he experienced a pure state.

Therefore he now has a direct reference for what complete purity means.

Gurudev explains:

Because I experienced the pure state, now I understand what the purest state — Moksh — means.

This is the difference between information and experience.

The disciple isn't merely repeating his Guru's vocabulary anymore.

The Guru's teaching has become his own experience.

9. Gurudev's 5-step explanation of our bondage

This is one of the clearest frameworks in the lecture.

The current cycle

1. The inherent nature of my soul is pure.

↓

2. I have forgotten my true nature.

↓

3. Because of that forgetfulness, I harbour impure feelings — likes, dislikes, attachment, aversion, etc.

↓

4. Those impure feelings cause acquisition of impure karma.

↓

5. Those karmas lead to impure states of existence (gati).

And this has continued since time immemorial.

Now reverse the process

Gurudev says: what if the soul becomes the observer and remains aware of its pure nature?

Then:

Awareness of pure nature

↓

No harbouring of impure feelings / emergence of pure feelings

↓

No acquisition of impure karma

↓

Karmaless state

↓

Liberation

This is extremely important because it makes Moksh less abstract.

The problem begins with forgetfulness of who I truly am.

The solution therefore begins with awareness of the Self.

10. Moksh is a STATE, not a PLACE

This is probably the biggest conceptual point of the discourse.

Gurudev repeatedly says:

Moksh is not the name of a place.

Moksh is the name of the purest state of the soul.

And that state is:

  • completely pure,
  • free from karma,
  • indestructible,
  • permanent.

Once liberation occurs, the soul doesn't somehow fall back tomorrow.

There is no:

liberated today → worldly again tomorrow.

The liberated state is indestructible.

11. But isn't Moksh/Siddhashila at the top of the universe?

Gurudev anticipates exactly this objection.

Among Jains, Moksh is commonly associated with Siddhashila at the top of the universe / 14 Rajlok.

Gurudev clarifies that he is explaining Moksh from the spiritual viewpoint, not giving a geography lesson.

His argument is clever:

Suppose the place itself were Moksh.

Then every soul present there should experience liberation and bliss.

But according to the cosmological understanding he invokes, other embodied beings can also be present in that region, including souls in very low states of existence.

They obviously aren't liberated merely because of location.

Therefore:

Location ≠ Moksh.

What distinguishes a Siddha?

Nijshuddhata — complete purity of the soul.

So the essential meaning of Moksh is not:

“Where am I?”

It is:

“What is the state of my soul?”

12. Liberation can therefore be understood even while the body exists

This leads to a subtle point.

Gurudev recalls verse 113:

“Keval nij swabhavnu, akhand varte jnan…”

When there is uninterrupted awareness/abidance in one's true nature, Omniscience manifests.

Then liberation necessarily follows.

So Gurudev says that in one sense, even while the body remains, such a soul can be spoken of as already liberated/accomplished, because there is nothing further spiritually left to accomplish.

The remaining non-destructive karmas may continue until the body ends, but the spiritual work is complete.

Again, this reinforces:

Moksh fundamentally describes a state of the soul, not simply an event involving the physical body.

13. Then what exactly is Moksh Marg?

Once Moksh is properly understood, the sixth truth becomes obvious.

If:

Moksh = complete purity of the soul

then:

Moksh Marg = whatever brings the soul to complete purity.

Gurudev calls it:

  • the means,
  • the way,
  • the efforts,
  • the path

through which the purest state is attained.

And the one path is the practice of the Three Jewels:

1. Samyak Darshan — Right Faith

2. Samyak Jnan — Right Knowledge

3. Samyak Charitra — Right Conduct

He cites the Tattvartha Sutra:

“Samyak darshan jnan charitrani mokshmargah.”

Together, these constitute the path to liberation.

14. The Guru did not invent this path

Gurudev makes another important clarification.

The Sadguru explains the path to the disciple.

But it isn't something the Guru invented.

It isn't:

  • from His “pocket,”
  • from His head,
  • or something He personally created.

It is the eternal path of liberation propounded by the Nirgranth Lords.

Those Lords themselves broke the internal knots of:

attachment + aversion + ignorance

and attained Omniscience.

Having directly attained it themselves, they revealed the path to the world.

The disciple's Sadguru then makes that eternal path understandable and experiential for him. Pasted text(7)

15. The husband-wife “in short” example 😂

Gurudev returns to sankshep — conciseness with another funny example.

Imagine a husband and wife having a long argument about whether they should go to a movie.

After a huge discussion:

“In short, you don't want to come, right?”

Gurudev's point is basically:

Why have the enormous discussion if that was the conclusion? 😂

Similarly, spiritually:

In short:

Moksh = purity of the soul. Purifying the soul = path to Moksh.

But the greatness of the Sadguru is that His concise explanation isn't superficial.

It contains everything.

16. The portrait and locket example

This is probably Gurudev's best example for “concise but complete.”

Imagine you have a huge portrait of someone — perhaps 5–7 feet tall.

Now take the same photograph and put it inside a tiny locket.

Does shrinking it mean:

  • one arm disappears?
  • one leg disappears?
  • one eye disappears?

No.

The photograph becomes smaller, but remains complete.

That is what the Sadguru's teaching is like.

Large portrait = elaborate teaching
Tiny locket = concise teaching

The size changes.

The completeness doesn't.

That is “Sankshepma Sakal Marg.”

Gurudev calls this an extraordinary quality of the Enlightened Master — mastery over scripture and the ability to compress an enormous spiritual teaching without losing its essence.

17. The disciple has not merely memorized — he has absorbed the Guru

Near the end Gurudev points out something beautiful.

The disciple summarizes essentially the entire dialogue — verses 45 onward — in an incredibly small number of verses.

Gurudev jokingly says:

Maybe the disciple is cleverer than the Guru!

But then explains:

This is grace.

The Guru wants the disciple to go ahead.

And more importantly, the disciple's ability to summarize everything shows how completely he has absorbed his Guru.

The words may still resemble scripture.

But now there is something different:

“Language of scriptures, and touch of the experience.”

That's the transformation.

Earlier:

Guru says → disciple hears.

Now:

Guru says → disciple realizes → disciple speaks from experience.

The disciple isn't parroting the Guru.

The Guru's teaching has become his own realization.

18. And what naturally arises after realization? Gratitude.

This sets up the next section of Atmasiddhi.

The disciple looks back at what his Sadguru has given him:

  • understanding of the soul,
  • destruction of delusion,
  • Self-realisation,
  • understanding of Moksh,
  • understanding of Moksh Marg.

And Gurudev asks:

If you repeatedly remember someone's complaints or faults, what arises?

Anger.

But if you repeatedly remember everything your Sadguru has done for you, what arises?

Gratitude.

Hence:

“E upkar amaap.”

This favour/benevolence is immeasurable.

And that gratitude naturally turns into servitude:

I am Your servant. I want this body and this life to serve You.

The next four verses therefore become:

2 verses of gratitude + 2 verses of servitude.

The deepest message of this lecture

I think Gurudev's entire discourse can be understood through one question:

“Am I actually becoming internally purer?”

Because once:

Moksh = Nijshuddhata

then everything else gets measured against that goal.

Seva is not the goal.

Projects are not the goal.

Religious activity itself is not the goal.

Even enormous effort is not automatically the goal.

They are valuable to the extent that they become part of the path toward inner purity.

If I perform tremendous seva but remain filled with:

  • attachment,
  • aversion,
  • expectations,
  • passions,
  • likes/dislikes,
  • ego,

then Gurudev is saying I should not confuse activity on the spiritual path with actual movement on the spiritual path.

The movement is:

Forgetfulness of Self → Awareness of Self Impure feelings → Purity Attachment/aversion/ignorance → Disentanglement Karma → Karmalessness Bondage → Moksh

And that makes the 5th and 6th Fundamental Truths beautifully simple:

Where am I trying to reach? My own completely pure state.

How do I reach it? By following the path that progressively brings me into that purity.

And once the disciple actually experiences what Gurudev had been pointing toward all along, the response isn't merely “I understand.”

It becomes:

“What You said, I have experienced.”

And from that realization comes the next natural feeling:

How can I ever repay the One who gave me this?

Which is why the Six Fundamental Truths conclude not merely in intellectual understanding, but in gratitude toward the Sadguru and a desire to serve Him.
