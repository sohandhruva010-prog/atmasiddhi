Theme: From the Guru’s Silence → Self-Realisation → Gratitude

This lecture marks a beautiful turning point in Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra. Until now, the disciple has been asking, the Guru has been answering, and the disciple has been understanding. Now the dialogue ends. The Guru becomes silent, and the disciple must actually do what he has been taught.

The movement is essentially:

Listening → Understanding → Contemplation → Meditation → Experience → Gratitude

1. The Dialogue Has Ended — But the Real Work Begins

At verse 118, the entire question-answer dialogue between the Guru and disciple comes to an end.

The Guru has answered everything. There is nothing more He needs to explain intellectually.

The Guru now:

  • assumes silence
  • withdraws from verbal activity
  • becomes absorbed within
  • enters sahaj samadhi — effortless Self-absorption
  • goes beyond speech, senses and thoughts

“Dhari maunta em kahi, sahaj samadhi mai” Having said this, He assumes silence and becomes immersed in His innate, effortless Self-absorption.

The disciple is simply watching his Guru. One moment the Guru was answering every question; the next, He has withdrawn completely within Himself. Pasted text

Important point

The Guru's teaching has not actually stopped because His words have stopped.

His state itself now becomes the teaching.

Until this point:

Guru taught through words.

Now:

Guru teaches through His state.

That is extremely important in understanding the Guru-disciple relationship here.

2. The Guru Turns Within — and the Disciple Is Pulled Within

The disciple sees the Guru absorbed effortlessly in the Self.

And something begins happening inside the disciple.

He does not merely think:

“My Guru is meditating.”

Rather, seeing the Guru's absorption activates the same movement within him.

He becomes inspired to turn within.

The transcript describes it beautifully:

Seeing his Guru absorbed in the Self → the disciple's own inner process of turning within becomes activated.

This is why Bapa says that in the presence of such a Guru, when the Guru meditates, the disciple should not think:

“He is meditating, so I'll finish some other work.”

The opposite should happen.

His meditation should pull you into meditation.

The Guru does not merely tell the disciple where to go.

The Guru's state draws the disciple there. Pasted text

3. Something Enormous Happens Between Verses 118 and 119

This is probably the single most important point of the lecture.

Verse 118

The Guru is absorbed in the Self.

Verse 119

The disciple begins by saying, essentially:

Through the teachings of my Sadguru, I attained unprecedented realisation.

So what happened between those two verses?

The disciple attained Self-realisation.

There is no long description of the event.

There isn't another twenty-verse explanation.

There is almost a silence between the verses — and within that silence, everything changes.

Before:

The disciple knew the Six Fundamental Truths intellectually.

After:

The disciple knows them experientially.

Bapa makes this distinction explicitly: what the disciple had understood at the intellectual level through the entire dialogue becomes realised at the experiential level between verses 118 and 119. Pasted text

This is the transition from:

“I understand what the soul is.”

to

“I know the Self.”

That is a completely different level.

4. The Final Command of the Guru: “Kar Vichar To Paam”

To understand how this happened, Bapa returns to verse 117.

The Guru had already described the true nature of the Self:

Shuddh — pure Buddh — knowledgeable/enlightened Chaitanyaghan — indivisible mass of consciousness Swayamjyoti — self-luminous Sukhdham — abode of bliss

Then comes the final instruction:

“Kar vichar to paam.”

Literal meaning

Think, and you will attain.

Contextual meaning

Contemplate deeply upon everything that has been explained regarding the Six Fundamental Truths, and realise your true nature.

This is the last verbal instruction given by the Guru before He becomes silent. Pasted text

That makes these words extremely significant.

The Guru does not say:

“Keep listening.”

He does not say:

“Ask me another question.”

He does not say:

“Memorise everything I said.”

He says:

Now contemplate.

Everything required has already been given.

5. The Worthy Disciple Actually Follows the Ajna

This is where the worthiness of the disciple becomes visible.

He doesn't hear:

Kar vichar to paam

and think:

“Beautiful line.”

He doesn't write it down and move on.

He doesn't wait for another satsang.

He does exactly what the Guru told him to do.

Bapa emphasises:

“This is the last command of my Gurudev.”

Therefore, the disciple takes it seriously.

The progression is:

1. Guru gives the ajna

Kar vichar to paam.

2. Disciple contemplates deeply

He turns repeatedly over the truths the Guru has revealed.

3. Contemplation deepens

It is no longer merely intellectual reasoning.

4. Contemplation slips into meditation

5. Meditation culminates in direct experience

6. He attains Self-realisation

The Guru showed the path.

The disciple walked it.

And therefore he experienced the pure Self. Pasted text

This connects directly with the earlier teaching:

The Guru can show the path, but the disciple has to eat the food himself.

Here, the disciple finally does exactly that.

6. Understanding Has Become Experience

This is a major distinction to remember.

Before verse 119:

“The Guru says the soul exists.”

After verse 119:

“I know the soul exists.”

Before:

Faith in the Guru's experience.

Now:

Experience of the truth within oneself.

The Six Fundamental Truths are therefore no longer simply doctrines or philosophical conclusions.

They have become the disciple's lived reality.

And once that happens, Bapa says:

“One who knows the soul knows everything.”

The disciple is therefore no longer waiting impatiently for the Guru to open His eyes and continue speaking.

There is no:

“Bapa, I have one more question.”

Something much deeper has occurred.

The questions have reached their end because the questioner has reached the Self.

7. What Does the Disciple Want to Do Now?

This part is beautiful.

After attaining Self-realisation, he does not immediately ask for more knowledge.

He doesn't say:

“What should I study next?”

He doesn't ask for another discourse.

He wants to do one thing:

Express gratitude.

And Bapa makes an important distinction:

Feeling gratitude vs. expressing gratitude

Of course the disciple feels gratitude.

That is obvious.

But a worthy disciple does not stop there.

He expresses it.

If ordinary people say “thank you” for something as small as directions or being handed a spoon, how could a disciple receive the gift of Self-realisation from his Guru and remain silent?

That becomes the heart of the next section. Pasted text

8. The Entire Mood of Atmasiddhi Now Changes

Bapa describes the earlier portion almost as belonging to the head.

There were:

  • doubts
  • arguments
  • reasoning
  • philosophy
  • explanations
  • Six Fundamental Truths
  • clarification of the path

But now:

“That was touching the head. This is touching the heart.”

The next nine verses — 119 to 127 — are the disciple's deeply personal response after realisation.

This is no longer primarily:

“What is true?”

Now it becomes:

“Gurudev, look what happened to me because of You.”

That is why this section carries such a strong feeling of bhakti, surrender and gratitude. Pasted text

9. Structure of Verses 119–127

Bapa divides these nine verses into two groups.

Verses 119–123 — The Disciple Describes His Realisation

These five verses describe:

What happened to me through Your grace?

The disciple essentially revisits the Six Fundamental Truths — but now from experience.

It becomes a kind of final revision, except this revision is no longer academic.

He has absorbed what the Guru taught, and that understanding has transformed into Self-realisation.

The disciple recognises:

The soul exists.

The soul is eternal.

In its true nature, the Self is understood differently from karmic doership and enjoyership.

Liberation is the completely pure state of one's own Self.

The path of liberation is whatever leads to that purity.

So the Six Fundamental Truths return once again — except now they are being spoken by the realised disciple, rather than explained by the Guru. Pasted text

10. Verses 124–127 — The Disciple Expresses His Gratitude

After describing what he has realised, the disciple turns completely toward the Guru.

Verses 124–127 express his:

  • gratitude
  • indebtedness
  • devotion
  • surrender
  • reverence
  • recognition of the Guru's grace

So the complete structure is:

Verses Focus

119–123 What I realised through Your grace

124–12What You mean to me because You gave me this 7 realisation

Bapa describes these verses as portraying the disciple's bhakti, surrender, devotion and respect toward his Guru. Pasted text

11. The True “Impact” of the Guru

There had been a long dialogue.

Many questions.

Many explanations.

Many arguments.

Many examples.

But finally, there is only one meaningful question:

What was the impact?

What changed in the disciple?

The answer is not:

He learned Jain philosophy.

Not:

He memorised the Six Fundamentals.

Not:

He became good at answering questions.

The impact was:

He experienced the Self.

And with that experience came an effervescent joy from the depth of the Soul.

Not happiness caused by:

  • objects
  • people
  • circumstances
  • achievements
  • external events

It is described as a fountain of joy arising from within.

And alongside that joy arises overwhelming gratitude toward the One who showed him the way. Pasted text

12. This Entire Section Is “Thanksgiving”

Bapa ultimately summarises the section with one word:

Thanksgiving.

But this is not merely:

“Thank You for guiding me.”

It is something much deeper.

It is:

“You guided me. I followed exactly what You said. And I reached where You said I would reach.”

That is why Bapa distinguishes this thanksgiving from ordinary gratitude.

It is thanksgiving not merely for guidance given.

It is thanksgiving for:

the destination reached.

The Guru said it.

The disciple followed it.

The disciple experienced it.

And now he turns back toward the Guru and says:

Thank You.

That is the emotional foundation of verses 119–127. Pasted text

The Deepest Progression in This Lecture

I would remember this entire lecture through this chain:

Guru explains → disciple understands → Guru gives final ajna → Guru becomes silent → disciple contemplates → contemplation becomes meditation → meditation becomes Self-realisation → Self-realisation becomes joy → joy becomes gratitude.

Or even more simply:

Bodh → Vichar → Dhyan → Anubhav → Aabhar

Teaching → Contemplation → Meditation → Experience → Gratitude

That is really the whole lecture.

Most Important Point for Us

There is a subtle challenge in this lecture.

We can spend months listening to Atmasiddhi and become very good at explaining:

  • dehadhyas
  • samkit
  • antarshodh
  • anubhav, laksh, pratit
  • karta/bhokta
  • the Six Fundamental Truths
  • the path of liberation

But the Guru's final instruction is not:

“Understand more.”

It is:

“Kar vichar to paam.”

Contemplate it deeply and realise.

So the question after satsang becomes less:

“What did I learn today?”

and more:

“What did I actually take within?”

Because the worthy disciple did not merely admire his Guru's words.

He obeyed them deeply enough for the words to become experience.

And only then does his gratitude become complete.

Key Lines to Remember

“Kar vichar to paam.” Contemplate deeply and realise.

Between verses 118 and 119, understanding becomes experience.

The Guru's silence itself becomes a teaching.

A worthy disciple doesn't only feel gratitude; he expresses it.

Verses 119–123: “What happened to me through Your grace?”
Verses 124–127: “My gratitude and indebtedness toward You.”

This is thanksgiving not merely for guidance given, but for the destination reached.

One-line essence

The Guru said everything He needed to say and became silent; the worthy disciple followed His final command, turned within, experienced the Self, and now has only one thing left to say — Thank You.
