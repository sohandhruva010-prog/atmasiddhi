The section we’re in

  • The series is unpacking the Third Fundamental Truth:
    • The soul is the doer (karta) of karmas.
  • The disciple had doubts (influenced by various philosophies) and argued:
    • “The soul is NOT the doer.”

What already happened (Verses 71–73)

  • Verses 71–72: disciple gives five arguments/options to deny soul’s doership.
    • Four of them argue “soul is non-doer” (karmas do karmas; automatic;
      matter/prakriti; God).
    • One argues “even if soul does, it’s nature → can’t stop.”
  • Verse 73: disciple draws a conclusion (liberation becomes meaningless / myth), based on those five arguments.

What starts now (Verses 74–78)

  • The Guru answers the disciple’s five arguments one by one:
    • Verse 74 answers the first argument from Verse 71:
      • “karma j karta karma” (karmas do karmas / past causes present)
    • Verse 75 will answer the next two (“automatic” and “nature”) using a single key word: “jo” (“if”).
    • Verses 76–78 will address the remaining two options (matter/prakriti, God).

So this talk is: ✅ The Guru’s refutation of Argument #1 (“karmas do karmas”).

2) The disciple’s first argument (what the Guru is responding to) The disciple’s claim: “karma j karta karma”

Meaning: karma itself is the doer of karma. He frames it in both meanings of karma:

Meaning A: Karma = actions

  • A new action happens because of a previous action.
  • Chain continues: past → present → future.

Meaning B: Karma = karmic material particles (Jain sense)

  • Existing karmas attract new karmas.
  • Old karmic “layers” draw new karmic matter and keep the cycle going.
Disciple’s point:

Whether karma is action or karmic matter, “karma causes karma,” so the soul is not needed as the doer.

3) Verse 74: the Guru’s direct answer (line-by-line meaning)

The verse (as he recites) is:

Hoy na chetan prerna, kaun grahe to karma? Jadswabhav nahi prerna, juo vichari dharma (or marma).

3.1 Key vocabulary

  • Hoy na = if there is not / if absent
  • Chetan = conscious / soul
  • Prerna = inspiration / prompting / initiating force
  • Kaun grahe to karma?
    • who will “grahe” = who will grasp/receive/acquire/trigger karma?
  • Jadswabhav = nature of the non-sentient / lifeless matter
  • Nahi prerna = does not have inspiration (capacity to inspire)
  • Juo vichari = look again, contemplate, ponder
  • Dharma / Marma:
    • dharma here means nature/property of substances, not “religion”
    • marma means essence (essence of the nature of substances)

3.2 The Guru’s logical structure (the “root move”)

The Guru goes to the root of the argument and asks one decisive question:

“Who inspires karma?”

If there is no conscious inspiration, who/what starts the binding or action?

Because the disciple’s model (“karma does karma”) assumes:

  • karmic matter can “pull” karmic matter by itself, or
  • actions generate actions without any conscious trigger.

The Guru challenges that assumption.

4) The “marma vs dharma” textual discussion (important detail)

He pauses to discuss the exact word at the end of the verse.

4.1 What he says happened

  • In Param Krupalu Dev’s original handwriting, the last word is “marma” (essence).
  • In the commonly printed book versions, the word appears as “dharma.”

4.2 Why he thinks the change happened

  • Verse 74 ends with karma–marma (rhymes).
  • Verse 75 ends with karma–dharma (rhymes).
  • If verse 74 also ended with “dharma,” both verses would share the same end rhyme in a repetitive way (“karma–dharma, karma–dharma”), so “marma” keeps the poetic texture.

But:

  • “marma” is harder for people to understand (“essence of what?”).
  • So the wording was later standardized to “dharma” because:
    • what he means is: contemplate the nature of substances.

4.3 Practical instruction he gives

  • Follow the latest endorsed wording.
  • He also criticizes a transliteration inconsistency (marma in one place, dharma in another) as sloppy proofreading.
Why this matters spiritually (his point):
  • If an enlightened authority updated wording/instruction, follow the updated one.
  • Don’t cling to older versions just because you like them.

5) The Guru’s refutation of “karma causes karma”

5.1 The Guru’s core claim

Without “chetan prerna” (conscious inspiration), karma cannot be acquired/bound.

So:

  • karmas can’t “bind karmas” on their own
  • matter can’t initiate this process
  • the initiating factor must be consciousness.

5.2 Why matter cannot be the initiator

He gives several reasons, all pointing to one theme: matter lacks consciousness.

Matter cannot:

  • make a choice
  • make a decision
  • have feelings
  • have likes/dislikes
  • have attachment/aversion (raag–dvesh)
  • “wish” or “intend” anything

He asks questions like:

  • Can this clock decide: “I want to acquire karmas now”?
  • Can a pot be “angry” or “peaceful”?
Conclusion:
  • If something involves feelings/choice/intent, it is conscious activity.
  • Only the soul has conscious activity.

6) What “inspiration” actually means (he makes it concrete)

A key move: he doesn’t leave “prerna” vague.

6.1 Inspiration = impure feelings (raag–dvesh)

He says:

  • “Have you ever consciously wished: ‘May I have karmic bondage!’?”
  • No one does that.
  • Yet the enlightened say: you have always “inspired” karmic bondage.

So what does “inspiration” mean?

Answer:

Impure feelings, such as:

  • like/dislike
  • love/hate (he says this sounds harsh, so prefer like/dislike)
  • attachment/aversion (raag–dvesh)

This is the “spark” that turns ordinary matter into “bonded karma.”

7) The step-by-step reasoning he builds (very systematic)

He explicitly builds the argument in “steps,” like a proof.

Step 1 — Bondage requires inspiration

  • Sometimes karmas don’t bind.
  • Sometimes karmas bind even when you “don’t want them to.”
  • So there’s an “if-condition” behind bondage.
  • Therefore: bondage occurs only when inspiration is present.

Step 2 — Identify who has the capacity to inspire

Two candidates:

1. soul (conscious)

2. matter (non-conscious)

Step 3 — Only the soul can inspire

  • matter can’t inspire
  • soul can So the only initiator is the soul.

Step 4 — Without soul-inspiration, particles never become “karma”

He clarifies a technical Jain point:

  • In the atmosphere, subtle particles are called karman vargana.
  • When those particles attach to the soul due to inspiration, they are called karma.

So:

  • particles ≠ “karma” until bonded.
  • bonding requires chetan prerna.
  • without chetan prerna, there is no “karma” in the bonded sense and therefore no bondage.

Step 5 — Define inspiration precisely

  • Inspiration is the soul’s impure feelings (raag–dvesh).
  • If the soul is in equanimity, no new karmas bind.

And he briefly states the liberation trajectory:

  • equanimity → no new karmas
  • old karmas exhaust
  • karma-less → bodiless → mind-less pure existence (“satchidanandroop”)

8) Key concept: nature vs capacity (he previews why the next verse matters)

He emphasizes why he keeps saying “capacity”:

  • If you say “because soul exists it must inspire,” you accidentally imply:
    • inspiring/bondage is the nature of the soul.
  • But the Guru’s position is:
    • it is not the nature of the soul
    • it is a capacity (vaibhavik shakti) to have impure feelings

He foreshadows that Verse 75 will clarify:

  • the soul’s doership is real,
  • but bondage-causing inspiration is not the soul’s true nature.

9) Examples used, with what each proves

This talk is packed with examples; here they are with the purpose of each.

9.1 “Angry pot / peaceful pot” (feelings don’t belong to matter)

  • You can describe a pot as:
    • big/small/beautiful
  • but you don’t call it:
    • angry/peaceful/rude Purpose: feelings are not attributes of matter; they belong to conscious beings.

9.2 Switch and machine (prime initiator)

  • Once you turn on the switch, the machine spins on its own.
  • But the first “turning on” requires an initiator. Purpose: even if a chain continues automatically later, initiation requires conscious input.

9.3 Karman vargana vs karma + marriage analogy

  • Before marriage: “woman/lady”
  • after marriage: “wife”
  • same person, different designation based on relationship
  • similarly:
    • particles in environment = karman vargana
    • bonded to soul = karma Purpose: shows how matter becomes “karma” only after bonding via inspiration.

9.4 Handkerchief tied to handkerchief (and why it’s misleading without the “hand”)

He replays the earlier demonstration idea:

  • It looks like:
    • “karmas tie to karmas”
  • But he asks:
    • can the handkerchief tie itself without someone tying it?
  • A student answers:
    • it required “Saheb’s hand” to tie it.
Interpretation he wants:
  • the cloth is instrumental (nimit)
  • the conscious doer is primary cause
  • therefore karmas cannot bind karmas without conscious initiating activity

9.5 Stick hitting a person (instrumental vs primary cause)

  • Stick is inanimate.
  • Stick doesn’t “want” to hit.
  • A conscious person uses the stick with anger/hate to strike.

Purpose: matter can be an instrument; intention/impulse is conscious.

9.6 Picking up an unattended watch to steal (object isn’t the cause)

  • The watch being present is a condition/instrument.
  • The primary cause is the soul’s intention/desire to steal.

Purpose: clarifies:

  • instrumental cause (nimit karan)
  • primary/real cause (upadan karan) = soul’s impure feeling

9.7 “Pen and silken pouch” story (wisdom is not in objects)

Story:

  • disciple thinks Guru’s wisdom comes from a pen kept in a pouch.
  • steals pen + book, waits for wisdom to “drop.”
  • realizes: wisdom is not in pen/book; they are conduits.
  • real source is consciousness.

Purpose: crushes the deep superstition that “power resides in objects.” Maps directly to the verse:

  • jadswabhav nahi prerna (matter can’t inspire/know)
  • consciousness is the true source.

10) The direct “answer” to the disciple’s first argument

The disciple:

“Old karmas attract new karmas; karmas bind karmas.”

The Guru (Verse 74):

No—because karmas are inanimate matter. For new karmas to bind, there must be chetan prerna (conscious inspiration). Matter has no inspiration. Therefore karmas cannot be the doer of karmas.

He compresses the whole refutation into one decisive insight:

  • Primary cause: soul’s impure feelings (raag–dvesh)
  • Instrumental causes: objects, situations, body, etc.
  • Without the primary cause, bondage cannot happen.

11) What the Guru is also quietly teaching (beyond the refutation)

Even while “answering a doubt,” the teacher slips in practice guidance:

Equanimity stops new bondage

  • If the soul stays equanimous:
    • no new karmas bind
    • old karmas exhaust
    • eventual karma-less, bodiless state becomes possible

So Verse 74 isn’t just “philosophy.” It points to sadhana:

  • watch your inner reactions (likes/dislikes)
  • because those are the “inspiration” that binds you.
