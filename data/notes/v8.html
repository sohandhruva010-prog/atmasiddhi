Śrī Atmasiddhi Śāstra — Verse 8 The True Seeker (Atmārthī): Prudence, Context, and Right Action

Verse (Gujarati & Transliteration)

Jya jya je je yogya chhe, tāhā samajvu teh;
Tyā tyā te te āchare, atmārthī jan eh. 8

Transliteration: Jya jya je je yogya chhe, tāhā samajvu teh;
Tyā tyā te te āchare, atmārthī jan eh.

Word-to-Word Meaning
  • Jya jya — wherever
  • Je je — whatever
  • Yogya chhe — is appropriate / worthy of adoption
  • Tāhā samajvu teh — he understands that (first understands the fitness)
  • Tyā tyā — there
  • Te te — that (very thing)
  • Āchare — he acts / practises accordingly
  • Atmārthī jan eh — that (one) is the true seeker of the Self

Short Meaning One who understands what is appropriate in each situation and then acts accordingly is a true seeker of the Self.

1) Placement in the Introduction (Verses 1–8)

  • 3–5: Two deluded types — kriyājada (mechanical ritualist) & śuṣka-jñānī (dry intellectualist).
  • 6–7: Recommendations correcting both: adopt auxiliaries aimed at Self-realisation; don’t mistake means for end.
  • 8 (this verse): Portrait of the atmārthī — the one fit for Self-realisation through prudence (viveka) and context-sensitive action.

Verse 8 closes the first arc: from exposing two errors → prescribing corrections → defining the true seeker.

2) Core Quality Highlighted Here: Viveka (Discriminative Prudence)

An atmārthī is present-minded and discerning: 1) Sees freshly: “Jya jya — je je” → looks anew at where he stands and what is needed now (not SOP-copying).

2) Understands first: “Tāhā samajvu” → comprehension precedes obedience; he wants meaning, not mere compliance.

3) Acts accordingly: “Tyā tyā te te āchare” → he follows through, precisely and promptly.

Not bookish: “Last time we did X, so this time also X” is matārthī (sectarian rigidity), not atmārthī.

3) The Atmārthī’s Decision Loop

Scan → Understand → Decide → Do → Review.

  • Scan: What is the inner state today? What is the outer context (people, place, time)?
  • Understand: Which tool (silence, study, charity, fasting, rest, music, solitude, service…) is yogya? Why this tool?
  • Decide: Set a clear aim (supports nij-bhān / Self-awareness).
  • Do: Practise only that much as fits today.
  • Review: Did awareness/equanimity increase? If not, adjust tomorrow.

The atmārthī rejects always/never vows that ignore today’s truth; he chooses the fitting means for the living moment.

5) “Jya jya — je je” in real life: context-sensitive examples

  • Food/fasting: If too indulgent lately → adopt a fast/simple meal today. If becoming dry/irritable → take balanced nourishment; restore softness.
  • Silence/Seva: If looping in thoughts → choose silence/solitude. If becoming self-absorbed → serve; re-open the heart.
  • Study/Meditation: If emotions stormy → more meditation for stability. If concepts hazy → more study for clarity.

Key: The same act can be wise today and unwise tomorrow; wisdom is fitness to purpose.

6) “Understand, then act” (not just obey)

Surrender is valued, but mindless obedience is not atmārthī. Param Krupālu Dev seeks understanding (viveka) first; then surrendered action. Litmus: Can I explain why this is yogya now? If not, pause to understand.

7) Knowing Traps, Strengths, and Limits

The atmārthī is self-aware: - Traps: where sensual pulls or ego patterns snare him (e.g., “seva” turning into socialising, food becoming identity).

  • Strengths/limits: what he can do safely today (serve and leave; don’t sit to feast if that triggers old habits).
  • Therefore: he tailors practices to help rather than harm his inner state.

8) Calm Confidence vs. Tense Deadlines

He works quietly within — devoted, diligent, unhurried. No lethargy, no impatience. He doesn’t fix dates for Self-realisation; he fixes worthiness today. Aim: “Become more fit for His prasādi; Self-realisation will arise at its time.”

9) Two-by-Two Guidance (in the spirit of the talk)

For the ritualist becoming atmārthī:

1) Study & insight: read/reflect to gain tattva-dṛṣṭi; understand the significance behind rituals.

2) Right emphasis: keep forms, but only as means enhancing Self-awareness.

For the intellectualist becoming atmārthī:

1) Walk the talk: act per your own words; shake off infatuations.

2) Cultivate tyāg–virāg: introduce real practices that you feel lacking within.

10) A tiny daily protocol (5–10 minutes)

1) Check-in (1 min): What is most alive in me today (pulls, moods, clarity)?

2) Choose tool (30s): Which one action is yogya now (study / sit / serve / simplify / fast-light / music / reach-out)?

3) Set aim (10s): “This is to support nij-bhān.”

4) Do it (3–8 min).

5) Review (30s): One way awareness/equanimity changed.

11) Diagnostics & Reflections

  • Do I often say, “Because last time we did X”? Where am I avoiding fresh seeing?
  • When I “obey,” can I also explain why this is yogya now?
  • Which single trap (food/approval/comfort/distraction) needs a context-wise remedy today?
  • Where can I act today rather than stay in analysis?

12) Memorisation cue

Jya jya — je je — yogya chhe (pause) tāhā samajvu teh;
Tyā tyā — te te — āchare (fresh start) atmārthī jan eh.

One-breath Summary

The true seeker is prudent: he reads the moment, understands what fits, and acts so that every tool serves Self-awareness. He is neither rigidly mechanical nor merely verbal; he is alive, balanced, adaptive, and sincere — fit for Self-realisation.
