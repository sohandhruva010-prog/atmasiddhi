Verse 136 of the Upsamhar, and it completes the pair on Upadan and Nimit begun in Verse 135.

Verse 135 gave the principle:

“Sarva jeev chhe Siddh sam, je samje te thay;
Sadguru ajna Jindasha, nimit karan mai.”

Every soul has the inherent potential of the Siddha. But Sadguru's Ajna and contemplation of the Jina's state are powerful instrumental causes that help awaken and manifest that potential.

Now Verse 136 gives the warning:

“Upadannu naam lai, e je taje nimit;
Pame nahi Siddhatvane, rahe bhrantima sthit.”

In essence:

One who emphasizes the principal cause but abandons or disregards the instrumental cause does not attain liberation and remains established in delusion. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

The message is simple:

You must transform yourself.

But:

Don't therefore conclude that Sadguru is unnecessary.

And equally:

Sadguru is indispensable as a guide and inspirer.

But:

Don't therefore sit passively expecting Him to transform you.

Section 12 — Upsamhar

Verse 136: Trust Your Potential AND Trust the Facilitator Gurudev summarizes the two sides beautifully near the end:

1. Priceless potential of the Self — Upadan

2. Ceaseless guidance of Sadguru — Nimit

The path requires both. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

1. First, what exactly are Upadan and Nimit?

Gurudev gives very simple definitions.

Upadan — Principal Cause

The potential of the substance itself to undergo the required transformation.

For liberation:

The soul is the Upadan.

A table cannot attain enlightenment because consciousness isn't its nature.

But the soul can become liberated because the potential for:

  • infinite knowledge,
  • infinite perception,
  • infinite bliss,
  • infinite vigour

belongs inherently to it. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

Nimit — Instrumental Cause

That which aids, inspires or activates that potential.

Gurudev is very clear:

Nimit cannot give you Moksh.

Nimit cannot give you enlightenment.

It activates your energies/potential.

And one of the greatest Nimits is:

Sadguru. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

2. The POT example — which is more important?

Gurudev returns to the example from Verse 135.

To create a pot:

Clay = Upadan

because the clay itself transforms into the pot.

Potter = Nimit

because the potter assists that transformation.

Then Gurudev deliberately tries to make the audience choose one 😂:

“Finally the pot is made of clay. So clay is the important one, right?”

Audience:

Both.

Gurudev pushes:

“But where is the potter inside the finished pot? It's all clay!”

Still:

Both.

And that's exactly the answer he wants. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

3. Clay alone would remain clay for millions of years

Suppose someone says:

Only Upadan matters.

Gurudev asks:

Then why didn't the clay lying around for millions of years spontaneously become a pot?

It had the potential.

But the appropriate instrumental causes weren't operating:

  • potter,
  • wheel,
  • stick,
  • shaping process.

So:

Potential alone does not mean manifestation.

This is exactly what Gurudev said in the previous satsang about us:

You have Siddha-potential.

Beautiful.

But that potential has remained unmanifested since beginningless time.

So simply repeating:

“I have infinite knowledge! I am pure!”

doesn't manifest it. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

4. But the world's best potter cannot make a clay pot from WATER

Now take the opposite extreme.

“The potter is everything!”

Gurudev asks:

Then tell the world's greatest potter to make a clay pot from water.

He can't.

Why?

Because the appropriate Upadan isn't there.

Even the perfect Nimit cannot manufacture a potential that doesn't belong to the substance.

So:

Clay without potter → no pot.

Potter without clay → no pot.

Therefore:

Both are essential. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

And spiritually:

Soul without right instrumental support may leave its potential unmanifested.

Sadguru cannot become enlightened on your behalf.

Both have distinct roles.

5. “Qualified clay” — Gurudev's subtle addition

Gurudev adds that you don't merely need any clay.

You need clay with the proper qualities—such as enough stickiness to hold shape.

Then he immediately applies this spiritually:

You need to become a qualified Atmarthi — a true seeker.

Sadguru may be the perfect Nimit.

But am I receptive?

Do I have:

  • longing,
  • humility,
  • openness,
  • willingness to follow Ajna,
  • readiness to change?

This connects all the way back to Gurudev's earlier teaching:

“You'll reach only by the flight of worthiness.”

6. Why scriptures can appear to contradict each other

Now Gurudev connects Upadan–Nimit with the earlier Nishchay–Vyavhar discussion.

Some scriptures emphasize the absolute viewpoint.

They say:

YOU have to liberate yourself.

No other soul can change your modifications.

Nobody can give you Moksh.

Your effort is what matters.

All true—from that viewpoint.

Other teachings emphasize the relative viewpoint:

Everything happened because of Sadguru.

Without His grace, what could I have done?

Also true—from that viewpoint.

The problem begins when a partial truth is treated as the complete truth. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

7. Sometimes you SHOULD emphasize one side

This is important.

Gurudev isn't saying every sentence must always be:

“50% me, 50% Guru.” 😂

Imagine Gurupurnima:

“BAPA, thank You for everything. Of course, technically I was 50% responsible because I am the Upadan…”

That's not devotion.

During bhakti, the relative viewpoint may appropriately dominate:

“Without YOU, I would have attained nothing.”

And that's beautiful. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

But during meditation, if you sit saying:

“Sadguru, please meditate for me.”

that also makes no sense.

At that moment:

You have to make the effort.

So Gurudev's principle remains:

Whichever viewpoint is appropriate can be emphasized—but never negate the other.

8. Absolute viewpoint — “I liberate myself”

Gurudev deliberately examines liberation entirely through Nishchay/Upadan.

From this viewpoint:

1. The soul becomes free through its own effort.

2. No other soul can interfere with its modifications.

3. No other substance can make it liberated.

4. Instrumental causes don't themselves perform the soul's transformation.

All of this is true from the absolute viewpoint. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

And this viewpoint gives us something extremely important:

Responsibility.

If someone insults me and I become angry:

“They made me angry.”

No.

From Nishchay:

My weakness produced my anger.

I cannot outsource responsibility for my internal state. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

9. Relative viewpoint — “Only because of Sadguru”

Now Gurudev flips completely.

From Vyavhar/Nimit:

“I became free only because of Sadguru.”

Perfectly valid.

In fact, the disciple can say:

“Don't tell me I possess infinite knowledge and bliss. Look at my present condition!”

“Whatever spiritual awakening I have is what YOU gave me.”

Again:

Completely appropriate from this viewpoint. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

But neither viewpoint alone constitutes the entire path.

10. The TWO EYES analogy

This is one of the best analogies in the satsang.

Can you see with one eye?

Yes.

Then Gurudev asks:

“Why don't you destroy the other?”

Obviously not.

Sometimes you may consciously close one eye to focus on something.

But:

temporarily emphasizing one eye ≠ declaring the other useless.

Likewise:

Sometimes emphasize Upadan.

Sometimes emphasize Nimit.

But never destroy either from your understanding. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

This is exactly the same principle as:

Mukhya vs Gaun

from the Nishchay–Vyavhar satsang.

Emphasized ≠ exclusively true.

Subordinated ≠ false.

11. Guru may deliberately push you toward Upadan

Suppose you've lived around Sadguru for years and developed this mentality:

“Guru will do everything.”

But you're:

  • lazy,
  • not meditating,
  • not contemplating,
  • not making effort.

Then Guru may tell you:

“I am not here to do everything for you. YOU have to make the effort.”

At that moment, don't quote:

“But BAPA, Sadguru is the cause of liberation!”

😂

Guru is deliberately emphasizing your Upadan and Purusharth because that's what you need. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

12. And Guru may push you toward Nimit when ego rises

Now suppose the opposite happens.

You begin thinking:

“I did this.”

“My effort.”

“My knowledge.”

“My spiritual progress.”

Then Guru reminds you:

Who held your hand?

Who showed you the path?

Who gave you this perspective?

Who inspired you?

Now the Nimit viewpoint becomes spiritually necessary because it dissolves ego.

Gurudev gives a beautiful image:

Identify with the bubble → ego.

Identify with the ocean → dissolve.

So the appropriate viewpoint depends on what medicine you need at that moment. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

13. Verse 136 specifically warns the Upadan-only seeker

Now Param Krupalu Dev says:

“Upadannu naam lai…”

Putting forward the principal cause:

“The soul has all the potential.”

“I have to do it.”

“Nobody can do anything to me.”

“Guru can't enter my soul.”

Then:

“…e je taje nimit.”

He begins abandoning or disregarding the instrumental cause.

And Param Krupalu Dev gives the consequence:

“Pame nahi Siddhatvane.”

He does not attain liberation.

“Rahe bhrantima sthit.”

He remains established/entangled in delusion. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

14. Why does Param Krupalu Dev emphasize THIS side?

Technically, someone could make the opposite mistake:

Only Guru matters. I don't need effort.

That's also wrong.

Gurudev explicitly says Param Krupalu Dev intends us to avoid both extremes.

But this verse specifically warns against:

Upadan-only spirituality.

Why?

Because this is the road toward the Shushkjnani — dry intellectualist we've repeatedly encountered.

And as we saw in Verse 137, that road can eventually become extremely dangerous. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

15. How the dry intellectualist begins losing Guru

The logic sounds sophisticated:

“Guru is only Nimit.”

“Ultimately I have to do everything.”

“Guru cannot enter my soul.”

“Guru cannot modify my soul.”

Therefore:

Why bhakti?
Why surrender?
Why Guru Ajna?
Why Guru's grace?

Slowly:

Guru-bhakti disappears.

Humility disappears.

Ajna disappears.

Spiritual practice disappears.

And knowledge becomes:

dry, hollow,

barren.

No fruit appears in the form of virtues. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

This connects perfectly with Verse 137's warning.

16. “Bhakti is for people who don't have intellect”

Gurudev describes the attitude humorously:

“Let the youngsters do bhakti.”

“Let people who aren't intellectual do that.”

“I'll study scripture.”

But if studying scripture increases:

  • pride,
  • dryness,
  • lack of devotion,

while producing no:

  • humility,
  • surrender,
  • Self-awareness,
  • virtues,

then the knowledge has failed to produce its intended fruit. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

Again:

the problem isn't scripture.

the interpretation is wrong.

17. “But scriptures themselves say only YOU can liberate yourself!”

Exactly.

Gurudev acknowledges this.

Scriptures do say things like:

Only you can elevate yourself.

Only you can ruin yourself.

Nobody else can fundamentally do it for you.

So the Upadan-only person says:

“See! I'm right!”

Gurudev responds:

The sentence is right.

Your interpretation of its PURPOSE is wrong. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

This is one of the most important ideas in the entire Upsamhar:

A scripturally correct sentence can still produce a spiritually incorrect conclusion.

18. Why did the scriptures emphasize “YOU”?

To:

WAKE YOU UP.

Gurudev says we have apparently:

  • encountered Sadgurus before,
  • heard scriptures before,
  • practiced before,

yet remained in transmigration.

So Guru says:

Wake up!

“You need to make the effort.”

The purpose is to remove:

Pramad — indolence.

It was not intended to make you conclude:

“Therefore Sadguru contributes nothing.” Pasted text(20261116-003838)

That's the key.

19. “Take the support of Sadguru to focus on the SELF”

Gurudev gives perhaps the cleanest synthesis of the whole pair:

Take the support of Sadguru to focus on yourself.

Why:

  • Guru?
  • scripture?
  • bhakti?
  • satsang?
  • Ajna?

Ultimately:

to turn awareness toward the Self.

Sadguru isn't trying to make you permanently dependent on His personality.

He is helping you awaken to your true nature.

So:

Nimit points you toward Upadan.

And:

Upadan becomes receptive to Nimit. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

That's the harmony.

20. The TWO LEGS analogy

Gurudev gives another very simple image.

To walk:

You need two legs.

Do they move at exactly the same instant?

Not necessarily.

One steps.

Then the other.

But can you say:

“Since only one is moving right now, I'll cut off the other?”

Obviously not.

Likewise, on the spiritual path:

sometimes Purusharth is emphasized.

sometimes Guru's grace/support is emphasized.

But the journey requires both. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

21. Two aspects of spiritual pursuit

Near the end Gurudev summarizes everything into two beautiful phrases:

1. Priceless Potential

Your soul's immense inherent capacity.

2. Ceaseless Guidance

Sadguru's compassion, inspiration and direction.

Gurudev says Sadguru's compassion:

never stops.

But that guidance must meet a seeker who is willing to:

  • ponder,
  • contemplate,
  • meditate,
  • make effort,
  • transform. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

22. “He sees this in me—why can't I?”

Gurudev gives a beautiful picture of how Nimit activates Upadan.

Sadguru says:

“You are pure.”

“You possess this potential.”

And the disciple begins wondering:

“He sees this in me. Why can't I see it?”

That creates:

wondering

↓

thinking

↓

contemplating

↓

meditating

↓

experiencing This is exactly how the Nimit doesn't give the experience but helps awaken the movement toward it. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

23. The final formula: TRUST both

Gurudev brings the entire teaching toward Samyak Darshan — Right Faith.

A seeker needs:

1. Trust in his immense potential.

“My soul truly is capable of liberation.”

And:

2. Trust in the Facilitator.

“Sadguru truly knows the path and His guidance is for my spiritual welfare.”

When both become firm:

trust in Self + trust in Sadguru

the seeker gains the proper orientation toward liberation. Pasted text(20261116-003838)

24. The aspiring singer example — “Divine Symphony”

Gurudev ends with a beautiful analogy.

An aspiring singer possesses:

the voice.

But a music teacher teaches the singer:

When:

the singer's potential

Gurudev says spiritual pursuit is the same:

Upadan + Nimit

properly aligned.

And that becomes the spiritual “divine symphony.” Pasted text(20261116-003838)

How Verses 135–136 fit together

These two should really be remembered as a pair.

Verse 135 — THE PRINCIPLE
Upadan exists:

Every soul has Siddha-potential.

Nimit exists:

Sadguru Ajna and contemplation of Jindasha assist its manifestation.

Therefore:

Both causes matter.

↓ Verse 136 — THE WARNING

Don't hear:

“The soul itself is the Upadan”

and conclude:

“Therefore Guru is unnecessary.”

That creates:

  • pride,
  • dryness,
  • loss of bhakti,
  • loss of humility,
  • loss of Ajna,
  • abandonment of spiritual means,

and eventually:

“Pame nahi Siddhatvane, rahe bhrantima sthit.”

You remain in delusion rather than attaining liberation.

And now Verses 131–136 form one complete teaching

This is worth seeing because Gurudev has now finished the first major section of the Upsamhar.

Verses 131–134
Nishchay + Vyavhar

Don't choose:

pure nature OR spiritual practice.

Understand your pure nature and practice appropriately.

↓

Verses 135–136

Upadan + Nimit Don't choose:

my potential OR Sadguru.

Trust your potential and take refuge in the appropriate instrumental cause.

So Param Krupalu Dev repeatedly destroys our tendency to think:

Either / Or.

The Enlightened vision keeps saying:

Understand BOTH correctly.

The deepest practical takeaway

For me, the cleanest takeaway from this satsang is:

Sadguru cannot walk for me. But without Him, I may not even know where to walk.

If I say:

“BAPA will do everything.”

then I'm ignoring the Upadan.

I still have to:

  • contemplate,
  • meditate,
  • follow Ajna,
  • reduce attachment,
  • reduce aversion,
  • become less reactive,
  • cultivate virtues,
  • turn inward.

BAPA cannot experience my Self for me.

But if I swing to:

“I have to do everything. Why do I need BAPA?”

I've made the opposite mistake.

Because who helped me recognize:

what I should become?

Who showed me:

where I'm fooling myself?

Who gives me a living example of:

  • happiness,
  • equanimity,
  • acceptance,
  • seva,
  • surrender,
  • love?

Who can say:

“You're going in the wrong direction. Change now.”

That's the Nimit.

So the healthiest spiritual attitude isn't:

“I can do it myself.”

Nor:

“He will do it for me.”

It's:

“HE has shown me what I am capable of becoming. Now let me sincerely become it.”

That preserves both:

Humility

because I recognize Guru's immeasurable contribution.

and:

Responsibility because I know the transformation must happen within me.

And I think Gurudev's final two phrases capture the entire thing perfectly:

Trust your immense potential.

Trust the Facilitator.

When the two align—

the soul willing to transform

with

Sadguru ceaselessly guiding the transformation—

Gurudev calls the result:

a Divine Symphony.
