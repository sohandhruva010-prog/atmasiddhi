Extensive Study Notes (Scripture as Support when a Living Sadguru isn’t found)

Built from your Video 13 transcript. Structured for flowing study and ready to print as a PDF.

1) Where Verse 13 fits in the arc

  • Verse 9: Need of a Guru (drop nij-pakṣ, adopt Guru’s view–conviction–conduct).
  • Verse 10: Five marks of a Sadguru.
  • Verse 11: For the seeker today, the living (pratyakṣ) Sadguru’s upkār exceeds that of the non-present (parokṣ) Jina (by benefit to you).
  • Verse 12: Without a Sadguru’s teaching, you neither understand the Jina’s nature nor can your devotion bear fruit.
  • Verse 13: Crisis clause: If a qualified seeker cannot presently find a qualified living Sadguru, what should he do? Answer: Take refuge in scripture that establishes the existence and nature of soul and non-soul; use it as sustainer and guide until the meeting occurs.

2) Verse 13 — sense and short meaning

Sense (as conveyed in the talk): - “In the absence of a living Enlightened Mentor, the scriptures that establish the existence of the soul, etc. are a reliable support for worthy seekers.”

Short meaning (two verbs implied): - When pratyakṣ Sadguru-yoga nahi (no access to a living Sadguru), ātmādi-astitvā-nirūpaka śāstra (texts that expound the existence & nature of the soul and other substances) become your ādhār (prop), for the deserving (supātra).

3) Key terms clarified

  • Pratyakṣ Sadguru-yoga nahi — literal absence of contact with a living Sadguru (not laziness in seeking). Ātmādi astitvana — “the existence of the soul, etc.” → six substances (ṣaḍ-dravya): jīva (soul) and five non-souls (ajīva: pudgala—matter, dharma—medium of motion, adharma—medium of rest, ākāśa—space, kāla—time). Includes their natures, modifications, bondage & liberation mechanics. (Not “the soul’s attributes etc.” only.)
  • Nirūpaka śāstra — scriptures that establish/explain these fundamentals.
  • Supātra — deserving seekers: sincere, disciplined, humble, eager for truth.

4) Why scripture (śāstra) — the “availability logic”

Dev (Tirthankars): present only in the 4th era—not accessible now. Guru (living Sadguru): accessible in the 5th era, but limited by a body (cannot be in two places at once; you may not find Him yet). Śāstra: can be printed, distributed, carried; available anytime, anywhere (even at 2 am, in a town with no temple or saints). → Hence, in this crisis, śāstra becomes your lifeline.

5) What scripture is (and is not)

  • Is: Āpta-vachan—reliable speech of realised Masters of the past; distilled wisdom about soul, bondage, and liberation.
  • Is not: a substitute for a living Sadguru; it cannot on its own dispel your delusion or tailor-correct your blind spots.

Use-case: Scripture is the bridge you walk until you reach the surgeon (Sadguru). It steadies, clarifies, and prepares you.

6) The seeker’s crisis that Verse 13 answers

  • You value a living Sadguru and are actively searching, but haven’t found one yet. Exposure to many views causes confusion (devotion-only, meditation-only, service-only, study-only camps). Discouragement looms. Verse 13 says: Don’t stall. Anchor in śāstra that teaches jīva–ajīva truths; let it sustain worthiness until the meeting.

7) Which scriptures?

  • Those that establish jīva–ajīva, bondage–liberation, causes (internal & external), and the means— the mokṣa-śāstras / dharma-śāstras.
  • Not artha-śāstra (economics) or kāma-śāstra (sensuality, erotica). Choose soul-centric texts.

8) How to use scripture (overview; details follow in Verse 14)

1) Read/Listen regularly (assigned selections if you have prior guidance; else begin with foundational jīva– ajīva works). 2) Contemplate (note causes, effects, and personal implications). 3) Practise small, testable steps; observe change; refine.

Caution: Do not get comfortable here. Keep the search for a living Sadguru alive; scripture is support, not destination.

9) Benefits you can expect (and what you can’t)

Can expect: - Orientation in fundamentals (what is Self/non-Self; why bondage; how release begins). - Stability against fads and one-sided emphases. - Growth in worthiness (humility, detachment, inner honesty).

Cannot expect: - Self-realisation directly by book-study; books don’t inspect you or dissolve delusion by themselves.

10) Practical plan (30–45 days)

A. Core daily loop (20–25 min): - Read (10 min): one passage on jīva–ajīva / bondage–liberation. - Reflect (5 min): What conduct must change today? One small vow. - Report (5–10 min weekly): If you have any guide, send a summary; if not, journal honestly.

B. Weekly (30–45 min): - Concept map: six substances → their key attributes → how they relate to bondage/liberation. - Bias-check: List where you skew (e.g., devotion-only); add one balancing practice.

C. Monthly (45–60 min): - Worthiness audit: detachment, humility, sincerity, desire for truth. Give each 1– 5; write next step.

11) “Why–Which–How” — from the verse

  • Why scripture? Āpta-vachan—reliable words of realised Masters; universally available.
  • Which? Ātmādi-astitvana nirūpak texts—those that establish jīva–ajīva, bondage/liberation fundamentals.
  • How? Read–Listen–Contemplate–Practise (details to be expanded in Verse 14).

12) Warnings and analogies from the talk

  • Self-study danger: Done without surrender, it can breed dry intellectualism (the very derailer Verses 1–8 warned about).
  • Analogy (unmarried course-taker): If a marriage hasn’t happened yet, take courses/travel to stay growing—but don’t mistake that for the marriage. Likewise, scripture ≠ Sadguru; keep seeking Him.

13) Reflection prompts

1) Where exactly am I stuck in the absence of a Guru (devotion vs detachment; knowledge vs practice)? 2) Which one śāstra (soul-centric) will I commit to for 30 days? What daily slot? 3) What sign would tell me my worthiness is increasing (e.g., quicker opinion-dropping, kinder speech, steadier inner state)?

14) One-page recap

  • Problem: A worthy seeker hasn’t yet found a living Sadguru.
  • Instruction: Take refuge in jīva–ajīva scriptures as support and authentic guide—but do not accept them as a substitute for the Sadguru.
  • Why: They’re reliable (āpta-vachan) and available anywhere/anytime.
  • How: Read–Listen–Contemplate–Practise (Verse 14 deepens the how).
  • Posture: Keep the search and longing for the living Sadguru burning.
