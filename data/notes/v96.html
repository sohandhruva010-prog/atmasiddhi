Sixth Fundamental Truth (Moksh Upay)

  • The 6th Fundamental Truth = mokṣa upāy (means/path/method to attain liberation).
  • The disciple’s doubts about this last Fundamental are structured as:
    • Verses 92–96: disciple’s doubts + supporting arguments (the “problem statement”)
    • Verses 97–118: Guru’s systematic resolution (the “answer / solution”)

This video focuses on Verse 96

  • The disciple has already given:
    • 3 arguments (v.92, v.93, v.94)
    • a gloomy conclusion (v.95)
  • Now comes his final verse (v.96): a re-rise of confidence and deep longing.

2) Quick recap: the disciple’s 3 arguments (v.92–v.94)

The disciple’s doubt is basically:

“Even if liberation exists, I don’t see an undisputed, universally accepted path to reach it.”

He supports it using 3 arguments:

Argument 1 (v.92): Infinite karmas vs short human life

  • Karmas accumulated since infinite time → how can they be destroyed in one short lifespan?
  • For liberation, two things must happen:

1. Stop acquiring new karmas (no fresh bondage)

2. Destroy past karmas (remove the backlog)

Argument 2 (v.93): Too many views / schools of thought

  • Many opinions + many philosophies give different paths.
  • “Which one is true? I can’t decide.”

Argument 3 (v.94): Confusion about eligibility (caste/gender/garb)

  • “Which caste or gender leads to liberation?”
  • “Which garb (dress/attire) leads to liberation?”
  • Clothes can be changed (though not always easy), but caste/gender is seen as fixed → so the confusion feels high-stakes.

3) The mood shift: from confusion → frustration → sadness (v.95)

By verse 95, the disciple is no longer only “confused” — he becomes:

  • anxious
  • frustrated
  • sad / depressed
  • almost hopeless

His emotional logic becomes:

“If I can’t know the path, then what is the use of knowing the soul, karma, liberation etc.?”

So the “tone” becomes:

  • “This was all futile.”
  • “Maybe I wasted your time.”
  • “I sacrificed comforts, stayed here, studied… what did I gain?”

This is important: Your guru is showing how a seeker can slip from intellectual doubt into an inner crisis.

4) Verse 96: the dramatic turnaround (core of this video)

In verse 96, the disciple suddenly regains:

  • certainty
  • enthusiasm
  • joy
  • deep faith in the Guru’s ability

The guru highlights: This is not a small emotional fluctuation — it reveals the disciple’s worthiness and the power of Guru-bhakti + trust.

5) Verse 96 text + word-by-word meaning

Verse 96 (as spoken in the discourse)

“Panche uttar thi thayu samadhan sarvang;
Samju moksh upay to uday uday sadbhagya.”

Line 1: “Panche uttar thi thayu samadhan sarvang”

  • Panche = five
  • Uttar = replies / answers
  • Thi = through / by
  • Thayu = happened / became
  • Samadhan = resolution, satisfaction, clearing of doubts
  • Sarvang = complete, total, entire

✅ Meaning: “Through your replies to my first five doubts, my doubts have been completely resolved.”

Key subtle point your guru stresses:

  • The disciple is honest:
    • He is fully convinced about five (not pretending about the sixth yet).

Line 2: “Samju moksh upay to uday uday sadbhagya”

  • Samju = if I understand
  • Moksh upay = means/path of liberation
  • To = then
  • Uday uday = dawn/dawning (said twice for intensity)
  • Sadbhagya = good fortune (not ordinary luck, but spiritually meaningful fortune)

✅ Meaning: “If I understand the path of liberation too, that will be the dawn (the rising) of my great good fortune.”

Why “uday uday” twice?

Your guru explains it as:

  • emphasis on eagerness
  • emphasis on enthusiasm
  • like the disciple is already dancing internally because he is sure it will happen.

6) Why did his optimism suddenly return?

Your guru answers this directly:

The disciple goes into “memory lane”

He remembers:

  • Earlier too, he had huge doubts:
    • existence of soul
    • eternity of soul
    • doership
    • receivership/enjoyership
    • liberation
  • And each time, the Guru’s replies were so complete that:
    • “I don’t need extra sessions.”
    • “No iota of confusion remains.”

So even though the sixth still feels difficult, he thinks:

“The Guru who resolved those cyclones can resolve this cyclone too.”

The “connection” had loosened for a moment

Your guru points out:

  • confusion + too many external options → the disciple briefly slips into low faith
  • then he refocuses on:
    • Guru’s compassion
    • Guru’s capacity
  • and the faith is “regained.”

7) Milton analogy: Paradise Lost → Paradise Regained

Your guru uses John Milton as a memory hook for “regained”:

  • Milton wrote Paradise Lost after deep personal tragedy (blindness, loss, grief).
  • Years later he wrote Paradise Regained — symbolizing acceptance and recovery.
Spiritual lesson your guru is conveying:
  • When you argue with reality → you fall into “Paradise Lost.”
  • When you accept and re-center → “Paradise Regained.”
  • But in spiritual life, you don’t have to take 20 years to regain; you can regain quickly by returning to Guru-centeredness.

8) A powerful side-teaching: “Sansar” and acceptance

Your guru says:

  • World = sansar, linked to the idea of slipping/changing (“sru” as a root mentioned in the talk).
  • Meaning:
    • arrangements change
    • situations change
    • health/wealth change
    • external “good times” won’t remain

So the instruction is:

  • Focus on the unchanging (inner truth, Self, right direction).
  • In “good” situations: deepen satsang calmly.
  • In “bad” situations: become more spiritual, not more worldly.

9) Verse 96 as a “character sketch” of a worthy disciple

Your guru repeatedly says: this verse isn’t only content; it reveals the disciple’s inner quality.

Traits highlighted

1) Humility

  • “I can’t decide; I’m not capable; you show me.”
  • Not trying to appear smart.

2) Faith + surrender

  • Even after despair in v.95, he does not quit.
  • He re-anchors in Guru.

3) Intense longing

  • His happiness is linked to receiving the true method from the Guru.

4) Honesty (no false claims)

  • He says clearly:
    • “I’m convinced about five.”
    • “I still need clarity about the sixth.”

10) Story: “Guru should not be given vikalp (options)”

Your guru tells a modern anecdote:

  • An NRI comes for only 3 days; has a $30,000 meeting/deal.
  • Sadhu gives appointment day after tomorrow at 3:30pm.
  • The man feels a moment of hesitation but accepts.
  • When asked, he admits the conflict—but says:
    • “I will cancel/reschedule the deal.”
    • “Guru is more important.”
    • He will not ask the Guru for an alternative time.

Your guru’s key line:

  • “Guru should not be given vikalp.” (vikalp = options/alternatives)

Meaning (as taught here):

  • If the Guru sets the time, don’t negotiate like:
    • “Can I come earlier instead?”
    • “Can you do this small task?”
  • That subtle bargaining is not reverence; it puts the Guru into your convenience-system.

Why is this included here? Because it mirrors the disciple’s worthiness:

  • The disciple is not trying to “manage” the Guru.
  • He is ready to align himself fully.

11) The river analogy: why humility is necessary

Your guru says:

  • If you stand stiff like a palm tree on the riverbank, you won’t benefit.
  • To drink water, you must bend, cup hands, and receive.
Spiritual translation:
  • To benefit from Guru and truth, you must bend through humility.

12) Bhagya vs Sadbhagya: what is “real fortune”?

Your guru distinguishes:

Ordinary fortune (bhagya)

  • health, wealth, comforts, luxuries, gifts
  • comes from meritorious karma
  • but is temporary

Good fortune (sadbhagya)

  • association of Sadguru
  • satsang, seva
  • the opportunity to ask and receive the right method
  • this is spiritually priceless

Crucial point:

Material things can’t be carried into the next life, but:

  • conviction of truth
  • devotion
  • faith can continue.

13) Merit “balance” analogy (bank example)

He gives a clean practical metaphor:

  • You have a limited balance of merit.
  • Two “books”:
    • one increases balance (deposit)
    • one decreases balance (cheque/spending)

If you “spend” merit only on pleasures:

  • later you may not have the merit-balance needed for satsang/seva opportunities.

Message: Don’t waste spiritual opportunity-capital.

14) What the disciple is confident about (quiz-ready)

Your guru basically gives two R’s (he even flags them):

1. Resolve:

  • “My Guru will surely resolve my inner spiritual crisis.”

2. Receive:

  • “I will surely receive the undisputed path of liberation.”

And once received:

  • he is implying a commitment:
    • “I’ll plunge headlong, leave no stone unturned.”

15) The core takeaway your guru is trying to convey

This video is not just “meaning of a verse.”

It is training you to notice the inner mechanics of the spiritual journey:

  • Doubts are natural.
  • Confusion can turn into frustration and hopelessness.
  • The antidote is not forced positivity.
  • The antidote is:

1. remembering Guru’s past upkaar (benevolence)

2. regaining connection

3. letting enthusiasm rise again

4. staying humble and ready to receive the method

And the disciple models this perfectly in verse 96.
