• Text/Section: Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra, Section 2 — Matārthina Lakṣaṇ (attributes of a religious bigot).
  • Verse 27 (previous talk): Bigot’s false beliefs about scriptures and external attire as causes of liberation.
  • Verse 28 (this talk): Bigot’s wrong notions about the components of dharma—especially vows (vrat) and austerities/penance (tapa).

The Verse (as explained)

“Lahyu swaroop na vruttinu…” — He has not understood the nature of his tendencies (vrutti: instinctive urges/modifications of mind; inclinations from past impressions). “Grahyu vrat abhimaan…” — He practises vows/penances but with pride—“with a view to earn respect from others.” “Grahe nahi parmarthne, leva laukik maan.” — He does not understand the spiritual significance (parmarth) and instead aims to “take worldly honour” (laukik maan).

Core message: He gives up something (food/comfort) to get something (applause/status). That’s religious bigotry, not spirituality.

Key Terms (as used in the talk)

  • Vrat — vow/restraint.
  • Tapa — austerity/penance.
  • Vrutti — tendencies/instinctive urges; “modifications or thoughts arising in your mind” (kept simple as tendencies).
  • Abhimaan — pride.
  • Parmarth — spiritual purpose/meaning.
  • Laukik maan — worldly honour, attention, public respect.
  • Parna — “breaking of the fast” (distinct from “breakfast”).
  • Ayambil / Ayambil Oli — type/series of austere eating.
  • Upvās — fasting.
  • Unodari — “eat less.”
  • Loksanjna — “what will people say?” social regard.

What the Bigot Gets Wrong About Vows & Austerities

1) Wrong Belief

“External vows/austerities = true conduct.” He equates externals (what is visible) with inner virtue (what counts).

  • He assumes doing the vow is spirituality, irrespective of the inner motive or shifts in tendencies.
  • Clarification from the teacher: Externals can help you reach true conduct, but they are not true conduct by themselves.

2) Wrong Conduct

“Grahyu vrat abhimaan” — creating pride out of vows/tapas.

  • He enjoys the attention, privileges, “a special place to sit,” even people touching his feet after a “maaskshaman — month-long fast.”
  • He flaunts austerities and loves the praise:

“Did you enjoy it?” If yes, you fall under “grahyu vrat abhimaan.”

Result: He misses the purpose of vows/tapas—checking, correcting, redirecting tendencies toward the Soul—and instead creates new wrong tendencies (ego, display).

The Diagnostic the Verse Demands

Q1. “Lahyu swaroop na vruttinu?”
  • Did I observe my tendencies during the vow?
  • When hunger pangs rose on day 6, did I watch the mind (“idli without sambhar and chutney will also do”) or did I chase cravings (“Bring snacks from Nadiad… papad from Surat”)?
Q2. “Grahyu vrat abhimaan?”
  • Was I quietly intent on inner change, or was I “flaunting”—seeking photos, praise, status?

If your answers reveal motive-drift (to weight loss, attention, status), you’re in the error this verse exposes.

The Examples (his words, to make it concrete)

A. “Parna” spectacle after fasting
  • “Parna has to be grand.”
  • Menu & demands: “Idli sambhar,” “Pav bhaji from Santosh Sagar,” “cheese rolls from Haji Ali,” “What do you get at Rahul? (Cottage cheese rolls)” — and he quips: “I have never touched Rahul or Rahul’s cottage cheese rolls. Never.”
  • Look & logistics: “Who comes at 6 in the morning…? Hairdresser! Make-up artist!” “Two days prior… to the safe, to bring some jewellery.” “Has the photographer been informed?” “40–50 things in silver utensils.”
B. Fasts for the wrong objective
  • Weight-loss fasting: someone says, “I did it because my belly had increased considerably.” → The teacher sharply condemns misusing spiritual practice for vanity/weight goals and stresses fasts are for inner work, not body-image wins.
C. “Sham and show” vs substance
  • Before renunciation, some even plan a photo shoot: “Packaging has become so important, the insides have remained hollow and empty.” Anecdote: He was told to wear a “dress like a king” with a turban jewel worth ₹3 crore; he didn’t want any of it — detachment should be present months before renunciation.
D. Spirituality as fashion statement
  • Yoga: “Your yoga postures are not as good as your yoga mat.” “If you have seen the yoga dresses! Those are the ones uploaded on Facebook.”
  • Same with: “8-day fasts,” “meditation courses.”
E. Attention vs Truth
  • “Being a symbol of pride is not praiseworthy on this path.”
  • Some won’t accept a Sadguru’s correction because public attention feels too good.

The Positive Teaching: Why Take Vows/Tapas at All?

Because of two kinds of tendencies (vruttis):

1. “Swabhāvik vrutti” — tendencies leading to the Soul.

2. “Vaibhāvik vrutti” — tendencies leading to worldliness (“not worthiness but worldliness”).

Spiritual endeavour =
  • Become aware of tendencies (he prefers the softer word “awareness” over “check”).
  • Check → correct → redirect them from worldliness to the Soul.
  • Use vows/tapas as tools to support this inner redirection.

If you aren’t aware, both things run in parallel: vows & wrong tendencies. Then vows fail their purpose and breed new ego (display, “flaunting”).

What to Watch For (red flags)

  • Objective drift: From “spiritual welfare” to “weight loss/attention.”
  • Delight in praise: Special seating, public bows, social buzz.
  • Event-ification: Treating parna as a show—menu demands, jewellery, photographer, silverware.
  • Loksanjna fear: “What will people say if I stop fasting?”
  • Resisting real guidance: Ignoring a Sadguru because you might lose face.

Exemplary Contrast: Lalluji Muni

  • He told Param Krupālu Dev in Khambhat (Diwali days): despite “fasting every alternate day”, subtle carnal tendencies remained (“I’m not happy with my state of brahmacharya.”)
  • A charlatan might say: “Do two fasts and try.”
  • Param Krupālu Dev prescribed “unodari — eat less.” → No public praise (people see you eat twice a day), but he actually got free: “now I’m free of it.”
  • Point: A seeker wants freedom from tendencies, not public image.

The Two Summative Errors (write these down)

1. Wrong Belief: “External vows/austerities are true conduct.” → Correction: They can lead to true conduct; they’re not identical to it.

2. Wrong Conduct: “Grahyu vrat abhimaan” — turning vows into ego fuel (seeking “laukik maan”). → Correction: Maintain humility, privacy, and inner focus; let vows reduce pride, not inflate it.

Practice Checklist (simple & actionable)

  • Before a vow:
    • Name your inner objective (detachment? Sense-control? Equanimity?).
    • Decide how you’ll observe tendencies (journal, brief check-ins).
  • During a vow:
    • Note cravings, irritations, mind’s tricks; gently redirect.
    • Avoid special treatment/announcements; keep it simple.
  • After a vow (parna):
    • Keep it plain; skip spectacle.
    • Ask: Did this reduce attachment and increase inward stillness?
    • If you detect pride, do repentance/atonement and simplify the next practice.
  • When advised (by Sadguru/mentor):
    • Prefer efficacy (unodari) over image (heroic fasts).
    • Drop loksanjna; keep the purpose front and center.

One-Page Recap

  • Verse 28 unmasks a bigot’s two errors about vows/austerities:

(1) treating externals as true conduct, (2) turning them into ego displays.

  • Right lens: Vows/tapas are tools to observe, correct, and redirect vruttis toward the Soul.
  • Wrong lens: Using them to gain attention, status, or fashionable identity (“sham and show”).
  • True success: After the practice, there is less attachment and more inward stillness.
  • Follow guidance that reduces tendencies (“unodari”), not the guidance that inflates image.
