1) Placement of this verse in the 108–113 spiritual ladder

The speaker begins by revising the sequence:

Verse 108 = preparation Verse 109 = first phase of right belief: vyavhar samkit Verse 110 = second phase of right belief: shuddh samkit / experiential faith Verse 111 = third and highest phase of right belief: parmarth samkit Verse 112 = right conduct Verse 113 = omniscience

So verse 111 is the highest phase of samkit before the section moves into charitra in verse 112 and kevaljnan in verse 113.

The progression is:

Preparation → Faith in Sadguru’s words → Direct glimpse of the Self → Constant inward flow → Right conduct → Omniscience

2) The three samkits from Vachanamrut Patrank 751

The speaker reads from Vachanamrut Patrank 751, where Param Krupalu Dev explains the three kinds of samkit mentioned in Atmasiddhi.

1. First samkit: faith in the words of the Enlightened One

This corresponds to verse 109.

It includes:

  • faith in the words of the Enlightened One
  • unprecedented passion for His commands
  • devotion to the Enlightened One
  • restraining self-will

This is vyavhar samkit because it depends on:

  • Sadguru
  • His teachings
  • His words
  • His commands

It is true right faith, but it is still based on the authority and trustworthiness of the Enlightened One.

2. Second samkit: brief but distinct experience of the Self

This corresponds to verse 110.

The speaker calls it:

  • shuddh samkit
  • experiential faith
  • faith arising from a first-hand glimpse

It is brief, like a flash, but distinct and unforgettable.

He says: it may be only a flash, but it is also a splash of colour on your state. It transforms the aspirant’s vision forever.

3. Third samkit: thoughtless experience of the Self

This corresponds to verse 111.

This is parmarth samkit.

It is not just a one-time glimpse. It is a deeper, more regular, more powerful flow of Self-experience. The Enlightened One is now deeply connected with the Self through:

  • direct experience
  • constant awareness
  • firm conviction

This is the main subject of the whole video.

3) Verse 111: word-by-word meaning

The verse revolves around these key words:

Varte nijswabhavno anubhav laksh pratit;
Vrutti vahey nijbhavma, paramarthe samkit.

“Varte”

Means: there prevails, there exists, there remains active.

“Nijswabhavno”

Means: of one’s own true nature.

This verse is not about external conduct, scriptures, rituals, or philosophy. It is about the true nature of the Self.

“Anubhav”

Means: experience.

The speaker clarifies that this is not intellectual knowledge. It is direct experience of the Self.

“Laksh”

Means: awareness, focus, inclination, aim, gaze.

It means the Enlightened One’s inner attention is constantly directed toward the Self, even while engaged in worldly activity.

“Pratit”

Means: firm conviction.

This conviction is not based on books or someone else’s statement. It is rooted in direct experience.

“Vrutti vahey”

Means: the tendency flows.

“Nijbhavma”

Means: inward, toward one’s own true state.

“Paramarthe samkit”

Means: this is called right faith in the highest, absolute sense.

4) Short meaning of verse 111

Where there exists direct experience, constant awareness, and firm conviction of one’s own true nature, and where the inner tendency flows toward the Self, that state is called parmarth samkit, the highest form of right belief.

5) The three words that reveal the state of the Enlightened One

The whole verse is explained through three words:

1. Anubhav — direct experience

2. Laksh — constant awareness / inclination

3. Pratit — firm conviction

The speaker says that if you understand these three words, you understand the state of an Enlightened One.

This verse describes:

  • the state of the Guru
  • the state of the Enlightened Being
  • the state of parmarth samkit
  • the deep flow of Self-experience

6) Difference between shuddh samkit and parmarth samkit

This is one of the central teachings of the video.

Shuddh samkit: verse 110

In verse 110, the aspirant gets a glimpse of the Self.

The speaker calls it:

  • a flash
  • a splash
  • a short interview with the pure Self
  • a momentary experience

It may last only for seconds, but it changes the aspirant’s vision forever.

However, the aspirant cannot enter that state whenever he wants. It happened once, but it may not happen again immediately. The next experience may come after days, months, or much effort.

Parmarth samkit: verse 111

In verse 111, the experience becomes deep, repeated, and increasingly stable.

It is not necessarily constant no-mind experience, because the Enlightened One still has a body and duties. But the connection with the Self becomes constant through one of three modes:

  • direct experience
  • awareness
  • conviction

The speaker says:

Verse 110 is a short interview with the Self. Verse 111 is a constant interview with the Self.

7) The “Rs. 10,000” example: one-time gift vs regular income

To explain the difference between verse 110 and 111, he gives an example:

Suppose someone gives you Rs. 10,000 once. That is nice, but it does not change your status.

But imagine you receive Rs. 10,000:

  • every month
  • every week
  • every day
  • every hour

Now your status changes.

Similarly:

  • Shuddh samkit is like receiving the experience once.
  • Parmarth samkit is like receiving that inner richness again and again, regularly, deeply, and naturally.

The value of the experience may be the same, but the frequency and depth are different.

8) Why verse 111 is needed

The speaker asks: why is verse 111 needed if verse 110 already described Self-experience?

Because the Enlightened One still lives in the world.

He still has:

  • a body
  • duties
  • physical needs
  • karmic circumstances
  • roles to play
  • worldly interactions

An Omniscient Being has no such inner struggle. A Liberated Soul has no body at all. But an Enlightened One still has to live through the body.

So verse 111 answers:

How does an Enlightened Soul live while still embodied?

This verse explains what happens in the Enlightened One’s:

  • free time
  • active time
  • sleep

9) The three states of an Enlightened One

The speaker explains anubhav, laksh, and pratit by connecting them to three situations:

Situation State of Enlightened One Word

When free / not active Direct Self-experience Anubhav

When active in worldly duties Awareness / inclination toward Self Laksh

When asleep Firm conviction remains Pratit

This is the heart of the discourse.

10) Anubhav: the Enlightened One in moments of retirement

What is “retirement” here?

It does not mean retirement from a job at age 60 or 75.

It means:

  • not engaged in worldly duties
  • not physically or mentally active
  • free from external work
  • inwardly available

In such moments, the Enlightened One naturally goes into anubhavdhara — the flow of Self-experience.

What is this experience like?

He describes it as:

  • direct experience
  • beyond the senses
  • blissful
  • peaceful
  • relaxing
  • steady
  • contented
  • equanimous
  • no-mind
  • nirvikalp
  • thoughtless absorption

The Enlightened One wants to:

  • retain this state
  • prolong it
  • go deeper into it
  • remain immersed in it

This is not like ordinary meditation done for a fixed 30 minutes. The Enlightened One derives immense joy from this state, so He wants to stay there.

11) Contrast: what ignorant people do in free time

The speaker contrasts the Enlightened One’s free time with the ignorant person’s free time.

When an ignorant person is free, he usually does one of two things:

1. Seeks entertainment

Examples:

  • phone
  • television
  • laptop
  • restaurants
  • theatre
  • movies
  • sports
  • swimming
  • newspapers
  • sleeping late
  • “chilling”

2. Dwells in useless thoughts of past and future

Examples:

  • “How will my son’s wedding happen?”
  • “What will happen in the next Covid wave?”
  • “They did not celebrate my birthday properly.”
  • “Nobody really wanted to stay with me.”
  • “How will we plan this event?”

The point:

The ignorant person fills free time with entertainment or useless thoughts. The Enlightened One fills free time with Self-experience.

12) Laksh: the Enlightened One during activity

The next question: what happens when the Enlightened One is not free?

He may be:

  • eating
  • bathing
  • speaking
  • teaching
  • running a business
  • fulfilling family duties
  • interacting with people
  • attending to physical needs

At such times, He cannot remain in a complete no-mind state because activity requires some mental and physical functioning.

So the state is not anubhavdhara, but lakshdhara.

What is laksh?

Laksh means:

  • awareness
  • inclination
  • focus
  • gaze
  • inner aim
  • inward orientation

Even while active, the Enlightened One’s inner focus remains on the Self.

The no-mind state may not be present, but the awareness of the pure nature does not disappear.

13) “This is not my goal; this is only my role; I am pure Soul”

This is one of the most memorable mantras from the video.

When performing worldly duties, the Enlightened One’s awareness is:

This is not my goal. This is only my role. I am the pure Soul.

The speaker reduces it to:

Goal, role, soul.

Examples:

  • earning money is not His goal
  • business is only His role
  • family role is not His goal
  • speaking is only His role
  • eating is only a bodily need
  • the true identity is the pure Soul

He says Param Krupalu Dev would sign some letters as “Embodied Rajchandra”, showing the awareness that the body is called Rajchandra, but the Self is beyond it.

14) Why the Enlightened One still performs duties

The question arises:

If He enjoys the bliss of the Soul, why does He do worldly activity?

Answer:

Because of past karmas.

The soul has acquired certain karmas, and those karmas have manifested. Due to the limitations of embodiment, the Enlightened One may have to:

  • marry
  • run a business
  • raise children
  • interact with people
  • perform duties

The speaker uses Param Krupalu Dev’s life:

  • shuddh samkit manifested in 1947
  • parmarth samkit became unobstructed in 1948
  • yet He had to live as:
    • husband
    • businessman
    • father
    • householder

The speaker says: see the karmas and see the state — untouched.

15) Duties must be performed perfectly and dispassionately

He makes a subtle point:

If the Enlightened One does not perform duties properly, others may get disturbed and passions may arise in them.

But if He performs them with attachment, He loses dispassion.

So He must perform duties:

  • perfectly
  • responsibly
  • without attachment
  • without aversion
  • without likes-dislikes
  • with inner awareness

He is active, but inwardly detached.

16) Wedding or war: same inner awareness

The speaker gives two extremes:

  • a wedding
  • a war

Even if the Enlightened One is a king and must defend his kingdom, He remains detached.

Whether it is:

  • wedding
  • war
  • business
  • family
  • conversation
  • eating
  • public duty

His only aim remains:

When will I again abide in my true Self?

17) The plane above the clouds analogy

The Enlightened One is described as beyond everything.

Like a plane flying above clouds:

  • black cloud: He is beyond
  • white cloud: He is beyond
  • no cloud: He is still beyond

He is beyond:

  • body
  • senses
  • mind
  • pure feelings
  • impure feelings
  • even the cloudless state

He is the eternal, constant, unchanging Self.

18) The valuables-in-the-pocket analogy

A person carrying valuables in his pocket keeps checking:

  • Is it still there?
  • Has it fallen?
  • Is it safe?

Even if he does not open the pocket, he touches it from outside.

Similarly, the Enlightened One keeps returning to the Self again and again.

When He gets time:

  • He may read scripture
  • enter contemplation
  • become absorbed
  • become speechless
  • become motionless
  • enter silence
  • withdraw from the physical
  • enter no-mind absorption

But if karma interrupts, He returns to worldly activity.

19) Higher gunasthanak = shorter intervals between absorption

He gives a refined point:

The higher the gunasthanak, the shorter the interval between two states of absorption.

Meaning:

  • as the soul rises spiritually, the gap between deep inner absorption decreases
  • the Enlightened One returns inward more and more quickly
  • the Self becomes the natural centre

20) No attachment to objects, persons, situations, or passions

Because of constant awareness, the Enlightened One does not get attached to:

1. objects

2. persons

3. situations

4. passions

Even if likes or dislikes arise subtly, He does not identify with them.

He sees them as passing modifications, not as “I”.

21) Pratit: conviction even during sleep

The third word is pratit — firm conviction.

The speaker asks: what about sleep?

An embodied soul needs sleep. During sleep:

  • there is no active thought
  • no deliberate awareness
  • no no-mind meditative experience
  • no contemplation
  • no “I am the soul” thinking

So what remains?

Pratit — firm conviction.

The conviction “I am the pure Soul” never disappears.

22) The three states clarified

He gives a technical clarification:

Anubhav

State beyond thought No-mind Nirvikalp Experience

Laksh

State with thought Awareness during activity

Sleep

State without thought Not experience, not awareness, but conviction remains

He calls sleep sushupti.

23) Aditya example: name-identification remains even in sleep

He gives a simple example:

Suppose Aditya is sleeping. Someone calls:

“Aditya!”

He wakes up.

Why?

Was he chanting “I am Aditya, I am Aditya” in his sleep? No.

Still, the identification with the name is deeply rooted, so he responds.

Someone else hearing “Aditya” will not wake up because they do not identify with that name.

Similarly, for the Enlightened One, the conviction “I am the pure Soul” is so deeply rooted that it does not disappear even in sleep.

24) Mosquito example: something remains alert even in sleep

He gives another example:

A mosquito bites you while you are sleeping. You react.

You were asleep, but something in you responded.

He uses this to explain:

An Enlightened Soul in sleep is also alert — not awake, but aware in the sense of deep-rooted conviction.

He clarifies:

When we say an Enlightened Being never sleeps, it does not mean He lies awake all night with closed eyes.

It means the conviction of the Self never disappears.

25) Experience, awareness, conviction — at least one is always present

For an Enlightened Soul:

  • in retirement: experience
  • in activity: awareness
  • in sleep: conviction

At least one of the three remains.

This is why He is constantly tuned to the Self.

26) Flight analogy: even asleep, the Enlightened One progresses

He gives a flight example:

You board a night flight, fall asleep while the plane is still on the runway, and still travel toward your destination.

Similarly:

Even in sleep, the Enlightened One is moving toward the goal.

Why?

Because the state of the soul is grounded in:

  • experience
  • awareness
  • conviction

He continues to shed karmas even while asleep.

27) “Vrutti vahey nijbhavma”: tendencies flow inward

This phrase describes the entire state.

Even when externally active, the Enlightened One’s inner tendencies flow toward the Self.

He gives a relatable example:

If someone has had a breakup but hopes for a patch-up, and then attends a wedding, where will the tendency go?

Not toward:

  • decoration
  • food
  • clothes
  • people

It will keep going toward one’s own inner issue.

Similarly, for the Enlightened One, the tendency keeps flowing toward the Self.

He says:

Remove “problem” from that example and write “one’s own nature.” That is vrutti vahey nijbhavma.

28) Raman Maharshi example

He then gives the example of Raman Maharshi.

As a young boy, Raman Maharshi saw death and had a deep inner questioning. He experienced something like an out-of-body experience or flash.

The speaker connects it with Jain terminology:

  • that flash would be called shuddh samkit
  • a momentary glimpse of Self

Then Raman Maharshi left home for Arunachalam with little concern for worldly arrangements.

His one practice became:

Who am I? Who am I? Who am I?

This was:

  • his meditation
  • his devotion
  • his austerity
  • his chant

He became absorbed deeper and deeper.

The speaker says that even though Raman Maharshi did not know English or Hindi, foreigners could sit in his aura and understand deeply.

The point:

  • one focused question, “Who am I?”, led to deep absorption
  • from flash to stable inward flow: experience, awareness, conviction

29) Why this verse is unparalleled

The speaker repeatedly praises this verse as extraordinary.

He says:

  • the state of the Enlightened One is explained in only three words
  • nothing can be added
  • nothing can be removed
  • one word removed and the state is no longer fully revealed

He says this verse alone can show Param Krupalu Dev’s unprecedented speech.

The three words are enough:

Anubhav. Laksh. Pratit.

30) Final meaning of parmarth samkit

Parmarth samkit is the highest phase of right belief.

It is a state where:

  • the Enlightened One has direct experience of the Self
  • when not in direct experience, He has constant awareness of the Self
  • when even awareness cannot be active, firm conviction remains
  • tendencies flow inward
  • every thought, feeling, and action propels Him toward the true nature
  • He remains detached from objects, persons, situations, and passions
  • He performs roles perfectly but without attachment
  • He longs to return to the no-mind state
  • He lives in the world but is inwardly rooted in the Self

The speaker says:

This is the state of the Enlightened One.

31) The highest phase of right faith before conduct begins

Verse 111 completes the journey of right belief.

The three phases are:

1. Vyavhar samkit

Faith in Sadguru and His teachings.

2. Shuddh samkit

Momentary direct experience of the Self.

3. Parmarth samkit

Deep, repeated, stable inward flow through experience, awareness, and conviction.

After this, verse 112 moves into charitra — conduct.

Verse 113 moves into kevaljnan — omniscience.

32) Practical takeaways for a seeker

1. Don’t stop at intellectual belief

Faith in Guru and scripture is essential, but the path must move toward experience.

2. A single glimpse can transform life

Even a momentary glimpse of the Self changes vision forever.

3. The goal is not worldly activity

The mantra:

This is not my goal. This is only my role. I am the pure Soul.

4. Do duties perfectly but dispassionately

The Enlightened One does not escape duties; He performs them without attachment.

5. Check what you do in free time

Ignorant soul: entertainment or useless thoughts. Enlightened soul: deeper Self-experience.

6. In activity, keep inner awareness

Even when working, talking, eating, or serving, the tendency should flow inward.

7. Conviction must become deep-rooted

The conviction “I am the pure Soul” should become as deep as your current identification with your name.

8. The path becomes constant

In parmarth samkit, spirituality is not limited to meditation time. It continues through:

  • silence
  • activity
  • duty
  • sleep

33) One-line summary

Parmarth samkit is the highest right faith, where the Enlightened One remains connected to the Self through direct experience in retirement, constant awareness in activity, and firm conviction even in sleep.
