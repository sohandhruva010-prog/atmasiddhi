Verse: 100 (Atmasiddhi Shastra) Key line: “Raag–Dwesh–Ajnan e mukhya karmani granth; thay nivrutti jehati…” Central teaching: The main knots that keep karmic bondage tight are attachment (raag), aversion (dwesh), and ignorance/delusion (ajnan). Withdrawing from these is the undisputed path of liberation.

1) Where we are in the Guru’s solution (big-picture context)

This satsang is continuing the Guru’s structured, step-by-step explanation of the “undisputed path of liberation”:

  • Verse 98: Introduced the foundation pair:
    • Karmabhaav = identification with karmic states (impure modification) → bondage-direction
    • Mokshbhaav = abiding in Self (pure modification) → liberation-direction
  • Verse 99: Gave a universal compass:
    • Whatever causes bondage = bondage-path
    • The state that destroys those causes = liberation-path
  • Verse 100 (this video): Tightens the focus further:
    • Three root knots are named explicitly: raag, dwesh, ajnan
    • Liberation is framed as nivrutti (withdrawal) from them

So the progression is very deliberate:

(98) state-definition → (99) cause-rule → (100) root-knots identified

2) The opening “refinement” principle (dirty cup & saffron tea)

Before entering the verse, the speaker makes an important preparatory point:

A) Liberation is in your nature, but it doesn’t “manifest” without refinement

He gives the example:

  • If a cup is dirty, you won’t pour saffron tea into it.
  • Similarly, if your thoughts are dirty / impure, the state of liberation won’t manifest.

Core message: Even though purity is your nature, you still need transformation, refinement, improvement for that nature to shine unobstructed.

This links to the path idea: liberation is not “imported from outside,” but revealed as obstructions and knots are removed.

3) A strong diagnosis: “dharma lifeless, sins full of life”

This section is blunt (and meant to provoke honest self-check):

A) Many religious rituals become “kriyajada” (mechanical, lifeless)

He uses the idea:

  • Kriyajada = ritual done like a machine; no inner life (jada = lifeless)

He urges: check your own:

  • Atmasiddhi reading
  • Kshamapana
  • Dev Vandan
  • Pratikraman Do you do it as a living inner process—or as “blabbering” without awareness?

B) But sinful/worldly indulgence is done with full excitement

He points out:

  • Before the indulgence: excitement
  • During: enjoyment
  • After: discussion (replaying it, savoring it)

His question: Why is life and attention poured into bondage-causes, but lifelessness poured into dharma?

C) What brings “life” into dharma?

He says:

  • Your mumukshuta (yearning for liberation) matters
  • But even that yearning is strengthened by Sadguru’s grace and association

He even frames the enthusiasm at “Verse 100” as not merely personal effort:

  • “This is His aura… we are in His electromagnetic field.”

Whether you take that literally or devotionally, the functional point is: True receptivity and living engagement is a grace-enabled state, not a casual habit.

4) Three levels of listening: Shravan Kriya → Shravan Kala → Shravan Yog

This is a major “how to listen” teaching (very useful for all future verses).

1) Shravan Kriya — the activity of listening

  • Ears hear words.
  • Everyone present is doing this.

2) Shravan Kala — the art of listening

  • Listening + using intellect
  • Something changes in your understanding (an addition/correction in your “equations”)

3) Shravan Yog — listening while connected to the soul

This is the highest level he emphasizes:

  • The speaker becomes like a mirror
  • You begin seeing yourself in the teaching:
    • your impure states
    • your pure direction
    • your stuck places
  • It becomes introspection/inner practice during listening itself

Mirror vs binoculars (D-D teaching)

He warns: don’t make listening “binoculars” (to look at others’ faults). Make it darpan (mirror).

His “D-D” cue:
  • Durbin (binoculars) vs Darpan (mirror)

5) Verse 100 — Word-by-word unpacking (key Gujarati terms)

He explicitly breaks the verse into parts:

A) Raag, Dwesh, Ajnan

  • Raag = attachment, liking, clinging, pull
  • Dwesh = aversion, hatred, rejection, push
  • Ajnan = ignorance (here: deluded / misleading knowledge)

B) Mukhya karmani granth

This is an important correction:

  • Many think granth = book/scripture
  • Here granth means knot (gaanth / knot)

So:

Raag–Dwesh–Ajnan are the main knots of karma.

C) Thay nivrutti jehati

  • Nivrutti = withdrawing / receding / turning away
  • Meaning: the method is not “decorate bondage,” but retreat from these three

D) “That is the path of liberation”

He says he won’t over-explain that last part:

  • You asked what path is → withdrawing from these is the path

6) Why are these called “main knots”? (The knot analogy is central)

He gives multiple knot analogies because “knot” is the lived experience of being stuck:

A) Thread / sewing example

  • Thread runs smoothly until there’s a knot
  • Once knotted, you can’t proceed unless you untie or cut

B) Hair-combing example

  • Hair combs easily when knot-free
  • Knots make it painful, slow, stuck

C) Relationship example

  • Friendships are smooth until a misunderstanding “knots” the relationship
  • Communication loosens/unties it; then smoothness returns

Spiritual translation: Progress could be smooth, but these knots stop movement forward.

So the Guru is pointing:

You feel stuck not because path doesn’t exist— you feel stuck because knots aren’t being addressed.

7) The three knots and the whole bondage cycle (ignorance → “I” → “my” → likes/dislikes → bondage)

This is one of the most important causal chains in the entire discourse.

Step 1: Ajnan (ignorance)

He makes a critical clarification:

Ajnan does NOT mean “no knowledge”

  • Soul cannot be without knowingness (jnan is an attribute of soul)
  • “No knowledge” would mean inanimate (jada), which contradicts soul-nature

Ajnan here means: wrong / misleading / deluded knowledge

He distinguishes two kinds of “not-knowing”:

  • due to knowledge-obscuring karma (less knowledge)
  • due to delusion (misleading knowledge)

Here, the emphasis is:

You can be highly intelligent and still deluded.

Because IQ ≠ Self-awareness. You can have sharp intellect and still identify wrongly.

Step 2: Ignorance creates I-ness in the non-self

He says:

  • “I” wrongly placed in body/name/personality = ignorance
  • This is the root error: I = body

Example he gives in essence:

  • “I and the body” (discriminative) vs “I am the body” (deluded)

Step 3: From “I = body” arises my-ness

Once “I” is placed wrongly, “my” spreads automatically:

  • my sister
  • my car
  • my clothes
  • my status
  • my image

Step 4: From my-ness arises raag & dwesh

Now preferences intensify:

  • I like this (raag)
  • I dislike that (dwesh)

Step 5: Raag/Dwesh produce desires → expectations → disappointments (+ uncertainty, fear)

He calls each of the three “potent poison” because they carry baggage:

  • desires
  • expectations
  • disappointments and also uncertainty + fear

Key quote-idea:

Fulfillment of expectations is in others’ hands; staying without expectations is in your hands.

This line is a direct practical medicine:

  • Don’t gamble your peace on something outside your control.

Step 6: This keeps the wheel of transmigration moving

Ignorance is singled out as the root:

  • Not even attachment-aversion are first
  • Ignorance is first Because it generates the whole chain.

8) The “stuck despite doing everything” warning

This is a strong practical critique:

You might:

  • keep longer silence (maun)
  • do more self-study
  • do more bhakti/devotional singing
  • do more rituals

But if you refuse to touch the actual knots:

  • attachment
  • aversion
  • ignorance

Then you remain stuck.

He also points out knots can be:

  • conscious
  • subconscious
  • unconscious (including deep conditionings, past-life impressions)

So he says: even conscious knots need communication/forgiveness to loosen—imagine the deeper layers.

9) What does real progress look like? (Measurable inner direction)

He gives a very clear “progress indicator”:

If you are progressing on the path of liberation, you should see:

  • less ignorance
  • less attachment
  • less aversion

Not necessarily “zero immediately,” but decreasing.

He even offers a compassionate step:

  • Don’t obsess about removing instantly
  • Loosen the knot
    • communicate if needed
    • forgive if needed
    • don’t let it harden into a tight knot

10) Conditioning examples: why two people react opposite to the same thing

He gives vivid illustrations to show how impressions/conditioning drive likes-dislikes:

A) Choosing a person / attraction example

  • Same person seen by family and by the attracted individual
  • Different inner impressions produce different perception

B) Weather example (cloudy/rainy)

  • One person feels “depressing”
  • Another feels “wonderful weather,” thinks of masala tea, fragrance of mud Different samskar/impressions → different labeling of the same situation.

Purpose: to prove that likes/dislikes are often not “truth,” but knot-conditioning.

11) “Nirgranth” — the liberation direction in one word

This is a beautiful doctrinal bridge:

“Removing knots = becoming nirgranth”

  • Nirgranth literally: without knots

He emphasizes:

  • Not “knotless dress”
  • Knotless mind:
    • conscious/subconscious/unconscious knots dissolving

And he links this to:

  • Paramguru Nirgranth Sarvajna Dev
    • Nirgranth (knotless)
    • Sarvajna (omniscient)

So the path is:

loosen knots → remove knots → nirgranth → full purity → omniscience → liberation

He hints the next verse will speak of Sahaj Atmaswaroop (natural true nature).

12) The standard of evaluation: NOT activity, but knot-reduction

This is repeated strongly near the end:

“I’m not going to see your activity. I will see your likes, dislikes, and ignorance.”

Meaning:

  • Silence is not automatically spiritual if it’s full of expectation and resentment.
  • Ritual is not automatically liberating if inner knots keep tightening.

He gives a practical example:

  • If you take silence but expect others to understand your gestures → expectation rises → disappointment → knot tightens.

So the right move could be:

  • either don’t expect
  • or don’t take silence—speak clearly But don’t keep the knot and pretend it’s “practice.”

14) One-page revision summary

Verse 100 in essence:

  • Main karmic knots: raag, dwesh, ajnan
  • Liberation-path: nivrutti (withdrawal) from these

Key clarifications:

  • Granth = knot, not book
  • Ajnan = deluded/misleading knowledge, not “no knowledge”
  • Ignorance produces:
    • I-ness in non-self
    • then my-ness
    • then likes/dislikes
    • then desires → expectations → disappointment + fear
    • transmigration continues

Progress marker:

  • Not “more practices,” but less ignorance + less attachment + less aversion

Shortcut word:

  • Nirgranth = knotless (mind), direction to Sarvajna and liberation
