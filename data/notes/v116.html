Video 131 Notes: Verse 116 — “You Are of the Nature of Liberation”

Based on the transcript you shared.

1) Context: From the essence of religion to the nature of the Self

The previous verse, verse 115, explained the essence of religion:

When false identification with the body ceases, you no longer remain the doer of karmas or the endurer of their fruits.

That is dharmano marma — the essence of dharma.

Now, in verse 116, Param Krupalu Dev moves deeper. If dharma means retreating from impure feelings and becoming established in the true nature of the Self, then the next question is:

What is that true nature of the Self?

Until now, the discourse has spoken about moksh as a state — the purest state of the soul. Now the Guru explains that moksh is not something foreign or external. It is the manifestation of what the soul already is in its eternal nature.

So verse 116 begins the direct description of the soul’s inherent nature.

2) Verse 116

E j dharmathi moksh chhe, tu chho moksh swaroop;
Anant darshan jnan tu, avyabadh swaroop.

Simple meaning

Only that religion described in the previous verse leads to liberation. As the pure Self, you are liberation itself. You are infinite perception and infinite knowledge, and you are of unobstructed nature.

3) Word-by-word meaning

E j dharmathi moksh chhe

Only through that dharma is liberation attained.

“That dharma” refers to the previous verse: the cessation of false identification and the establishment in the knowing-witnessing mode.

Tu chho moksh swaroop

You are of the nature of liberation.

Not merely that you can attain liberation someday, but that your true nature is already unbound, free, and pure.

Anant darshan jnan tu

You are infinite perception and infinite knowledge.

The true nature of the soul has infinite capacity to perceive and know.

Avyabadh swaroop

You are of unobstructed nature.

This includes unobstructed bliss and unobstructed energy.

4) The link with verse 115

Verse 115 said:

Give up false identification, remain in the knower-witness mode, and doership and endurership dissolve.

Verse 116 says:

Only that dharma leads to liberation.

So the dharma of verse 115 is not sectarian religion. It is not merely a ritual, practice, label, or inherited tradition.

It is the inner state where:

1. false identification is destroyed,

2. awareness turns toward the true Self,

3. impure feelings are retreated from,

4. the soul abides in itself.

If asked briefly, “What is dharma?” the speaker says the shortest answer is:

Abidance in the Self.

5) Dharma and moksh are both within

A major point in this discourse is that dharma is not outside and moksh is not outside.

Moksh is not an external award. It is not a prize given by someone. It is not obtained from outside.

Moksh is the purest state of the soul.

Therefore, dharma must also awaken within.

The speaker says:

Dharma has to be awakened within because moksh is a state within.

Outer activities may support dharma, but they are not dharma in the deepest sense unless something inward happens.

For example, one may say:

  • I did bhakti.
  • I did pranayama.
  • I did meditation.
  • I did japa.
  • I did rituals.
  • I studied scriptures.

These may be good and auspicious, but the question is:

Did something awaken within?

If the Self is absent from the practice, it is not pure dharma. At best, it is an attempt toward dharma.

6) Dharma as inner manifestation

The speaker gives a beautiful distinction:

Dharma

The true nature is manifesting.

It is the process.

Moksh

The true nature has fully manifested.

It is the culmination.

So:

Manifesting = dharma. Manifested = moksh.

Dharma is the gradual awakening and expression of the true nature. Moksh is the complete manifestation of that same nature.

This is why the verse says:

E j dharmathi moksh chhe.

Only that dharma, which is connected to the Self, leads to liberation.

7) The universality of this teaching

The speaker stresses that Param Krupalu Dev is not saying:

  • only Jain dharma leads to liberation,
  • only one mantra leads to liberation,
  • only one ritual leads to liberation,
  • only one tradition leads to liberation.

Rather, the message is universal.

Whether one chants the Navkar Mantra or the Gayatri Mantra, the real question is:

Are you moving from doer-receiver mode to knowing-witnessing mode?

If yes, that is dharma.

If the inner state is shifting toward the Self, then the path is genuine.

This makes the teaching deeply universal.

8) The fourfold explanation of dharma

The speaker breaks dharma into four connected movements:

1. Annihilation of false belief False identification with body and non-self begins to dissolve.

2. Awakening toward the true nature The soul begins to recognise its real identity.

3. Retreating from impure feelings Passions, attachments, and aversions begin to weaken.

4. Abiding in the Self The soul becomes established in its pure nature.

These are not four separate paths. They are four aspects of the same inward movement.

In the shortest form:

Dharma is abidance in the Self.

9) Connection with Tattvartha Sutra

The discourse connects this verse with the famous first aphorism of the Tattvartha Sutra:

Samyak darshan-jnan-charitrani mokshmarg.

Meaning:

Right faith, right knowledge, and right conduct together are the path of liberation.

The speaker explains that verse 116 expresses the same truth without using those exact technical words.

Here:

  • belief in the true Self = right faith,
  • understanding the true Self = right knowledge,
  • abiding in the true Self = right conduct.

Together, these become the path of liberation.

10) Every verse is complete, yet connected

The speaker makes an important observation about Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra.

Each verse is complete in itself, yet every verse is also part of a continuous flow.

Verse 116 has roots in verse 115, and verse 117 will have roots in verse 116.

This is described as a flow, not separate drops. Every verse is like a drop, but not a disconnected drop. It belongs to the stream of Param Krupalu Dev’s direct experience and clarity.

The teaching is both:

  • interconnected, and
  • complete in every verse.

11) “Tu” — the affectionate address of the Guru

The word tu means “you.”

The speaker spends time on this because it shows the intimacy between the Guru and the disciple.

Param Krupalu Dev could have said:

The soul is of the nature of liberation.

That would be philosophically correct, but impersonal.

Instead, He says:

You are of the nature of liberation.

This makes the teaching direct, personal, intimate, and loving.

The speaker says:

First connection, then correction.

The Guru is not giving dry philosophy. He is speaking directly to the disciple’s heart.

12) “Tu chho moksh swaroop” — You are of liberation nature

This is the first major description of the soul’s true nature.

Moksh swaroop means:

  • you are always free,
  • you are always unbound,
  • you are devoid of all afflictions in your true nature,
  • you were free,
  • you are free,
  • you will remain free.

This does not mean the present worldly state is already liberated in manifestation. It means the soul’s eternal nature is liberation itself.

Liberation is not something added from outside.

It is the manifestation of what is already latent.

13) The apparent contradiction: If I am already free, why attain liberation?

The speaker raises a subtle question.

The first line says:

Only that dharma leads to liberation.

This sounds like liberation is something to be attained.

But immediately after, the verse says:

You are of the nature of liberation.

This sounds like liberation is already your nature.

So which is true?

Both.

The distinction is:

Eternal nature

The soul is always pure, free, and of the nature of liberation.

Manifested state

That nature is not presently manifest due to ignorance, delusion, and karmic covering.

So liberation as nature is always true.

Liberation as state must be manifested.

This is the key distinction.

14) The millionaire rickshaw driver analogy

This is the central analogy of the discourse.

A millionaire has a young son. Before dying, the father sells his property and puts the wealth into a fixed deposit for twenty years. The child grows up unaware of this wealth. With no one to support him, he becomes a rickshaw driver.

Now the question arises:

While he is driving the rickshaw, is he a millionaire or not?

In one sense, yes. The wealth belongs to him.

In another sense, no. He is not living like a millionaire because he does not know, believe, or possess the wealth.

This explains the soul’s condition.

The soul is of liberation nature, but it is living like a beggar due to ignorance.

15) Being is not enough

The rickshaw driver is a millionaire, but because he does not know it, he lives in poverty.

Likewise, the soul is pure, free, and infinite in nature, but because it does not know this, it suffers.

So the first lesson is:

Being is not enough. Knowing is needed.

The soul must know its true nature.

16) Knowing is not enough

After twenty years, the bank publishes a notice. The rickshaw driver reads it. It has his name and his father’s name. For a moment, he feels excited.

But then he thinks:

This cannot be me. I am only a poor rickshaw driver.

He knows about the possibility, but he does not believe it applies to him.

Similarly, seekers read scriptures saying:

  • you are pure,
  • you are divine,
  • you are of the nature of liberation,
  • you are like God in nature.

But they think:

This is about great souls. This does not apply to me.

So the second lesson is:

Knowing is not enough. Believing is needed.

This is where the role of the Sadguru becomes crucial.

17) Believing comes through the Sadguru

The rickshaw driver meets his father’s friend, who knew the truth. The friend convinces him:

This notice is about you. This wealth is yours.

In the analogy, the father’s friend represents the Sadguru.

The Sadguru convinces the seeker:

What the scriptures say about the pure Self is about you.

The Sadguru turns scriptural information into living faith.

So the progression becomes:

1. being,

2. knowing,

3. believing.

18) Believing is still not enough

The rickshaw driver becomes happy and confident. But if someone asks for a ride and he says, “No, I am a millionaire,” people will laugh.

Why?

Because he has not yet completed the formalities. He does not yet have custody of the money.

Similarly, a seeker may gain knowledge and faith, but until that becomes conduct and experience, he cannot fully enjoy the wealth of the Self.

So the fourth step is:

Conduct.

In spiritual language:

  • right knowledge,
  • right faith,
  • right conduct.

When these unite, liberation manifests.

19) The full analogy mapped spiritually

Rickshaw driver analogy Spiritual meaning

The son is actually a millionaire The soul is actually of liberation nature

He does not know about the wealth The soul is ignorant of its true nature

He reads the notice The seeker encounters scripture

He doubts it applies to him The seeker does not truly believe the scripture

Father’s friend confirms the truth Sadguru gives conviction

He completes the formalities The seeker practises right conduct

He gains custody of wealth Liberation manifests

He enjoys the wealth The soul enjoys inner bliss

The conclusion:

Being is not enough. Knowing is needed. Knowing is not enough. Believing is needed. Believing is not enough. Conduct is needed. Through conduct, the wealth is actually enjoyed.

20) Nature of liberation versus state of liberation

The analogy clarifies the distinction:

Nature of liberation

The soul is inherently free, like the rickshaw driver is inherently the rightful owner of the wealth.

State of liberation

The soul actually manifests and enjoys freedom, like the rickshaw driver finally receives and uses the wealth.

So when the Guru says:

You are moksh swaroop,

He is describing the soul’s eternal nature.

When He says:

By that dharma you attain moksh,

He is describing the manifestation of that nature as a realised state.

21) “Anant darshan jnan tu” — You are infinite perception and knowledge

The second line begins with two more qualities of the Self:

  • anant darshan — infinite perception,
  • anant jnan — infinite knowledge.

This does not mean the present ignorant state already functions with infinite knowledge.

At present, knowledge is obstructed by jnanavarniya karma, knowledge-obscuring karma. That is why one forgets even yesterday’s events.

But the speaker clarifies:

I am not talking about your present state. I am talking about your inherent capacity.

The soul’s true nature has infinite knowledge and perception, though these are not presently manifested.

22) “Avyabadh swaroop” — unobstructed nature

Avyabadh means unobstructed.

The speaker explains that this includes two more infinite attributes:

  • unobstructed bliss,
  • unobstructed energy.

So the verse includes the four infinite attributes of the soul.

23) The Anant Chatushtay — four infinite attributes

The second line of verse 116 contains the famous anant chatushtay, the four infinite attributes of the soul.

1. Infinite knowledge — anant jnan

The soul has the capacity to know all.

2. Infinite perception — anant darshan

The soul has the capacity to perceive all.

3. Infinite bliss — anant sukh

The soul’s nature is limitless bliss.

4. Infinite energy — anant virya

The soul’s nature is infinite power or energy.

These four are fully manifested in omniscience.

The speaker says Param Krupalu Dev has woven the anant chatushtay into the second line of verse 116.

24) Qualities versus virtues

The speaker makes a subtle distinction.

When these attributes are inherent but not manifested, they may be called qualities of the soul.

When they manifest, they are virtues of an Omniscient One.

So:

  • as inherent nature, they belong to every soul,
  • as manifested virtues, they belong to the Omniscient One.

This means the glory of the Omniscient One is not foreign to us. It is the full manifestation of what is latent in every soul.

25) The tragedy of the present state

After describing the soul’s infinite nature, the speaker contrasts it with our present condition.

The soul is infinite bliss, yet we seek happiness outside.

The soul is infinite energy, yet we feel tired.

The soul is infinite knowledge, yet we forget even basic things.

The soul is infinite perception, yet its awareness is limited.

This contrast is meant to awaken spiritual urgency.

The Guru is saying:

Look at your real nature. See what you have reduced yourself to through ignorance.

26) The Guru lifting the veil

The discourse describes the Guru as lifting the veil of ignorance covering consciousness.

Like lifting a ghunghat, the Guru gradually reveals the disciple’s true identity.

He first shows the disciple the impure state, then reveals the pure Self.

The Guru’s message is:

It is time to reclaim your identity.

This is a deeply personal instruction. The seeker must stop identifying with the rickshaw-driver state and recognise the millionaire nature.

27) Spiritual pursuit as reclaiming identity

The path is not about becoming something totally new.

It is about reclaiming what was always true.

The seeker does not manufacture purity. The seeker removes false identification and allows the inherent nature to manifest.

This is why the verse is so powerful:

You are of liberation nature.

The Guru is not flattering the disciple. He is revealing the truth.

28) Practical implication: Is the Self present in your practice?

The speaker gives a direct test.

If you say, “I was chanting,” ask:

Where was the Self in that chanting?

If you say, “I did japa,” ask:

Was I connected to the Self?

If you say, “I performed a ritual,” ask:

Did it awaken abidance in the Self?

If the Self is absent, the practice may be auspicious, but it is not yet pure dharma.

Dharma must have connection with the Self.

29) The path of liberation in one sentence

The speaker summarises:

To develop faith in such a nature of the Self, to realise its importance, and to become immersed and engrossed in it, is the path of liberation.

So the path is:

1. know the true nature,

2. believe in the true nature,

3. value the true nature,

4. become absorbed in the true nature,

5. manifest the true nature.

That manifestation is moksh.

30) Five qualities explained in verse 116

The speaker concludes that verse 116 explains five qualities of the soul:

1. You are of the nature of liberation.

2. Infinite perception is your nature.

3. Infinite knowledge is your nature.

4. Infinite bliss is your nature.

5. Infinite energy is your nature.

The last two are contained in avyabadh swaroop, unobstructed nature.

Verse 117 will continue the same subject and explain five more qualities of the soul.

31) Key terms

Dharma

The inner awakening and abidance in the Self; the manifestation-in-process of the true nature.

Moksh

The full manifestation of the soul’s true nature; liberation.

Moksh swaroop

The soul’s inherent nature as always free and unbound.

Dehadhyas

False identification with the body.

Jnata-drashta

Knower-seer; witness mode.

Anant darshan

Infinite perception.

Anant jnan

Infinite knowledge.

Avyabadh swaroop

Unobstructed nature.

Anant sukh

Infinite bliss.

Anant virya

Infinite energy.

Anant chatushtay

The four infinite attributes: infinite knowledge, infinite perception, infinite bliss, and infinite energy.

32) Main takeaways

Verse 116 says that only the dharma described in verse 115 — the cessation of false identification and abidance in the Self — leads to liberation. Liberation is not outside; it is the manifestation of the soul’s inherent nature. The Guru lovingly tells the disciple, “You are of liberation nature.” This does not mean the present state is already liberated; it means the eternal nature is free, but must be known, believed, practised, and manifested.

The millionaire rickshaw driver analogy shows that merely being of that nature is not enough. One must know it, believe it through the Sadguru’s conviction, practise accordingly, and finally gain the living experience of that wealth. Verse 116 then reveals the soul’s infinite nature: infinite knowledge, infinite perception, infinite bliss, and infinite energy. These are latent in every soul and fully manifest in omniscience. The path of liberation is to understand, trust, value, and become absorbed in this true nature.
