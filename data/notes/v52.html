• He opens by linking back to the disciple’s Verse 45 doubt: “Soul can’t be seen by eyes → therefore it doesn’t exist.”
  • He says Verse 51 already corrected this:
    • Soul cannot be perceived by eyes because it is the seer itself.
    • But “not seen” does not mean “can never be grasped.”
    • It can be grasped through abadhya anubhav = the constant experience of consciousness.
  • He frames it as:
    • Negative: not seen by eyes
    • Positive: grasped through constant knowingness
  • Now he says: “this is not the total solution” — and he moves into the next step: strengthening conviction through the attribute of knowledge.

1:09–3:34 — Verse 52 starts: each sense has its own limited “department”

He begins teaching the verse phrase-by-phrase:

Key words

  • pratyek = each / every
  • indriya = sense / sense-organ
  • nij nij = its own / respective
  • vishay = subject-matter / domain
  • bhan = knowledge / knowing

Core idea (he drills it very clearly)

  • Each sense knows only its own field:
    • Eyes → see
    • Ears → hear
    • Nose → smell
    • Tongue → taste
    • Skin → touch
  • He emphasizes the limitation:
    • Even if something is very near, nose still can’t see.
    • Each indriya is “restricted” to its own faculty.

Then comes the punch:

  • The soul has knowledge of all five.
  • He says: “I’ll explain with an example.”

3:34–5:06 — The big logical pressure point: if senses are limited, who knows the whole?

He sets up the problem the disciple would raise:

  • If the “soul” were just another name for a sense, then:
    • it would also have only one limited channel (like smell-only, sight-only, etc.)
  • But our experience is the opposite:
    • We know the whole object, using the combined inputs.
  • So he says: There must be one “HOD / coordinator.”

That’s the doorway into his main example.

5:06–7:58 — Example 1: Mahila Gruha Udyog as the “five senses” model

He uses Nemiji’s Mahila Gruha Udyog like a “five-department company”:

  • One person handles production
  • One handles packing/design
  • One handles recipes
  • One handles marketing
  • One handles accounts
  • Workers in each area typically know their own part.

Then he pushes the key contrast:

  • The person doing production may not know sales details.
  • The person doing packaging may not know production realities.
  • But someone knows the whole chain: from where it’s made → to where it’s eaten → even big orders (Taj catering) + inspection + future plan.

So he asks:

  • If one entity knows everyone’s department, that entity is different from each department worker.
Translation into Verse 52’s point:
  • Each sense is limited to its own “department.”
  • But there is an entity that has knowledge of all five departments.
  • Therefore, that entity must be separate from the senses.
  • That entity is what the Guru calls atma / soul.

He explicitly says:

“Person here means entity.”

8:04–9:23 — Extra strengthening: senses can’t even function without the “coordinator”

He adds another layer beyond just “knowing”:

  • No sense functions independently of the soul.
    • Like: if Nemiji says “stop,” operations stop.
    • Similarly: if the soul says “I don’t want to see,” seeing won’t happen.
  • He also adds memory:
    • Eyes are no longer seeing the sight, yet recollection remains.
    • So eyes cannot be the “soul.”

This builds toward his recurring death/brain point.

9:57–12:30 — Brain + dead body: “everything is there, yet it stops”

He anticipates the “brain explanation” objection:

  • Someone may say: “brain receives and orders.”
  • He responds (in his style):
    • A dead person still has a skull, still has a brain…
    • yet it doesn’t function.
  • He mentions breaking skull bones at cremation, etc. (very vivid, intentionally grounding it).
  • He recalls an old conversation in Udaipur where the response was basically: “that’s just how it is.”

Then he introduces his “slow persuasion” approach:

  • He says (carefully):
    • “I have a theory: the soul left the body.”
    • He calls it a theory because he’s moving step-by-step with the audience.
    • Medical science may not accept an invisible non-tangible entity, but spirituality explains death in this way:
      • Soul leaves → senses/heart/brain stop
  • He says: this will be explained further in the next verse too.

12:37–16:20 — Guru’s tone: the disciple isn’t “wrong,” he is “stuck”

This is an important “meaning behind it” section:

  • The Guru won’t insult the disciple.
  • The disciple is right about one thing:
    • For worldly/material knowing, senses are primary tools.
  • The problem starts when the disciple becomes stuck at:
    • “Therefore senses must also be able to know soul/karma/etc.”
  • He clarifies:
    • Karma is still (in some ways) “tangible” compared to soul,
    • but soul is not even that.

Then he says what Verse 52 adds:

  • Senses are instruments.
  • Soul holds the reins of all senses.
  • Soul orchestrates the body and harmonises the inner stream of thoughts.
  • Yet:
    • Soul is not even perceived
    • and not praised
  • He makes it playful with the “PP” joke: perceived + praised.

16:48–21:48 — Makeup / kohl humor → deeper message: the body is inert, beauty is “because of me”

He turns the idea into a striking reflection:

  • People decorate eyes (eyeliner/kohl).
  • But really, if you think deeply:
    • you’re adorning a body that is inert by itself.
  • The “shine” of the living body is because of the soul’s presence.

He recites a line he says he chanted for hours at 17–18:

  • “Prati dehe je shobhanikta chhe, te maari…” The beauty in every body is because of me (meaning: the soul), not makeup.

Then he reinforces with the death contrast:

  • Once soul leaves:
    • body becomes hard, blackens, smells
    • cremation becomes urgent
  • So the value/meaning of body is borrowed from the soul.

He links it to Devvandan language:

  • “Atmathi sau heen” everything is secondary/trivial compared to the soul.

He also makes the “primacy” point sharply:

  • Today: primacy of body
  • What the disciple will reach: primacy of soul
  • Soul gives meaning to objects and occurrences through knowledge-radiance (consciousness).

22:16–26:58 — Sense-by-sense list + the strict rule: one sense can do only one job

He repeats the verse’s foundation carefully:

  • Ears → hear sounds
  • Eyes → see forms
  • Nose → smell
  • Tongue → taste
  • Skin → touch sensations (he says 8 types exist but doesn’t go into detail)

Then he states a very “logic-class” rule:

  • One sense cannot do two functions.
  • Two senses cannot do one identical function.
  • One does one.
  • This is what “nij nij vishay” really means.

He even jokes about powerful eyesight:

  • No matter how strong your eyes are, they won’t start doing the ear’s job.

27:08–30:32 — Example 2: Pizza = one object, five channels… but recollection shows you are not the senses

He uses pizza as a clean multi-sense object:

  • Eyes catch shapes/colours (circle, red/white, etc.)
  • Nose catches smell
  • Touch catches warmth/crispness
  • Ears could catch sizzling (if it’s a sizzler)

Then he asks the key question:

  • When you recollect pizza, everything comes back together:
    • sight + smell + warmth + taste memory + the whole package
  • Therefore:
    • you are not just eyes
    • not just nose
    • not just touch
  • You are the integrator:
    • the one receiving all “reports”
    • the “HOD” where the messages arrive

He says:

  • “Now who is that me — I have to figure it out.”
  • For now he labels it as the “Supreme Soul / Pure Consciousness” directionally.

30:32–33:52 — The conclusion he wants you to land on

He states the logic cleanly:

  • If soul were identical with senses, soul would have limited knowing like each sense.
  • But it doesn’t.
  • Soul knows the whole—so it must be separate.
  • Soul is the:
    • coordinator
    • integrator
    • orchestrator
    • harmoniser
    • storehouse + reviver (memory)

Then he gives the meditation pivot:

  • The chief attribute of soul = knowingness
  • Meditation will repeatedly come back to:
    • pure knowingness
    • witness-like awareness
    • but ultimately it “boils down to” knowingness

He names the Jain term:

  • panchendriya jiva = a soul (consciousness) possessing a body with five sense organs.

33:52–36:52 — He turns it into practice: known vs knower, sankalp-vikalp vs bliss

He brings it straight into sadhana:

  • If you focus on the known (objects):
    • you get sankalp–vikalp
    • likes/dislikes
    • mental noise
  • If you focus on the knower:
    • you move into peace and stillness
    • he prefers calling it bliss (not “joy”)
      • “joy” feels extrovert/excited
      • “bliss” feels quiet, deep, inward
  • He tells you: reserve the word bliss for the bliss of the Self.

36:59–37:20 — Final punchline: “Senses know their own faculty” is (ultimately) a convenient formality

He ends with a deliberate twist:

  • Someone says: “senses know their own faculty.”
  • He says: Rubbish—senses are non-living.
    • They are body parts.
    • They are inert.
    • They function only because of my presence (meaning: the soul).
  • Then: “Recite the verse.”

This is his signature move:

  • He first speaks in practical language (“eyes know seeing”),
  • then ends by pulling you back to metaphysics:
    • the real knower is the soul; senses are instruments.

Quick “Verse 52” takeaway in one line

Each sense is limited to one domain, but the “knower” who holds all five domains together (and can recall them later) must be a separate entity—this coordinating knowingness is the soul.
