1) What is the Guru “doing” here?

He is not giving a scripture-based rebuttal. He’s making the disciple see the argument collapses by itself.

  • The disciple’s claim (from verse 60): “Soul is created with body; dies with body.”
  • Guru’s method: Ask who knows/witnesses the soul’s creation & destruction. Then show every possible “knower” option is impossible.

The speaker calls the disciple’s claim:

  • imaginary theory
  • unthinkable as an experience
  • logically impossible

2) “Link” summary: two possible options after Verse 62

Verse 62 asked: who experiences/knows the soul’s birth & death?

The link says there are only two candidates:

Option 1: Body is the knower

  • Eliminated already in verse 62:
    • body is jada (inanimate, no knowingness),
    • has form,
    • is visible (object), not knower.
  • So it cannot know soul’s creation/destruction.

Option 2: Soul is the knower

  • Verse 63 now destroys this option:
    • “Soul knows its own creation and destruction” is shown illogical/unthinkable.

So verse 63 = solution to option 2, meaning: elimination of it.

3) Verse 63 (core line) — word-by-word meaning

Gujarati line being explained:

“Jena anubhav vashya e utpann layanu jnan, te tethi juda vina thay na keme bhan.”

He explains it like a principle of knowing:

Key idea (principle)

If you want to know the creation and destruction of a substance, the knower must be different from that substance.

Breakdown

  • Jena anubhav vashya e “Upon whom this experience depends”
  • utpann layanu jnan “knowledge of origination (birth) and dissolution (death)” (here: of the soul)
  • te… tethi juda vina “that entity… without being separate from it”
  • thay na keme bhan “cannot possibly know / cannot have that knowledge”
Short meaning he gives:

“One who claims to know the birth and death of the soul must be different from the soul.”

4) Why “soul knows its own birth/death” is impossible (the ‘prior + survive’ test)

He repeats it in multiple ways so it sets in your intellect.

A) To know a birth, the knower must exist before it

  • If A knows B is born, then A must be present prior to B’s birth.
  • Apply it to soul:
    • To know “soul was born,” the knower must exist before the soul exists.
    • That sounds absurd because we’re trying to make soul itself the knower.

B) To know a death, the knower must remain after it

  • If A knows B died, A must survive B’s death, even briefly.
  • Apply it to soul:
    • If “I (the soul) died at 10:30,” to know that death, “I” must exist at 10:45.
    • But if the soul is destroyed at 10:30, who is the ‘I’ at 10:45?

This is why he calls the disciple’s claim unthinkable as an actual experience.

5) The A/B demonstration (to make it painfully clear)

He uses a simple logic form:

Rule

  • If A knows the creation of B → A must exist before B
  • If A knows the destruction of B → A must exist after B
  • Therefore: A must be separate from B

He then uses two volunteers (Nemiji & Maulik) to show:

  • When A and B are different people, the logic is easy:
    • A can be older and witness B’s birth
    • A can outlive B and witness B’s death

But if you set:

  • A = Soul
  • B = Soul

It becomes nonsense:

  • Soul must be before soul and after soul.

That’s the “weirdness” he wants the disciple to see.

6) Why he becomes dramatic with “A & B”

He explicitly says the drama is on purpose because the disciple claimed:

  • Body and soul are born together
  • Body and soul die together

Guru’s logic forces this:

  • To know soul’s destruction, some knower must survive soul’s destruction.
  • But:
    • Body can’t know anything (no knowingness),
    • Soul can’t survive its own destruction (that would contradict destruction).

So the disciple’s “born together/die together” claim collapses.

7) The “drop options one by one” conclusion (end of verse 63 reasoning)

Option 1: Body knows soul’s birth/death

  • Rejected: body is lifeless / no consciousness.
  • Body doesn’t even know itself, so it can’t know soul.

Option 2: Soul knows its own birth/death

  • Rejected: requires being prior to itself + surviving itself.
  • If it survives destruction, it’s not destructible → implies permanence.

So both options fail.

8) The tempting remaining “escape”: a third agency

He says: logically, the only remaining possibility would be:

  • a third conscious knower
  • separate from the soul
  • that can witness soul’s creation/destruction

But he immediately points out:

  • There is no evidence supplied for such an agency.
  • If someone says “God/Brahma told me”:
    • first prove such an agency exists (and that it truly knows this).

He connects this to the Third Fundamental Truth (soul is the doer):

  • Later he will argue there is no creator-God who prompts all actions,
  • because that would make “God” responsible for crimes (murders, etc.),
  • and people conveniently claim “God’s will” only when it suits them.

So:

  • No body
  • No soul knowing its own destruction
  • No proven third agency → the claim “soul is created/destroyed” is unsupported.
