The “6-verse course” (108–113) is re-established again

The speaker reminds us:

  • Verses 108 to 113 = the entire path “from preparation to liberation” in six stanzas.
  • In scriptures, the same journey is explained using 14 gunasthanak (stages of spiritual development), but Param Krupalu Dev compresses it.

Clean sequence (phases, not “types”)

He carefully says “phase” so you don’t confuse it with types of samkit (like upsham/kshayopsham/kshayik).

108 = preparation 109 = 1st phase of samkit (right belief) 110 = 2nd phase 111 = 3rd phase 112 = right conduct (complete/ideal conduct is “yathakhyat charitra”) 113 = kevaljnan (omniscience)

2) What’s realistic for us: “achievable targets” vs far-fetched

He makes a very practical point:

  • 113 (omniscience) = too far-fetched for where most of us are.
  • 112 (perfect conduct) = also too far-fetched.
  • 111 (third right belief) = possible, but very difficult (he says: “possible because Guru has done it,” but needs tremendous effort).
  • 108, 109, 110 = achievable targets (he repeats: targets, not “final goals”).

Why he’s spending whole sessions

He says he will take:

  • whole session on 108
  • whole session on 109
  • whole session on 110 because these are the reachable milestones where we need maximum clarity.

3) Recap of 108 and 109 (quick but important)

108 = purity first (plate analogy)

A memorable image:

  • Before serving food, the plate must be clean.
  • Similarly:
    • The soul must be purified before Self-realisation becomes actual/manifest.

So 108 is: cleaning the “plate” (inner purification / readiness).

109 = Sadguru association → Sadguru instruction → faith (vyavahar samkit)

He links the verse logic:

  • First: Sadguru yog = association
  • Then: Sadguru bodh = instruction/teaching from the Sadguru (given through “love and lectures”)
  • Result: unwavering faith in the Guru + the Guru’s teachings

Why it’s called vyavahar samkit

He defines this clearly:

  • It’s samkit because it is right faith.
  • It’s vyavahar because it is still dependent on someone/something external:
    • Sadguru, scriptures, Bhagwan, etc.
  • If it were purely based on oneself (direct internal certainty), that would be nishchay.

4) Today’s focus: Verse 110 (Shuddh Samkit / experiential faith)

The speaker says: now the aspirant can be called:

  • disciple (shishya)
  • devotee (bhakt) because he has taken refuge in the Guru.

What changes from 109 → 110?

  • In 109: you meet the Sadguru and receive teachings → faith arises.
  • In 110: you follow the Sadguru’s guidance:

“Varte Sadguru laksh” = following the Sadguru’s guidance

This “following” produces an inner transformation that leads to Self-experience (shuddh samkit).

5) A major teaching: dharmafal vs dharmaprem (fruits vs love)

This is one of the most important “practical-life” sections in the transcript.

The key shift he says Sadguru brings

Before Sadguru:

  • religious practices are done for dharmafal (the fruits/results of religion)

With Sadguru’s guidance:

  • the same practices are done for dharmaprem (love/reverence for धम )

He says: you may continue rituals if they’re true—you don’t need to stop—but the inner orientation changes completely.

His childhood story (how “fruit-motivation” works, and why it felt incomplete)

He describes religious classes (pathshala) as a child:

What he disliked:

1. memorisation-focused (just memorise verses)

2. the whole emphasis was on dharmafal:

  • do X → get celestial results
  • reduce hellish suffering
  • collect punya “points”

The ayambil-counting example

He describes being told:

  • washing your plate and drinking that water = punya of an ayambil So as a child:
  • he began counting (even writing diary-like tallies: “four ayambils on Monday”)

But he felt something was missing:

  • “Where is the love for God?”
  • “Where is liberation?”
  • He says he didn’t even hear about liberation then.

He also acknowledges:

This “fruit motivation” is how religion often starts:

  • like giving a child a toy/chocolate as motivation
  • worldly motivation or “other-worldly” motivation—still motivation.

But: Sadguru transforms it into love and liberation-oriented sincerity.

6) Three-stage progression of motivation (very important)

He explicitly outlines three stages:

Stage 1: Dharma starts with “fal” (fruits)

  • practices done for reward/punishment logic
  • “What will I get if I do this?”

Stage 2: Dharma becomes “prem” (love + reverence)

  • practices done with sincerity because you see:
    • impurities being destroyed
    • purity emerging
  • focus becomes liberation, not celestial reward

Stage 3: Even liberation-focus drops into pure present awareness

He references a phase described in Apurva Avsar:

  • “Let pure equanimity prevail irrespective of embodied or liberated state.”

Meaning:

  • even desire for liberation drops
  • bondage/liberation both are seen as modifications
  • “I am the pure soul—untouched, unbound.”

He says: This deeper transformation is only possible through varte Sadguru laksh (following true guidance).

7) “Great honour” in rituals: the emotional proof that spirituality has entered

He says: when Sadguru adds spirituality to rituals, feelings arise within.

He uses Gujarati terms:

  • samvedan = sensation/inner felt experience
  • spandan = vibration

Not “no-mind” necessarily, but something experiential.

Mechanical vs living ritual

  • If there’s no love/reverence, ritual becomes a burden (pure vyavahar).
  • If there is great honour, it becomes alive and meaningful.

How do you know you have “great honour”?

He lists three signs (very memorable):

1. You feel happy while doing it

2. When you’re not doing it, everything feels tasteless

3. You cannot tolerate condemnation

  • not that you must react
  • but you cannot tolerate hearing it criticized
Personal example (condemnation)

He recalls hearing someone criticized Shrimad Rajchandra and he cried for hours— not because his ego was attacked, but because love cannot tolerate condemnation of the beloved.

8) Nala–Damyanti story: love reacts to the name itself

He tells a story from Nala–Damyanti:

  • Someone says: “Nala has come to our field.”
  • But “nala” is also a kind of grass.
  • Damyanti hears “Nala has come” and rushes—clothes worn hurriedly—eyes sparkling.

Point:

  • When love is present, even hearing the name triggers deep movement.
  • He says: a seeker should have that kind of love for sadhana (spiritual pursuit).

He clarifies:

  • he’s not saying everyone must cry or dance
  • but “some chemistry should change in you” (his phrase).

9) Ritual rules: necessary but not the purpose

This is a major “balance” teaching in the transcript.

Game analogy: rules are needed, but nobody plays for rules

  • No game can be played without rules (carrom, chess, cards, etc.)
  • But nobody plays for the rules; they play to enjoy.

He applies it to religion:

  • practices need discipline/rules
  • but when rules dominate and love/essence is lost:
    • you become narrow
    • sometimes even “inhuman” in behavior

He repeats this warning multiple times:

  • religion becomes a list of rigid rules, making people narrow and sometimes inhuman.

10) A powerful definition: “Till when should you do vyavahar?”

He offers a striking, non-bookish answer.

Instead of saying “till the 12th gunasthanak” (which he calls bookish), he says:

If sugar or salt is on your tongue and you still don’t realize it— then you should continue vyavahar.

Meaning:

  • until your inner sensitivity is so refined that you clearly know “this is sweet / this is salty” (i.e., direct spiritual discernment), you need:
    • right disciplines
    • right choices
    • right effort

Related teaching: don’t imitate “choiceless awareness” too early

He warns:

  • don’t jump to “choiceless effortless awareness” prematurely
  • right now: you must make right choices based on your state:
    • agitated → meditate
    • dry → go for puja
    • dull → do swadhyay etc.

He says:

  • 110 involves right choice + right effort
  • 111 is where “no choice, no effort” becomes natural

11) “Wow → Vow”: how to convert inspiration into steady change

He gives a very usable method:

  • Listen to discourses with a free mind (just listen and understand)
  • “wow-feelings” arise
  • then bind those wows into vows (sankalp)

He literally plays with the words:

  • W-o-w
  • V-o-w
  • “Before ‘Vow,’ get ‘Wow.’”

12) The verse itself: key words and meaning (Verse 110)

He points out: “mat, darshan” keeps appearing again and again in Atmasiddhi (verse 9, 17, 105-ish themes), showing how central open-mindedness is.

Core phrase (as discussed in the transcript)

  • Mat-darshan = opinions + beliefs + prejudices + ideologies + philosophies
  • Agrah taji = give up insistence/stubborn clinging (he says: if not the opinion, at least drop the insistence)
  • Varte Sadguru laksh = follow the Sadguru’s guidance
  • Lahey shuddh samkit = attains shuddh samkit (experiential belief/faith)
  • Jema bhed na paksh = in which there is no difference/division

Important nuance he repeats

  • Accepting isn’t enough.
  • “Varte” = follow. So:

1. understand + accept

2. then follow the Guru’s direction

13) A big obstacle addressed: inherited beliefs and wrong-guru beliefs

He explains why “mat-darshan-agrah” becomes such a barrier:

Two ways beliefs get installed in you

1. Inherited (family tradition, parents, grandparents)

2. gained from a wrong guru (asadguru / charlatan)

Then, from those you build concepts about:

  • the path of liberation (how rituals should be, what attire, which instruments, which method)
  • the Enlightened Soul (how an Enlightened One “should” walk, smile, talk, etc.)

He calls this out strongly:

  • An ignorant person making concepts about how an omniscient should be—this is upside down.

Sect example (Sthanakvasi vs Deravasi)

He gives vivid contrasts:

  • one side: “don’t offer flowers / don’t light lamps / don’t burn incense (violence)”
  • other side: “give me all the roses / best incense for worship”

His point:

  • these are deep conditioned beliefs
  • the problem isn’t necessarily that you have a practice
  • the real hurdle is insistence that everyone must do it your way

He states it explicitly:

Not exactly the belief, but the insistence of the belief is the hurdle.

14) Open mind is required for discovery (parachute analogy)

He repeats the principle you’ve seen in earlier videos:

  • no discovery with a closed mind
  • you can’t go on an adventure with a closed mind

He uses a sharp metaphor:

A parachute (and a mind) must be open— if closed, it is useless.

15) Insistence = rigidity = violence (and it blocks soul-growth)

He lays out the chain:

  • clinging to “my idea” → rigidity
  • rigidity → stems the growth of the soul
  • insistence → becomes a form of violence (emotionally, relationally; you start hurting)

He adds a practical correction:

  • it’s okay to know only one idea
  • but know it’s one among many
  • otherwise you think “my idea is the only idea,” and you cling.

16) “Following Guru” in real life: the Ayambil example

This is a very concrete test-case of varte Sadguru laksh:

  • You come to Guru for permission: “May I do Ayambil?”
  • Guru says: “No—today do selfless service / scriptural study instead.”
  • If you insist (“But it’s Oli; scriptures say…”):
    • you are cherry-picking scriptures
    • increasing swachhand (self-willed behavior)

He says bluntly:

  • If scriptures alone could do your welfare, why did you come to the Guru?

So the remedy:

  • swachhand is restrained in the presence of an Enlightened Mentor
  • and the disciple must choose: follow the Guru’s direction, not self-will dressed up as “religion.”

17) Inner-practice emphasis shifts in 110: contemplation → meditation → absorption

He explicitly compares the practice focus:

  • 109: more stress on introspection (self-purification phase)
  • 110: more stress on contemplation and meditation

The flow he describes (very testable)

  • contemplation deepens → reflection deepens
  • thoughts begin diminishing
  • suddenly: you get absorbed in meditation

He says this might appear in the quiz:

Engrossed in contemplation → absorbed in meditation

State-based guidance

  • If mind is still running / dirty / violent / toxic:
    • do more contemplation and scriptural study
    • don’t force meditation prematurely
  • If mind becomes steady:
    • you “slip into” meditation naturally

He criticizes over-instruction/guided approaches:

  • if you’re anxious waiting for the next instruction, you’re not absorbed.

18) What “shuddh samkit” experience is like (as described)

The “no-mind” description

He describes deep meditation:

  • world becomes muted (vehicles, fan/AC)
  • thoughts become subtle, floating
  • then even that disappears: “pure sky”
  • posture/time awareness fades
  • even vow-timings (“how long did I meditate?”) stop mattering

The key event

At a blessed moment:

  • he experiences Pure Consciousness
  • gets glimpses of it

Crucial: momentary but unforgettable

He repeats:

  • it is a glimpse
  • momentary (not permanent yet)
  • but the taste cannot be forgotten
  • vision transforms completely

He uses the “taste memory” analogy (Vietnamese coffee example):

  • even one sip can be unforgettable
  • similarly, one glimpse changes everything.

Transformation effect

He says:

  • you have “tasted permanence”
  • now the temporary will feel temporary
  • you’re less likely to get trapped again

He gives “light in a dark place” metaphors:

  • you see the layout once; even if it becomes dark again, you now know
  • curtain opens briefly; later curtain closes, but you still “see” inwardly because you’ve seen once.

He also uses an “instant transformation” analogy involving electricity (important idea: sudden transformation), but as a safety note: don’t try anything dangerous—the point is simply “instant change can happen.”

19) Why 110 and 111 are different (as promised)

He clearly distinguishes:

Verse 110 = high disciple state

  • Self-experience occurs after effort
  • it may not repeat the next time
  • it is not under your control
  • it might happen again after months

He calls this the samkit of a supreme disciple.

Verse 111 = Guru / Enlightened state

  • experience becomes steadfast and controllable
  • “whenever he wants to be beyond thoughts, he can”
  • stable mastery

He says he’ll explain the exact difference next session.

20) “Jema bhed na paksh” — the main meaning to retain

He gives multiple possible meanings but tells you clearly which to pick.

Meaning he wants you to pick (primary)

Once shuddh samkit (experiential faith) is attained:

  • irrespective of religion
  • irrespective of sect
  • irrespective of time
  • irrespective of place
  • irrespective even of “which True Guru’s refuge”

the experience of the true Self is the SAME.

He insists:

  • don’t say “similar”
  • say same

Why?

  • vyavahar samkit (faith based on teachings) can vary in source:
    • faith in Param Krupalu Dev vs Christ vs Krishna, etc.
  • but experiential Self-knowledge is beyond theoretical differences.

He sums it:

  • theories can differ
  • but when attachments/aversions weaken and awareness steadies, the experience is identical.
