Extensive Study Notes (Understanding Jina through the Living Sadguru)

Built from your Video 12 transcript. Structured to read end-to-end, and ready to print as a PDF.

1) Where Verse 12 sits in the flow

  • Verse 9: Need of a Guru (drop nij-pakṣ, adopt Guru’s view–conviction–conduct).
  • Verse 10: Five inner marks of a Sadguru (Ātma-jñān, Sam-darśitā, Vichare uday-prayog, Apūrva vāṇī, Param-śrut).
  • Verse 11: Why living (pratyakṣ) Sadguru’s upkār to you now is greater than non-present (parokṣ) Jina’s.
  • Verse 12: How that upkār operates: Without a Sadguru’s teaching you neither comprehend the nature of the Jina nor can your devotion yield fruit.

2) Verse 12 — sense and short meaning

Original sense (as conveyed in the talk): - “Sadgurunā updeśh vaṇ (without the Sadguru’s teaching) samjāy na Jin-rūp (one cannot understand the nature/state of the Jina). Samjyā vaṇ upkār sho? (Without this understanding, what benefit can arise from worship/devotion?)”

Short meaning: Only through a living Sadguru’s insight can a seeker comprehend (samjāy) and thereby attain (pāmāy) the Jina-state. Mere ritual praise of Jinas, without understanding Jina-rūp, remains largely traditional custom, not liberating devotion.

3) Clarifying key terms

  • Jina — “Conqueror” of the inner enemies; includes state (omniscience, dispassion) and upkār (benefit done to beings).
  • Parokṣ Jina — not accessible in body; contact via scripture (śāstra) and idol (mūrti). (Different Jain sects emphasise differently, but at least one of these two must be accepted.)
  • Pratyakṣ Sadguru — living Enlightened Mentor who carries Verse 10’s marks and teaches, guides, corrects.
  • Upkār — tangible benefit you receive (clarity, correction, kindling of Self-love).

4) Why scripture/idol alone can fall short for you

1) Risk of missing the essence or misinterpreting multi-viewpoint teachings. 2) Doubts remain: a book/ idol cannot personally dispel your specific confusion. 3) Result: delusion → confusion → frustration → (sometimes) depression.

Hence Verse 12’s claim: The Sadguru is the bridge from parokṣ truth to your pratyakṣ understanding and practice.

5) What the Sadguru uniquely provides for Jina-understanding

A. He explains the Jina’s inner state: dispassion (vītarāgata), omniscience (sarvajñatā), purity—what is truly worthy of devotion.

B. He explains the path the Jina trod: how inward turning, right belief, and passion-weakening matured— so you see how to walk rather than only whom to worship.

C. He relocates devotion from tradition to truth: - Traditional devotion: names, forms, genealogies, colours, stories. - True devotion: reverence to the state and a resolve to become like that.

D. He gives the keys: precise contemplations and practices that unlock Self-realisation (the “keys to open the door to SR”).

E. He turns “pratiṣṭhā” inward: The line-drawing paste (añjana-śalākā) is for your eyes—to “see Bhagwan in the stone,” i.e., to see Jina-rūp beyond form.

6) Goal vs Target — a helpful map

  • Goal: Liberation (mokṣa).
  • Target: Self-realisation (first milestone).
  • Means: Inspire your target by Jinas as supreme model, but reach it through the Sadguru’s living teaching that clarifies Jina-rūp and aligns your practice.

If the goal doesn’t inspire you, the target won’t be pursued; the Sadguru ensures inspiration becomes implementation.

7) The verse’s gentle correction to common habits

  • Habit: Equating devotion with temple routines, stutis/stotras, decorations, and ritual know-how (swastika variants, puja garments, stories, lineage facts).
  • Correction: Those are fine as expressions—but without understanding Jina-rūp, they don’t reshape the ego.

Shift: From adoring “what He looked like / whom He was related to” → to adoring “what He is and how He became that.”

8) From “Samjāy” to “Pāmāy” — the two verbs of Verse 12

1) Samjāy (Comprehend): Through Sadguru’s teaching you grasp what the Jina is and how that state arises. 2) Pāmāy (Attain): That understanding translates into practice under ājñā, melting ego and delusion; devotion becomes transformative.

Therefore: Worship with understanding → benefit accrues; worship without understanding → mostly cultural comfort.

9) Practical guidance — making devotion fruitful

A. With idols and scriptures - Read the assigned śāstra under Sadguru’s lens; note where understanding changed your conduct that week. - In front of the idol, name a state, not a story: “I bow to dispassion, omniscience, compassion.”

B. With the living Sadguru - Bring specific doubts; request ājñā; report back after attempting. - Watch how He reads a verse/situation; adopt His view–conviction–conduct triad.

C. At home - Swap one decorative routine for one inner practice (e.g., 5 minutes of inward-turning after puja). - Weekly, replace one “fact” you like to recount about a Jina with one state-insight and a practice it demands of you.

10) Reflection prompts

1) Which ritual of mine is most identity-defining—and what state was it meant to point to? 2) Where has a book/idol failed to remove my doubt—and what did a living word from the Guru change in me? 3) This week, what one command of the Guru will I follow that directly trains dispassion or inwardness?

11) One-page recap

  • Claim: Without a living Sadguru’s teaching, the nature of the Jina is not truly understood; without that understanding, worship yields little towards Self-realisation.
  • Bridge: Sadguru translates parokṣ truth into pratyakṣ clarity—explains the state and the path, gives keys, and turns devotion from tradition to transformation. Two verbs: Samjāy → Pāmāy (understand → attain).
  • Method: Idol/śāstra honoured through the Sadguru’s lens; practice under ājñā; devotion aimed at state, not story.

12) Glossary (quick)

  • Ājñā — command/guidance of the Sadguru.
  • Añjana-śalākā — consecration touch; here, reframed as opening our eyes.
  • Jina-rūp — the Jina’s true nature/state and the benefit He confers.
  • Parokṣ/Pratyakṣ — non-present / living, accessible.
