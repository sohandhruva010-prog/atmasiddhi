Video 131 Notes: Verse 117 — “Pure, Knowledgeful, Consciousness, Self-Luminous, Abode of Bliss”

Based on the transcript you shared.

1) Context: Verse 117 continues the description of the soul

Verse 116 began the direct description of the soul’s true eternal nature. It said:

1. You are of the nature of liberation.

2. You are infinite perception.

3. You are infinite knowledge.

4. You are infinite bliss.

5. You are infinite energy.

Now verse 117 continues the same subject and gives five more attributes of the soul.

The speaker reminds us that the soul does not have only ten attributes. The soul has infinite attributes. Verses 116 and 117 only give ten major pointers so the seeker can turn inward and contemplate the glory of the Self.

2) Verse 117

Shuddh buddh chaitanyaghan, swayamjyoti sukhdham;
Biju kahie ketlu? Kar vichar to paam.

Simple meaning

You are pure, knowledgeable, an indivisible mass of consciousness, self-luminous, and the abode of bliss. What more can be said? Deeply contemplate on this and realise the Self.

3) Word-by-word meaning

Shuddh

Pure.

The soul is pure in its true nature.

Buddh

Possessor of infinite knowledge capacity.

Not merely “intelligent” in the ordinary sense, but one whose inherent nature has the capacity to know all.

Chaitanyaghan

An indivisible mass of consciousness.

The soul is full of consciousness throughout. No part of it is unconscious.

Swayamjyoti

Self-luminous, self-radiant, self-illuminating.

The soul does not need another object to illuminate it. It knows itself and the non-self.

Sukhdham

Abode of bliss.

The soul is the home of eternal, infinite, indestructible bliss.

Biju kahie ketlu?

What more can be said?

The Guru has explained the path, the means, the state, and the nature of the soul. Now words are reaching their limit.

Kar vichar to paam

Contemplate deeply and realise.

This is the Guru’s final practical instruction: do not merely listen; think deeply, absorb, and realise.

4) The soul has infinite attributes The speaker gives a powerful image: the celestial beings of the highest heaven, Sarvarthsiddhi, are all Self-realised. They spend countless years in satsang, discussing the attributes of the soul. They do not need to eat, work, socialise, or entertain themselves. Their sole interest is the soul.

They discuss one attribute after another for countless years, without repeating even one attribute, and still they do not complete the description of the soul.

The point is:

The soul’s glory cannot be exhausted by speech.

So verses 116 and 117 are not a full description. They are a map, a doorway, a direction for contemplation.

5) Attribute 1: Shuddh — Pure

The soul is pure by nature.

This purity means the soul is bereft of all other objects and all other feelings. Nothing foreign can truly enter or alter the soul’s eternal nature.

The speaker gives the example of bhel at Chowpatty Beach. If sand mixes into bhel, the bhel becomes impure because another object has entered it. But the soul is not like that. Even though the soul is associated with body, karma, and impure modifications, these do not penetrate or alter its true nature.

So the state may be impure, but the nature remains pure.

This distinction is crucial:

State

The soul’s present condition may be impure due to anger, ego, deceit, greed, attachment, aversion, and ignorance.

Nature

The soul’s eternal nature remains pure and untouched.

The speaker says that the soul has been in an impure state since beginningless time, but even countless years of impure states cannot affect the pure nature of the soul.

6) Crystal and red flower analogy

The speaker revisits the crystal example.

A pure colourless crystal placed near a red flower appears red. But it has not become red. The redness belongs to the association, not to the crystal.

Similarly:

  • due to the association of body and karma, the soul appears impure,
  • due to impure modifications, the soul’s state appears impure,
  • but the soul itself does not become impure.

The inherent nature remains pure.

The speaker summarises beautifully:

Purity remains latent in the worldly state; purity is manifested in the liberated state.

So purity is not newly created in liberation. It was always the soul’s nature. Liberation simply manifests it fully.

7) Boiling water analogy

The speaker gives another example: boiling water.

Boiling water is hot in its current state, but the nature of water is cooling. If boiling water is poured on fire, it extinguishes the fire. Its current modification is hot because of an external instrumental cause, but its inherent nature remains cooling.

Likewise:

  • the soul’s current state may be impure,
  • anger and delusion may be manifest,
  • but the soul’s nature remains pure.

When the instrumental causes of impurity are removed, the soul’s natural purity manifests.

8) Attribute 2: Buddh — Possessor of infinite knowledge

Buddh here means the possessor of infinite knowledge capacity.

Every soul has the inherent capacity to know all. If this capacity is fully manifested, the soul can know:

  • the self,
  • the non-self,
  • all substances,
  • all times,
  • all worlds,
  • all objects worthy of knowing.

The speaker emphasises that the soul does not need to travel to the object to know it. Just as the eyes remain in place and objects are reflected in vision, the soul’s knowledge can know directly when unobstructed.

But in the present state, this capacity is covered by jnanavarniya karma, knowledge-obscuring karma. Because of this covering, we depend on the senses:

  • eyes to see,
  • ears to hear,
  • tongue to taste,
  • skin to touch,
  • nose to smell.

This dependence is not our true nature. It is our poor present state.

The true nature of the soul is direct knowledge without a medium.

9) Every soul is Buddh by nature

When knowledge is fully manifested, we call such a being enlightened, or Buddh.

But from the standpoint of inherent nature, every soul is Buddh because every soul possesses infinite knowledge capacity.

So:

Manifested state

The Enlightened One is Buddh because knowledge has manifested.

Inherent nature

Every soul is Buddh because the capacity is already present.

This is a major theme of these verses: the Omniscient One’s manifested glory is our own latent nature.

10) Attribute 3: Chaitanyaghan — Indivisible mass of consciousness

Chaitanya means consciousness. Ghan means compact, dense, solid, full.

So chaitanyaghan means the soul is a dense, indivisible mass of consciousness.

The soul is not conscious in one part and unconscious in another. Every part of the soul is conscious. Consciousness cannot be divided or separated from the soul.

The speaker says:

Every part of the soul has consciousness.

The soul may expand or contract according to the body it occupies — for example, an elephant body or an ant body — but consciousness is not lost, stretched thin, divided, or broken. The soul remains full of consciousness.

11) Conscious and non-conscious remain distinct

Even though the soul is closely associated with the body, the two do not become one.

The body is jad — non-conscious. The soul is chetan — conscious.

Even in close association:

  • the body does not become conscious,
  • the soul does not become non-conscious,
  • the soul does not transfer its consciousness to matter,
  • matter does not alter the soul’s conscious nature.

The conscious remains conscious. The non-conscious remains non-conscious.

This protects the central distinction between Self and non-self.

12) Attribute 4: Swayamjyoti — Self-luminous The speaker says this is the last slightly difficult attribute.

Jyoti means light or flame. Here it refers to the light of knowledge — jnanjyoti.

This light of knowledge is:

  • eternal,
  • all-pervading in its capacity,
  • capable of knowing the Self,
  • capable of knowing the non-self,
  • capable of knowing all.

It is swa-par prakashak — it illuminates both the Self and the non-self.

13) Meaning of “swayam”

Swayam means self-dependent, independent, not dependent on anything else for existence.

A fire depends on fuel. A lamp may depend on oil. An electric light depends on electricity. The body depends on food and breath.

But the soul does not depend on anything else for its existence or illumination.

It is self-luminous.

The speaker gives the example of the sun:

To see the sun, you do not need a torch.

The sun illuminates itself. Similarly, the Self does not need another object to reveal it.

14) To know the Self, withdraw from objects

This is one of the most practical points in the discourse.

To know external things, we turn outward. But to know the Self, we must withdraw from external objects.

The Self is not known by adding more objects, more experiences, more possessions, or more sensory pursuits.

The speaker says:

To realise the Self, withdraw from objects. Do not add objects.

This is the opposite of worldly seeking. In the world, we think more objects will give more happiness or knowledge. In spirituality, the Self is known by turning away from dependence on objects and focusing inward.

15) Attribute 5: Sukhdham — Abode of bliss

Sukh means bliss. Dham means abode.

So sukhdham means the soul is the abode of bliss.

This bliss is:

  • eternal,
  • infinite,
  • indestructible,
  • independent,
  • beyond the senses,
  • beyond the mind,
  • beyond objects.

The speaker says that among the six substances spoken of in Jain philosophy, only the soul has the attribute of happiness. The other five are non-conscious and cannot possess happiness.

Therefore, if you want true happiness, you must abide in the soul.

Not in cars. Not in clothes. Not in company. Not in possessions. Not in sense pleasures.

The soul alone is the abode of bliss.

16) Why do we seek happiness outside?

The speaker answers simply:

Because bliss is inherent but not experienced.

If the bliss of the soul were manifest, we would not run outside for happiness.

We seek happiness in external pursuits because we do not know or experience our true nature.

But external happiness is not true happiness because it is dependent. If happiness depends on an object, person, situation, or condition, then it must be temporary.

And because it is temporary, it brings fear and anxiety:

  • What if this goes away?
  • What if this changes?
  • What if I lose this person?
  • What if the situation does not remain the same?

So external happiness carries insecurity within it.

17) External happiness is subjective

The speaker makes another important point: worldly happiness is not objective; it is subjective.

It depends on your concepts.

Something appears desirable because your mind has labelled it desirable. If your concept changes, your happiness changes.

The same person, object, or situation that once seemed delightful may later feel burdensome.

So worldly happiness is not in the object itself. It is dependent on mental projection and changing concepts.

True bliss is different. It belongs to the soul and is independent of external conditions.

18) The bliss of the soul is transcendental

The bliss of the Self is not sensory pleasure.

It is not mental excitement.

It is not emotional satisfaction.

It is beyond:

  • senses,
  • mind,
  • objects,
  • circumstances,
  • dependency,
  • fear,
  • insecurity.

This is why the speaker says it cannot truly be understood through discourse alone. Words can only give a map. The bliss of the soul must be experienced.

19) “Biju kahie ketlu?” — What more can be said?

After describing the soul as pure, Buddh, chaitanyaghan, swayamjyoti, and sukhdham, the Guru says:

What more can be said?

This is not impatience. It is the limit of language.

The Guru has explained:

  • the true nature of the soul,
  • how to abide in it,
  • how to retreat from impure states,
  • how pure modification manifests,
  • what instrumental causes help,
  • what the path is,
  • what liberation is.

Now the remaining work is not more listening. It is contemplation and realisation.

20) The Guru’s compassion

The speaker says the Guru shows the soul’s glory but also admits that the exact glory of the soul cannot be put into words.

The Guru inspires the disciple to:

  • climb untold heights,
  • go deep within,
  • plumb immeasurable depths,
  • focus the tendencies inward,
  • practise continuously,
  • realise the Supreme State.

This is the compassion of the Sadguru: He does not merely describe the soul; He urges the disciple to experience it.

21) “Kar vichar to paam” — Think deeply and realise

This is the final instruction of the Guru.

It does not mean casual thinking. It means deep contemplation.

Not merely:

  • listening,
  • taking notes,
  • memorising,
  • repeating words,
  • admiring the verse.

It means allowing these truths to enter the deeper levels of consciousness.

The speaker explains:

Deeply contemplate on what I have told you, and you will attain Self-realisation.

So kar vichar to paam means:

1. Understand the teaching.

2. Reflect deeply.

3. Let it sink within.

4. Focus inward.

5. Realise the Self.

22) “Kar vichar to paam” as a mantra

The speaker shares that this phrase became like an independent mantra among seekers of Param Krupalu Dev.

It can be applied spiritually and practically:

Think over it and realise.

It means do not live superficially. Do not react blindly. Do not accept things mechanically. Contemplate. See clearly. Realise.

The speaker also shares that a small book titled Kar Vichar to Paam was his first encounter with Param Krupalu Dev’s teachings, and that the phrase deeply shaped his spiritual journey.

23) The ten attributes from verses 116 and 117

Verse 116 gave five:

1. Moksh swaroop — of the nature of liberation.

2. Anant darshan — infinite perception.

3. Anant jnan — infinite knowledge.

4. Anant sukh — infinite bliss.

5. Anant virya — infinite energy.

Verse 117 gives five more:

1. Shuddh — pure.

2. Buddh — possessor of infinite knowledge.

3. Chaitanyaghan — indivisible mass of consciousness.

4. Swayamjyoti — self-luminous.

5. Sukhdham — abode of bliss.

Together, these ten are not an exhaustive list. They are a contemplative doorway into the infinite glory of the soul.

24) Practical spiritual instruction from this verse

This verse is not only metaphysical. It gives a direct practice.

When you feel impure, remember:

My state may be impure, but my nature is pure.

When you feel ignorant, remember:

My current knowledge is obstructed, but my nature is infinite knowing capacity.

When you feel body-bound, remember:

I am consciousness, distinct from the non-conscious body.

When you seek happiness outside, remember:

I am the abode of bliss.

When you feel spiritually overwhelmed, remember:

The Guru has given the essence: contemplate deeply and realise.

25) Main takeaway

Verse 117 completes the description of the soul’s true nature by showing five more attributes: purity, infinite knowing capacity, compact consciousness, self-luminosity, and bliss. The soul’s present state may be impure, ignorant, dependent, and restless, but its eternal nature remains pure, conscious, self-radiant, and blissful. The Guru has now said all that can be said in words. The remaining step is not more information, but deep contemplation. Kar vichar to paam — think deeply, turn within, and realise the Self.
