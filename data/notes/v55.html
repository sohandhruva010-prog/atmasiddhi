0:54–1:30 — Disciple’s 3rd argument (Verse 47): “Why isn’t soul known like pot/cloth?”

2:06–2:48 — Verse 55 recited + quick meaning (he starts with the verse itself)

He gives the key line and translates it with rhythm:

“Ghat, pat adi jaan tu, tethi tene maan;
Jananar te maan nahi, kahie kevu jnan?”
  • Ghat, pat adi = pot, cloth, etc.
  • Jaan tu = you know them
  • Tethi tene maan = so you believe they exist (because you know them)
  • Jananar = the knower of these objects
  • Te maan nahi = you do not accept/believe in that knower
  • Kahie kevu jnan? = “What should one say about such knowledge?” (strange/ridiculous)

He emphasizes the question mark tone: it ends like a challenge—“decide whether your reasoning is sane.”

2:48–4:54 — Core logic: accepting the known + knowledge but rejecting the knower is absurd; knower is implicit

He builds the argument step by step:

  • You believe in pot/cloth because you have knowledge of them.
  • But you refuse to accept the knower of those objects.
  • He says: that’s bizarre because:

1. If the knower is absent, the knowledge should be absent too.

2. If knowledge exists, there must be a knower.

  • So the knower’s presence is implicit in the fact of knowing.

1. “Implicit” = implied/suggested though not explicitly stated.

  • The disciple accepts:

1. the objects (jneya: pot, cloth)

2. the knowledge of them (jnan)

  • But rejects:

3) the possessor of knowledge (jnata / jananar)

And he keeps repeating the punch:

  • You accept the known, you accept knowledge, but reject the knower—“kahie kevu jnan?”

4:54–6:10 — Senses are only the medium; the knower must be beyond senses; this is logic (nyay), not self-realisation
