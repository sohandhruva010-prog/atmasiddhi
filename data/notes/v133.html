Verse 133 of the Upsamhar, and it builds directly on verses 131–132. Gurudev now takes the balance of Nishchay and Vyavhar and gives us a practical test for whether we have understood either one correctly.

The verse is:

“Gacchmatni je kalpana, te nahi sadvyavhar;
Bhan nahi nijroopnu, te nishchay nahi saar.”

In essence:

Sectarian distinctions and insistence on one's religious group are not right conduct. And knowledge of the absolute viewpoint that does not increase awareness of one's true Self is not spiritually beneficial. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

So Param Krupalu Dev corrects both extremes:

Wrong Vyavhar: “My sect, my customs, my group are superior.”

Wrong Nishchay: “I can speak beautifully about the pure soul, but I am no closer to experiencing it.”

The test is not what I call my path or how much philosophy I know. The test is: Are attachment and aversion decreasing? Is Self-awareness increasing?

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 133: The Test of Right Conduct and Right Knowledge

Gurudev begins with a reminder from Verse 132:

Nishchay + Vyavhar must remain together.

He gives a simple computer analogy:

Software without hardware? Useless.

Hardware without software? Also useless.

Likewise:

Absolute viewpoint without appropriate conduct is incomplete.

External conduct without awareness of the Self is incomplete.

True spirituality is their integration. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

1. Gurudev's elephant story — “I have faith in God!”

Before entering the verse, Gurudev tells a funny but meaningful story.

A saint visits a town famous for elephants and asks to see them.

The locals warn him:

It's mating season. The elephants become wild and impulsive.

The saint confidently replies:

“Don't worry. I have faith in God!”

They go.

Suddenly an elephant charges at him.

What does the saint do?

He runs for his life. 😂

The local asks:

“But didn't you say you have faith in God?”

The saint replies:

“I have faith in God. I don't have faith in that elephant!”

It sounds clever—but Gurudev asks:

Then what exactly did “I have faith in God” mean? Pasted text(20261112-232142)

2. Be clear what you mean when you say “I am spiritual”

That's the point of the elephant story.

We say:

“I love spirituality.”

“I'm following the spiritual path.”

“I have faith in Guru.”

But Gurudev asks:

Are we actually clear about what spirituality means?

Because spirituality isn't:

only Nishchay

and it isn't:

only external Vyavhar.

Gurudev says:

“Integration of both, combination of both is spirituality.” Pasted text(20261112-232142)

Verse 133 then shows what happens when each is misunderstood.

3. First line — the mistake of one-sided VYAVHAR

“Gacchmatni je kalpana, te nahi sadvyavhar.”

Gurudev explains:

Gacchmat

Sect, creed, religious group and its particular beliefs/customs.

Kalpana

Imagined distinctions/notions.

Sadvyavhar

Right conduct/practice.

So:

The imagined distinctions and insistences of sect and creed do not constitute right spiritual conduct. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

This is Param Krupalu Dev correcting someone who thinks:

“My particular religious group's customs ARE the path of liberation.”

4. Group is not the problem — GROUPISM is

Gurudev makes this distinction very clearly.

Having groups is practical.

Five people may study together.

Different communities naturally have:

  • different customs,
  • different organizations,
  • different methods,
  • different terminology.

That's fine.

The problem begins when:

group → groupism

and then:

“WE are the best.”

“Our Ashram is best.”

“Our sect is best.”

“Our method is the only correct one.”

“Our customs are the real Dharma.”

Now religion has become another vehicle for the ego's desire to distinguish itself from others. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

5. The desire to stand out enters religion too

Gurudev says human beings have a deep desire to:

  • differentiate,
  • distinguish,
  • divide,
  • stand out.

Even in a group of five:

“I want to be the best.”

Parents reinforce it:

“Come first.”

“Be the best dancer.”

“Be better than everyone.”

Then this same psychology enters spirituality:

“Our religious group is superior.”

And from there comes:

comparison → competition → superiority → conflict.

The religious realm is not automatically immune from ego. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

6. Groups can build bonds; groupism builds barriers

Gurudev gives a beautiful contrast.

Sectarianism:

builds barriers instead of bonds.

And then he recalls:

“Maitri bhaavnu pavitra jharnu…”

the prayer for universal friendliness.

We sing about maitri—friendship toward all.

But then spend our energy proving:

“My sect is superior to yours.”

Those two things don't match.

Gurudev says sectarianism:

  • weakens people,
  • creates competition,
  • produces superiority,
  • divides communities.

So although the activity appears “religious,” spiritually it may actually be increasing the very tendencies Dharma is supposed to reduce. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

7. The Muhpatti example

Gurudev gives an example of sectarian distinctions over how a mouth-covering is worn.

One group wears it one way.

Another group another way.

And eventually:

different sect identities arise around the distinction.

Gurudev's point isn't that the practice itself must be discarded.

He says essentially:

Do it if appropriate.

But:

Don't make it the essence of Moksh Marg.

Don't:

  • obsess over it,
  • insist everyone must do it your way,
  • create superiority around it,
  • confuse a sect-specific practice with the absolute path of liberation. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

8. Customs can differ; the spiritual PURPOSE can still be shared

This is one of the nicest parts of the lecture.

Suppose another spiritual tradition uses different:

  • language,
  • customs,
  • concepts,
  • external practices.

But someone says:

“We need to eliminate the lower tendencies within us—attachment, aversion, etc.”

Gurudev says essentially:

Join them!

We might call those tendencies:

impure feelings,

or:

delusion.

Someone else may use different words.

Words aren't the important thing. The substance is. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

That's a very direct continuation of Verse 128's:

Correct, not reject.

9. Unity in diversity

Gurudev calls the proper attitude:

perceiving unity in diversity.

Your customs may differ from mine.

Your terminology may differ.

Your religious background may differ.

But ask:

Are we both moving toward the higher?
Are our lower tendencies weakening?

If yes, then why must difference automatically become division?

Gurudev says when genuine Sadguru-bhakti and spiritual understanding arise, many artificial distinctions begin either to collapse or harmonize. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

10. What does sectarian insistence actually produce?

This is where Gurudev gives the philosophical reason it cannot be Sadvyavhar.

Sectarian insistence produces two things:

Toward MY group:
Raag — attachment

“Our customs.”

“Our Guru.”

“Our way.”

“We're superior.”

Toward another group:
Dwesh — aversion

“They're wrong.”

“Their practices are inferior.”

“Let me criticize them.”

So the net result is:

Raag ↑ Dwesh ↑

But what is Moksh Marg supposed to do?

Raag ↓
Dwesh ↓

Therefore, by definition:

Sectarianism cannot be Sadvyavhar. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

This is probably the cleanest logic in the entire first half.

11. Gurudev's definition of Sadvyavhar

He eventually gives a beautifully simple definition:

Right conduct is conduct through which attachment and aversion decrease.

That's the measurement.

Not:

Which sect taught it?

Not:

Which ritual does it resemble?

Not:

What clothes do you wear?

Not:

Which institution are you from?

Ask:

Does this practice reduce raag-dwesh?

If yes, Gurudev says that is spiritually meaningful conduct.

He even says, in essence:

I don't need to know which sect you belong to. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

12. Pratikraman example — form vs essence

Gurudev uses Pratikraman.

One person may criticize another:

“You don't even perform it in our exact traditional form!”

But Gurudev asks:

What is Pratikraman fundamentally trying to produce?
  • introspection,
  • looking back at one's faults,
  • repentance,
  • transformation.

The external form may vary:

  • across traditions,
  • across places,
  • across time,
  • compared with 2,000 years ago.

But can you recognize the underlying spiritual substance?

If you're so focused on external differences that you miss the shared purpose, you've confused:

your sect's path

with:

the absolute path of liberation. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

13. Now the SECOND line — the mistake of one-sided NISHCHAY Param Krupalu Dev now turns to the opposite person:

“Bhan nahi nijroopnu, te nishchay nahi saar.”

Meaning:

If awareness of one's true Self is not increasing, then that adoption of the absolute viewpoint is not spiritually beneficial. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

This is aimed at the person who can speak endlessly about:

  • pure soul,
  • eternal nature,
  • consciousness,
  • substance,
  • attributes,
  • modifications,
  • Nishchay,
  • Samaysaar…

but internally:

nothing is changing.

14. The first line corrects the mechanical ritualist; the second corrects the dry intellectualist

Gurudev briefly connects this back to the beginning of Atmasiddhi.

First line:
Kriyajada / one-sided Vyavhar

Externally religious.

Attached to:

  • customs,
  • forms,
  • sect,
  • ritual.

But missing the essence.

Second line:
Shushkjnani / one-sided Nishchay

Intellectually sophisticated.

Can discuss the Self beautifully.

But has little actual:

Bhan — awareness of the Self.

So Param Krupalu Dev corrects both in one verse. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

15. Tattvacharcha is not the problem

Gurudev is careful here.

Discussing spiritual fundamentals is good.

Studying metaphysics is good.

Reading texts from the absolute viewpoint is good.

The problem is:

What fruit is it producing?

If I spend years discussing the Self but my Self-awareness doesn't increase, something has gone wrong.

Gurudev uses the term:

Swaroopanusandhan

Connection with / focus on the true Self.

That is what all this study should deepen. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

16. Beautiful words can still be BARREN

Gurudev calls merely intellectual spiritual language:

beautiful but hollow.

And:

barren.

Why barren?

Because barren means:

It produces no fruit.

So ask:

I studied Samaysaar.

I studied Yogvasishtha.

I studied Atmasiddhi.

I can explain Dravya, Guna, Paryay.

Fine.

What fruit appeared?

Is there more:

  • Self-awareness?
  • soul-consciousness?
  • detachment?
  • inner stillness?
  • freedom from body-identification?

If not:

“Te nishchay nahi saar.”

That use of Nishchay hasn't fulfilled its purpose. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

17. Why did you study the scripture in the first place?

This is a really good self-check Gurudev gives.

Why study scriptures describing the pure Self?

Was the goal:

“I finished another book.”

“I can quote another Acharya.”

“I know terminology others don't.”

No.

The goal was:

to experience the Self.

So after studying it three times, five times, ten times:

Am I nearer to myself?

That's the measurement.

Not:

How many scriptures have I completed?

But:

How much has the distance between my attention and my true Self decreased? Pasted text(20261112-232142)

18. High philosophy + same body-identification = problem

Gurudev becomes very direct.

You may speak beautifully about:

the glory of the soul.

But:

body-identification is unchanged.

sensual indulgence is unchanged.

self-will is unchanged.

Then what exactly did all that philosophy accomplish?

And if someone suggests:

“Maybe add some bhakti.”

the one-sided Nishchay person says:

“Bhakti is Vyavhar.”

“We believe in Nishchay.”

But Gurudev asks:

What if that bhakti is exactly what helps break your self-will?

What if devotion to Guru helps reduce:

  • body-identification,
  • sensuality,
  • ego,
  • swachhand?

Then refusing it in the name of Nishchay may actually keep you farther from the Self. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

19. Spirituality should make you MORE ethical, not less

This is another important warning.

Someone becomes so fascinated with “higher spirituality” that ordinary teachings about:

  • honesty,
  • ethical business,
  • truthful behavior,
  • restraint,

start feeling beneath them.

“Why are we talking about ethics? Where is the soul?”

Gurudev strongly rejects that.

A spiritual person should radiate ethics.

If your metaphysics is becoming loftier while your conduct becomes less ethical, your understanding is not becoming higher.

Something has gone badly wrong. Pasted text(20261112-232142)

20. The common problem in BOTH extremes

Now we can see something beautiful.

The sectarian ritualist says:

“My external way is right.”

The dry intellectualist says:

“My philosophical understanding is right.”

They look opposite.

But both contain the same underlying problem:

Ego has captured spirituality.

One says:

My group.

The other says:

My understanding.

One increases attachment/aversion through sectarianism.

The other keeps body-identification, sensuality and self-will intact while talking about the pure soul.

Neither reaches the essence.

21. Two tests from Verse 133

I think this is the easiest way to remember the whole lecture.

Test 1 — For Vyavhar

Ask:

Is this reducing my attachment and aversion?

If my religious practice makes me:

  • more judgmental,
  • more competitive,
  • more sectarian,
  • more attached to “our way,”
  • more hostile toward another way,

then regardless of how religious it looks:

“Te nahi Sadvyavhar.”

That isn't right conduct.

Test 2 — For Nishchay

Ask:

Is this increasing my awareness of the Self?

If my study gives me:

  • vocabulary,
  • quotes,
  • philosophical confidence,
  • intellectual superiority,

but doesn't increase:

  • Self-awareness,
  • detachment from body-identification,
  • inwardness,
  • connection with the Self,

then:

“Te Nishchay nahi saar.”

That use of Nishchay isn't spiritually fruitful.

How Verses 131–133 now fit together
Verse 131

Don't abandon the means after hearing about the pure Self.

Keep Nishchay as the goal; continue Sadhan.

↓

Verse 132

Why? Because Nishchay and Vyavhar are both partial viewpoints.

The complete path integrates both.

↓

Verse 133

Now TEST whether you're using them correctly.

Right Vyavhar:

Raag-dwesh decrease.

Right Nishchay:

Self-awareness increases.

That is extremely logical.

The deepest practical takeaway

The strongest thing Gurudev gives us here is a way to stop measuring spirituality by labels.

Not:

“I am Jain.”

“I follow Shrimadji.”

“I belong to this Ashram.”

“I study Samaysaar.”

“I understand Nishchay.”

“I do this ritual.”

“I do this seva.”

Instead, Param Krupalu Dev makes the measurement internal:

What is happening to my raag-dwesh?

and:

What is happening to my Self-awareness?

That is much harder to fake.

I could do enormous seva and become more attached to:

my seva, my team, my way, my recognition.

Then the activity may look spiritual while raag-dwesh increased.

I could study profound philosophy and become proud:

“These people don't understand the soul like I do.”

Then my vocabulary improved while Self-awareness did not.

Conversely, someone from a completely different tradition may sincerely be reducing:

  • ego,
  • attachment,
  • aversion,
  • lower tendencies,

and moving inward.

Gurudev's teaching is:

Don't become so obsessed with the label that you miss the substance.

So Verse 133 gives us two extraordinarily simple spiritual KPIs:

Vyavhar → Is raag-dwesh decreasing?
Nishchay → Is Atma-bhan increasing?

If yes, the path is producing fruit.

If not, don't protect the mistake by saying:

“But this is our tradition…”

or:

“But scripture says the soul is pure…”

Return to the purpose.

Because ultimately the path is not about proving:

my sect is right

or:

my philosophy is sophisticated.

It is about:

reducing attachment and aversion,

increasing awareness of the true Self,

and moving toward liberation.

That is the balance Param Krupalu Dev is protecting in this verse. Pasted text(20261112-232142)
