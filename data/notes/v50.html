• He starts by saying: to strengthen the conviction about soul vs body (their separateness), the Sadguru repeats the first three parts of the previous verse (Verse 49).
  • He notes this “repeat with tiny change” pattern happens elsewhere too (he mentions 38 and 108 style repetition—same structure, one word changed).
  • Here the same pattern occurs again:
    • Verse 49 = principle
    • Verse 50 = example of that principle
  • Key teaching method he attributes to his Guruji:
    • A principle should be supported by an example.
  • He jokes about people counting how many examples he gives (35, 52 etc.) and clarifies he doesn’t “pre-plan” examples like a quiz—examples arise naturally in teaching.
Main setup:

Verse 49 told you the truth as a principle. Verse 50 will make the truth stick using a sharp, memorable example.

2:40–3:57 — Verse 50: repeated core + one new line (the example)

He reintroduces the same core from Verse 49:

  • Bhasyo = appears
  • Dehadhyasthi = due to false identification with the body
  • Atma deh saman = soul and body appear as one
  • Pan… te banne bhinna chhe = but in reality they are separate

Then comes the only new part:

  • “Jem asi ne myan” = like the sword and the sheath.

He even demonstrates with hand motion:

  • pulling the sword out of the sheath (direction doesn’t matter; what matters is you understood the action).

4:00–4:35 — What he will explain (two targets)

He says he won’t re-explain the first three parts (you’ve grasped them already). He will focus on two things:

1. Why repeat the same words?

2. Why is the sword–sheath example so perfect?

Then he gives the short meaning.

4:21–5:16 — Short meaning of Verse 50

  • Due to false identification, soul and body appear one.
  • But they are actually separate entities— like “asi” (sword) and “myan” (sheath).
  • He encourages using the Gujarati terms:
    • asi = sword
    • myan = sheath So whenever you hear “asi ne myan,” you immediately recall the teaching.

5:16–7:05 — The spiritual punch: body is façade, soul is the “spark”

He begins expanding the meaning behind the example:

  • The body is like the sheath: an outer covering, a façade.
  • The soul is like the sword: the inner spark, the real “power.”
  • A sheath may be beautifully decorated (gold thread, red cover, nice outer look)…
    • but if the sword is blunt or not in good condition, it’s useless for real purpose.
  • Translation into inner life:
    • Don’t get hypnotised by outer appearance (body-only living).
    • Cultivate the ability to value and “see” what’s within.

He then connects to embodiment:

  • A Siddha Bhagwan (Liberated Soul) is easy to understand: only soul remains.
  • The real challenge is when soul is embodied (sword inside sheath).
  • Therefore we must develop a different kind of “seeing eye” (not the physical eye).

7:05–10:13 — “Seeing-eye” (not these eyes) + Holy Spirit framing

  • He says the Holy Spirit / Divine Spirit idea exists across traditions:
    • Christian “Trinity” language appears in his talk (Father–Son–Holy Spirit).
    • In our language: God, Guru, Soul (Holy Spirit = pure soul).
  • The soul is quiet and invisible to ordinary eyes,
    • except to the “seeing eye” (the inner vision of Enlightened Masters).
  • He shares a quick personal note about mesmerism/hypnosis (he doesn’t go into the full story due to time), just to point out:
    • We get “mesmerised” by appearances.
    • We need inner clarity that saves us from wrong pull.

Then he returns clearly:

  • Verse 50 repeats three parts to emphasise separateness.

10:13–13:32 — Why repetition is done: to make it penetrate like a mantra

He explains the purpose of repeating the exact same words:

Why emphasise?

Because Param Krupalu Dev wants a clear picture of the harm caused by false identification:

  • Identifying body and soul as one produces:
    • attachment and aversion
    • foolish seeking of happiness outside
    • the “stupidity” of running in wrong direction for lifetimes

Why repeat the exact same letters/words?

  • So the message penetrates into the heart.
  • It becomes easy to memorise (you’re basically memorising one verse’s structure).
  • He calls it mantra-like:
    • Mantra practice needs repetition, not variety.
    • Devotional song expects variety—too much repetition there feels irritating.
  • Here the goal is:
    • repeat, repeat, repeat…
    • until it becomes your inner baseline:
      • “I am a pure, peaceful, powerful soul.”

He says: this is the outcome of discriminative knowledge— you discriminate correctly and your “I” shifts to soul-nature.

13:32–14:15 — Illusion vs delusion (his quick clarification)

  • He distinguishes loosely:
    • “Illusion” can be linked to not seeing the soul (outer-level confusion)
    • “Delusion” is deeper: ignorance makes you believe body = I
  • Bottom line remains:
    • they appear one,
    • but they are separate with distinct characteristics.

Then he turns fully to the example’s aptness.

14:15–20:24 — Why “asi–myan” is the PERFECT example (he gives multiple reasons)

He says he’ll draw attention to how perfectly the example fits the disciple’s mistake.

A) Key strike: even the sword can’t be seen when inside the sheath

  • Disciple’s logic: “If I can’t see it, it doesn’t exist.”
  • Guru’s silent reply through the example:
    • Even a material sword cannot be seen when it is inside the sheath.
    • Yet you don’t conclude the sword is nonexistent.
  • So:
    • “Not noticeable” ≠ “not existing.”

B) Embodiment parallel

  • Sword-in-sheath ≈ Soul-in-body:
    • when “within,” it’s not directly noticeable to eyes,
    • but it still exists.

C) “Pervasion” point: sword occupies the whole sheath

  • The sword isn’t only in the middle; it occupies the full sheath.
  • Likewise, soul pervades the whole body (not a pea-shaped pearl sitting somewhere).
  • He mocks the “blue pearl” fantasies:
    • If you see a blue pearl, don’t assume it’s soul.
    • Soul pervades the body; it’s not a tiny located object.

D) Priority check: which matters—sword or sheath?

  • He asks the audience:
    • Sword is more important than sheath.
    • Soul is more important than body.
  • Then he challenges daily life:
    • Your whole schedule looks like body is priority:
      • eat, sleep, treadmill…
    • Where is the soul-care?

E) Mutual capacities: what sword can do, sheath cannot (and vice versa)

  • Sheath’s job = cover/protect.
  • Sword’s job = real action/strength (war, defence, purpose).
  • Translation:
    • Body is a cover for the soul.
    • Body itself is unconscious and devoid of happiness (he states it strongly).
  • Practical instruction:
    • Yes, keep the sheath/body in decent condition (eat properly, live properly),
    • but not at the cost of the sword/soul.
  • He calls it absurd if:
    • you’re willing to let the “sword” break,
    • while obsessing over decorating the sheath.

F) Final metaphysical anchor

  • Even though body and soul occupy the same space and have been together since time immemorial,
    • they never become one.
  • Past–present–future: they remain separate.

20:24–end — Transition forward: root cause shown, now doubts will be answered

  • He concludes: these two verses (49–50) have delivered the root cause of the disciple’s doubts:
    • false identification makes soul-body appear one.
  • Now Sadguru will move into addressing each doubt in sequence (in the coming verses).
  • Ends by saying they’ll sing the verse again.

Quick “cheat sheet” (terms + takeaways)

Key terms
  • Bhasyo: appears
  • Dehadhyas: body-identification habit (“I am the body”)
  • Atma deh saman: soul/body appear as one
  • Bhinna chhe: separate entities
  • Jem: like
  • Asi: sword
  • Myan: sheath
Core takeaways
  • Visibility is not the test of existence (sword proves it even materially).
  • Soul is not a tiny located object; it pervades the embodied state.
  • Body is a cover; soul is the essence/power.
  • Repetition is deliberate: to turn the message into a mantra inside you.
