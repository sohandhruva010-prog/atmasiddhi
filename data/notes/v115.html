Video 130 Notes: Verse 115 — The Essence of Religion: Ending False Identification

1) Context: Verse 115 as one of the “pinnacle verses”

After verse 114 explained that delusion of beginningless time can vanish instantly on awakening, the discourse moves to verse 115.

Verses 115, 116, and 117 are called kalash gatha — pinnacle-like verses. Just as the kalash sits at the top of a temple, these verses sit at the summit of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra’s teaching.

The speaker says that if one understands only these three verses deeply, one has grasped the essence of the entire scripture.

Verse 115 reveals the essence of dharma.

2) Verse 115

Chhute dehadhyas to, nahi karta tu karma;
Nahi bhokta tu tehno, e j dharmano marma.

Simple meaning

When false identification with the body ceases, you are no longer the doer of karmas, nor the endurer of their fruits. This alone is the essence of religion.

3) Word-by-word meaning

Chhute

Ceases, stops, is renounced, is given up.

Dehadhyas

False identification with the body.

It means:

  • considering the body as “I”
  • considering body-related things as “mine”
  • identifying with the non-self
  • confusing the soul with the body, mind, relationships, possessions, and roles
To

Then.

Nahi karta tu karma

You are not the doer of karmas.

Nahi bhokta tu tehno

You are not the endurer or enjoyer of the fruits of those karmas.

E j dharmano marma

This alone is the essence, secret, or heart of religion.

4) The central teaching

The verse gives the core of spirituality:

When false identification ends, doership and endurership dissolve.

As long as the soul identifies with the body, it thinks:

  • I am doing
  • I am suffering
  • I am enjoying
  • I am insulted
  • I am praised
  • I am sick
  • I am successful
  • I am hurt

But when the false identification ceases, the soul remains in the knower mode.

The activities of body, speech, and mind may continue, but the inner identification changes.

5) Why this is called the essence of religion

Many people think religion means:

  • performing duties
  • doing rituals
  • chanting mantras
  • doing austerities
  • attending satsang
  • serving others
  • studying scriptures
  • following customs
  • wearing certain clothes
  • belonging to a sect

The discourse does not reject these activities. It says they may be useful because the body and mind must be engaged somewhere. It is better to engage them in auspicious activities than in inauspicious ones.

But the speaker makes a crucial distinction:

These activities are not the essence of religion.

The essence is:

Are attachment, aversion, and ignorance decreasing? Is awareness of the pure Self increasing? Is false identification with the body dissolving?

That is dharmano marma — the secret of religion.

6) Religion versus spirituality

The speaker says this is a spiritually profound verse because it reveals the inner essence of religion.

Religion, when understood only externally, becomes a collection of:

  • rituals
  • rules
  • methods
  • disciplines
  • sectarian practices
  • outer identities

But spirituality asks:

Is the soul moving closer to its true nature?

The essence of religion is not external performance. It is inner transformation.

7) The humorous “garam-naram-maram-dharam” teaching

A woman once complained that her husband became very angry.

The speaker gave a simple poetic answer:

If he becomes garam — hot, angry, you become naram — soft, gentle. This is the maram of dharam — the essence of religion.

The point is that religion is not merely about reacting according to ego. Religion means softening the false self and remaining in awareness.

8) What is false identification?

False identification means the “I” and “mine” are wrongly placed.

Instead of identifying with the pure Self, one identifies with the non-self.

False “I”
  • I am the body.
  • I am the mind.
  • I am my personality.
  • I am my role.
  • I am my social identity.
  • I am the one being praised or insulted.
False “mine”
  • my family
  • my body
  • my possessions
  • my position
  • my success
  • my pain
  • my ideas
  • my reputation

The problem is not that these things exist. The problem is identifying with them.

9) How false identification creates karma

The chain is:

False identification → I-ness and my-ness → likes and dislikes → passions → karmic activity → bondage

When the body is taken as “I,” everything connected with the body becomes “mine.” Then likes and dislikes arise.

For example:

  • If someone praises “me,” I become pleased.
  • If someone criticises “me,” I become hurt.
  • If something benefits “mine,” I become attached.
  • If something harms “mine,” I become angry.

These passions lead to karmic bondage.

Therefore, with false identification, the soul becomes:

  • karta — doer of karma
  • bhokta — endurer of karma

10) What happens when false identification ceases?

When dehadhyas ceases, the activity may continue, but the inner identity changes.

The speaker gives the example of eating.

An ignorant person eats. An Enlightened One may also eat. The outer activity may look the same.

But inwardly, the ignorant one identifies:

I am eating. I like this. I dislike this. This is for me.

The Enlightened One remains as an indifferent spectator:

The body is eating. I am the knower.

So the same activity continues, but bondage does not arise.

11) The “knower mode”

A central phrase in the discourse is knower mode.

The speaker uses several equivalent words:

  • spectator
  • observer
  • knower
  • witness
  • indifferent spectator

To remain in the knower mode means:

Activities occur, but I remain aware as the soul, not as the doer.

This is the heart of the verse.

The speaker humorously connects it to “cactus asana,” but the meaning is serious: remain open, alert, and detached as the knower.

12) Not stopping activity, but changing identity

The teaching is not:

Stop all activity.

The teaching is:

Do whatever is appropriate, but remain a witness.

One may still:

  • eat
  • speak
  • serve
  • study
  • work
  • perform duties
  • care for others
  • do rituals
  • do meditation

But the inner state must be:

I am not the doer. I am the knower.

This is the spiritual essence.

13) Why merely chanting “I am the knower” is not enough

The speaker warns that simply repeating:

“I am a knower and seer.”

does not make one a knower.

Just as saying “water, water” does not quench thirst, repeating “I am the knower” does not dissolve doership unless one actually experiences the state.

One must experience:

  • activity continuing
  • doership dissolving
  • endurership dissolving
  • the witness remaining stable

That experiential shift is the essence of dharma.

14) If there is no doership, there is no future endurership

If false identification ceases, new karmic bondage stops.

If there is no new bondage, there will be no future fruits to endure.

The sequence is:

No false identification → no doership → no new karmic bondage → no future endurership

This explains the first layer of the verse.

15) The deeper question: What about past karmas?

The speaker then raises an important question.

The verse says:

You are not the doer and not the endurer.

This is clear for future karmas. If one no longer identifies, one no longer binds new karmas.

But what about karmas already bound in the past?

If karmas were acquired before enlightenment, and now they mature, must the Enlightened One not endure them?

The law of karma says:

As you sow, so shall you reap.

So does enlightenment cancel the law of karma?

16) The answer: the law of karma continues, but inner endurership dissolves

The law of karma continues even for Enlightened Ones.

Past karmas may still mature and affect:

  • the body
  • external circumstances
  • physical pleasure
  • physical pain
  • worldly situations

But the Enlightened One remains established in the knower mode.

The karmic fruit reaches the body, but not the inner Self.

Therefore, from the outer perspective:

The body experiences the fruit of karma.

But from the spiritual perspective:

The soul does not endure it.

This is why the verse says:

You are not the endurer.

17) The camera lens analogy

The speaker refers to shifting the camera lens.

If the camera focuses on the body, one can say:

  • pain-producing karma is maturing
  • pleasure-producing karma is maturing
  • disease has come
  • comfort has come

But if the camera focuses on the inner state, one sees:

  • peace
  • cheerfulness
  • quietness
  • non-reactivity
  • pure awareness

From that inner perspective, nothing is touching the Enlightened One.

So spiritually, he is not the endurer.

18) The doctor and photograph example

A sick person visits a doctor. The doctor says, “Show me the photo.”

The patient takes out a passport-size photograph.

But the doctor meant an X-ray.

The point:

When you go to the Guru, do not show your outer photograph. Show the X-ray of your inner state.

Do not merely say:

  • I am doing seva.
  • I am attending satsang.
  • I am doing rituals.
  • I am studying scriptures.

The Guru wants to see:

  • What are you feeling?
  • Where are your attachments?
  • Where are your aversions?
  • Where is your ego?
  • Where is your false identification?
  • Are you in knower mode?

The Guru diagnoses inner states, not outer appearances.

19) Command, not suggestion

The speaker says that for true disciples, the Guru does not merely “suggest.”

A suggestion is for admirers. A disciple longs for ajna — command.

When the Guru sees the inner X-ray, he gives the remedy as command:

Do this. Do not do this.

This is not harshness. It is grace.

20) The fruit of remaining in knower mode

When one remains in the knower mode:

  • new karmic bondage stops
  • old karmas shed
  • doership dissolves
  • endurership dissolves
  • inner peace remains stable
  • attachment and aversion weaken
  • the soul moves toward liberation

This is not a small practice. It is the essence of religion.

21) Bhagwan Mahavira and the knower mode

The speaker gives the example of Bhagwan Mahavira.

During his spiritual practices, intense pain was inflicted on his body by:

  • celestial beings
  • human beings
  • animals
  • snakes

These are called upsarg — external afflictions.

But Bhagwan Mahavira remained in the knower mode.

Because he did not react through attachment or aversion, a massive shedding of karmas occurred.

The teaching:

Pain alone does not purify. Pain endured in knower mode destroys karmas on a vast scale.

22) Small austerity versus the essence of religion

The speaker contrasts small renunciations with the deeper secret.

If one gives up one sweet, a small amount of sin or attachment is avoided. That is good, but it is limited.

But remaining in the knower mode during karmic fruition is like a spark falling on a heap of cotton.

A single spark can burn the entire heap.

Similarly:

The spark of enlightenment and knower mode can destroy heaps of karmas.

This connects back to verse 114: the effect can be instant and massive.

23) The stack of cotton example

The image:

There is a huge stack of cotton. One spark touches it, and the whole thing turns to ashes.

Likewise, there may be heaps of karmas. But if the spark of enlightenment appears and the soul remains in knower mode, karmas are shed rapidly.

This is the power of dharmano marma.

24) Scriptural perspective versus spiritual perspective

The speaker makes an important distinction.

Scriptural perspective

From a technical scriptural view, the soul becomes fully non-doer and non-endurer at the 14th gunasthanak, when all karmas are completely exhausted.

At that point, no karmic activity remains.

Spiritual perspective

From the spiritual view, the moment enlightenment occurs and the soul becomes established in the knower mode, it is no longer truly the doer or endurer.

Why?

Because the soul is no longer identifying with the body, passions, or karmic personality.

Param Krupalu Dev highlights the spiritual perspective in this verse.

25) The karmic personality is divorced

The speaker says:

Because false identification is renounced, you have nothing to do with the karmic personality and karmic manifestations anymore.

This is a powerful phrase.

The karmic personality includes:

  • body
  • temperament
  • habits
  • external situations
  • pleasure and pain
  • social identity
  • karmic circumstances

When false identification ceases, the soul divorces itself from this karmic personality.

The body may undergo pleasure or pain, but the soul does not become internally bound by it.

26) Why this ends transmigration

Transmigration continues because of:

  • false identification
  • doership
  • attachment
  • aversion
  • karmic bondage
  • endurership
  • renewed reaction

When false identification ceases, the cycle is broken.

The soul remains as the knower.

Without doership and endurership, new bondage stops and old karmas are shed.

This is how transmigration ends.

27) Essence of religion in one line

The essence of religion is:

Remain as the pure knower by dissolving false identification with the body.

Or even shorter:

Let dehadhyas cease.

Everything else supports this.

28) Key terms

Dehadhyas

False identification with the body.

Karta

Doer of karma.

Bhokta

Endurer or enjoyer of karma.

Dharma no marma

The essence, secret, or heart of religion.

Knower mode

The state of remaining as witness, observer, spectator, or knower.

Ajna

Command of the Guru.

Upsarg

Affliction or hardship inflicted externally.

Gunasthanak

Stage of spiritual development.

Karmic personality

The body-mind-karmic identity shaped by past karmas.

30) Practical application

When waking up, the main question is not only:

  • Should I meditate?
  • Should I do prayers?
  • Should I do yoga?
  • Should I study?
  • Should I serve?

Do what is appropriate. But the deeper question is:

Can I remain as the knower while doing it?

In every activity, check:

  • Is false identification arising?
  • Is doership arising?
  • Is endurership arising?
  • Am I reacting through likes and dislikes?
  • Can I remain a witness?
  • Can I see the body and mind acting while I remain the soul?

This is the daily practice of verse 115.

31) Final summary

Verse 115 reveals that religion is not merely rituals, duties, austerities, or auspicious activities. The essence of religion is the cessation of false identification with the body. When dehadhyas ceases, the soul remains as the knower. Then doership dissolves, endurership dissolves, new karmic bondage stops, and even old karmas no longer touch the inner state. This is dharmano marma — the secret and heart of dharma.
