Verse 137 of the Upsamhar, and it marks an important transition in the conclusion of Atmasiddhi.

Verses 128–136 cautioned the seeker about subtle philosophical mistakes: one-sided Nishchay, one-sided Vyavhar, misunderstanding Upadan and Nimit, and using correct principles incorrectly. Now Param Krupalu Dev asks:

What can eventually happen to someone who keeps going down that wrong path while believing himself spiritually advanced?

The answer is severe: he can become a hypocrite—someone whose spiritual speech rises higher and higher while his actual inner state remains filled with delusion.

The verse is:

“Mukhthi jnan kathe ane, antar chhutyo na moh;
Te pamar prani kare, matra Jnanino droh.”

In essence:

One may verbally preach lofty spiritual knowledge while internally remaining bound by delusion. Such a pitiable being ultimately acts in betrayal/irreverence toward the Enlightened Ones. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 137: When Spiritual Knowledge Becomes Hypocrisy

Gurudev's warning is not:

“Don't study philosophy.”

It is:

Don't let your speech become more enlightened than your life.

Because if:

what I SAY ↑↑↑

while

what I AM remains unchanged,

the growing gap becomes hypocrisy. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

1. This begins the SECOND part of the Upsamhar

Gurudev reminds us of the structure.

Verses 128–136

Caution the seeker about spiritual principles and practices.

The basic warning was:

“Jo iccho parmarth toh…”

If you genuinely desire the Supreme Truth, make the right effort and don't lose direction through one-sided understanding. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

Now the remaining verses describe three spiritual states:

Verse 137

The hypocrite / pseudo-Jnani

↓

Verse 138

The true Mumukshu / seeker of liberation

↓

Verses 139–140

The true Jnani / Enlightened One

↓

Finally everything culminates in:

“Ho Vandan Aganit.”

Innumerable obeisance to the Enlightened One. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

So Param Krupalu Dev is now almost saying:

You've heard all this philosophy. Now learn how to distinguish spiritual states.

2. Why so much warning about the “dry intellectualist”?

At the beginning of Atmasiddhi, Param Krupalu Dev criticized both:

Kriyajada

Mechanical ritualist.

and:

Shushkjnani

Dry intellectualist.

But Gurudev notices that toward the end, Param Krupalu Dev seems especially concerned with the Shushkjnani.

Why?

Gurudev's explanation is that modern people are increasingly:

  • logical,
  • intellectual,
  • capable of reading scripture,
  • capable of interpreting scripture,
  • confident in their own interpretations.

None of that is inherently bad.

But the danger is:

Knowledge makes it very easy to appear spiritually advanced. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

3. The mechanical ritualist vs dry intellectualist

Gurudev makes a striking comparison.

A mechanical ritualist may:

  • fast,
  • pray,
  • perform rituals,
  • do religious activities,

without deeply understanding their purpose.

That may not produce liberation.

But at least, within the framework Gurudev is explaining, they may still be engaging in auspicious activities.

The dry intellectualist can become more dangerous.

Why?

Because he may use:

“I am the pure soul.”

to abandon discipline altogether.

Eventually he may become:

  • irreligious,
  • unethical,
  • immoral,

while still believing himself spiritually advanced.

And because he possesses knowledge and communication skills, other people may mistake him for a Jnani too. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

4. “Mukhthi Jnan Kathe” — knowledge is only on the MOUTH

The first phrase is very precise:

Mukhthi

From the mouth / verbally.

Jnan kathe

Preaches knowledge.

So this person can speak beautifully about:

  • soul,
  • purity,
  • Nishchay,
  • liberation,
  • consciousness,
  • scriptures,
  • detachment.

Gurudev calls them:

“honeyed words of wisdom.”

They sound wonderful.

The person may be:

  • well-read,
  • well-versed,
  • intelligent,
  • eloquent,
  • an excellent speaker.

But none of those things automatically establish:

Atmajnan — Self-realisation. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

5. Shastrajnan ≠ Atmajnan

This is one of the central distinctions.

Shastrajnan

Knowledge of scripture.

Atmajnan

Direct Self-knowledge / Self-realisation.

We can easily become impressed by the first:

“Wow, he knows so many verses.”

“He explains philosophy beautifully.”

“He answers every question.”

“His discourse is incredible.”

But Gurudev says:

Don't confuse scriptural mastery with Self-realisation.

A true Jnani may absolutely possess extraordinary scriptural knowledge.

But scriptural knowledge by itself doesn't prove Jnanidasha. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

6. “All that glitters is not gold”

Gurudev gives the analogy:

Brass and gold can both look yellow.

But:

All that glitters is not gold.

Likewise:

  • beautiful speech,
  • spiritual appearance,
  • large audiences,
  • impressive personality,

can attract us.

That's fine as a starting point.

But Gurudev says:

Go further and discern.

A white robe doesn't automatically mean a pure heart.

Millions attending someone's lectures don't automatically prove enlightenment.

But equally, a huge following doesn't prove someone is false either—Tirthankars themselves had enormous congregations.

So:

Don't make superficial criteria absolute. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

7. Gurudev's test: “Can you see your GOAL in Him?”

This is a beautiful line.

Before recognizing someone as your Sadguru, Gurudev says:

“You should be able to see your goal in Him.”

Your:

  • intellect,
  • understanding,
  • experience,
  • heart

should become convinced.

Then refuge can arise.

Don't rush merely because someone speaks beautifully. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

8. Three characteristics of the dry intellectualist here

Gurudev summarizes this person very clearly:

1. A lot of scriptural knowledge

Often excellent memory and understanding.

2. No Self-knowledge

The Self has not actually been experienced.

3. Does not live what he speaks

His conduct and inner state don't match his discourse.

That's the person Verse 137 is portraying. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

9. Neither the GOAL nor the MEANS

Remember Verse 131:

Keep Nishchay as your goal and continue Sadhan.

This person has lost both.

He isn't:

established in the pure Self,

and he isn't:

sincerely practicing the means that increase Self-awareness.

So:

No genuine abidance in Self.

No genuine spiritual practice toward it.

What remains?

Talking.

That's why Gurudev calls him an impractical theorist. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

10. Spiritual catchphrases can create an IMAGE

Gurudev humorously describes how someone can memorize:

  • catchwords,
  • punchlines,
  • fancy spiritual phrases,

and keep dropping them everywhere.

Why?

To create an impression:

“Look how deeply immersed I am in the soul.”

They may even learn the external mannerisms associated with inwardness.

Close the eyes.

Pause dramatically.

Appear absorbed.

And Gurudev jokes that everyone thinks:

“He has reached the seventh Gunasthanak!” 😂

The problem is that genuine inwardness can look similar externally.

So an ordinary person must use discernment, not just visual impressions. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

11. The difference between a Jnani and an imitator is PURPOSE

This is subtle.

A genuine Jnani may:

  • speak beautifully about the Self,
  • close His eyes,
  • remain inward,
  • appear absorbed.

The hypocrite may imitate exactly those things.

So what's one key difference Gurudev highlights?

The true Jnani isn't trying to show you that He loves the soul.

He simply does.

The imitator's purpose is:

“People should think I am immersed in the Self.”

That's a completely different inner motivation. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

12. “Profit of pride”

Gurudev uses a powerful phrase for the hypocrite's motivation:

Profit of pride.

Why preach?

Not primarily to awaken others.

Not primarily because truth is overflowing.

But:

“What will this do for my image?”

The currency being earned is:

  • admiration,
  • status,
  • recognition,
  • followers,
  • importance.

So even spiritual knowledge becomes another business of the ego. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

13. “Antar Chhutyo Na Moh” — inside, delusion remains

Now comes the second half of the diagnosis.

Outwardly:

Lofty knowledge.

Inwardly:

Moh remains intact.

Gurudev describes the inner state:

  • attachment,
  • aversion,
  • worldly desires,
  • passions,
  • craving for comfort,
  • craving for luxury,
  • reactivity when circumstances change.

So externally:

“Everything is inferior to the soul!”

Internally:

“I hope I get this comfort.”

“I hope this happens my way.”

“Why wasn't I given that?”

That's the contradiction. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

14. Detachment does NOT mean lack of possessions

Gurudev gives a useful distinction using:

Sudama

Little/no worldly comfort, yet detached.

King Janak

Surrounded by a kingdom and luxury, yet detached.

Therefore:

Detachment is about craving, not merely possession.

You can have little and desperately crave more.

Or possess much without inner dependence on it.

So Gurudev isn't simply checking:

“Does this speaker live comfortably?”

He's checking:

What is the inner dependence?
What happens when comfort is removed? Pasted text(20261116-003420)

15. Adverse situations expose the inner state

Gurudev gives another practical test.

When everything is comfortable, someone may appear:

  • calm,
  • spiritual,
  • detached.

But change the situation slightly.

Then:

  • irritation,
  • anger,
  • frustration,
  • reactivity.

appear.

So Gurudev is saying:

Don't evaluate detachment only when everything is going according to preference.

Adversity reveals how much inner dependence remains. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

16. The ego may become STRONGER because of spiritual knowledge

This is one of the most important warnings.

We assume:

More spiritual knowledge → less ego.

Not necessarily.

If the knowledge hasn't transformed the person:

Knowledge becomes new material for ego.

Now the person can boast:

“Ask me any verse.”

“I know Samaysaar.”

“I know Niyamsaar.”

“I know Uttaradhyayan.”

Add:

  • excellent memory,
  • excellent speaking ability,
  • followers,

and the ego has more reasons to inflate than an ordinary person's ego. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

This is probably one of the strongest warnings for anyone seriously studying spirituality.

17. The craving changes from money to spiritual STATUS

The ego may become subtler.

Maybe the person doesn't care as much about ordinary status.

But now wants:

  • spiritual fame,
  • titles,
  • importance,
  • special seating,
  • followers,
  • admiration,
  • honorifics.

Gurudev jokes about someone being upset if they're called:

“Shri…”

instead of:

“Param Pujya Shri…”

😂

But the point is serious.

The object of ego changed.

Ego itself did not. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

18. This verse is a “SIREN”

Gurudev says Verse 137 is a warning for all of us, even if we don't consider ourselves Jnanis.

We shouldn't dismiss it:

“This is about fake Gurus. Doesn't apply to me.”

Because the underlying tendencies can appear in ordinary seekers too:

  • wanting recognition for knowledge,
  • wanting importance in seva,
  • enjoying admiration,
  • craving comfort,
  • reacting badly when circumstances change,
  • speaking higher than we're living.

Gurudev says if these dominate, we may not even qualify as a good spiritual aspirant, forget being a Jnani. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

19. How hypocrisy develops

Gurudev describes it almost like two lines moving apart.

External speech:

↑ ↑ ↑ ↑

Higher philosophy.

More quotes.

More lectures.

More spiritual image.

Internal state:

↓ ↓ ↓ ↓ Same desires.

Same ego.

Same attachment.

Same reactivity.

Same craving.

As the distance increases:

Hypocrisy increases.

Eventually the person becomes trapped by the image he created. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

20. “Pamar Prani” — a pitiable being

Param Krupalu Dev uses a strong word:

Pamar

Lowly / wretched / pitiable.

Gurudev explains why the state is so pitiable.

This person has:

  • intelligence,
  • memory,
  • scripture,
  • speech,
  • understanding,

yet none of it produces spiritual welfare.

Someone who doesn't know may still learn.

But this person thinks he already knows.

And because he wants to be regarded as enlightened, his entire effort goes toward maintaining the image rather than actually becoming pure. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

21. Then comes the WORST part: “Jnanino Droh”

Up to now, the person is harming himself.

But Param Krupalu Dev says the situation can deteriorate further:

“Matra Jnanino Droh.”

Betrayal / hostility / irreverence toward the Enlightened Ones.

Gurudev emphasizes that Jnani here means the entire category of Enlightened Ones.

Why?

Because the person's problem is no longer with one individual.

He has become opposed to genuine Enlightenment itself. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

22. Why would a pseudo-Jnani oppose a REAL Jnani?

The psychology Gurudev explains is very logical.

Imagine someone has spent years building the image:

“I am spiritually advanced.”

He has:

  • followers,
  • admiration,
  • status,
  • reputation.

Then he meets a genuine Enlightened One.

What should he do?

Show reverence.

Even if he doesn't become His disciple:

at least respect, honor, devotion.

But now a problem appears.

If he bows to the real Jnani:

“What will my followers think?”

What if they realize:

“This person is greater?”

What if they leave?

So ego creates:

comparison

↓

jealousy

↓

animosity

↓

hostility. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

23. Then he HAS to find something wrong with the Jnani

To preserve his own image, the pseudo-Jnani begins:

“His lecture was good…”

BUT…

“This thing He does doesn't make sense.”

“A real Enlightened One wouldn't behave like that.”

“His conduct here is questionable.”

Why?

Not necessarily because he's sincerely investigating truth.

Gurudev says psychologically he needs to prove the Jnani wrong to maintain his own position.

Otherwise his entire spiritual identity is threatened. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

This is where hypocrisy turns into Jnanino Droh.

24. Comparison → jealousy → animosity → hostility → disrespect

Near the end Gurudev gives the whole downward progression:

Comparison

↓

Jealousy

Animosity

Hostility

Severe karmic consequences

And Gurudev says:

Stop somewhere.

Examine yourself before one stage turns into the next. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

25. The Goshala–Mahavir example

Gurudev gives Goshala as the major historical example.

Goshala spent around six years in close association with Mahavir, serving Him and learning by observing Him.

Yet ego and craving for his own spiritual status remained.

Eventually he separated and established his own following.

He became extremely influential and gained many followers.

Then the rivalry escalated.

According to the account Gurudev narrates, Goshala:

  • claimed superiority,
  • denied Mahavir's Omniscience,
  • insulted Him,
  • criticized His conduct,
  • developed intense hostility,
  • ultimately directed tejoleshya toward Mahavir with the intention of harming Him.

Mahavir remained calm.

The attack ultimately rebounded upon Goshala himself. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

Gurudev's reason for telling the story is not merely historical.

It illustrates how far:

ego + jealousy + spiritual rivalry

can take someone who initially lived in the proximity of a genuine Enlightened Being.

26. Six years physically near Mahavir — yet spiritually far

This is perhaps the saddest part of the example.

Goshala had extraordinary physical proximity.

Six years.

He served Mahavir.

Observed Him.

Learned from Him.

Yet Gurudev points toward the inner failure:

He did not recognize:
“I am ignorant; He is Enlightened.”

Instead, the concern became:

“How can I make a profit of pride?”

So again:

Proximity to Guru ≠ surrender to Guru.

You can be physically extremely close while ego remains untouched. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

This connects strongly with Verse 129:

The physician may be present.

But you still need to follow the regimen and medicine.

27. Open hostility vs hidden hostility

Gurudev then makes a strong distinction.

Goshala openly opposed Mahavir.

People could at least see:

“He is against Him.”

But Gurudev says something can be even more deceptive:

Publicly:

folded hands, apparent respect.

Privately:

jealousy, hostility, resentment.

That is a deeper form of hypocrisy because the external display conceals the opposite internal state. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

The central point is the same:

Outer presentation and inner reality must not become opposites.

28. The four passions are hidden inside this one verse

Near the end Gurudev beautifully connects the verse with the four Kashays:

Krodh — anger

Hostility toward the Jnani.

Maan — ego

Wanting status, superiority, recognition.

Maya — deceit

Projecting an enlightened image while internally deluded.

Lobh — greed/craving

Desire for comfort, luxury, admiration and worldly pleasures.

Gurudev marvels that Param Krupalu Dev has effectively woven all four into this tiny verse. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

29. This verse is a WARNING, not an attempt to scare us

Gurudev explicitly says:

“It's not scaring, it's warning.”

Param Krupalu Dev isn't threatening the seeker.

He is showing:

If you take this road, see where it leads.

The compassionate warning comes before the mistake.

Because once intense karmas are bound, merely regretting afterward may not erase their consequences.

The intensity of later remorse may not equal the intensity with which the karma was originally bound. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

So Gurudev calls this verse:

the pinnacle of Param Krupalu Dev's compassion.

How this connects to the entire Upsamhar

Verse 137

The deepest practical takeaway

I think this verse gives us one extremely powerful self-check:

Is my inner state catching up with my spiritual vocabulary?

Because studying Atmasiddhi itself gives us a lot of language.

We now know words like:

  • Nishchay,
  • Vyavhar,
  • Upadan,
  • Nimit,
  • Atmabhranti,
  • Nijshuddhata,
  • Samkit,
  • Swaroopanusandhan,
  • Atma-bhan,
  • Moksh Marg.

That's beautiful.

But Verse 137 almost immediately asks:

What has actually changed inside?

Am I:

  • less reactive?
  • less attached to comfort?
  • less dependent on recognition?
  • less comparative?
  • less jealous?
  • more ethical?
  • more humble?
  • more receptive to correction?
  • more aware of the Self?
  • more reverential toward genuine spiritual greatness?

Because otherwise I can make a very dangerous mistake:

mistaking my ability to explain spirituality for spiritual growth.

And I think Gurudev's distinction between the genuine Jnani and the imitator is especially powerful:

The imitator wants everyone to see that he is immersed in the Self.

The Jnani simply is immersed in the Self.

One needs the image.

The other doesn't.

So perhaps the safest response to learning more and more Atmasiddhi is not:

“Now I understand so much.”

but:

“Now I have more light in which to examine myself.”

And that is exactly how Gurudev ends this satsang:

Stop.

Examine.

Assess.

Foresee where the tendency will take you.

Especially when you notice:

comparison → jealousy → animosity.

Don't wait for it to become hostility.

If we genuinely love our soul even a little, Gurudev says, we should recognize the direction early and stop. Pasted text(20261116-003420)

That, rather than merely being afraid of becoming a “fake Jnani,” feels like the practical purpose of Verse 137 for us.
