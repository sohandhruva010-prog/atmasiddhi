• We’re in the section on ātmārthī na lakṣaṇ – the traits of a true seeker.
  • Verses 34, 35, 36 = what lives in the ātmārthī’s mind before he even meets a Sadguru:
    • His convictions,
    • His inner criteria,
    • His planned attitude once he finds a Sadguru.
  • Important nuance he stresses:
    • He prefers the word “conviction” over “belief”:
      • Belief becomes right belief only when a Sadguru endorses it (leading to samyak darshan).
      • Until then it’s a deep conviction in the seeker’s heart, not yet stamped by a Sadguru.
  • Verse 34 (yesterday):
    • His conviction about what a Sadguru must be: “Atmajnān tya munipanu” – Where there is Self-realisation, there is saintliness / true monkhood.
    • Self-realisation (Enlightenment) is must.
      • Scriptural knowledge – very good.
      • Renunciation – very good.
      • Monkhood – very good.
      • Austerities – very good.
      • Being family/sect Guru – also good.
      • But all these add; they are not the core.
      • Enlightenment is non-negotiable.
  • Verse 35 (today): Now, having clarified what kind of Guru he wants, this verse shows what kind of devotion he plans to have towards such a Guru, even before he finds Him.
  • It’s like someone thinking, “When I have a wife/husband, I’ll behave like this…” – Here, “when I have a Sadguru, my devotion will be like this.”

2. The verse line: “Pratyaksh Sadguru prāptino ganey param upkār…”

The main words:

  • Pratyaksh Sadguru – A living Enlightened Master (not just a past one, not just an idol).
  • Prāptino – upon attainment, on obtaining.
  • Ganey – he reckons, considers, feels.
  • Param upkār – a supreme benefit, an incomparable gain.

So:

“On attaining a pratyaksh Sadguru, the true seeker considers this a supreme benefit.”

He clarifies:

  • Some translations say “great favour” – he avoids that:
    • If it’s a “favour,” who exactly is “doing the favour”? God? Sadguru? Karma?
    • To avoid confusion, he uses “supremely beneficial”:
      • It is the greatest possible benefit to the seeker.

And:

For him, this benefit is greater than anything or anyone:

  • Greater even than idols of Jinas,
  • Greater than scriptures,
  • Greater than the teacher who taught him scriptures.

He has already made the Sadguru his “Supreme Court”:

  • Not High Court, not Sessions Court:
    • Final authority.
  • For some:
    • Sadguru is the first and last word:
      • After that, no asking others, no roaming around for second opinions.
      • “I will not even listen to myself over Him.”

(He jokes about a Bollywood line from Om Shanti Om – the spirit is:

“I won’t even listen to myself; His word is final.”)

All this is packed into that one phrase: “ganey param upkār.”

3. The second line: “Trane yog ekatvathī varte ajnadhar”

Key phrases:

  • Trane yog – the three “yog” = energies of:
    • Mind (mann) – thoughts, inner intentions.
    • Speech (vachan) – words.
    • Body (kāya) – physical actions.
  • Ekatvathī – with unity, in unison, as one.
  • Varte – he lives, he acts, he behaves.
  • Ajna-dhār – holding/maintaining the command, living in the Sadguru’s ajna:
    • Command,
    • Instruction,
    • For some, “advice” or “guidance” (depending on devotion level).

So he paraphrases:

All my three energies – mind, speech, body – will work together, in one direction, to follow His command.

And he notes:

  • If you’re not yet a disciple, you may call it:
    • “Advice,” “guidance,” “suggestion.”
  • But for a disciple:
    • It is ajna – command.
    • Because for a disciple, Sadguru’s word is not a casual suggestion.

4. Short meaning of verse 35 (as the teacher gives it)

  • A true seeker of the soul:

1. Considers attaining a living Enlightened Mentor as a unique, supremely beneficial opportunity.

  • “Once in a lifetime” type of opportunity.

2. Then he:

  • Surrenders to Him through all three energies:
    • Mind, speech, body,
  • And acts as per His command / guidance, with inner devotion, in every way.

He adds the word “inner” devotion on purpose:

  • Because there is a lot of outer display of devotion:
    • Touching feet,
    • Folding hands,
    • Sweet, respectful language.
  • All good – but:

True devotion means: Does He have a place in your heart, or only in your room/temple?

5. Aspiration is not enough: You need a true Guru… and He must come first

He recaps:

  • A true seeker:
    • Aspires for Self-knowledge / Self-realisation.
    • But that alone is not enough.
  • You also need a true Guru in life:
    • One who blesses you with:

1. Knowledge – conceptual clarity.

2. Awareness – vigilance, so knowledge can be applied at the right moment, not just in notes.

3. Purity – blossoming of virtues:

  • Friendliness (maitri),
  • Appreciation (pramod),
  • Compassion (karunā),
  • Neutrality (madhyasthata), etc.

So:

  • Atmārthī wants Self-realisation, yes.
  • But he realises:

“At this initial stage, where I can easily waver, I need a living Sadguru who constantly blesses me with knowledge, awareness, and purity/virtues.”

6. Two successes: Jnāni ni prāpti & Jñān ni prāpti

He uses a beautiful Gujarati pair:

Jnāni ni prāpti – attaining the Enlightened One (meeting a Jnāni) Jñān ni prāpti – attaining Enlightenment (Self-knowledge)

If you get both, he says, life is “krut-krutya” – complete and successful.

He explains:

1. Jnāni ni prāpti – getting association of a Jnāni:

  • This is due to past merits (puṇya).
  • Strong puṇya:
    • You get an Enlightened Master (Satpurush / Sadguru).
  • Weak puṇya:
    • You get:
      • “Flashy cars, comforts, luxuries,” etc.
  • So:

Strong puṇya → Jnāni. Weak puṇya → fancy possessions.

2. Jñān ni prāpti – attaining Self-realisation:

  • This is due to present self-efforts.
  • Not just karmic fruit:
    • It’s about how you:
      • Surrender,
      • Follow ajna,
      • Internalise and live the teachings.

He notes:

  • In past lives:
    • Many times you met:
      • Omniscient Lords (Tirthankars),
      • Enlightened Masters,
    • But because of lack of proper effort, you missed the chance.

In this era (5th āra):

  • Unfortunately:
    • You cannot meet a Tirthankar directly.
  • Fortunately:
    • You can still meet a living Sadguru (pratyaksh Sadguru).

He emphasises: The verse says “pratyaksh Sadguru”, not just “Sadguru”:

  • Because weak ātmārthī:
    • Will feel more comfortable with:
      • Past Enlightened Masters,
      • Idols and photos,
      • Who never scold, never challenge ego.
  • A strong ātmārthī:
    • Says:

“No, I want a living Sadguru who will challenge my ego and transform me.”

So:

Choosing only past Gurus (for comfort) = sign of a weak ātmārthī. Daring to choose a pratyaksh Sadguru = sign of a strong ātmārthī.

7. Two supreme benefits of a pratyaksh Sadguru (Ambalalbhai’s commentary)

He brings Ambalalbhai’s commentary (checked/endorsed by Param Krupalu Dev) which lists two main reasons why the association of a living Sadguru is “param upkār” (supremely beneficial):

1) All doubts, confusions, curiosities get resolved

  • There are confusions:
    • That scriptures can’t resolve,
    • That Google can’t resolve,
    • That your own mind keeps spinning.
  • In the presence of a pratyaksh Sadguru:
    • These questions quietly clear up:
      • Sometimes by asking directly.
      • Sometimes by just sitting in His aura / darshan.
  • Result:
    • Huge relief and lightness: “Now I know what I have to do with my life.”
    • Even before taking action, just clarity itself feels like half the work is done.

2) Uncontrollable flaws and passions go with slight effort (“alp prayāse jāy”)

  • Things like:
    • Anger, jealousy, comparison, strong likes/dislikes.
  • Previously:
    • You tried hard, yet they stayed.
  • In Sadguru’s association:
    • With small effort, you see:
      • Anger subsiding faster,
      • Jealousy loosening,
      • Old prejudices melting.
  • This is from verse 18’s phrase: “alp prayase jāy” – with slight effort they go.

He says:

  • The joy from this is so deep that:

“Sometimes, I feel I don’t even need Self-realisation;
this freedom itself feels like liberation!”

It’s like:

  • Half-liberation here,
  • The other half when delusion is fully destroyed.

He explains why this happens:

  • We are often unaware of our own faults:
    • We can define “anger, pride, deceit, greed” in theory,
    • But don’t see how they live in our own abilities, activities, achievements.
  • Sadguru can:
    • See our flaws clearly:
      • Their intensity,
      • How deep their roots are,
      • How long it will take to uproot them.
    • He:
      • Draws our attention to them,
      • Warns about pitfalls on “our way” (not just “the path”).
  • Once He points it out:
    • You automatically start:
      • Protecting yourself,
      • Correcting yourself.
    • Then indeed, “with slight effort” many flaws drop away.

Because of these two benefits, the ātmārthī deeply feels why a pratyaksh Sadguru is “param upkār” even before Self-realisation itself.

8. Aulkhan, Ahobhāv, Arpantā – the 3 A’s of relating to Sadguru

He brings in three Gujarati words and checks them with Rita:

1. Aulkhan – Recognition:

  • Recognising the Sadguru by His qualities, not just by:
    • How much you love Him,
    • Or the emotional high you feel.
  • Seeing:
    • Atmajnān,
    • Equanimity,
    • Unique speech,
    • Knowledge,
    • The virtues described in verse 10.

2. Ahobhāv – Reverence:

  • Deep awe, respect, devotion upon recognising His state.

3. Arpantā – Surrender:

  • Offering yourself to Him,
  • Letting His ajna govern your life.

He says:

Remember these three always: Aulkhan – Ahobhāv – Arpantā Recognise – Reverence – Surrender.

Without this progression, devotion stays sentimental and doesn’t become transformational.

9. Trane yog ekatvathī varte ajnadhar – in real life

He now dives deeper into the second line:

  • Trane yog = three energies:
    • Physical – body, outward action,
    • Mental – inner thoughts, intentions,
    • Verbal – spoken words, communication.
  • He purposely does not say “tan, mann, dhan” (body, mind, wealth):
    • In the world, wealth is counted as “power.”
    • But here, wealth is not a spiritual energy.
    • The meaningful energies are:
      • Physical, mental, verbal.

Trane yog ekatvathī – All three energies aligned, in one direction.

Vartē ajnadhar – Living in His ajna, carrying out His commands.

He explains:

  • For a disciple:
    • It’s not suggestion.
    • Sadguru may outwardly say, “If you want, do this or that,” but you must read His eyes:
      • Where is the sparkle?
      • What lights Him up as you speak?
      • There you understand His true wish.
  • With ajna in the heart, discipline becomes natural.

10. Vinay vs avinay: Example of Vishal & Queen Victoria

He illustrates inner devotion & discipline with a concrete example:

  • They were eating together (March 2020).
  • Atmarpit Vishal:
    • Finished eating,
    • Washed his plate before Bapa had finished.
  • He said:
    • “This is avinay (lack of humility/respect).”
    • If you were dining with Queen Victoria:
      • Would you get up and wash your plate before she finishes?
      • Social etiquette says no.
    • Similarly, with Sadguru:
      • Even basic manners reflect inner vinay.

Point:

  • When Sadguru has a place in your heart:
    • You don’t need a list of 33 rules of respect.
    • You simply think:

“He is the Supreme;
how would I automatically behave before someone supreme?”

  • Then:
    • You naturally won’t:
      • Walk ahead of Him,
      • Finish before Him,
      • Behave casually.
  • It shouldn’t be by-heart rules; it should come from the heart.

So:

True devotion = place in the heart → Thoughts, words, actions naturally align with His honour and His ajna.

11. Final synthesis of verse 35

Putting it all together, in the flow of the talk:

  • Before even meeting a Sadguru, the ātmārthī has decided:

1. If I ever attain a pratyaksh Sadguru:

  • I will recognise Him by His qualities (Aulkhan),
  • Feel deep reverence (Ahobhāv),
  • And surrender to Him (Arpantā).

2. I will consider this attainment as: “Param upkār – the supreme benefit of my life,” greater than any other thing or person.

3. Then:

  • With mind, speech, and body together (trane yog ekatvathī),
  • I will live fully in His ajna (varte ajnadhar),
  • Following His commands with inner devotion.
  • With such clarity and inner preparedness:

“When he finally meets a pratyaksh Sadguru and surrenders, the Sadguru’s grace envelopes him, and Enlightenment comes as naturally as leaves to a tree, wings to a bird, and fragrance to a flower.”

That is the devotional mindset and resolve captured by verse 35: how an ātmārthī plans to respond to a Sadguru – long before he actually meets Him.
