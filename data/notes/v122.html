Detailed Notes — Verse 122: “Athva Nijparinam Je, Shuddh Chetnaroop…”
From Non-Doer of Karma to Doer & Enjoyer of Pure Consciousness

This lecture continues directly from Verse 121. In the previous verse, the disciple realised:

As long as delusion prevails, I am the doer and enjoyer of karma. When my attentiveness turns inward toward my own Self, I become the non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.

But that leaves an important question:

If the Soul is no longer the doer or enjoyer of karma, does its doership and enjoyership disappear completely?

No.

Verse 122 completes the picture:

The Soul always remains a doer and enjoyer because activity is its nature. What changes after Self-realisation is what it is the doer and enjoyer of.

In ignorance, it is the doer and enjoyer of impure modifications/karma.

In Enlightenment, it becomes the doer and enjoyer of its own pure modifications — shuddh chetna — in a nirvikalp, thought-transcending state.

1. Where We Are in the Journey

Bapa begins by reminding us of the disciple's entire spiritual position.

His:

  • Only goal: Liberation
  • First target: Self-realisation
  • Instrumental causes/tools: Sadguru, His association, teachings, answers, clarification of doubts, conducive circumstances
  • Effort: Observing the Guru immersed in the Self and becoming inspired to turn inward
  • Result: His tendencies finally flow within and Self-realisation occurs.

And now the disciple is expressing what he has personally experienced.

Verses 119–120

Conviction regarding the First and Second Fundamental Truths:

1. Soul exists.

2. Soul is eternal.

Verse 121

Experiential conviction regarding the Third and Fourth Fundamental Truths:

3. Soul is the doer.

4. Soul is the enjoyer.

Verse 122

The same Third and Fourth Fundamental Truths are understood at an even deeper level:

What exactly is the Enlightened Soul the doer and enjoyer of?
Verse 123

Will move into the disciple's conviction regarding the:

5. Possibility of liberation.

6. Path to liberation.

2. The Words Showing This Is Experience, Not Theory

Bapa again emphasizes something beautiful about these verses.

The disciple is not repeating philosophy.

Certain words reveal direct experience:

Bhasyu — experienced

From the earlier verse.

Avyu — attained

Something actually arose through the Enlightened Mentor's teachings.

Vrutti vahi — my attentiveness flowed Not:

“If someone's attentiveness flows inward, this happens.”

But:

“My attentiveness flowed inward.”

This is autobiographical.

The disciple is saying:

“Gurudev, this happened to me.”

That is why this entire section has such a different quality from the earlier philosophical discussion.

3. Verse 122

The verse is:

Athva nijparinam je,

Shuddh chetnaroop;
Karta bhokta tehno, Nirvikalp swaroop.

Bapa breaks down the words:

Athva

“Or,” “alternatively,” “in other words.”

It means:

What was said in the previous verse can also be understood this way.

Nijparinam
  • Nij = one's own
  • Parinam = modifications

So:

My own modifications.

Shuddh chetnaroop

Of the nature of pure consciousness.

Karta bhokta tehno

I am the doer and enjoyer of those pure modifications.

Nirvikalp swaroop

In an unperturbed state, beyond thought activity.

So the short meaning becomes:

“In other words, I became the doer and enjoyer of my own modifications, which are of the nature of pure consciousness, in a state beyond thought activity.”

4. Why Does Verse 122 Begin With “Athva” — In Other Words?

Because Verse 122 is not contradicting Verse 121.

It is explaining the same reality from the other side.

Verse 121 says:

I became the non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.

Verse 122 says:

I became the doer and enjoyer of my own pure modifications.

These are not two different experiences.

They are the same experience expressed positively and negatively.

Negative expression:

I am not the doer/enjoyer of karma.

Positive expression:

I am the doer/enjoyer of my pure consciousness.

So:

Non-doer of karma ≠ non-doer absolutely.

This distinction is the heart of the lecture.

5. The Core Principle: Doership Never Disappears

This is probably the single most important philosophical point to understand.

The Soul is naturally active.

Therefore, wherever there is activity, there is a doer of that activity.

So even after:

  • Self-realisation,
  • complete purification,
  • liberation,
  • Siddhahood,

the Soul does not become some inactive, lifeless entity.

Doership remains because activity belongs to the nature of the Soul.

What changes is the object of that doership.

Bapa makes a crucial distinction:

Doership itself is the nature of the Soul.

But:

Doership of karma is merely a state of the Soul under ignorance.

The state can change.

The nature does not.

6. The Entire Difference in One Table

State Doer of Enjoyer of Ignorant Soul Impure modifications / Impure modifications / karmic karma experience

Enlightened Soul Pure modifications Pure consciousness / inner bliss

Regarding other Non-doer Non-enjoyer substances

So the transformation is not:

Doer → no doer

It is:

Doer of karma → doer of pure Self

And not:

Enjoyer → no enjoyer

but:

Enjoyer of impurity/karmic states → enjoyer of pure inner bliss.

7. What the Disciple Is Actually Saying to Gurudev

Bapa asks us not to treat this merely as philosophy.

Imagine the disciple speaking directly to Gurudev:

“O Gurudev, by Your grace, Your teachings and Your commands, I channelised my attentiveness toward the Soul.”

“I became antarmukh — Soul-focused.”

“When karmas manifested, I became only their spectator.”

“I did not get involved.”

“I did not harbour impure feelings.”

“Instead, I experienced pure consciousness.”

“I was immersed in an extraordinary inner bliss.”

That makes Verse 122 completely different emotionally.

It is not:

“According to Jain philosophy, the Enlightened Soul is…”

It is:

“Gurudev, this is what I experienced because of You.”

8. “Gurudev, I Became a Spectator”

This carries forward the central experience from Verse 121.

Karmic manifestations may still occur.

But instead of:

karma manifests → I identify → I react → attachment/aversion → new karma

the disciple experiences:

karma manifests → I know it → I remain spectator → no involvement.

He says:

“I just became a spectator.”

No impure reaction.

No compulsive involvement.

No new identification.

And when this outward involvement ceases, what remains?

Pure consciousness.

The energy that was constantly spilling outward into:

  • likes,
  • dislikes,
  • ego,
  • desire,
  • reactions,

is now channelised inward toward the Self.

9. “My Ego and Desires Vanished”

Bapa deliberately uses the word vanished, rather than merely “diminished,” while describing the disciple's immediate experience.

From a technical gunasthanak perspective, subtler passions may still remain.

But that is not what the disciple feels in the moment of direct experience.

In that nirvikalp state:

“I see no ego.”
“I see no desire.”
“I see no impurity.”
“The world has become muted.”

There is no reaction.

No desire.

No karmic doership.

No karmic enjoyership.

Instead:

“I am in nijbhaav — my natural disposition.”
“I am in Atmanand — the bliss of the Self.”

10. Nature vs. Present State — Very Important Bapa makes another crucial distinction.

What is the inherent nature of an ignorant Soul?

Pure.

What is the inherent nature of an Enlightened Soul?

Pure.

The nature of the Soul never became impure.

What differs is the present modification/state.

Ignorant Soul

Nature = pure Present modification = impure

Enlightened Soul

Nature = pure Present modification = pure

This distinction matters tremendously.

Self-realisation is not:

turning an inherently impure Soul into a pure Soul.

Rather, the Soul's modifications become aligned with the purity that was always its inherent nature.

11. A Fundamental Principle: One Substance Cannot Modify Another Substance

Bapa then gives the metaphysical principle underlying the whole verse:

The Soul cannot be the doer or enjoyer of the modifications of another substance.

A substance cannot fundamentally alter another substance.

Therefore:

I cannot truly be the doer of your modifications.

And:

You cannot truly be the cause of my inner modifications.

This is where many ordinary statements become spiritually questionable.

We say:

“You made me angry.”

“Because of you, I'm unhappy.”

“His personality makes my life miserable.”

“She ruined my mood.”

According to the principle being explained here, this cannot be the ultimate truth.

Another person may be an external circumstance.

But:

The Soul is the doer and enjoyer only of its own modifications.

12. This Completely Changes the Meaning of Responsibility

Suppose someone behaves badly toward me.

Normally I say:

“You made me upset.”

The teaching says:

That person's behaviour belongs to their substance/modifications.

My anger belongs to my modification.

Therefore I cannot spiritually place the ownership of my inner state onto them.

This is extremely demanding because it removes one of our favourite escapes:

blaming the outside world for what is happening within.

The other person may have acted wrongly.

That is separate.

But my:

  • anger,
  • hatred,
  • jealousy,
  • attachment,
  • forgiveness,
  • compassion,
  • equanimity,

are my modifications.

And therefore:

I am their doer and enjoyer.

13. Ignorant and Enlightened Souls Follow the Same Principle

Self-realisation does not change the metaphysical law.

The Soul is always the doer and enjoyer of its own modifications.

So:

Ignorant Soul

Produces impure modifications.

Therefore:

Doer and enjoyer of impure feelings.

Enlightened Soul

Produces pure modifications.

Therefore:

Doer and enjoyer of pure feelings/pure consciousness.

Bapa stresses:

A principle remains a principle.

Enlightenment does not suddenly alter the nature of reality.

What changes is the Soul's state.

14. Three Things the Enlightened Soul Is Not the Doer Of

Bapa essentially gives us three categories.

The Enlightened Soul is:

1. Non-doer of the modifications of other substances

Because one substance cannot modify another.

2. Non-doer of impure modifications

Because in the Enlightened state being described, those impure modifications do not arise.

3. Doer of its own pure modifications

This is the positive side.

So the final answer is:

Enlightened Soul = non-doer of the other + non-doer of impurity + doer of pure modifications.

15. Verse 78 Has Now Become the Disciple’s Experience

This is one of the most beautiful connections in the lecture.

Earlier, in Verse 78, Gurudev had taught:

If the Soul is aware of its true nature, it is the doer of its pure nature.

If it is not aware of its true nature, it becomes the doer of karma.

At that time:

Guru spoke.

Disciple listened.

Now:

Disciple has experienced it.

This is why Bapa says it is almost as though the disciple has taken the Guru's teaching into a laboratory and personally verified it.

The disciple can now say:

“Gurudev, what You told me was true. I experienced it.”

16. The Doership Was Not Destroyed — It Was Replaced

This word is very useful.

After Self-realisation:

Doership remains.

Enjoyership remains.

But their objects have been replaced.

Before:

Doership of karma
Enjoyership of karma

After:

Doership of pure consciousness
Enjoyership of pure consciousness

Bapa says the disciple experiences:

“The doership and enjoyership of karma have now been replaced with the doership and enjoyership of the pure modifications of the Soul.”

Those pure modifications are:

Shuddh chetnaroop

And the state is:

Nirvikalp swaroop

17. Why Does Param Krupalu Dev Say Shuddh Chetna, Not Shuddh Chetan?

This is the technical heart of the lecture.

Bapa asks:

Why didn't Param Krupalu Dev simply say:

Shuddh chetan — pure conscious substance?

Why specifically:

Shuddh chetna?

Because:

Chetan

refers to the conscious substance — the Soul itself.

Chetna

here refers to its conscious modification/state.

The Soul — chetan dravya — remains conscious.

But its modifications can be:

  • pure
  • impure

Therefore the verse is specifically describing the disciple's experienced modification/state, not merely stating that the Soul is a conscious substance.

18. Chetan vs. Chetna

Keep this distinction clear:

Chetan

The conscious substance itself — Soul.

Chetna

The modification/sensation/state of that conscious substance.

The conscious substance does not cease being conscious.

But its modifications can vary.

Therefore we have:

Shuddh Chetna

Pure conscious modification.

Ashuddh Chetna

Impure conscious modification.

The Soul remains chetan in both cases.

What changes is its chetna — its modification/state.

19. The Three Types of Chetna

Bapa then introduces a technical classification.

There are three forms relevant here:

1. Karma Chetna

Feeling of doership.

2. Karmafal Chetna

Feeling of enjoyership/endurance.

3. Shuddh Chetna

Pure consciousness.

The first two belong to ashuddh chetna — impure consciousness/modification.

The third is pure.

So:

Ashuddh Chetna
  • Karma Chetna
  • Karmafal Chetna
Shuddh Chetna
  • Pure conscious modification

20. Karma Chetna — “I Do, I Do, I Do”

Karma chetna is:

The feeling of doership regarding the non-Self.

Its language is:

“I did this.”

“I made this happen.”

“Without me, this wouldn't happen.”

“If I don't do it, who will?”

“This entire thing happened because of me.”

The crucial point:

You cannot actually modify another substance.

But:

You can believe that you do.

And that false belief creates the feeling of doership.

That is karma chetna.

21. The Dog and the Cart

Bapa recalls the line from Narsinh Mehta:

“Hu karu, hu karu, e j ajnanta…”

“I do, I do” — that itself is ignorance.

The example:

A cart is moving.

A dog happens to be walking underneath it.

The dog imagines:

“I am carrying the cart.”

But the cart would move regardless.

The dog is merely moving alongside it.

This is the illusion of doership.

We participate in an event and conclude:

“It happened because of me.”

Bapa's point:

Participation does not automatically mean absolute doership.

The constant inner claim:

I did. I do. I will do.

is karma chetna.

22. The Rooster and the Sunrise

Another excellent example comes from Swami Rama Tirtha.

A woman owned a rooster.

Every morning:

1. The sun rose.

2. The rooster crowed.

Because the two happened together repeatedly, she concluded:

“The sun rises because my rooster crows.”

Then pride entered.

She could threaten people:

“If you don't listen to me, I won't let my rooster crow tomorrow — then the sun won't rise!”

Obviously absurd.

But Bapa's point is that we do something similar.

Two things occur together:

I participated + result occurred

and immediately:

“I caused the result.”

The Enlightened understanding is:

You may have been an instrument.

Therefore:

Instead of pride — gratitude.

23. Doership Gives Birth to False Pride

This is one of the strongest practical sections of the lecture.

The sequence becomes:

“I did it”

↓

feeling of doership

↓

false pride

↓

arrogance

↓

jealousy

↓

selfishness

↓

discrimination / likes-dislikes

↓

spiritual downfall

Bapa is careful:

Doership and pride are not exactly the same.

But:

False pride follows the feeling of doership very easily.

Once I believe:

“I made this happen.”

the next thought naturally becomes:

“Look how capable I am.”

Then:

“Has anyone else ever done something this good?”

And ego begins expanding.

24. Why “I Did It” Feels So Natural

Bapa says this habit has existed for so long that it feels instinctive.

Anything happens and immediately:

I.

“I organised it.”

“I fixed it.”

“I thought of it.”

“I helped them.”

“I made this project work.”

“I brought everyone together.”

“I did the seva.”

It almost feels like doership is our inherent nature.

But Bapa distinguishes:

It is not the Soul's inherent pure nature.

It is a deeply ingrained tendency.

Because it has been repeated for so long, it now feels natural.

25. The Need to Feel Important

Why does this doership become so powerful?

Because we want others to see:

“I matter.”

“I am important.”

“Notice me.”

“Recognise what I did.”

“Acknowledge my contribution.”

This enters everything.

Bapa gives ordinary examples of how much human behaviour is shaped by wanting to impress others.

And then gives a beautiful line:

“It's nice to be important; more important to be nice.”

26. The Peacock Example — We Show Only Our Beautiful Side

Bapa gives another powerful introspection tool.

A peacock opens its feathers.

From one side:

  • beautiful colours,
  • extraordinary patterns,
  • magnificent appearance.

But from the other side, it looks much duller.

Similarly:

We show ourselves the side we like.

“I am disciplined.”

“I am hardworking.”

“I am caring.”

“I am a good leader.”

“I am good at seva.”

“I am intelligent.”

These things may genuinely be true.

The problem is not recognising a quality.

The problem is:

seeing only that side.

What about:

  • my anger?
  • my pride?
  • my need for appreciation?
  • my jealousy?
  • my impatience?
  • my selfishness?
  • my defensiveness?

If I saw both sides, pride would have much less space to arise.

27. “The Cooking Is Good” vs. “I Am Good”

This is such a subtle and useful distinction.

Suppose you cook something delicious.

People eat it and say:

“This is excellent.”

That may simply be factually true.

But the ego quietly shifts:

“The cooking is good”

into

“I am good.”

That is the beginning of false pride.

Then:

“I am better.”

Then:

“I know more.”

Then:

“I am the leader.”

Then:

“I can speak however I want.”

Then:

“I like the people who praise me.”

“I dislike the people who correct me.”

And the entire chain of attachment and aversion begins.

Bapa calls this the beginning of the downfall.

28. Check How Often “I” Appears in Your Conversations

Bapa gives a very practical test:

Take a five- or ten-minute conversation and notice:

How many times did I say “I”?

“I did…”

“I said…”

“I thought…”

“I made…”

“I helped…”

“I decided…”

“I didn't receive credit…”

“I wasn't appreciated…”

And then compare:

How many times did “Guru's grace” enter?

Bapa jokingly compares the ignorant tendency to ICICI:

“I see, I see, I…”

Everything revolves around:

I.

“I didn't get credit.”

“I didn't get acknowledgment.”

“I didn't get appreciation.”

Whereas the Enlightened orientation is likened to Dena Bank — dena, to give:

Keep giving.

29. One of the Best Practical Teachings: Do Good Without Worrying Who Gets the Credit

Bapa says:

You can be very good if you don't worry who gets the credit.

You did seva?

Wonderful.

You enjoyed serving?

Wonderful.

Work is complete?

Then:

Over.

But if afterward the mind starts:

“Who received the credit?”

“Did they mention my name?”

“Did Bapa know I did it?”

“Did anyone appreciate me?”

“Why did that person receive recognition?”

then the seva has moved into a very different inner space.

This is where the lecture becomes directly practical.

The external action may still be seva.

But internally, karma chetna may have entered.

30. Karmafal Chetna — “I Am Suffering / I Am Enjoying”

Now comes the second form of impure chetna.

Karma Chetna
“I do.”
Karmafal Chetna
“I experience/endure/enjoy this.”

Its language is:

“I am suffering.”

“I am enjoying.”

“I am miserable because of this.”

“I am tired of you.”

“Your nature is making me suffer.”

So:

Karta-bhaav

Feeling of doership → Karma Chetna

Bhokta-bhaav

Feeling of enjoyership/endurance → Karmafal Chetna

31. “I Am Tired of Your Nature”

This sentence is a perfect example of karmafal chetna.

“I am tired of your nature.”

What are we really saying?

“Your modifications are creating my suffering.”

But according to the principle Bapa explained:

One substance cannot be the doer of another substance's modifications.

So another person's nature may be difficult.

Their behaviour may be unpleasant.

But my inner suffering is still my modification.

The false belief is:

“I suffer because of you.”

That is karmafal chetna.

32. Karma Chetna vs. Karmafal Chetna

A very easy way to remember:

Karma Chetna

I am causing the outside.

“I did this.”

Karmafal Chetna

The outside is causing me.

“You made me suffer.”

Notice how ego operates in both directions.

When something good happens:

“I did it.”

When something bad happens:

“You did it to me.”

That is almost the perfect ego arrangement:

Take ownership of success.

Export ownership of suffering.

Verse 122 cuts through both.

33. Then What Is Shuddh Chetna?

The disciple says:

“I am no longer experiencing myself as karma chetna.”
“I am no longer experiencing myself as karmafal chetna.”

Instead:

“I am experiencing myself as shuddh chetna.”

Meaning:

an undefiled conscious state.

There is no false doership regarding the non-Self.

There is no false enjoyership regarding the non-Self.

There is:

pure conscious manifestation of the Soul.

So from the positive viewpoint:

I am the doer and enjoyer of my pure manifestation.

From the negative viewpoint:

I am the non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.

Same state.

Two ways of expressing it.

34. The Three Chetna States — Simple Summary

1. Karma Chetna

“I do.”

False feeling of doership regarding the non-Self.

2. Karmafal Chetna

“I suffer/enjoy because of this.”

False feeling of enjoyership regarding the non-Self.

3. Shuddh Chetna

Pure consciousness.

No false involvement with the non-Self.

The Soul is the:

doer of its pure modification

and:

enjoyer of its own pure bliss.

35. Now the Final Word: Nirvikalp Swaroop

The verse does not merely say:

“I experienced pure consciousness.”

It describes the quality of that experience:

Nirvikalp Swaroop

Bapa translates it approximately as:

unperturbed nature

or:

without thought activity

But he carefully distinguishes nirvikalp from merely nirvichar.

The disciple's mind has become completely steady in consciousness.

There is:

  • no wandering,
  • no running,
  • no disturbance,
  • no thought activity.

His chitt is stable in chaitanya.

36. The Ignorant State Is Full of Vikalp

In ignorance:

thought after thought after thought.

“I want this.”

“I don't want that.”

“Why did this happen?”

“What will happen tomorrow?”

“Why did he say that?”

“What does she think of me?”

“Did I get enough credit?”

“I am enjoying this.”

“I am suffering.”

“I need more.”

This creates constant instability.

Vikalp → disturbance.

But in the moment of Self-experience:

No vikalp arises.

The consciousness is not running anywhere.

It is steady in itself.

37. Even the Thought “I Am Enjoying the Bliss of the Soul” Is Absent This is perhaps the deepest part of the lecture.

You might imagine Self-realisation like this:

“Wow! I am experiencing the Soul!”

But Bapa says even that thought is absent.

Not:

“I am enjoying the bliss.”

Not:

“I created this beautiful state.”

Not:

“I want this state again.”

Not:

“I was enjoying this state.”

Not even:

“I am the doer and enjoyer of the Soul.”

Because the moment that thought appears, there is again a distinction:

“I” + “the Soul I am enjoying.”

That creates duality.

In nirvikalp experience:

There is no such division.

There is simply the experience.

38. No “I” and Soul — Only Oneness

This is extremely subtle.

Normally:

I am experiencing X.

There is:

1. Me, the experiencer.

2. X, the experienced.

But here the Soul is experiencing itself.

So the thought:

“I am enjoying the Soul”

would itself create an artificial separation.

Bapa therefore says:

No duality.

There is not:

“I” over here

and

“Soul” over there.

There is:

Oneness with pure consciousness.

39. Even Spiritual Desire Becomes Silent

Earlier, desire for liberation was necessary.

The disciple intensely wanted:

“I want Self-realisation.”

That longing drove the entire journey.

But at the actual moment of experience:

“I want Self-realisation”

is also absent.

Even:

“I want to remain in this bliss”

is absent.

Because all wanting is a movement of thought.

In nirvikalp:

wanting stops.

conceptualisation stops.

thought stops.

the experiencer-experienced division stops.

There is simply pure consciousness.

40. The Disciple Revels in the Bliss of the Self

When one attains oneness with the pure, unblemished nature:

He becomes non-doer of karma.

He becomes non-enjoyer of karma.

But positively:

He becomes the doer and enjoyer of pure consciousness.

And:

He revels in the novel bliss of the Soul.

This bliss is described as:

  • beyond the senses,
  • beyond ordinary thought,
  • not dependent upon an external object,
  • arising from the Self itself.

42. Verse 121 vs. Verse 122

Verse 121 asks:

What stopped?

Answer:

Doership and enjoyership of karma.

Verse 122 asks:

What remained?

Answer:

Doership and enjoyership of pure consciousness.

So:

Verse 121 = freedom from karma.

Verse 122 = abidance in pure consciousness.

That distinction makes these two verses fit beautifully together.

43. An Even Deeper Connection With Verse 115

Remember Verse 115:

When false identification ceases, you are no longer the doer of karma nor the endurer of its fruits — this is the essence of religion.

Then Verse 121:

“Gurudev, my attention actually turned inward and I experienced myself as non-doer/non-enjoyer of karma.”

And now Verse 122:

“Gurudev, I realised that I did not become inactive. I became the doer and enjoyer of my own pure consciousness.”

So these teachings keep converging on one point:

Stop identifying with the non-Self and abide in what you actually are.

44. The Practical Teaching: Catch “I Did It”

One of the easiest ways to bring this lecture into daily life is to notice:

How quickly does “I did it” arise?

You organise something successfully.

Immediately:

“I did it.”

Someone appreciates the event.

“They should know how much I did.”

Someone else gets praised.

“But that was my idea.”

Someone forgets your contribution.

“Nobody appreciates me.”

That is exactly where the teaching becomes alive.

The issue is not:

Stop working.

It is:

Observe the feeling of doership entering the work.

45. Especially in Seva

This is particularly powerful in seva.

Externally:

I served.

Internally, two completely different things can happen.

One state:

“I did this.”

“I worked harder than everyone.”

“Did Bapa know?”

“Did Nemiji know?”

“Why was that person appreciated?”

“Why didn't anyone mention me?”

That is the territory of karma chetna.

Another state:

I received the opportunity.

I gave what I could.

The work happened.

I enjoyed serving.

Over.

No need to construct an identity around it.

Bapa's line captures it beautifully:

If you don't worry about who gets the credit, you can be very good.

46. Catch “You Made Me Feel…”

The second practical application is karmafal chetna.

Notice:

“You made me angry.”

“You ruined my mood.”

“Because of you I can't be peaceful.”

“Your behaviour makes me miserable.”

The lecture asks us to distinguish:

Their behaviour = their modification.

My reaction = my modification.

That does not mean approving of harmful behaviour.

It means refusing to surrender ownership of my inner state.

That is a profound form of spiritual responsibility.

47. The Two Great Ego Statements

The entire technical discussion can almost be reduced to two statements:

Karma Chetna
“Because of me, this happened.”
Karmafal Chetna
“Because of you, this happened to me.”

And the movement toward shuddh chetna is:

Neither.

I remain aware of my own consciousness and my own modifications.

48. The Role of the Sadguru in This Transformation

The disciple never presents this awakening as his personal achievement.

He says:

“By Your grace…”
“By Your teachings…”
“By Your commands…”
“I channelised my attention toward the Soul.”

That is extremely significant because the lecture simultaneously destroys doership while describing the disciple's greatest spiritual accomplishment.

He does not say:

“Look what I achieved.”

He says:

“Gurudev, what You taught me has become my experience.”

That is the devotional beauty underneath the entire metaphysics.

49. Guru’s Teaching → Disciple’s Experience

The entire path can be seen as:

Guru teaches:

If unaware of Self → doer of karma.

Disciple listens.

Disciple contemplates.

Disciple follows the Guru's ajna.

Attention turns inward.

Disciple experiences pure consciousness.

Karma chetna falls.

Karmafal chetna falls.

Shuddh chetna manifests.

Disciple returns to Guru:
“Gurudev, I experienced exactly what You had taught.”

That is why Bapa says the worthy disciple has gained:

Experiential conviction of the Guru's teachings.

52. Important Distinctions for the Quiz / Revision

Is the Enlightened Soul a non-doer?

Not absolutely.

It is a:

non-doer of karma

but:

doer of pure modifications.

Does doership disappear in liberation?

No.

Doership belongs to the active nature of the Soul.

What disappears?

Doership of karma / impurity.

Can the Soul be the doer of another substance's modification?

No.

It can only be the doer/enjoyer of its own modifications.

What is the ignorant Soul the doer/enjoyer of?

Impure modifications.

What is the Enlightened Soul the doer/enjoyer of?

Pure modifications / pure consciousness.

What is Karma Chetna?
Feeling of doership — “I do.”
What is Karmafal Chetna?
Feeling of enjoyership/endurance — “I suffer/enjoy.”
What is Shuddh Chetna?

Pure, undefiled conscious modification.

What is Nirvikalp?

A state beyond thought activity where even the thought “I am enjoying the bliss of the Soul” does not arise.

53. The Deepest Practical Introspection From This Lecture There are really three things to catch throughout the day:

1. “I did.”

Doership.

Someone praises something:

Do I immediately claim it?

2. “You made me feel.”

Enjoyership/endurance.

Something unpleasant happens:

Do I immediately make someone else responsible for my inner state?

3. “Who gets the credit?”

Pride.

After doing something good:

Can the work simply be complete?

This lecture is technical on the surface, but these three questions make it intensely practical.

54. The Spiritual Shift in One Sentence

Before Self-realisation:

“I am changing the world, and the world is changing me.”

After the understanding presented here:

“I am the doer and enjoyer only of my own modifications.”

And in the disciple's direct Enlightened experience:

“My modifications became pure consciousness itself.”

That is an enormous shift.

55. The Beauty of Nirvikalp

The highest point of this lecture is not merely:

“Don't take credit.”

That is still a preparatory practical teaching.

The actual experience described by the verse goes much deeper.

There comes a moment where even:

“I am being humble.”

is gone.

“I am witnessing.”

is gone.

“I am enjoying the Soul.”

is gone.

“I have attained something.”

is gone.

“I want to remain here.”

is gone.

Even the spiritual “I” becomes silent.

There is simply pure consciousness experiencing itself.

That is why the final word:

Nirvikalp Swaroop

is so important.

56. Verse 122 in the Disciple’s Voice The deepest way to understand the verse may be to hear it as the disciple speaking:

“Gurudev, when I followed Your teachings and my attention turned within, I realised that I had not become inactive. The doership and enjoyership remained, because they belong to the nature of the Soul. But they had completely changed. I was no longer the doer and enjoyer of karma. I was simply the doer and enjoyer of my own pure conscious modification. I became a spectator of karmic manifestations. I saw no impurity, no ego, no desire, no reaction. I was immersed in the bliss of the Self. And in that experience, even the thought ‘I am enjoying this bliss’ was absent. There was no ‘I’ separate from the Soul — only pure consciousness, nirvikalp.”

That is the experiential heart of this lecture.

57. Connection Across Verses 120–122

This progression is beautiful:

Verse 120 — Who am I?

I experienced myself as pure consciousness, eternal and distinct from the body.

Verse 121 — What happens to karma when I turn within?

I become non-doer and non-enjoyer of karma.

Verse 122 — Then what am I the doer and enjoyer of?

My own pure conscious modifications, in a nirvikalp state.

So:

120 = Identity
Who am I?
121 = Freedom
What am I no longer bound up in?
122 = Pure functioning
What remains when impurity disappears?

And that prepares the disciple to move into the Fifth and Sixth Fundamental Truths in Verse 123.

Key Lines to Remember

Non-doer does not mean non-doer absolutely; it means non-doer of karma.

Doership and enjoyership are the nature of the Soul; doership and enjoyership of karma are states under ignorance.

Ignorant Soul → doer and enjoyer of impure modifications.

Enlightened Soul → doer and enjoyer of pure modifications.

The Soul cannot be the doer or enjoyer of the modifications of another substance.

Karma Chetna = “I do.”
Karmafal Chetna = “I suffer/enjoy.”

Shuddh Chetna = pure, undefiled conscious modification.

Participation does not mean absolute doership — the dog walking beneath the cart is not carrying the cart.

Doership easily becomes the foundation of false pride.

Do the seva; enjoy the seva; don't worry who receives the credit.

“Because of me this happened” = Karma Chetna.

“Because of you I am suffering” = Karmafal Chetna.

The disciple no longer experiences either; he experiences Shuddh Chetna.

Nirvikalp means even “I am enjoying the bliss of the Soul” is absent.

There is no separate ‘I’ enjoying the Soul — there is oneness with pure consciousness.

What Gurudev taught in Verse 78 has now become the disciple's own experience.

One-Line Essence Verse 122 is the disciple telling Gurudev: “When, through Your grace and teachings, my attention turned inward, I discovered that I did not become inactive — the Soul can never lose its natural doership and enjoyership. What disappeared was my doership and enjoyership of karma. Instead, I experienced myself as the doer and enjoyer of my own pure conscious modification — shuddh chetna — so deeply absorbed in the Self that even the thought ‘I am enjoying this bliss’ disappeared. There was no ego, no desire, no reaction and no separation between ‘me’ and the Soul — only the nirvikalp experience of pure consciousness.”
