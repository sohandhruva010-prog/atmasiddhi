Atmasiddhi Śāstra — Verse 17

“Samkit tene bhakhyu, karan gani pratyakṣa” Detailed, flowing notes from the talk transcript you provided

0) Where the talk picks up

  • Question posed: What state does one attain when one destroys swachhand (self-will) by taking refuge in a pratyakṣa Sadguru and acting strictly per His intent (āśay / ājñā)?
  • Answer in one line (Verse 17): Negative: “Swachhand, mat, āgrah—taji (give them up).” Positive: “Varte Sadguru-lakṣh” — live exactly as per the Sadguru’s guidance. Result: Samkit (Right Belief) — not ‘will lead to’, but ‘is’ samkit, says Bhagavān, because this state is the direct, obvious cause (karan gani) of Self-experience (pratyakṣa).

1) The groundwork (revisiting the previous verse)

  • Non-negotiable clarity: If you want Self-realisation, you must uproot ego and its attendants (desire, attachment, lust, etc.). These consume energy and keep the mind obsessive and self-referential (“I, and what I want”).
  • Seeing rightly: As long as you look at reality through self-will (swachhand), you kindle krodh-mān-māyā-lobh (anger, pride, deceit, greed). Remedy: Learn to see through the Guru’s eyes—as He guides, as He directs.

2) The Negative Triad to renounce (defined exactly as in the talk)

“Swachhand, mat, āgrah taji…” — give these up first.

1. Swachhand — self-willed behaviour/attitude. Even if you don’t outwardly go against the Guru, an inner stance of “I would have decided otherwise if I could” is still swachhand.

2. Mat — your opinions/viewpoints, and giving them undue importance (a bias that won’t consider other angles).

3. Āgrah — insisting on your viewpoint; obstinacy (jidd). With āgrah, even 100 talks won’t penetrate; you just “warm the seat.” It wastes merit and harms your spiritual welfare.

Bottom line: These three have hurt your spiritual progress immensely; they must be renounced (taji/tajo).

3) The Positive Turn: one rule of life

“Varte Sadguru-lakṣh” — live in alignment with the Sadguru’s intent/command (ājñā).

  • In practice this means: Faith: “My Sadguru is right.” Love: “I want to act as He guides.” Surrender: “Whatever comes in the way, I will give it up in obeying His ājñā.”

If these feelings—faith, love, and surrendership—are strong in you (even if progress feels small), the Lord calls this Samkit (Right Belief). Not “it will lead to samkit”—it is samkit.

He uses two precise phrases:

  • Achal prem — unwavering love.
  • Samyak pratīti — right faith (sound conviction).

4) Why alignment is harder than leading (the talk’s analogies)

  • Follower vs. leader (car analogy): “Follow my car”: the follower must stay utterly focused—words, meaning, intention. A truck/lorry can block view; the follower stresses to keep up. Lesson: Following a Guru demands attention, humility, and exactness.
  • Game: “Change Leader Change” (Lille, France): When the den-giver looks away, the leader casually changes actions; followers feel the heat—they must match within seconds and not get caught. Lesson: Being a follower requires alertness and agility; it’s not passive at all.
  • Light interludes (food/lunch jokes): The humour underscores a serious point: our likes/dislikes (even about food) reveal opinionatedness and insistence—the very mat and āgrah we must catch and relax.

5) The doorway virtue: Vinay (humility/reverence)

  • Because swachhand is strong, we lack vinay for the Sadguru.
  • Without vinay, the whole inner chain won’t arise: prem (love), śraddhā (faith), arpanta (surrender).
  • Guideline: When your noisy mind says, “This seems right to me,” ask: Which holds priority—my opinion or the Guru’s intent? The Enlightened have said: This path requires vinay.

6) Putting the knife where it hurts (clear self-diagnosis)

  • Swachhand — “If I were allowed, I’d decide differently.” Notice the attitude, not only behaviour.
  • Mat — over-valuing your own perspective; unwilling to see others’ sides.
  • Āgrah — stubborn insistence; you want others to agree with you too.
  • Effect: Massive spiritual harm.
  • Immediate prescription: Taji! — Renounce them now, and “varte Sadguru-lakṣh.” Direct the mind to obey; this softens the heart and melts the ego.

Practical mantra: Understand and surrender. If understanding lags, surrender first (without abandoning the effort to understand).

7) Declaring the result: This state itself is Samkit

“Samkit tene bhakhyu” — Bhagavān Himself calls this state Samkit (Right Belief).

  • You may say, “But I haven’t had Self-realisation yet!” The Lord replies: entertaining these feelings (faith, love, surrender to Sadguru’s ājñā) is Samkit.
Two kinds of Samkit (as taught in the talk)
  • VS — Vyavahār Samkit: Faith in the Sadguru (you trust Him and live by His ājñā).
  • NS — Niścaya Samkit: Faith in the Self born of direct experience (nirvikalp Self-experience; no-thought state).
Key relation:

VS is the immediate, direct cause of NS. In logic, you can sometimes place the effect in the cause—if the seed is sown, you can already count the fruit.

  • Hence, Bhagavān uses the word “Samkit” for this cause-state (VS) because NS is bound to come. “Whether today, 2 lives, 5 lives, or 25—it’s inevitable once the cause is alive.”
Memorables (from the talk’s quick map):
  • Verse for VS (Right Belief as faith in Sadguru): 17
  • Verse for NS (Right Belief as Self-experience): 110
  • Verse connected with Omniscience: 113

8) A crisp reading of the verse (as explained)

  • Negative clause: Swachhand, mat, āgrah—taji.
  • Positive clause: Varte Sadguru-lakṣh.
  • Judgment: Samkit tene bhakhyu.
  • Reason: Karan gani pratyakṣa. → Because this state is the obvious cause of direct Self-experience, the Omniscient, Dispassionate Lord calls it Samkit.

9) “By imagination? Impossible.”

  • The talk insists: You cannot reach the Goal by imagination; only by a living Sadguru’s guidance (pratyakṣa Sadguru).
  • So: stop DIY spirituality that keeps you center-stage; shift to obedience (prem-śraddhā-arpanta).

10) Handholds you can use today

Self-check (daily, 3 minutes):

1. Where did swachhand surface (attitude or act)?

2. Which opinion (mat) did I over-weight?

3. Where did I insist (āgrah) and close the door to learning?

One rule to practice:

Before acting on a charged issue, pause and ask: “What is the Sadguru’s ājñā here?” Then do that, even if ego resists.

Two feelings to stoke:
  • Achal prem — “I will not let love wobble when my preferences are crossed.”
  • Samyak pratīti — “I will anchor in conviction, not moods.”
Short prayer (aligned with the talk):

“May I see through my Guru’s eyes;
may my opinions relax;
may obedience melt my ego.”

11) Glossary (as heard in the talk)

  • Swachhand — self-willed attitude/behaviour (“I’ll decide my way”).
  • Mat — personal viewpoint; giving it undue importance.
  • Āgrah — obstinate insistence; trying to make others accept your view.
  • Vinay — humility/reverence toward the Guru.
  • Samkit — Right Belief (Right Vision).
    • VS (Vyavahār Samkit): Faith in Sadguru → living by ājñā.
    • NS (Niścaya Samkit): Faith in Self springing from direct Self-experience (nirvikalp).
  • Karan gani pratyakṣa — “knowing it as the immediate cause of the direct experience.”
  • Krodh-mān-māyā-lobh — anger, pride, deceit, greed (the four kashāyas/passions).

12) The talk’s spirit, in a nutshell

Give up self-will, opinionatedness, and insistence. Obey the Enlightened Mentor with unwavering love and right faith. This very state is Samkit, because it is the seed that unfailingly bears the fruit of direct Self-experience.

Selected touchstones from the talk (short quotes/paraphrases)
  • “By imagination, impossible; only by pratyakṣa Sadguru’s guidance.”
  • “Follower must be focused; leadership is easy—obedience is hard.”
  • “If these feelings (faith-love-surrender) are alive in you, Bhagavān calls it Samkit.”
  • “Place the effect in the cause—if the cause is present, the effect must arrive.”
