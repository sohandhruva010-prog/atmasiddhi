Core insight. Today’s session draws us nearer to the radiant presence of Param Krupalu Dev – Shrimad Rajchandraji, the composer of Atmasiddhi Shastra, and the compassionate circumstances of its creation.

1) From Ignorance to Awakening

The discourse begins with the root cause of suffering — ignorance (ajñān). Because of ignorance, desires arise; from desires emerge disturbances: hope and despair.

Hope brightens the heart, despair breaks it.

This oscillation between hope and despair keeps humanity bound in restlessness — across all latitudes and all times. The problem isn’t the world; it’s our ignorance and misplaced dependence.

When mistakes occur, the conscience bites, and under its whip we lose hope and faith. The heart droops, tired and weary.

The Master identifies this drooping heart as the target of compassion — it must be encouraged and rekindled by an Enlightened Soul.

2) The Role of the Enlightened Ones

Who can awaken the ignorant? Certainly not the sleeping. Only those who are awake can awaken others.

“The task to awaken us from the deep slumber of ignorance falls upon the Enlightened Souls.”

Through Their aura, guidance, and words of truth, the Enlightened Ones have transformed countless souls. Their mission is to pull seekers out of delusion and guide them to Self-awareness.

Two things happen to a seeker who awakens from ignorance: 1. Freedom from transmigration – end of the cycle of birth and death.

2. Becomes a lighthouse for others – an inspiration and guide.

Thus, awakening is not a casual event but a spiritual craft, and the world is blessed with a few master craftsmen – the Enlightened Ones.

3) The Supremely Benevolent Craftsman – Shrimad Rajchandra

Amongst such awakened Masters stands Shrimad Rajchandra, lovingly called Param Krupalu Dev — wisdom incarnate, compassion embodied, and a creator of a spiritual era (Yugpurush).

From this point onward, the speaker refers to Him as Param Krupalu Dev, the One who holds an everlasting place in our hearts. His life is described as a constant and ceaseless pursuit of spirituality, continuously abiding in the awareness of the Self.

His inner state:

  • Free from all attachment to objects, wealth, fame, or position.
  • Detached even from His own body.
  • Absorbed constantly in His pure consciousness.
  • Nothing — no activity or circumstance — could drag Him from that awareness.

“Constantly and continuously absorbed in His Pure Self.”

His writings reflect this sublime state — filled with spirituality and beneficial to all true seekers (atmārthis).

4) His Nectar-like Writings

Param Krupalu Dev’s writings are known as Vachanamrut — Vachan (words) + Amrut (nectar). They are the nectar-like expressions of enlightenment — gentle, profound, and transformational.

They serve as motivational and practical guides for aspirants walking toward Self-realisation and liberation.

5) The Enlightened Soul and Guide

Before discussing His work, the speaker emphasises that Param Krupalu Dev was first and foremost an Enlightened Soul — a Sadguru, leading others to divine experience.

He didn’t merely teach about liberation; He ensured that seekers attained it through His living presence and compassionate guidance.

“He led them to divine experience.”

His message echoed the essence of the Tirthankar Bhagwans’ teachings — He preached nothing new, but distilled the eternal essence of truth for a new generation.

6) Compassion – The Source of the Shastra

His compassion compelled Him to share His realisations with the world. It was not ambition, but outpouring compassion that inspired Him to compose Atmasiddhi Shastra — a timeless masterpiece and concise scripture revealing the path to Self-realisation.

“His compassion compelled Him to share His spiritual achievements with aspirants of Self- realisation.”

Atmasiddhi Shastra is a rare jewel, written in Gujarati, that offers complete clarity on the six fundamentals of the soul (shatpada):

1. The soul exists

2. It is eternal

3. It is the doer of karma

4. It is the enjoyer of karma

5. Liberation exists

6. There is a path to liberation

7) The Occasion and Setting

The text was composed in Nadiad, on Aso Vad Ekam, in the Gujarati year V.S. 1952, corresponding to 22nd October 1896.

It was written in a single sitting of 1½–2 hours — a moment of divine outpouring. Every verse (gāthā) is compact yet profound, capturing the philosophical depth of ages.

“In a single sitting of two hours, at the age of twenty-nine, He composed this beacon of light.”

Why this date?

He chose Aso Vad Ekam, the birth anniversary of Lalluji Muni, one of His dearest disciples — not Sharad Purnima, not any grand occasion, but a day sanctified by devotion and discipleship.

8) The Worthy Receivers

Three devoted seekers were instrumental in bringing this scripture from His heart to humanity:

1. Saubhagbhai of Sayla – whose request led to the composition.

2. Ambalalbhai of Khambhat – who was present with a lantern during the composition.

3. Lalluji Muni (Laghuraj Swami) – for whom the day was chosen; it was his birthday.

Each was deeply surrendered — embodying devotion, obedience, and purity of heart.

“Saubhag’s request, Ambalal’s lantern, and Lalluji’s birthday — all three woven into this divine creation.”

9) Seeds of Atmasiddhi – The Six Fundamentals Letter

Before Atmasiddhi Shastra, Param Krupalu Dev wrote ‘Chha Padano Patra’ (The Letter on Six Fundamentals) in 1894 (V.S. 1950) to Lalluji Muni in Surat. This letter became the blueprint for the later composition.

Saubhagbhai struggled to memorise the letter as it was in prose and requested that it be rendered in poetic form. Out of compassion, two years later, Param Krupalu Dev composed Atmasiddhi Shastra — transforming prose into verse and vision.

10) The Rare Devotion of the Three Disciples

The three disciples recognised that the Master they met “does not belong to this world.” They surrendered their lives to His command (ājñā) — not for nine months, but for life.

The speaker offered three prostrations — one each for Saubhagbhai, Ambalalbhai, and Lalluji Muni, symbolising humility, devotion, and service.

“O Lord, I seek not knowledge but pure devotion.”

The flood of devotion that filled their hearts mirrors the ideal of Self-pursuit and Self-realisation enshrined in Atmasiddhi Shastra.

11) Significance of the Work

Atmasiddhi Shastra is a magnum opus, a concise scripture that presents the path to liberation in a lucid, logical, and scientifically structured way.

It can: - Begin the seeker’s journey,

  • Guide and inspect progress throughout, and
  • Reward the aspirant with Self-realisation (Samkit).

“It helps begin the journey, guides through it, and ultimately leads to liberation.”

The speaker expresses deep gratitude: generations to come will remain indebted to this Enlightened Soul for gifting such clarity and compassion.

12) A Personal Offering of Gratitude

The speaker acknowledges feeling unworthy — “beast-like, ignorant, foolish, materialistic” — yet profoundly grateful that Param Krupalu Dev offered such a masterpiece even to the unrefined hearts of this world.

“He has gifted Atmasiddhi Shastra to people like us — ignorant and unrestrained — out of sheer compassion.”

Through this Shastra, we are invited to rise above our animal instincts and rediscover our divine potential.
