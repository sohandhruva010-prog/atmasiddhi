Topic: Designated abodes (heaven/hell etc.) exist for karmic fruition — by “dravya swabhav” (self-nature of substances); the subject is very deep

1) Why this verse comes now (context + the disciple’s doubt)

What was already settled in Verses 82–85

  • The soul is the receiver of the fruits of actions (karma-fal).
  • God is not necessary as the fruit-giver (karmas fructify by their own nature and then get exhausted).

The disciple’s remaining worry (from Verse 81)

He had said: If God is not established, then:

1. No law/order will prevail in the world (jagat-niyam problem)

2. No designated places (bhogyasthan) like heaven and hell, where karmic results can be experienced.

So he’s basically asking:

  • “Fine, the soul receives fruits… but where does it receive them?”
  • “If there are rewards/punishments of huge magnitude, do abodes exist for them?”

The teacher says: Even if you’re not interested in “who created heaven/hell,” you should be interested in the concept of designated places, because otherwise your understanding of receivership stays incomplete.

2) Verse 86 — The line + word meanings (as explained)

Verse phrase (core)

“Te te bhogya visheshna sthanak dravya swabhav, gahan vaat chhe.”

Breakdown:

  • Te te = “those and those / respective”
  • Bhogya visheshna = specific kinds of experiences/fruits to be undergone (the “type of fruition”)
  • Sthanak = designated places/abodes
  • Dravya swabhav = by the inherent nature of substances (self-nature)
  • Gahan vaat chhe = this subject is very deep, intricate, difficult to grasp

The essential meaning

  • To experience different types of karmic fruits, there are corresponding designated abodes.
  • These abodes exist due to the inherent nature of substances (dravya swabhav) — not because some external “creator” must actively build them.
  • Full mechanics of “how exactly” is very deep and not the main topic of Atmasiddhi Shastra; here, you’re being given what you need for the present conclusion.

3) What the Guru is doing in this verse (function of Verse 86)

He is “winding up” the karma section

He explicitly says:

  • This is the last verse where he’s primarily wrapping karma-related discussion.
  • Next fundamentals move toward:
    • Liberation exists
    • A path to liberation exists

So Verse 86 is like:

  • “Yes, designated places exist.”
  • “No, I won’t unpack the entire cosmology here.”
  • “Know that the karma territory is vast; don’t become overconfident.”

4) “Gahan vaat chhe” — Why he keeps saying the subject is deep

He gives two reasons why it’s not explained in full detail here:

Reason 1: It’s genuinely vast and difficult (Karnanuyog / metaphysics)

He says this is part of Karnanuyog (karmic metaphysics) — extremely deep.

  • He mentions the Karmagranths (scriptures on karma philosophy):
    • The basics you learned (bhaavkarma/dravya karma) are pre-entry basics.
    • There are six Karmagranths, and even the later ones are mentally exhausting and subtle.
    • He shares that even for him, later Karmagranths took repeated study to comprehend.

✅ Point: Atmasiddhi is not trying to be a full encyclopedia of karmic cosmology. It gives the essential logic needed for liberation.

Reason 2: Heaven/hell details are not “relevant to the present proof”

At this point, the question is:

  • “Is receivership possible?” Not:
  • “How many heavens exist?”
  • “How many hells exist?”
  • “What exact kinds of pain exist there?”
  • “Minute-to-minute scheduling of karmic programs”

So he says: if you want the expanded map, go to other scriptures (he specifically mentions Tattvartha Sutra as a source for deeper detail later in the talk).

5) A huge practical pivot: not only actions—intentions, words, thoughts also bind

He uses this verse to emphasize something very practical and sobering:

Key teaching

  • All acts have consequences.
  • Not only actions—intentions also carry seeds of merit/demerit.
  • Not only “doing”:

1. what you do (deed)

2. what you say (speech)

3. what you think/aspire (thought) …all leave a mark on the soul.

He intensifies it further:

  • Even thoughts in dreams leave impressions.

✅ The message is: Don’t think karma is only about outward behavior. Inner life matters heavily.

6) “Don’t act over-smart” — warning against shallow conclusions

He calls out the tendency to say:

  • “It doesn’t seem karmas can do all this…”

And he responds:

  • With limited intelligence and limited study, it’s not wise to draw sweeping conclusions about the soul and universe.

This isn’t meant as insult—he’s saying:

  • The system is massive (innumerable souls × constant activity → vast karmic diversity).
  • So humility + study is required.

7) Why “designated places” are logically needed (his event-venue analogy)

He builds a very intuitive point:

If there are countless karmic fruits…

…there must be “space/abodes” where they can be undergone.

Example: Pratishtha Mahotsav logistics

He compares it to organizing a large religious event:

  • If you invite 10,000–30,000 people, you must arrange:
    • places to stay
    • dining areas
    • tents (tambu)
    • separate zones for different functions (buffet vs sit-down dining)

He even mentions how they were already planning:

  • one dining arrangement “on the hill / behind the yajnasthal” etc.

What it proves: For large-scale fruition, designated spaces are necessary—like real-life event planning requires venues.

8) “You can’t be hanged at the Taj” — the strongest designated-place illustration

He gives a blunt legal-life example:

  • If someone must be punished (hanged unto death), it happens in a designated place (like jail).
  • You cannot demand:
    • “Hang me at the President’s house”
    • “Hang me at the Taj / Oberoi”
  • There are places meant for certain outcomes.

What it proves: To experience specific consequences, you must be in an environment suited for that kind of consequence. That’s the common-sense structure behind bhogyasthan.

9) The four forms of existence (the “map” he uses to answer heaven/hell)

He frames it as the four gatis (four destinies):

  • Dev = celestial beings (heavenly abodes)
  • Narki = hellish beings (hell abodes)
  • Manushya = human
  • Tiryanch = animals (and broader non-human births)

He notes: be consistent with language, but the idea is:

  • Heaven, Hell, Human, Animal.

The logic he gives (very important)

Your dominant inner pattern (bhaav) + karmic stock leads to corresponding destiny:

1. Extremely auspicious feelings (extreme shubh bhaav) → upward destiny (celestial/heaven)

2. Extremely inauspicious feelings (extreme ashubh bhaav) → downward destiny (hell)

3. Mixed / mild (a blend, not extreme) → middle realm (human/animal range)

He makes it very “human-intuitive”:

  • People naturally gesture up for good and down for bad.
  • Mixed naturally sits “in the middle.”

10) Urdhvalok / Adholok / Madhyalok — he teaches this through yoga language

This section is half-humor, half-memory tool.

He connects the “three loks” to familiar directional words:

  • Adho = downward → Adholok (lower world / hell realms)
  • Urdhva = upward → Urdhvalok (upper world / heavens)
  • Middle → Madhyalok (middle world — where we are)

Yoga anecdote (Dr. Mansi + “rest in adho”)

He jokes about how yoga instructors say:

  • “Now rest in adho…” (and people feel exhausted)
  • and how it’s harder for someone with a big belly, etc.

What it proves: Not a metaphysical proof—this is a mnemonic: Adho = down (hell), Urdhva = up (heavens), Madhya = middle (our realm).

11) Two classic questions people ask (he explicitly lists them)

He says: once you accept designated places, two questions naturally arise:

1. Who takes the soul there? If someone must go to “12th heaven,” how does the soul travel there?

2. Who created those abodes? Heaven/hell—how do such places exist?

He says: both answers are packed into “dravya swabhav” (in a compressed way).

12) Question 1: How does the soul “go” to the right abode?

His answer

  • The soul goes on its own, under the influence of karmas.
  • Karma is like the navigator/guide that pulls the soul in the correct direction.
Example: Going to America (flight analogy)

He asks:

  • How do you go to America? Usually by aeroplane.
  • You take a flight, and your airline/route determines where you end up.

Then he maps it:

  • Under karmic influence, you “end up taking the flight” that lands you in the correct destination.

✅ Meaning: No external carrier is needed; karma itself becomes the directing force.

“48 minutes before death” detail

He adds a striking traditional claim:

  • It’s said that about 48 minutes before leaving the body, one’s feelings begin to trend toward the next destiny.
  • So the last phase of inner state can hint where the soul is going.

He gives examples:

  • If someone is absorbed in divine name/remembrance → suggests “good class flight” (celestial).
  • If someone is obsessing only about water/food/medicine → suggests lower births (animal-like fixation).

He points to Tattvartha Sutra for detailed mapping of who goes where.

13) A very practical caution: “Don’t just pray for service in every birth…”

He says:

  • People pray: “May I serve you in every birth.”
  • But service (as understood spiritually) is realistically possible in human birth.

So check:

  • Are your thoughts/deeds such that you will even get human birth again?

✅ This ties back to the “every thought leaves a mark” warning.

14) Question 2: Who created heaven/hell? How can such abodes exist?

His short answer (as far as this text goes)

  • Sthanak (designated places) exist to match types of fruition.
  • Their existence is due to dravya swabhav — inherent nature of substances.
  • The arrangement is beginningless (“since time immemorial”).

He acknowledges:

  • This is not a full cosmology lecture.
  • It’s a glimpse, because the main focus is receivership and liberation.

His deeper hint: soul + matter association creates endless modifications

He says:

  • Soul and matter both have limitless power.
  • When jiva (soul) and pudgal (matter) associate, there arise:
    • different modifications in the soul (feelings states)
    • different modifications in the universe (structures/environments)

This is his “meta-explanation”:

  • Realities and abodes can emerge as natural modifications, not necessarily as a deliberate construction project by a deity.

15) “Combination creates new things” — his everyday modification examples

To help you feel what “dravya swabhav / modification power” means, he gives a long chain of examples:

A) Atoms combine → new phenomena arise

He points out:

  • Matter breaks and recombines → new forms keep arising.
  • New diseases appear over time.
Example: AIDS (new disease example)

He says:

  • In earlier school years, they hadn’t even heard of AIDS.
  • Later it emerged; combinations change; new outcomes appear.

Point: Matter’s modifications can produce new realities without a conscious “designer” deciding every detail.

B) Juice combinations — playful but meaningful

He reminisces:

  • Earlier options were few (orange, sweet-lime, watermelon, apple).
  • Then combinations began:

Examples he lists:

  • Orange + sweet-lime → “Ganga-Jamuna juice”
  • Add pineapple → he jokes it becomes “Mara-Mari juice”
  • Orange + strawberry etc. → “Tipsy” (Matunga reference)

Point: Even simple mixtures yield new “products.” Likewise, vast cosmic structures can be seen as emergent modifications.

16) Science analogy: Big Bang theory as a “no-creator” model (he uses it as logic support)

He gives a quick “in very short” science-style story:

  • There was only energy/darkness.
  • An imbalance occurred (reason unknown).
  • A “bang” occurred.
  • With bang: sound/light.

Om / Amen / Ameen connection

He claims:

  • The primal sound was “Om,” hence its resonance across religions.
  • Even “Amen/Ameen” is presented as an echo of that.
Important note (how he frames it):
  • He says this is logical, not necessarily the final truth.
  • His point is: science also proposes creation without a conscious creator, via energy modification.

17) “Who decided Earth is round?” — shape + geography jokes to press the same point

He asks:

  • Who decided Earth should be a sphere/globe?
  • Why not triangle/square?

Then:

  • Who decided Atlantic should be between America and Africa?
  • Who decided there should be mountains, oceans, water bodies?

He’s emphasizing:

  • In the science model: “it’s modification of energy.”
  • In his philosophical framing: “it’s modification of substances / dravya swabhav.”

Point: Complex order can exist without invoking a personal planner for each feature.

18) Auschwitz / concentration camps — why he brings this up (and what it’s proving)

This is not random; he uses it to illustrate the emotional shock people feel when imagining “hell”:

  • He says: if you start asking “who created hell,” you might imagine God must be extremely cruel.
  • To show what “unimaginable cruelty” looks like in human history, he references:

Example set

  • He visited Auschwitz (Poland).
  • He describes unimaginable cruelty:
    • people being suffocated/bricked in (standing, tiny vent)
    • brutal methods that go beyond simply killing
  • He mentions heaps of:
    • spectacles
    • hair
  • He mentions the scale:
    • “60 lakhs”
  • He notes WWII period (1939–1945) and says it became widely known after Hitler’s suicide.
What this section is doing philosophically:
  • It’s not a proof of hell.
  • It’s explaining why people emotionally recoil and say:
    • “How could such suffering exist?”
    • “Who could create such a place?”
  • Then he brings you back:
    • This subject is deep and not the main scope here.
    • But your questions should arise and can be explored through deeper karmic/cosmology texts.

19) The clean answer packed into one sentence (what you should retain)

The “Atmasiddhi-level” conclusion of Verse 86

  • To undergo specific karmic fruits, corresponding abodes exist.
  • Their existence and the soul’s movement to them occur by dravya swabhav (the inherent nature and mechanics of substances: soul + karma + cosmos).
  • Full detail is beyond the present scope, hence: “gahan vaat chhe.”
