• Topic: Third Fundamental Truth → The soul is the doer of karmas (karta of karma-bandh / actions-with-bondage).
  • The disciple has been thinking deeply and ends up with two big possibilities:

1. Soul is the doer

2. Soul is NOT the doer

He explores what each possibility would imply.

2) Disciple’s two “master options” and their consequences

Option A: “Soul is the doer of karmas”

  • If the soul is the doer, and karmas bind the soul and cause bondage…
  • then the disciple worries:
    • “Maybe doing/accumulating karmas is the soul’s nature.”
    • If it’s nature, it cannot be removed or destroyed, so it continues forever.
  • Conclusion (disciple’s fear):
    • If it is the soul’s nature to bind karma → there is no liberation.

Key idea: If doership = nature, then bondage is eternal → moksha becomes impossible.

Option B: “Soul is NOT the doer”

  • If the soul is not the doer, the disciple says:
    • karma is evident (diversity is seen), so someone/something must be the doer.
    • Without alternatives, you can’t even claim “soul is non-doer.”

So the disciple gives 4 non-soul doer options:

1) “Karmas bind karmas”

  • Past karmas inspire new karmas and they bind.
  • So the chain continues.

2) “Automatically, without reason”

  • No need for soul effort.
  • No need even for past karmas.
  • New karmas just “bang” and bind for no reason.

3) “Prakruti (matter) does everything”

  • Sankhya-style view:
    • It seems the soul (purush) is bound/doing,
    • but actually prakruti is doing,
    • soul remains unbound, unattached, non-doer.

4) “God’s inspiration does everything”

  • The disciple says: inspiration is needed.
  • But:
    • matter can’t inspire,
    • soul (according to this view) is unbound and doesn’t inspire,
    • so a third agency is needed → God inspires all action and bondage.
  • Conclusion from this view:
    • soul is non-doer, unbound
    • no point pursuing liberation (since bondage is not truly “mine”).

3) Quick recap: how Guru already solved earlier options (verses 74–75)

The speaker now switches into a “rapid recap / quiz mode”:

3.1 Solving “karma j karta karma” (karmas do karmas) → Verse 74

  • How did Guru eliminate it?
    • With: “Hoy na chetan prerna” (If there’s no inspiration from consciousness, who will acquire/bind karma?)

Meaning:

  • Matter can’t inspire.
  • For karmic bondage, conscious inspiration is required.

So “karmas bind karmas” collapses because karmas are matter → no inspiration.

3.2 Solving “automatic bondage” → Verse 75

  • How did Guru solve “karmas bind automatically”?
    • With: “Jo chetan kartu nathi” (If the soul’s conscious activity isn’t there, karmas don’t bind.)

He clarifies:

  • karmic particles may “pass” in the atmosphere,
  • they may “touch” the soul,
  • but they do not bind unless the soul’s inspiration/impure feelings are present.

3.3 Solving “doership is soul’s nature” → also Verse 75

  • Same key phrase:
    • “Jo chetan kartu nathi”
  • Why this refutes “nature”:
    • The presence of “jo” (if) shows it is conditional.
    • Anything conditional (sometimes happens, sometimes not) cannot be “nature.”
    • So it may be capacity/tendency, but not eternal unchangeable nature.

The practical picture he repeats

  • If soul is in equanimity, karmas just pass and go away.
  • If soul enters likes/dislikes (impure feelings), karmas bind.

4) Transition: Now we move to Verse 72’s two remaining claims

He says clearly:

  • Verse 71’s key doubts are resolved in 74 and 75.
  • Now we must solve Verse 72’s two claims:

1. Doership of prakruti (matter does bondage)

2. Doership of God (God inspires everything)

He previews:

  • These will be solved in:
    • Verse 76 = negates prakruti-doership
    • Verse 77 = negates God-doership

This video focuses on Verse 76 (prakruti-doership).

5) Verse 76: Resolving the “prakruti does bondage” doctrine

5.1 Setting up the disciple’s Sankhya-influenced claim

  • Disciple (influenced by Sankhya) says:
    • soul is pure, pristine, unbound
    • prakruti binds karma
    • so prakruti is responsible, not soul

The speaker notes:

  • disciple shifted doership from purush (soul) to prakruti (matter).

5.2 Guru’s answer begins (Verse 76)

Key phrase highlighted:

  • Keval hota asang jo…

He unpacks each part:

“Keval” = absolutely / only / completely

“as◌ंग” = unattached

“jo” = if

So:

  • “If the soul were absolutely unattached…”

Then Guru asks a direct experiential question:

“Bhasat taney na kem?”

  • If you are absolutely unattached/pure…
  • Why don’t you experience the soul right now?

This is a major move:

  • Guru attacks the claim using experience-based logic:
    • “If you were completely pure and unbound, you’d have immediate direct experience.”

6) The apparent surprise: Guru seems to “accept” asang

The speaker notes the emotional twist:

  • At first it sounds like Guru is rejecting “asang,”
  • but then Guru says:

“Asang chhe parmarthathi”

  • The soul is unattached…
  • from the absolute (paramarth / nishchay) viewpoint.

So Guru is not denying the soul’s purity entirely. He is refining how it is true.

7) The key correction: nature vs state (the big teaching inside this verse)

The speaker explains:

  • Guru “skips in one line” from:
    • inherent nature → to
    • pure state attained through experience

Meaning:

  • The soul’s nature is non-attached,
  • but the soul’s current state is not experienced as non-attached.

And this mismatch is the evidence that the disciple’s “absolute purity in the present” claim is incomplete.

8) Why “If you were absolutely pure, you’d experience it from the beginning”

The speaker extends Guru’s logic:

If the soul were:

  • totally pure,
  • with no deluding karma,
  • no knowledge-obscuring karma,
  • no veil…

Then:

  • you’d have been experiencing the soul “from the beginning” (birth / earliest life).
  • you wouldn’t even have had the first doubt (existence of soul).
  • the first two truths (existence, eternity) wouldn’t need proving to you.
  • spiritual development “stages” wouldn’t be needed.

He also links it to Jain stages:

  • He mentions stages of spiritual development (1–14).
  • If purity were already fully present in state, stages become meaningless.

Main point: Your present non-experience of the soul is itself evidence of a veil/association.

9) The “veil” example (handkerchief + clock)

He gives a simple demonstration:

  • Clock exists, but you can’t see it because it’s covered.
  • That “covering” is like:
    • karmic veil / ignorance veil

So:

  • The soul exists and is pure in nature,
  • but its experience is blocked in the present state.

10) Then the Guru says again: “Asang chhe” (Soul is unattached)

This is deliberate:

  • after proving you aren’t “absolutely unattached in experience,”
  • Guru still affirms:
    • soul is unattached from the absolute/nature viewpoint.

So now the disciple must hold:

  • two viewpoints at once, not one.

11) Two viewpoints explained (absolute vs relative)

He introduces the classic Jain framework:

  • Absolute viewpoint (nishchay nay / real viewpoint) → focuses on the nature
  • Relative/practical viewpoint (vyavahar nay) → focuses on the state

He insists:

  • both are “true in their own way.”
  • confusion happens when you take one viewpoint as the entire truth.

12) Illustration with the “braids vs cheeks” (focus changes answer)

He calls a person forward and jokes:

  • Is she “okay” or “wonderful”?
  • Both can be said depending on focus.

Then he gives the coded memory hook:

  • focus on braids vs focus on cheeks (he calls it “C-C”)

Meaning:

  • different focus → different statement can be valid
  • similarly:
    • focus on nature → soul is pure
    • focus on state → soul is impure

13) Anekant + ahimsa: how to communicate disagreements

He extracts a communication lesson (even though it’s not “just metaphysics”):

  • In disagreement:

1. First accept what is right in the other person’s view (ahimsa)

2. Then explain what is missing/incomplete (anekant)

So Guru’s method is:

  • “Yes—soul is pure” (accept)
  • “But not ‘keval’ in present experience” (correct the partial truth)

He calls it:

  • first ahimsa, then anekant

14) The “keval” warning: where the disciple goes wrong

He says the disciple’s mistake is not “sada asang” (always unattached in nature). The mistake is adding keval (only/absolutely) without distinction.

He flags four words (for quiz memory):

1. only pure

2. absolutely pure

3. always pure

4. completely pure

He explains:

  • “always pure” fits nature
  • “only/absolutely/completely pure (right now, in state)” creates contradiction with lived impurity.

15) The contradiction trap: if you dump impurity into mind/intellect only

He highlights a philosophical problem:

  • If you say soul is absolutely pure,
  • you may try to place anger/lust/etc. into mind-intellect only.
  • But if you also say mind/intellect are non-living matter:
    • how can non-living have feelings?

So:

  • Either feelings belong to a conscious entity,
  • or you must redefine mind/intellect as conscious (which creates other issues).

He uses this to show:

  • the “absolutely pure in state” position breaks under analysis.

16) The key turn back to doership: “who acquired these karmas?”

He ties it directly to the Third Fundamental Truth:

  • You are not experiencing your pure self → there is a veil → karmas/ignorance influence exists.
  • If karmas exist around the soul in bondage form…
    • who acquired them?
    • You.

Therefore:

  • the soul is the doer (in the current state sense).

He states it plainly:

  • If you are impure and bound,
  • and bondage happens by soul’s inspiration,
  • the soul is the doer of karmas.

17) Final consolidation: what the verse proves

What is true from the absolute viewpoint

  • Soul is unattached (as its eternal nature)

What is true from the relative viewpoint (present condition)

  • Soul’s state is impure/tainted
  • thus it indulges in likes/dislikes (cravings/aversions)
  • this inspires bondage
  • therefore, it is the doer of karmas right now

How state becomes aligned with nature

  • pan nijbhane tem:
    • when the soul experiences the Self
    • abides in it
    • karmas gradually drop
    • at the end, the state becomes asang too (liberation)

He frames it as:

  • nature is karmaless already (absolute viewpoint)
  • state becomes karmaless through realization and progress (relative viewpoint)

18) Takeaway: the disciple’s “prakruti does bondage” is corrected, not mocked

The Guru’s correction is subtle:

  • He doesn’t deny soul’s purity.
  • He denies the disciple’s one-sided absolutism.
  • He uses:
    • direct lived evidence (“you don’t experience it”)
    • plus viewpoint-logic (nature vs state)
  • and lands the intended conclusion for the section:
    • the soul is the doer of karmas (in the present state sense)
