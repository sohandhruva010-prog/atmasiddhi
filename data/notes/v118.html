Video 133 Notes: Verse 118 — The Guru Concludes and Enters Silence

Based on the transcript you shared.

1) Context: The final verse spoken by the Guru

This discourse is on verse 118 of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra. It is a deeply significant verse because it marks the end of the Guru’s teaching in the dialogue.

The Guru-disciple dialogue began at verse 45, when the disciple raised doubts such as: the soul cannot be seen by the eyes, its form is unknown, and so on. From there, the Guru gradually answered all doubts, clarified the Six Fundamental Truths, explained the path of liberation, and revealed the nature of the Self.

Now, in verse 118, the Guru says that everything essential has been said. The teachings of all Enlightened Ones converge here. Then He assumes silence and enters effortless Self-absorption.

2) Opening reflection: “I have scattered the seeds”

The discourse begins with a beautiful reflection from the Gujarati spiritual poet Makarand Dave:

“I have scattered the seeds with generosity; now it is up to the clouds and the Earth.”

The speaker applies this to the Sadguru.

The Sadguru has compassionately scattered the seeds of knowledge. He has given the disciple everything needed for liberation: the path, the method, the understanding, the warnings, the inspiration, and the vision of the Self.

Now, the Guru’s work is complete. Whether those seeds grow depends on:

1. Patrata — the disciple’s worthiness.

2. Purusharth — the disciple’s effort.

The Guru has given the gift of knowledge selflessly. Now the disciple must cultivate it.

This is a powerful transition: the teaching has been given; now responsibility shifts to the disciple.

3) The Sadguru has done His work

The Sadguru’s attitude is:

“Out of compassion, I have given you everything, O my disciple. Now it is up to your worthiness and effort.”

The Guru has not held anything back. He has shown the path of liberation fully and generously. He has not considered His own convenience, time, or effort.

But knowledge alone is not enough. The disciple must now internalise, contemplate, practise, and realise.

The Guru gives the seed. The disciple must become fertile ground.

4) “Do all the preparation, then do nothing”

The speaker shares another poetic reflection:

Prepare everything for spiritual practice:

  • incense,
  • lamp,
  • rosary,
  • daily prayers,
  • worship,
  • reverence,
  • rituals,
  • spiritual disciplines.

But after preparing everything, do nothing.

This does not mean laziness or neglect. It points to a very subtle state:

Choiceless effortless awareness.

One must prepare sincerely, but the deepest state is not created by restless doing. After preparation, one must become a knower-seer.

The spiritual practice reaches fulfilment when the seeker stops forcing and simply abides in awareness.

5) The sleep analogy: to sleep, do nothing The speaker gives a simple example.

If you want to fall asleep, you cannot force sleep. You cannot think, “I must sleep, I must sleep, I must sleep.” That very effort keeps you awake.

To slip into sleep, you must let go.

Similarly, to slip into deep awareness, one must let go of:

  • thoughts,
  • I-ness,
  • my-ness,
  • mental activity,
  • restless spiritual ambition.

This is not inactivity born of dullness. It is effortless inward stillness.

6) The gap between two thoughts

A memorable analogy is given from childhood.

The speaker’s mother taught him how to cross narrow roads in Walkeshwar: watch the space between two vehicles, and when there is enough space, cross.

The Guru teaches the same thing inwardly:

Between two thoughts, there is empty space. Hold that space and be still within.

This is a practical doorway into awareness.

Do not fight thoughts. Do not chase thoughts. Notice the gap between them. In that gap, there is stillness.

This is again connected to choiceless effortless awareness.

7) Structure of the final section

The speaker explains the structure of the closing verses.

Verses 97–117

Through the Guru, Param Krupalu Dev has conveyed the complete path of liberation.

Verses 115–117

These are called the three mystical pinnacle verses — kalash gathas. They reveal:

  • verse 115: the essence of religion,
  • verse 116: the nature of the Self,
  • verse 117: more attributes of the Self and the final instruction, “Kar vichar to paam.”
Verse 118

The Guru concludes the dialogue.

This verse is the final statement addressed by the Guru to the disciple before the Guru assumes silence.

8) Verse 118

Nishchay sarve jnanino, aavi atra samay;
Dhari maunta em kahi, sahaj samadhi mai.

Simple meaning

The conclusion of all Enlightened Ones has been encompassed here. Having said this, the Guru assumes silence and enters effortless Self-absorption.

9) Word-by-word meaning

Nishchay

Conclusion, firm conclusion, settled truth.

This refers to the final spiritual conclusion of the Enlightened Ones.

Sarve jnanino

Of all Enlightened Ones.

Not merely scholars, monks, priests, or ritualists — but those who have directly realised the Self.

Aavi atra samay Has converged here.

The teachings of all Enlightened Ones meet at this point.

Dhari maunta

Assuming silence.

The Guru withdraws from speech.

Em kahi

Having said this.

After declaring that all Enlightened Ones’ conclusions converge here.

Sahaj samadhi mai

Into effortless Self-absorption, innate ecstasy, spontaneous deep meditative state.

The Guru enters samadhi naturally, without effort.

10) Main meaning of the first line

Nishchay sarve jnanino, aavi atra samay

The Guru says:

“Whatever I have said is the conclusion of all Enlightened Ones.”

This means the teaching of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra is not a new sectarian invention. It is not a personal theory. It is not an isolated doctrine.

It is the universal essence of the path of liberation.

The speaker explains that Param Krupalu Dev is saying:

“I have not added anything. I have not subtracted anything. I have expressed what lies in the heart of all Enlightened Ones.”

This shows tremendous humility and reverence.

He does not say, “Only I have revealed this truth.” Instead, He says, “This is the conclusion of all Enlightened Masters.”

11) Universal message, not sectarian message

The teaching is not limited to one sect, one tradition, one external method, one ritual form, or one “ism.”

The outer expressions may differ:

  • different scriptures,
  • different languages,
  • different rituals,
  • different disciplines,
  • different times,
  • different places,
  • different cultures.

But the inner path is one.

The speaker says:

One ignorant soul may have a hundred opinions, but a hundred Enlightened Souls have one opinion.

Why? Because they have all experienced the same truth.

12) Different expressions, same experience

The speaker gives a beautiful explanation.

If several people experience a wonderful evening, one may express it through violin, another through painting, another through poetry, another through silence.

The expressions differ. The experience is one.

Similarly, Enlightened Ones may express the truth differently:

  • through silence,
  • through scriptures,
  • through poetry,
  • through direct instruction,
  • through devotional language,
  • through analytical language.

But the realised truth is the same.

Thus, the conclusions of all Enlightened Masters converge in the path explained in Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra.

13) What has the Guru already explained?

The speaker briefly reviews the major topics covered before verse 118.

From verse 97 onward, the Guru explained:

  • impurity of the soul and liberation,
  • causes of bondage,
  • attachment, aversion, and ignorance,
  • the eternal conscious nature of the soul,
  • deluding karma,
  • faith-deluding and conduct-deluding karma,
  • anger and forgiveness,
  • giving up opinions, beliefs, and insistence,
  • understanding the Six Fundamental Truths from all viewpoints,
  • non-discrimination of caste, gender, or garb on the path,
  • the stages from preparation to omniscience,
  • how long it takes to eliminate delusion,
  • the essence of religion,
  • the true nature of the Self.

So the Guru has covered all important aspects of the path of liberation.

There is nothing essential left to say.

14) The three groups in this section

The speaker divides this final segment into three groups.

Group 1: Verses 92–107

The Guru answers the doubts and arguments of the disciple.

Group 2: Verses 108–113 The Guru explains the stages of spiritual evolution:

  • preparation,
  • right faith,
  • experiential faith,
  • supreme right faith,
  • right conduct,
  • omniscience.
Group 3: Verses 114–118

The Guru gives inspiration and final instruction:

  • how delusion can vanish,
  • the essence of religion,
  • the soul’s true nature,
  • the final command to contemplate and realise,
  • the conclusion of the dialogue.

Verse 118 ends the third group, the Six Fundamental Truths, and the Guru’s speech activity.

15) Main meaning of the second line

Dhari maunta em kahi, sahaj samadhi mai

Having said this, the Guru assumes silence and enters effortless samadhi.

This is not ordinary silence. It is not silence due to tiredness, indifference, or lack of words.

It is the natural state of an Enlightened One.

The Guru spoke only because the disciple needed guidance. Now that the disciple’s doubts have been addressed and the path has been shown, the Guru withdraws from speech and returns to His natural state of Self-absorption.

16) Why does the Guru become silent?

The Guru becomes silent because His purpose has been served.

The disciple came with confusion and doubts. The Guru spoke out of compassion to:

1. impart religious teaching,

2. remove confusion,

3. establish firm conviction,

4. explain the Six Fundamental Truths,

5. urge the disciple to tread the path of liberation.

Once this purpose is fulfilled, there is no need for further speech.

The Guru does not speak out of attachment. He speaks only for the welfare of the disciple.

When that welfare has been served, silence is natural.

17) Speech is not the Guru’s main activity

The speaker explains that Enlightened Ones are primarily immersed in the Self. Their natural state is beyond thoughts — a no-mind state.

When thoughts arise due to karmic manifestation, they use those thoughts for the welfare of others. They impart teachings to worthy disciples.

But teaching is not their deepest activity.

Their main “activity” is dharma in the highest sense: being absorbed in the Self.

So when speech is no longer needed, the Guru returns effortlessly to Self-absorption.

18) Compassionate speech, effortless silence

The Guru’s speech is compassionate.

He does not speak to display knowledge. He does not speak because He enjoys debate. He does not speak to create followers. He speaks because the disciple is worthy and confused.

Seeing the disciple’s worthiness, the Guru gives the teaching in a concise way.

The speaker highlights three functions of the Guru’s speech:

1. He imparts religious teaching.

2. He removes doubts and confusion.

3. He establishes conviction and inspires pursuit.

Once this is done, He assumes silence.

19) The Guru has no attachment to the disciple

This is subtle.

The Guru has infinite compassion, but not attachment.

If the Guru had attachment, He would continue speaking to please the disciple. But He does not do that. He gives what is needed, not what attachment demands.

The speaker says that after the necessary teaching is given, any desire for the Guru to continue speaking may come from the disciple’s attachment.

The Guru says, in effect:

“I have given you the master key. All locks can be opened by this. Now use it.”

20) The master key

The Guru has given the disciple the master key of liberation.

That master key includes:

  • right understanding,
  • faith in the Self,
  • faith in the Sadguru,
  • awareness of false identification,
  • the knower-seer mode,
  • dispassion,
  • inner contemplation,
  • Self-absorption,
  • the understanding of the true nature of the soul.

Now the disciple must use the key.

The Guru cannot walk the path on behalf of the disciple. He can reveal it, clarify it, inspire it, and bless it — but the disciple must practise.

21) Meaning of “sahaj samadhi”

Sahaj samadhi means effortless, natural Self-absorption.

It is not produced by external arrangements. It is not dependent on posture, place, ritual, or preparation.

The speaker says:

  • effortlessly,
  • automatically,
  • without external dependency,
  • on its own accord.

The Guru does not need to “sit for meditation” in the ordinary sense. He naturally enters the state of inner absorption.

This is the state of an Enlightened One.

22) Difference between Guru and disciple

The speaker humorously says that the Guru can go into silence, but the disciples cannot immediately copy Him.

The disciples still have a duty: they must express gratitude. The next section will be the disciple’s thanksgiving.

This is important.

A disciple should not imitate the external behaviour of the Guru without having the inner state. The Guru’s silence arises from fulfilment and Self-absorption. The disciple’s silence may simply be incompleteness or avoidance.

So the disciple must continue with humility, gratitude, contemplation, and practice.

23) The Guru’s final movement

The Guru’s final movement is:

1. He declares that all Enlightened Ones’ conclusions converge here.

2. He ends the activity of speech.

3. He assumes silence.

4. He enters effortless Self-absorption.

This is a complete spiritual picture.

The Guru comes outward through speech only for the disciple’s welfare. Once that work is complete, He withdraws inward into His natural state.

24) Practical message for the seeker

The practical message of this verse is very strong.

The seeker should not remain dependent on more and more explanation forever. At some point, the teaching must become contemplation, and contemplation must become realisation.

The Guru has scattered the seeds. Now the disciple must provide the clouds and the Earth — worthiness and effort.

The seeker must ask:

  • Have I understood the teaching?
  • Am I contemplating it?
  • Am I becoming still between thoughts?
  • Am I practising the knower-seer mode?
  • Am I giving up false identification?
  • Am I using the master key given by the Guru?
  • Am I waiting for more words, or am I ready to internalise what has already been said?

25) Key analogies from the discourse

Seeds, clouds, and Earth

The Guru scatters the seeds of knowledge. Whether they grow depends on the disciple’s worthiness and effort.

Preparations and doing nothing

Prepare fully for spiritual practice, but finally enter choiceless effortless awareness.

Falling asleep

To fall asleep, you cannot force sleep. Similarly, to enter deep awareness, you must let go of restless activity.

Crossing the road

Just as one crosses the road by finding the gap between vehicles, one enters inner stillness by recognising the gap between thoughts.

Different expressions of the same experience

One person expresses a beautiful evening through music, another through painting, another through poetry, another through silence. Enlightened Ones may express differently, but their realised conclusion is one.

Master key

The Guru has given the key that can open all locks. The disciple must now use it.

26) Main takeaways

1. Verse 118 concludes the Guru-disciple dialogue.

2. The teachings of all Enlightened Ones converge in the path explained here.

3. Param Krupalu Dev is not creating a new sectarian doctrine; He is expressing the universal essence of liberation.

4. The Guru has said everything essential about the path.

5. The Guru speaks only out of compassion for the worthy disciple.

6. Once the purpose of speech is fulfilled, the Guru naturally returns to silence.

7. The Guru’s silence is not emptiness; it is effortless Self-absorption.

8. The disciple must now convert the teaching into contemplation, effort, worthiness, and realisation.

9. The path is universal, though its expressions may vary.

10. The Guru has scattered the seeds; now the disciple must make them grow.

27) Final summary

Verse 118 is the Guru’s final statement in the dialogue. He declares that the conclusion of all Enlightened Ones has been encompassed in the teachings given. The path explained is universal, not sectarian or newly invented. It is the essence realised by all Enlightened Beings across time. Having fulfilled His compassionate purpose — removing the disciple’s doubts and showing the path of liberation — the Guru withdraws from speech, assumes silence, and enters effortless Self-absorption. The disciple is now entrusted with the responsibility to contemplate, practise, and realise. The seeds have been scattered; now growth depends on worthiness and effort.
