He recaps where we are:

  • Verses 34, 35, 36 showed:
    • What an ātmārthī (true seeker) thinks and feels before a Sadguru.
    • His inner convictions, because he is a truth seeker.
  • Those verses were like scanning his heart:
    • 34 – What he wants in a Sadguru (Self-realised).
    • 35 – How he would relate to a living Sadguru (supreme benefit + total obedience).
    • 36 – What he believes about the path (one path, many valid means).

Now, from verse 37 onward, we shift from:

“What is in his heart?” to “What does he actually do?”

Verse 37 begins with:

“Em vichārī antare…” – “Thinking thus within…”

“Em” = everything in verses 34–36. So this verse shows what a true seeker does, based on all that inner preparation.

2. Link from the previous verse: Practices vs. Path

He briefly recalls verse 36:

  • There is one path of liberation in all three times.
  • Whatever practices lead to that path:
    • Are worthy,
    • Should be accepted/respected.

So the ātmārthī knows:

  • Not all practices destroy the impure state of the soul.
  • He avoids ashubh (unwholesome), takes up shubh (wholesome) practices only to increase worthiness.
  • But he’s very clear:

“I have not truly started walking towards truth until I take refuge in a living Sadguru.”

So meanwhile he doesn’t think:

  • “Let me enjoy now, and once I meet a Sadguru then I’ll become disciplined, austere, regular.”

Instead:

  • Even before meeting Sadguru:
    • He stops negative tendencies,
    • Cultivates virtues,
    • Raises his eligibility.

This clarity and worthiness are what verse 37 now expresses.

3. Verse 37 – Line by line

The verse:

Em vichārī antare, shodhe Sadguru yog;
Kaam ek ātmārthnu, bījo nahi mannrog.

3.1 “Em vichārī antare…”

  • Em vichārī – “Thinking thus”:
    • Thus = all the convictions from verses 34, 35, 36:
      • What is a real Guru,
      • How to relate to a living Sadguru,
      • One path, many valid practices.
  • Antare – within:
    • In his inner self, heart, mind.

It’s like he’s made a to-do list inside on 1st January. Now verse 37 says: “So what does he do from 2nd January?”

3.2 “Shodhe Sadguru yog”

  • Shodhe – he searches.
  • Sadguru yog – the association/proximity of a true Enlightened Mentor.

So:

“Thinking thus within, the true seeker searches for the association of a Sadguru.”

Not casually:

  • It’s not a random “If I get, I get.”
  • He realises:
    • Without Sadguru’s association, he may increase qualifications, but: “I’ve not yet taken even one real step towards the supreme truth.”

So he actively seeks Sadguru.

3.3 “Kaam ek ātmārthnu…”

  • Kaam here = kāmanā – desire / purpose / work.
  • Ek – only one.
  • Ātmārthnu – of the soul’s welfare, of Self-realisation.

So:

“The ātmārthī has only one desire, only one work, only one life-purpose: spiritual welfare / Self-realisation.”

Spiritual welfare includes:

  • Becoming worthy,
  • Self-realisation itself, and
  • Finally liberation.

So this one line means:

“My only work in life is ātmārth – spiritual welfare – the pursuit of the path of liberation.”

3.4 “Bījo nahi mannrog”

  • Bījo nahi – no other.
  • Mannrog – disease of the mind:
    • Mental cravings,
    • Inner compulsions,
    • Longings for anything other than Self-realisation.

So:

“He has no other mental disease of cravings – no other inner fever for:

  • honour,
  • wealth,
  • position,
  • worldly accomplishments,
  • pleasure,
  • people behaving ‘just so’ with him.”

This is the line he says he quotes the most:

“Kaam ek ātmārthnu, bījo nahi mannrog.”

He uses it when:

  • He spots mannrog in people:
    • Wanting justice,
    • Wanting to change or fix others,
    • Wanting to be treated in a particular way.

He says:

“Whenever I scream from the top of my voice, it is not against your ātmārth – it is against your mannrog.”

Because:

  • You came to Sadguru for ātmārth,
  • But midway your old cravings got activated.

So this one line is a constant recalibration:

“Come back. Your only job is ātmārth. No other mental craving.”

4. For a real seeker, nothing is more important than knowing the Self

He states very simply:

For a true seeker, nothing is more significant than knowing the Self / Self-realisation / truth.

At this point the seeker:

  • Doesn’t want more:
    • Doing (mechanical rituals),
    • Outer religious identity.
  • He wants being:
    • Abiding in the Self.

And at this point, he needs a Sadguru more than ever:

  • Not just someone to tell him:
    • “What to recite in the morning?”
  • But someone who can:
    • Direct him in the subtlest inner movement.

He defines Sadguru:

“Sadguru is a living representative of divinity.”

Just like:

  • In the Yajna, someone does “representation” of a region.
  • When Tirthankars are not physically present, A Sadguru is representing the divine live.

5. What surrender really is (not just coconuts & fafḍā)

He jokes:

  • In practice, we “surrender”:
    • Coconuts,
    • Fafḍā (snacks from Surendranagar),
    • Flowers, chocolates, etc.

He says all that is fine, but:

Real surrender means surrendering:

  • Your ambitions,
  • Your desires,
  • Your ego.

And this must be done:

  • With dedication and devotion, not compulsion.

He differentiates:

  • “I have to do it because I stay here” = not ātmārthī, maybe matārthī (bigot) or mānārthī (pride-seeking).
  • “I want to do it out of love” = real ātmārthī.

If there is no joy in surrender:

“Then you are not even close to being an ātmārthī.”

6. Unity of all three energies – trane yog ekatvathi (from verse 35)

He connects back to verse 35:

  • Mind,
  • Speech,
  • Body.

All three energies must be:

  • In unity, not split.

Examples:

  • Some:
    • Talk beautifully,
    • But don’t do what they say.
  • Some:
    • Act externally,
    • But inner feeling is missing.
  • True ātmārthī:

What he says, he feels in the heart, and executes in action.

This unity creates:

  • “Utmost devotion” required for true surrender.

7. The battle: inner confusion vs outer temptation

He describes the real battle:

Between internal confusion and external temptations.

He recalls his old diary (1980s):

“Life is a constant adjustment between the external situation and internal state.”

True spirituality:

  • Is not about:
    • Commenting on situations,
    • Challenging them,
    • Constantly changing them.
  • He spontaneously coins 3 C’s:
    • Commenting,
    • Challenging,
    • Changing.

Instead:

“Your main work is to preserve inner calm amidst all changes in body, relationships, possessions, situations.”

The Sadguru’s grace helps:

  • Win this battle with:
    • Inner negativities,
    • Outer attractions.

8. Link with verse 36: Why he must find a Sadguru

Verse 36 said:

  • Path is one,
  • Whatever practice leads to that path is worth adopting.

The ātmārthī now realises:

“But which practice is right for me?”

Examples he gives:

  • For Sohini, Guru may say:
    • “Look for a good boy and get married.”
  • For Dr. Mansi, Guru may say:
    • “Drop all good boys and stay focused.”

So:

  • A practice right for one,
    • May not be right for another.
  • We don’t know:
    • Our conscious, subconscious, unconscious.
    • Our karmic background or future.

So he concludes:

“Someone must guide me in which practice to adopt, whether to go into seclusion or seva, whether to step forward or stay where I am.”

Hence:

  • Association of a Sadguru is essential.
  • That’s why verse 37 begins:
  • “Em vichārī antare, shodhe Sadguru yog…” He doesn’t want:

“To take one step ahead, if that step actually takes me two steps back.”

So he actively searches for Sadguru.

9. The letter to Sanju: “Why did you ask for Sadguru for so many births?”

He recalls an old letter he wrote to Sanju (mid-1980s):

  • Sanju had gotten engaged; life heading into normal family flow.
  • In the letter he wrote (paraphrased):

“If the life you are living now is all you wanted, then why did you ask, for so many births, for a Sadguru and satsang?”

Because:

  • For many lives you prayed:
    • “May I get a Sadguru,”
    • “May I serve a Sadguru,”
    • “May I get satsang.”
  • Those aspirations brought you this birth’s fortune:
    • Association of a living Sadguru.
  • Now you cannot get defocused and live like everyone else.

The point:

Don’t waste the fruition of many-birth prayers by living only for worldly goals now.

10. The billionaire analogy: How single-pointed the goal should be

He asks playfully:

“Who wants to be a millionaire?” Then corrects: “Obviously everyone wants to be a billionaire.”

Analogy:

  • If someone truly wants to be a billionaire:
    • He’ll:
      • Eat, drink, sleep, attend functions.
    • But in his heart and mind:
      • Every act is oriented to making money.
      • Even dreams are about multiplying wealth.

Similarly, for an ātmārthī:

  • He will:
    • Get married or not,
    • Work,
    • Raise family,
    • Serve, travel, etc.
  • But one thing stays in the centre:

“Nothing should take me away from my goal of Self-realisation.”

Verse 37 is exactly this:

Kaam ek ātmārthnu – one desire only. All other duties are secondary, not the main script.

11. How he recognises a Sadguru (speech first, then virtues)

When strong punya (merits) ripen:

  • In his search (shodhe Sadguru yog),
  • He comes across an Enlightened Master.

Recognition sequence:

1. First, the speech hits:

  • He feels:

“He is reading my mind. He is answering questions I didn’t even ask.”

  • This is apurva vāni – unique, living speech.

2. Then, as association deepens, he sees virtues:

  • Ātmajñān – Self-realisation,
  • Samdarshita – equanimity,
  • Vichāre uday-prayog – living according to past karma,
  • Param-śrut – grasp of essential scriptures.

This parallels what we saw earlier:

  • Aulakhan – Ahobhāv – Arpaṇtā:

1. Recognise,

2. Reverence,

3. Surrender.

12. Side example: The “Daughters of Raj” election – elders as role models

He briefly narrates a sweet, instructive story:

  • A girls’ group – “Daughters of Raj”.
  • Initially:
    • Leader (Bathu) was selected, not elected.
  • After two years, she herself said:
    • “Now others should get a chance.”
    • They held an election via WhatsApp.
    • Counted votes.
    • Chose a new president (Samayra).
  • He was shocked and delighted:
    • No one told them to do this.
    • They absorbed the idea of:
      • Shared leadership,
      • Proper processes.

But he also felt alarmed:

“If they can absorb this so deeply just by watching elders, imagine what happens if they see:

  • ego clashes,
  • manipulation,
  • mismanaged seva.”

Message to elders:

  • Kids will copy:
    • Either your ego games,
    • Or your selfless seva.
  • So elders must live in a way that:
    • When children imitate them,
    • They become ātmārthī-like, not mannrogī.

This again underlines:

  • How crucial the right environment and Sadguru’s guidance are for true seekers.

13. Living with one desire: duties continue, goal stays central

He tells the seekers:

  • After the Yajna, you’ll:
    • Go back to your countries,
    • Resume work, family, responsibilities.
  • Some will leave now, some in March.

He emphasises:

“Continue with all your duties – but do not forget your ultimate goal.”

Even satsang and Yajna are temporary events;
Your goal must be:

  • Unbroken,
  • Unobstructed.

A true ātmārthī knows:

Sukh, shānti, salāmatī – Happiness, peace, security – lie within, not outside.

Therefore, he begins to:

  • Refrain from new initiatives:
    • Does his current duties,
    • But doesn’t start new projects that chain him further.
  • Refrain from new possessions:
    • A new car, for example:
      • More upkeep, more slavery.

He sees:

  • Desires for:
    • Lakṣmī – wealth,
    • Adhikār – status & power,
    • Parivār – family indulgence & emotional entanglement,
    • Worldly accomplishments,
  • Are gradually weakening.

He clarifies:

  • He’s not saying:
    • “Make family sad.”
  • He’s talking about:
    • Moha (delusion),
    • Rāg (attachment),
    • Excessive importance given to them.

14. Not getting trapped by honour & appreciation

As the seeker progresses:

  • Virtues become visible:
    • More peaceful,
    • More inward,
    • More balanced.
  • People respond with:
    • Respect,
    • Appreciation,
    • Special treatment.

But an ātmārthī thinks:

“I’ve not come here to impress anyone, or collect respect and praise. I’ve come only for Self-realisation.”

So:

  • If others praise – that’s their business.
  • He refuses to get trapped in:
    • Honour,
    • Fame,
    • Name,
    • Spiritual “image.”

He moves from:

“I want to be good in everyone’s eyes” to “I just want to be good, truly, in reality.”

Because all these cravings are:

  • Mannrog – disease of the mind.

“Anything you crave – other than Self-realisation – is mannrog.”

We all have this, he admits;
But:

“At least don’t increase it. Treat it; don’t aggravate it.”

15. The distilled essence of verse 37

Putting it all together:

1. Em vichārī antare

  • With the deep inner convictions from verses 34–36,
  • The ātmārthī constantly reflects within.

2. Shodhe Sadguru yog

  • He actively searches for the association of a living Sadguru,
  • Knowing that only Sadguru can:
    • Personalise his path,
    • Guide his practices,
    • Lead him safely toward liberation.

3. Kaam ek ātmārthnu

  • He has one work, one desire, one life-purpose:
    • Spiritual welfare / Self-realisation.

4. Bījo nahi mannrog

  • He vigilantly refuses:
    • Any other craving to become central.
  • Honour, wealth, power, image, emotional dramas:
    • All are recognised as mental disease if they become the focus.
  • He keeps returning to: “I came only for ātmārth. Everything else is secondary.”

So the 37th verse reveals:

Trait of an ātmārthī: He seeks Sadguru only for his spiritual welfare, has one single desire – Self-realisation – and refuses to let any other mental craving hijack his journey.

That is “Kaam ek ātmārthnu, bījo nahi mannrog” lived in real time.
