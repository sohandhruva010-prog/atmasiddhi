He reminds us of the big picture:

  • Atmasiddhi is about:

“The means and method of Self-realisation.”

So far we’ve covered:

1. Intro (1–23): Upodghāt – Guru, Sadguru, need for liberation etc.

2. Matārthī (24–33): Traits of the bigot.

3. Ātmārthī (34–42): Traits, worthiness, and journey of the true seeker.

In verse 42, Param Krupalu Dev said:

“I will explain the six fundamentals for reflection on the Self, in the form of a Guru–disciple dialogue.”

Now, verse 43:

  • Is part of a tiny section (two verses):
    • 43 – Names of the six fundamentals.
    • 44 – Purpose of studying them.
  • This verse simply names the six truths. Explanations start from verse 45 onward.

2. The six fundamentals – just naming them (Verse 43)

He recites and then unpacks the six padas:

1. Ātmā chhe – The soul exists.

2. Te nitya chhe – It is eternal.

3. Chhe kartā nijkarma – It is the doer of its own actions (karma).

4. Chhe bhoktā… – It is the:

  • enjoyer / endurer / receiver of the results.

5. Vali moksh chhe – Liberation exists.

6. Moksh upāy sudharma – There is a path to liberation, which is true religion.

He notes:

  • The verse uses “te”, not “ātmā nitya chhe”:
    • He follows that and says “it is eternal”, as a pronoun, not repeating the noun.

Let’s go one by one.

3. 1st & 2nd truths – Soul exists; it is eternal

1) Ātmā chhe – The soul exists

  • There is a Self beyond body & mind.
  • This truth will be challenged and defended in the dialogue starting verse 45.

2) Te nitya chhe – It is eternal

  • The soul:
    • Has always existed,
    • Will always exist.
  • Bodies come and go, but:

“I” – as consciousness – is beginningless-endless.

He’ll later connect this with:

  • Multiple births,
  • Infinite past suffering,
  • Eternal potential for liberation.

4. 3rd & 4th truths – Doer and Endurer

3) Chhe kartā nijkarma – The soul is the doer of its own karma

  • Not:
    • God,
    • Fate,
    • Some external agency.

He attacks a common idea:

  • “Whatever is happening is God’s will.”
    • If “will” = ajñā (command/instruction), fine.
    • But as:

“He writes my destiny, I’m helpless” – Jainism does not accept this.

  • Jain view:

“You write your own destiny through your own karmic actions.”

So:

  • Kartā = the one who does, chooses:
    • Acts,
    • Intentions,
    • Deep inner tendencies.

4) Chhe bhoktā – The soul is the bhoktā of the fruits

He suggests 3 English words and picks one:

  • Enjoyer – standard philosophical word, but:
    • Intuitively fits only pleasant results.
    • “Enjoyer of fever” sounds awkward in modern usage.
  • Receiver – you can receive and shelve:
    • Doesn’t fully convey that you undergo the result.
  • Endurer – he likes this best:
    • You undergo and bear the results:
      • Pleasant (merit),
      • Painful (demerit).

So:

“Chhe kartā” – you are the doer. “Chhe bhoktā” – you are the endurer of what you have done.

Not:

  • Karma evaporates,
  • Someone else decides,
  • God punishes or rewards.

If you stop being the doer, you stop being the endurer – then you are free (link to moksh).

5. 5th & 6th truths – Moksh exists; there is a path & true religion

5) Vali moksh chhe – Liberation exists

He asks:

“Is it compulsory to be stuck in the endless cycle of birth–death–rebirth?”

Answer:

  • Not at all.
  • You can disentangle yourself from the chain of samsār.

“Moksh chhe” =

“There is such a state as total freedom from birth–death.”

6) Moksh upāy – sudharma – There is a path; this path is true religion

He gives a nice worldly analogy:

  • You want to get married:
    • You don’t say:

“Let’s see, whenever destiny brings someone to my doorstep.”

  • No girl is going to knock and say:

“Can I marry you?”

  • You must follow some upāy:
    • Meet people,
    • Families,
    • Proposals, etc.

Even more so with moksh:

  • Moksh exists,
  • And there is a defined path:

“Do this, don’t do that.” it starts with dos & don’ts, and slowly progresses to pure being.

He reminds his two-step framework:

1. First: doing – disciplines, restraint, practice.

2. Then: being – effortless abidance in the Self.

He defines true religion simply:

“What is sudharma (true dharma)? The path of liberation itself.”

Not clan, costume, outer label – Whatever actually leads to moksh is true dharma.

6. Why we must know the six fundamentals

He asks:

“Is it necessary to stay stuck in the cycle of rebirths?”

  • No – because moksh exists and upāy exists.
  • But to walk the path:
    • You must know:
      • Who decides your destiny? (Doer/endurer).
      • What is soul?,
      • What is its nature?,
      • Is freedom possible?,
      • What are the means?.

He sums:

1. You want liberation – fine.

2. For that, you must know truth.

3. Epicentre of spirituality = soul.

4. So you must know the six truths about the soul.

Short:

“To get liberated, you need spiritual knowledge. Spirituality’s centre is the soul. Therefore you must know the nature of soul – that is the six fundamentals.”

7. Shrimad’s compassion: why he doesn’t stop at naming

He points out:

  • Verse 43 alone could have been enough:
    • He has already stated:
      • Soul exists,
      • Eternal,
      • Doer,
      • Endurer,
      • Liberation exists,
      • Path and means exist.

We could jump straight to:

“Aho! Aho! Shri Sadguru…” – “O Sadguru! You explained all six truths to me.”

But:

“He doesn’t want you to follow blindly. He wants:

  • Intellect to be convinced,
  • Virtues to be cultivated,
  • Experience to be your own.”

So:

  • Before the detailed logical dialogue (from verse 45),
  • Verse 43 familiarises us with:
    • The subject list,
    • The foundation of spiritual knowledge.

It’s like:

“Here is the syllabus: these six are what we’ll study deeply now.”

8. Six truths already woven in verse 1 (subtle layer)

He then gives a beautiful insight:

The first time you see the six truths named is verse 43. But they are subtly woven into verse 1 already.

He recites verse 1:

Je swaroop samjya vinā, pāmiyo dukh anant;
samjāvyu te pada namun, Shri Sadguru Bhagwant.

He shows how all six lie in there if you ponder deeply.

1) Swaroop – “Je swaroop…” → soul exists

  • “Je swaroop samjya vinā…” – without understanding the true nature (swaroop) of “je”.
  • “Je” = that which truly is – the Self.
  • So:

This implies: There is such a swaroop to know = soul exists.

2) Dukh anant → soul is eternal

  • “Pāmiyo dukh anant” – I have suffered infinite misery.
  • At present you’re only 20, 30, 54 etc… how infinite?
  • So “I” who suffered infinite dukh must have existed across countless births.
  • That implies:

Soul is eternal.

3) Samjya vinā (didn’t know) → soul is doer

  • Why am I suffering?
    • Because of my ignorance:
      • “Samjya vinā” – without understanding.
    • My not-knowing leads to wrong-doing.
  • I am responsible:

I am the doer of this ignorance-based karmic mess.

4) Pāmiyo → soul is endurer

  • “Pāmiyo dukh anant” – I have received / undergone infinite misery.
  • “Pāmiyu” is the word used:
    • Endured, experienced, received.
  • So:

Soul is the endurer / receiver (bhoktā) of karma’s fruits.

5) Samjāvyu te pada → pure state / liberation exists

  • “Samjāvyu te pada namun…”:
    • I bow at the Feet of the One who explained that.
    • “te pada” = that exalted state, pure state.
  • This points to:

There is such a pure, liberated state = moksh.

6) Samjāvyu te pada → means of liberation (main & instrumental cause)

  • Liberation happens by:
    • Main cause: Understanding (samjāvyu → samjyu).
    • Instrumental cause: Sadguru’s teaching.
  • This shows:
    • Moksh upāy clearly:
      • You must understand,
      • And that understanding comes through Sadguru.

So:

The six fundamentals and the means of liberation are already hidden in verse 1, even before they are formally named in verse 43.

9. Shrimad’s clarity at age 16: Mokshmālā / Bahu Puṇya Kera

He notes:

  • Atmasiddhi is written at age 29.
  • But Shrimad’s clarity on these fundamentals goes back to age 16, in:
    • Mokshmālā, especially the stanza:

“Hu kaun chhu? Kyāthi thayo? Shu swaroop chhe māru kharu? Konā sambandhe valgana chhe? Rakhu ke e pariharu?”

He shows:

  • Five questions implicitly yield the six fundamentals:

1. Hu kaun chhu? – Who am I? → Reflecting leads to:

  • “I am not body; I am soul.” → 1st pad: soul exists.

2. Kyāti thayo? – From where have I come? → Not just mother’s womb; why this birth, this set-up? → Pondering shows:

  • I existed before, and before that, infinitely. → 2nd pad: soul is eternal.

3. Shu swaroop chhe māru kharu? – What is my true nature?

  • If my nature is:
    • Pure,
    • Peaceful,
    • Powerful,
  • Then:

“Why am I so weak, restless, impure now?”

  • Pondering reveals:
    • I chose attachment, anger, etc. (doer),
    • Now I suffer (endurer). → 3rd & 4th pad: doer & endurer.

4. Konā sambandhe valgana chhe? Rakhu ke e pariharu? – “In which connections am I entangled? What should I keep, what should I renounce?”

  • Ultimately:
    • Not Parth or Maya literally,
    • But:

“Should I keep attachment or drop attachment?”

  • Through this reflection:
    • You become clear about:
      • What is bondage,
      • What is liberation,
      • What are the means (moksh & moksh upāy).

He has a 16-year-old stand up and say (as if Shrimad):

“For your welfare, I have written these 5 questions and 6 fundamental truths.”

He concludes:

“I thought shatpad first appeared in verse 43. Then I saw it hidden in verse 1. Now I see its seed in age-16 Mokshmālā. Shrimad had this clarity very early.”

10. Final condensation of Verse 43’s role

Verse 43:

  • Simply names the six core truths:
    • Soul exists.
    • Soul is eternal.
    • Soul is doer of karma.
    • Soul is endurer of karmic fruits.
    • Liberation exists.
    • There is a path to liberation (true dharma).
  • It:
    • Prepares us mentally,
    • Gives us a map of the territory ahead,
    • Signals:

“Now, enough emotional warming-up. It’s time for honest, direct, logical study of: Who am I? What is my nature? How do I get free?”

From the next verses:

  • Each fundamental will be:
    • Questioned,
    • Debated,
    • Explained,
    • Until:
      • Doubts are removed,
      • Conviction becomes stable,
      • The seeker is empowered to reflect and ultimately realise.

In short:

Verse 43 = Title slide of the main subject: “These are the Six Fundamentals you must know, if you truly want Self-realisation and liberation.”
