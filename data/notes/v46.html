Previously:

  • Verse 45 → 1st doubt:

“I can’t see the soul, don’t know its form, can’t sense it in any way → therefore, soul does not exist.”

Now in Verse 46:

  • The disciple isn’t done yet.
  • He says: “Okay, if you insist there is something called ‘soul’, then…”

Verse 46 gives his 2nd argument, which has three options:

“Soul = body, or soul = senses, or soul = breath (prāṇ). There is nothing separate besides these.”

2. The verse’s core: “āthvā deh j ātma…”

Param Krupalu Dev, in the disciple’s voice, says (in essence):

“Athvā deh j ātma, athvā indriya ātma, athvā prāṇ ātma;
It is false to believe something separate; there is no distinct sign (endhan) of a separate soul.”

He unpacks this as three “or’s” (athvā):

1. Or – soul is body itself.

2. Or – soul is the senses.

3. Or – soul is the breath (vital force).

Then the conclusion:

“It is useless and false to believe something called soul that is separate from body, senses, and breath – because there is no sign (endhan) of such a separate entity.”

We’ll go through each part like he did.

3. Option 1: “Soul is the body itself” – living body = soul

Athvā deh j ātma – “Or, the body itself is soul.”

The disciple’s logic:

  • A living body:
    • Moves – walks, talks, eats,
    • Knows – senses, thinks, responds.
  • A dead body:
    • Doesn’t move,
    • Doesn’t know,
    • We call it a corpse.

So he says:

“Maybe ‘soul’ is just another word for living body. Dead body = corpse. Living body = ‘soul’. It’s just a language trick, not a separate substance.”

No distinct “soul-stuff” apart from body. Just:

“When the body is alive, you call it ātma;
when it is dead, you call it dead body.”

He jokes that this is exactly the sort of question doctors / medical students ask:

  • “Why separate soul and brain?”
  • “Why not say brain + body = person?”

He recalls:

  • In Idar (Ajaybhai) and Udaipur (Hiteshbhai) both asked:

“Why do we need to consider brain and soul different?”

He answered Hitesh with a practical trap:

  • If someone has a brain haemorrhage:
    • Brain is damaged, but:
    • If the heart is still beating,
    • Is the person considered alive or dead?
  • If the heart stops completely:
    • Even if brain has some activity:
    • Is the person certified as dead?

He was leading him to see that:

  • Body parts and their functions don’t exhaust the meaning of life.

But the disciple in Verse 46 is still stuck at:

“Living body = soul. Why complicate it?”

4. Option 2: “Soul is the senses” – knowing faculty = senses

Athvā indriya ātma – “Or, the senses are the soul.”

Disciple anticipates an objection:

  • “You might say:
    • ‘Knowing’ (jñān, consciousness) is the attribute of the soul.”
  • He responds:

“Fine, then the knowing faculty is senses, not something else.”

Reasoning:

  • We know through five senses:
    • Eyes see,
    • Ears hear,
    • Nose smells,
    • Tongue tastes,
    • Skin touches.
  • You can’t say:

“Legs know something is spicy.”

  • It is tongue+brain which know that.

He argues:

  • Knowing stops when senses stop functioning:
    • Close the eyes:
      • You can’t describe what hand-movement he just did.
    • Block the nose:
      • You can’t smell anything.
  • So:

“Knowledge is directly tied to senses. When senses don’t work, knowing is blocked. So the knowing faculty (consciousness) = senses. Therefore, soul is just another name for the senses.”

He even does a live test:

  • “Close your eyes; tell me what gesture I just did.” → You can’t. Conclusion in his mind:

“Eyes were knowing, not some mystical soul.”

So second fallback:

“If body is not soul, okay – let’s call the senses the soul.”

5. Option 3: “Soul is the breath” – breathing = life

Athvā prāṇ ātma – “Or, the breath is the soul.”

Disciple’s reasoning:

  • Sometimes:
    • Senses/brain stop working,
    • Person is in coma:
      • Unresponsive,
      • No clarity,
      • Yet breathing continues.
    • Doctors still certify:

“He is alive.”

  • But once breath stops:
    • Even if the body is intact,
    • We declare:

“He is dead.”

So he concludes:

“Life is identified with breath. Therefore, breath = soul. Soul is just another name for vitality / prāṇ.”

He adds:

  • Breath has immense effect on:
    • Mental state:
      • Prāṇāyām can:
        • Relieve depression,
        • Calm restlessness.
    • Physical state:
      • Appetite,
      • Digestion.
  • So breath seems like:

“The real life-force. So why invent a separate ‘soul’?”

He summarises his three options:

1. Live body = soul,

2. Or body + senses = soul,

3. Or breath = soul.

In all cases:

“Soul is not a distinct substance separate from body, senses, and breath. It’s just a label for one of these aspects.”

6. “Nahi judu endhan” – “There is no separate sign”

He focuses on an important phrase:

“Nahi judu endhan” – “There is no separate sign/evidence of a distinct soul.”

He explains:

  • Endhan = sign, indicator, evidence, characteristic.
  • We distinguish:
    • Male vs female,
    • Hindu vs Muslim,
    • Etc.
  • Similarly, to distinguish:
    • Soul vs non-soul,
    • There must be some distinct sign.

Disciple says:

“I see body, senses, breath. I see their functions. But I see no extra sign indicating a separate ‘soul-substance’. So believing in a separate soul is useless, false, futile, meaningless.”

This touches a deep point:

  • In ordinary language we say:
    • “She is a good soul,”
    • “He is a gentle soul.”
  • But what are we pointing to?
    • Body’s abilities,
    • Mind’s talents,
    • Behaviour’s refinement.
  • So the disciple concludes:

“You’re just using ‘soul’ as a poetic name for body + mind + senses + breath. There’s nothing more than that.”

He notes something important:

  • Later, in verse 54:
    • Sadguru will say:

“Pragat-rūp chaitanyamaya – e endhan sadāy.” “The manifest form of pure consciousness – that is the sign (endhan) of soul.”

  • He will answer this “no sign” claim directly, using the same word Endhan.

7. Disciple’s inner state: rich in information, poor in experience

He describes the disciple psychologically:

  • He is:
    • Full of information,
    • Proud of his knowledge,
    • But lacking rich inner experience.

He says:

“He has emptiness of experience, but that emptiness is parading around as knowledge in his mind.”

His mind is set on empirical tests:

  • If something cannot be proven:
    • Through senses (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, skin),
    • Or discursive logic,
  • Then:

“Spiritual substance does not exist.”

He emphasises:

  • Problem is not in the object (soul),
  • The problem is:

“In the mode of seeing –

he wants to perceive soul with eyes and senses only.”

He contrasts:

  • Great Enlightened Ones are called Mahādarśī / Great Seers:
    • Not because of eye-power,
    • But because they have a different kind of seeing beyond senses.

He remembers:

  • First English book he found on Shrimad:
    • “The Great Seer” – Life and Letters of Shrimad.
  • At 9th standard:
    • He searched bookshops every Saturday,
    • Looking for any book on Shrimad,
    • Found this 200-page book near Bhavan’s,
    • Felt:

“If I only have this, I can live my whole life.”

In that book:

  • Nothing is about seeing with physical eyes,
  • Yet Shrimad is called The Great Seer:

“He sees reality without needing the eyes.”

Thus:

“Disciple thinks: ‘What I can see, I know exists. What I can’t see, I can’t know. What I can’t know, doesn’t exist.’ This equation is what Sadguru is about to dismantle.”

8. Refining: matter’s juices vs consciousness, and how Guru will use it

He uses a bile analogy:

  • Liver (yakrut) secretes bile (pitta):
    • Helps digestion,
    • Too little or too much = sickness.
  • He mimics materialist thinking:

“Similarly, brain secretes consciousness. It’s just a juice like bile. If brain malfunctions (haemorrhage, coma, drugs):

  • This ‘juice’ stops,
  • So we become unconscious, ignorant. No need for ‘soul’ beyond this.”

So disciple:

  • Does accept knowledge and consciousness,
  • But insists:

“Consciousness is just one function of body–senses–brain,

not a separate substance.”

He notes:

“Guru is going to pick that point – your acceptance of consciousness – and use it to show you the true sign of soul.”

All this second argument can be summarised, as the disciple sees it, as:

“If you insist on using the word ‘soul’, fine — but for me it is only:

1. Another word for live body, or

2. Another word for senses, or

3. Another word for breath. There is no additional, separate soul-substance with its own distinct signs.”

Guruji will now respond in the coming verses, showing:

  • Where the disciple’s observation is limited,
  • Where his reasoning is flawed,
  • And what the true sign (endhan) of soul really is.

Verse 46 thus completes the disciple’s second argument:

The soul is not a separate entity;
it’s just a label for body–senses–breath;
beyond them, there is no soul.
