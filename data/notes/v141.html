This satsang is a beautiful transition from Verse 140 into Verse 141, the penultimate verse of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra.

Gurudev first reinforces the distinction between a true Jnani and a Vachajnani, then Param Krupalu Dev gives what is essentially the “homework” after studying the entire scripture:

“Sthanak panch vicharine, chhatthe varte jeh;
Pame sthanak pachmu, ema nahi sandeh.”

In essence:

Contemplate the first five Fundamental Truths. Live according to the sixth. Then you will attain the fifth—Liberation. There is no doubt about it.

The wordplay is beautiful:

Think about the Five → Act on the Sixth → Attain the Fifth.

Section 12 — Upsamhar
Verse 141: Now That You Understand Atmasiddhi — WALK THE PATH
Part I — Final Clarification of Jnanidasha

1. The greatest danger is not ignorance, but the illusion that “I know”

Gurudev begins with a fantastic analogy.

Nowadays, someone develops a medical problem.

Before going to the doctor, they search everything online.

Then they visit the doctor almost to:

check whether the doctor knows enough. 😂

Gurudev says the Shushkjnani — dry intellectualist does exactly this spiritually.

He:

  • downloads information about the soul from scriptures,
  • learns terminology,
  • learns philosophy,
  • then approaches Sadguru almost to test His knowledge.

And Gurudev gives a powerful warning:

“The greatest enemy of oneself is not ignorance, but the illusion of knowledge.”

2. Knowledge can actually make humility DECREASE

Earlier, when you knew nothing:

“Saheb, I don't know. Please explain.”

There was:

  • humility,
  • openness,
  • devotion,
  • receptivity.

Then you study.

Now:

“I know this.”

“This concept I understand.”

“I know what He's going to say.”

And slowly the very knowledge intended to liberate you can become material for:

ego.

Gurudev warns that if this isn't checked, it can progress into:

comparison → jealousy → animosity.

This directly connects to Verse 137 and the warning about Jnanino Droh.

3. This is why Gurudev says Bhakti is needed almost until the end

Gurudev says:

“Bhakti is a must… till the last second before you attain Omniscience.”

His point is that as you climb higher spiritually, you don't need less:

  • humility,
  • devotion,
  • surrender.

You need them even more, because the pitfalls of subtle ego become more dangerous.

So increasing scriptural knowledge should ideally be accompanied by:

increasing humility + increasing devotion.

Otherwise:

knowledge of words increases,

while:

experience remains zero.

That's the illusion of knowledge.

4. How do I know whether I'm a genuine seeker?

Gurudev gives a simple verification:

How important is EXPERIENCE to me?

Not:

How much can I explain?

Not:

How many scriptures have I studied?

Not:

How much terminology do I know?

The real question:

Do I actually want to experience what these scriptures are describing?

That is why Param Krupalu Dev used such strong words in verses 139–140:

  • Bhrant — deluded,
  • Vachajnan — verbal knowledge.

If Moh hasn't actually been touched, the knowledge hasn't fulfilled its purpose.

5. Don't stop Swadhyay — just don't dig a pit with it

This distinction is important.

Gurudev isn't saying:

“Stop studying scriptures because knowledge creates ego.”

Quite the opposite.

Continue:

  • satsang,
  • swadhyay,
  • scriptural study.

These help keep you on the path.

But:

Don't create a pit for yourself with the very knowledge meant to save you.

Knowledge should expose delusion.

Not decorate it.

6. Likes and dislikes blur the intellect

Gurudev returns to the prism analogy.

Just as white light passing through a prism appears as different colors, consciousness seeing through the prism of Moh experiences:

  • differences,
  • likes,
  • dislikes,
  • attachment,
  • aversion.

And once emotions arise:

they blur our intellect.

We stop seeing clearly.

We see through:

“I like this.”

“I dislike this.”

“This person should behave differently.”

rather than seeing reality as it is.

7. The dog barking vs a PERSON “barking” 😂

Gurudev gives a brilliant example.

You're walking.

A dog sees you and barks.

Do you go home devastated?

“Why did that dog disrespect me?”

No.

You understand:

That's the dog's nature.

Your intellect remains clear.

But then you go home and a person speaks rudely.

Suddenly:

“How dare they!”

Anger.

Reaction.

Hurt.

Why?

The same acceptance disappeared because:

ego and expectations entered.

Gurudev asks:

If I can understand the nature of a dog, why can't I sometimes understand the nature/ tendency of a person and respond intelligently rather than emotionally?

8. The Jnani doesn't need this intellectual correction

For us:

emotions arise → use intellect → correct the reaction.

Atmasiddhi has helped transform our:

wrong thinking → right thinking.

So Gurudev says:

Use your intellect. Consult your intellect.

Ask:

Is this actually worth getting disturbed over?

But for the Enlightened One:

experience itself has changed the vision.

Gurudev therefore prefers the word:

Prajna — wisdom rather than merely intellect.

The Jnani doesn't need to repeatedly reason:

“The world is temporary, so I shouldn't get attached.”

The world has actually lost its attractiveness in His experience.

9. “When I was intelligent, I changed the world. When I became wise, I changed myself.”

Gurudev recalls a beautiful saying attributed here to Rumi:

When I was intelligent, I went out to change the world. When I became wise, I changed myself.

And Gurudev applies it very sharply.

We may say:

“I'm doing seva.”

“I'm bringing people together.”

“I'm uplifting society.”

All beautiful.

But before becoming obsessed with Lok Kalyan — uplifting the world, ask:

What about my own transformation?

If anger, ego, likes/dislikes and delusion remain untouched while I am busy “fixing” everyone else, something is backwards.

The first field of spiritual work is:

my own attitude.

Part II — Verse 141

The Homework After Atmasiddhi Gurudev now introduces Verse 141 as the:

Upantiya Gatha — penultimate verse.

He says verses 139–140 completed the description of the Enlightened One.

Now Param Krupalu Dev essentially asks:

“You've studied the scripture. What are you going to DO with it?”

This is the final recommendation before the concluding salutation.

10. Why the Upsamhar itself feels like an independent scripture

Gurudev recalls Pandit Sukhlalji's observation that these fifteen concluding verses could almost function as an independent scripture.

And after studying them, Gurudev says we can understand why.

The Upsamhar alone contains:

  • Guru,
  • soul,
  • philosophy,
  • spiritual practice,
  • Nishchay–Vyavhar,
  • Upadan–Nimit,
  • Mumukshu,
  • pseudo-Jnani,
  • true Jnani,
  • liberation.

It's almost the entire path compressed into fifteen verses.

11. The genius of Verse 141 — 5, 6, 5

Now comes Param Krupalu Dev's beautiful play of numbers:

Contemplate FIVE.

Act according to SIX.

Attain FIVE.

Gurudev loves this.

Param Krupalu Dev could simply have written:

“Follow the path and attain Moksh.”

Instead, He compresses the entire scripture into a tiny formula.

12. What are the FIVE to contemplate?

The first five Fundamental Truths:

1. The soul exists.

2. The soul is eternal.

3. The soul is the doer of its actions.

4. The soul is the receiver/enjoyer of the fruits of karma.

5. Liberation exists.

These are things we must:

  • ponder,
  • contemplate,
  • deliberate upon,
  • understand deeply,
  • become doubtless about.

Why?

Because these are truths about what reality already is.

You don't have to make the soul eternal.

It already is.

You don't have to create liberation as a possibility.

It exists.

Your work regarding these five is to UNDERSTAND them correctly.

13. “Sthanak Panch Vicharine” — don't merely memorize them

Gurudev stresses:

Vicharine = contemplate.

Not:

“Soul exists. Eternal. Doer. Enjoyer. Moksh exists. Done.”

The purpose is to arrive at what Gurudev calls:

Cogent Clarity.

A firm, logical, doubtless conviction:

Yes—the soul exists.

Yes—it is eternal.

Yes—I am responsible for my actions.

Yes—I experience their karmic fruits.

Yes—freedom from this bondage is possible.

That conviction becomes the foundation for actual spiritual pursuit.

14. But the SIXTH cannot merely be contemplated

The Sixth Fundamental Truth is:

There is a path to liberation.

And Param Krupalu Dev changes the verb.

For the Five:

Vicharine — contemplate.

For the Sixth:

Varte — ACT.

This is the heart of the verse.

You can contemplate:

“A path exists.”

forever.

But eventually:

you have to walk it.

Gurudev says:

The Sixth is not merely for understanding; it is to be acted upon.

15. What does acting on the Sixth actually mean?

From everything we've studied, the path means:

Attachment ↓
Aversion ↓

and simultaneously:

Self-awareness ↑

Gurudev summarizes it as:

reducing attachment and aversion while progressing toward awareness/abidance in the Self.

On one side:

Raag–dwesh decrease.

On the other:

Atma-bhan increases.

That's Moksh Marg in practice.

16. Why did we need the first Five if only the Sixth must be acted upon?

Excellent question.

Why not simply say:

“Here's Moksh Marg. Go.”

Because:

Conviction fuels practice.

If I'm not convinced:

the soul exists,

it is eternal,

I am the doer,

I receive the fruits,

liberation is possible,

then why would I sincerely restructure my life around Moksh Marg?

The first Five create the:

conviction

that makes the Sixth worth living.

So:

Five → clarity

↓

clarity → conviction ↓

conviction → effort according to Sixth

↓

Sixth → Fifth

Liberation.

17. The entire Atmasiddhi journey: QUESTION → QUEST

This is probably the most beautiful observation in the satsang.

Look at how the dialogue began.

The disciple had questions:

Does the soul even exist?

Isn't it temporary?

Is it really the doer?

How can it receive karma?

Is liberation possible?

They were intellectual questions.

Sadguru answered them.

But something unexpected happened.

Once the questions were resolved…

the disciple didn't simply go home satisfied.

The questions transformed into:

a QUEST.

Now:

“I want to EXPERIENCE this soul.”

That's the real success of Atmasiddhi.

18. The goal of satsang isn't merely to eliminate questions

This is a subtle but important point.

At first:

“I have doubts. Please answer them.”

Then Guru answers them.

We might think:

Great. Finished.

But genuine understanding creates something new:

yearning.

Now the seeker thinks:

Param Krupalu Dev has described such extraordinary splendour of the Self…

I want to experience it.

So:

intellectual curiosity

becomes:

spiritual longing.

And:

questioning

becomes:

quest.

That is the transformation Gurudev wants from this entire study.

19. A clarification from yesterday — “Prashant” does NOT simply mean suppressed

Gurudev briefly returns to Verse 139 because of a question from his mother.

Yesterday we said:

Moh may be destroyed or Prashant.

Gurudev clarifies:

Prashant does not simply mean “suppressed.”

Rather, across stages of spiritual development, progressively grosser categories of delusion/passions may have been destroyed while subtler forms remain.

So the overall Moh is described as pacified/not fully destroyed until complete destruction occurs.

His broader point is that Verse 139 beautifully includes all Enlightened states, from Self-realisation through Omniscience.

This is a technical clarification rather than the main theme of Verse 141, but useful to preserve.

20. If I act on the Sixth, what happens?

Param Krupalu Dev says:

“Pame sthanak pachmu.”

You attain the Fifth.

What is the Fifth Fundamental Truth?

Moksh exists.

So you don't merely understand that liberation exists.

You attain it.

That's the beautiful play:

Contemplate the Fifth as a truth.

Then:

act on the Sixth.

Eventually:

experience the Fifth as your state.

21. “Ema nahi sandeh” — there is NO doubt

Param Krupalu Dev doesn't say:

“Maybe.”

“Hopefully.”

“If you're lucky.”

He says:

There is no doubt.

Gurudev interprets this almost like Param Krupalu Dev giving a promise:

“Do this sincerely and liberation is certain.”

Why can He say it so confidently?

Because this isn't an experiment He's trying for the first time on us.

Gurudev says:

“Self-tested.”

The Enlightened One speaks from experience of the path.

22. TIME is not the main factor — EARNESTNESS is

This is another powerful point.

Gurudev says:

It's not how long you've been on the path.

It's:

How earnest is your effort?

Someone may have attended satsang for:

  • 30 years,
  • 40 years,

and still not have reached realization.

Another may awaken with extraordinary intensity in a short time.

Gurudev references scriptural examples of attainment occurring in very short periods—even 48 minutes —because of the intensity and correctness of the effort.

So don't think:

“I'm new. I have decades to go.”

Nor:

“I've been here for decades, so I must be advanced.”

Neither time measurement tells you the spiritual state.

Earnestness matters.

23. Atmasiddhi has shown the staircase — now YOU climb

Near the end, Gurudev gives two beautiful images.

Param Krupalu Dev has:

unveiled the staircase.

But:

you have to climb it.

He has:

pointed toward the water.

But:

you have to drink it.

That is essentially the whole meaning of Verse 141.

Sadguru has done His part:

  • explained the Self,
  • removed doubts,
  • explained bondage,
  • explained Moksh,
  • explained Moksh Marg,
  • warned us about pitfalls,
  • shown us the Mumukshu,
  • shown us the Jnani.

Now:

What will I do?

24. The final formula of Verse 141

Gurudev ends with four beautiful conditions:

1. The goal is in front of you.

Moksh / complete purity of Self.

2. The path is clear.

Reduce attachment–aversion and increase Self-awareness according to Sadguru's guidance.

3. Your heart is set on it.

Not merely intellectual interest—genuine longing.

4. The journey has begun.

You're actually making Satya Purusharth.

Then:

Liberation is certain.

That, Gurudev says, is the spirit/soul of this verse. Pasted text(20261119-001331)

How the entire Upsamhar has brought us here

This is why Verse 141 feels like such a satisfying conclusion.

Param Krupalu Dev first protected us from all the ways we might misuse the teaching:

Verse 128: Don't become one-sided.

129: Treat Atmabhranti through Sadguru, Ajna, Vichar and Dhyan.

130: Make Satya Purusharth; don't hide behind excuses.

131–134: Balance Nishchay and Vyavhar.

135–136: Balance Upadan and Nimit.

Then He showed us:

137: Don't become a hypocrite whose speech exceeds his state.

138: Become a genuine Mumukshu—Daya, Shanti, Samta, Kshama, Satya, Tyag, Vairagya.

139: Jnanidasha means Moh has been destroyed or deeply pacified.

140: Therefore the world becomes like leftover food or a fleeting dream; otherwise it's Vachajnan.

And now:

141: Enough understanding. WALK.

That's the movement.

The deepest practical takeaway For me, this satsang changes how we should look at everything we've just studied.

We've spent many satsangs understanding:

  • the soul,
  • karma,
  • Moksh,
  • Sadguru,
  • Nishchay,
  • Vyavhar,
  • Upadan,
  • Nimit,
  • Jnanidasha.

But Param Krupalu Dev doesn't let the scripture end with:

“Congratulations, now you understand Atmasiddhi.”

He essentially says:

So what?

Now:

What are you going to do with it?

The first Five Fundamental Truths give us a worldview:

I am soul.

I am eternal.

My actions matter.

I experience their consequences.

Freedom is possible.

If I genuinely become convinced of those five, then the Sixth stops being an interesting religious concept.

It becomes:

urgent.

Because if:

I am eternal,

and:

my present bondage isn't inevitable,

and:

liberation genuinely exists,

then why would I continue indefinitely feeding:

  • attachment,
  • aversion,
  • ego,
  • desires,
  • body-identification?

Now I want to walk.

And I think Gurudev's:

QUESTION → QUEST

is the perfect summary of what this entire Atmasiddhi study is supposed to do.

We may have started with:

“What does this verse mean?”

“Why is the soul eternal?”

“What is Nishchay?”

“What is Upadan?”

But if the study has worked, eventually the central question changes to:

“How do I experience this?”

That is the moment Atmasiddhi stops being something we are studying and starts becoming something we are living.

And Param Krupalu Dev ends Verse 141 on an incredibly positive note:

The goal is visible.

The path is clear.

Your heart is set upon it.

You have begun walking.

Then:

“Ema nahi sandeh.”

There is no doubt—you will attain liberation.
