Your Guru says: even if the disciple only asked “Is moksh possible?”, the Guru must still hint the process, because:

  • If you ask: “Can I reach Mumbai?” The correct reply naturally includes a hint like: “Go straight, take a left from the highway, keep going, you’ll reach Mumbai.” Even if you didn’t ask for the full route.

Similarly:

  • To prove moksh is possible, the Guru must briefly show: “If you walk this way, moksh manifests.”

He adds: detailed explanation comes later in the ‘Path of Liberation’ section (22 verses).

2) A strong claim about Atmasiddhi (how he frames this entire scripture)

He says very firmly:

  • Atmasiddhi is an end to doubts.
  • This is the 6th and last doubt of the disciple; there is no 7th doubt.
  • After the reply to doubt #6, that is the end of doubts.

He makes a subtle distinction:

  • Sometimes people “ask” not because they don’t understand, but to break self-will (self-driven choices masked as spirituality).
  • But “confusion” should not remain if Atmasiddhi is properly understood.

3) Structure of Verse 90 (he makes it very clean)

He says:

  • First line = cause of transmigration
  • Second line = how to end transmigration (how moksh manifests)

So the verse is doing two jobs at once:

1. diagnosis

2. cure

4) Verse 90: The exact core teaching (with simple meaning)

Line 1: Cause of transmigration

“Vityo kaal anant te, karma shubhashubh bhaav”

Meaning:

  • Infinite time has passed (not thousands, not millions—infinite)
  • spent in shubha–ashubha bhaav:
    • engaging in auspicious + inauspicious feelings
    • which produce corresponding bondage (punya/paap chains)

Important nuance he insists on:

He is not saying you were only bad.

  • “Shubhashubh” = good and bad both
  • Example he gives:
    • Sometimes you cling to possessions (selfishness)
    • Sometimes you want to give everything in charity (generosity)

So the problem is not “only bad.” The deeper problem is: you stayed inside shubhashubh-mode itself for infinite time.

Line 2: How transmigration ends / how moksh manifests

“Teh shubhashubh chhedta, upje moksh swabhav”

Meaning:

  • By cutting / snapping / breaking / destroying shubhashubh bhaav (any of these words is acceptable—he keeps repeating synonyms)
  • the soul’s moksh swabhav arises/manifests.

What does “moksh swabhav” mean?

He clarifies carefully:

  • Moksh is a state (not a “thing”)
  • But the purity of moksh is actually your inherent nature
  • So they use the phrase “moksh swabhav”:
    • not because moksh is “your nature” as a separate object,
    • but because pure, passionless being is your natural disposition.

“Latent → Manifest” explanation

He repeats this point in simple terms:

  • Purity is already there, but latent (covered)
  • As you destroy the causes, purity emerges
  • The more causes you cut, the more purity manifests
  • When it manifests fully, that complete state is called moksh

5) The “5 senses” wake-up call (his strong, practical scolding)

He uses a humorous but sharp teaching:

He asks people to count in Spanish:

  • uno, dos, tres, cuatro, cinco

Then he says:

  • You have five sense organs now,
  • but you behave like uno (one-sensed) or worse.
  • Even a worm/insect won’t harbor this much negativity.

Purpose: To show how shocking it is that with full human capacity, we still live in impulsive, negative, unexamined patterns.

6) Guru’s “sad warning” + the correct direction of repentance

He describes a pattern:

  • You indulge, later you regret, then you ask for grace.
  • But the cosmic law will prevail (cause-effect).

He corrects the mindset:

  • Don’t get stuck in: “Why did I do that? Why did I do that?”
  • Shift to: “What should I do so I don’t repeat it?”

He allows one round of analysis:

  • Analyze once to “plug the holes”
  • Then move forward with changed action.

7) Rewards & punishments trap (how the web tightens)

He says the soul has been stuck in:

  • reward and punishment thinking

He gives a powerful twist:

  • Earlier you called fruits of shubh “rewards”
  • But now if someone says “you’ll get a high celestial birth,” you might say:
    • “Why are you punishing me? Luxuries will distract me.”

So the point is:

  • Whether you call it reward or punishment,
  • the soul remains trapped in the same web of cause-effect.

8) Listening vs introspection (he’s very firm here)

He says:

  • You can learn only by introspecting, not merely by listening.
  • Listening makes you praise the speaker.
  • Internalizing/personalizing gives real energy.

He says the energy that was used to bind karmas should be redirected into:

  • eliminating / withdrawing / retreating / restraining / refraining (he repeats these like a “poem”)
Key practical advice:
  • Begin at least by reducing:
    • intensity
    • duration
    • frequency even if you can’t totally annihilate it yet.

9) Tea / steam engine example (very practical “start where you are”)

He jokes about how dependent people are on tea:

  • “If I don’t get tea in the morning, how will my brain function?”
  • “Can a steam engine work without steam?”

Then adds a specific example:

  • Jogani (name mentioned)
    • bowel movement (peristalsis) starts only after tea
    • without tea he becomes irritable with whoever comes

Why he uses this example: To show how we are controlled by habits and impulses—so don’t wait for some “perfect spiritual moment.” Start training restraint now.

10) Don’t wait for opportunity—create willpower (very important)

He says:

  • Don’t wait for karmas to give you a perfect opportunity.
  • Karmas will not “gift” you freedom.
  • You must create that will.

So:

  • Sadguru’s satsang is important
  • A satsang atmosphere + seekers around you matter because they push you forward.

11) “Don’t praise me as divine—get inspired” (a big correction)

He quotes a dynamic with Nemi:

  • Nemi says: “You are divine.”
  • Guru replies: “Then you are not taking inspiration from me.”

Because if you label the Guru as “divine-play,” you excuse yourself:

  • “He remained detached because he’s divine, not because of effort.”

He says:

  • Don’t put me in the attic by praising me.
  • Instead say:
    • “He remained detached in that situation—I can do it too. I will do it. I am doing.”

He also mentions being inspired by seekers too:

  • sometimes you get stuck and need peer inspiration.

12) Detachment is the key (and he defines it in two meanings)

He gives a mini-teaching he “reflected on”:

Two meanings of detachment

1. Viraag = disassociation (moving away from worldly pull)

2. Special attachment to One (a higher love) = which automatically creates detachment from everything else

Then he gives two routes, each with 3 steps.

Route 1 (he calls it Nemiji’s route, “2008 route”)

1. Love (special love for One / higher ideal)

2. Detachment

3. Renunciation

Meaning: if special love is real, detachment follows, then renunciation becomes natural.

Route 2 (“2016 route”)

1. Contemplation / discrimination (right–wrong, temporary–permanent)

2. Renunciation (practice giving up / self-restraint)

3. Detachment (complete vairagya) He says: choose the route that works for you, but the key is the same:

  • freedom from selfish desires + ego

He adds a crucial warning:

  • compassion and dispassion cannot be separated otherwise spirituality becomes selfish.

13) Shubhashubh fruits are one word: transmigration

He makes a decisive simplification:

  • Auspicious fruits + inauspicious fruits → if you put both in one word = transmigration

So he says:

  • I don’t even care now whether you go to heaven or hell, because both keep you in embodied wandering.

He explains why he calls it “wandering”:

  • even if you remain in a gati for millions of years,
  • it’s still limited time,
  • then you must change “house” (body/state).

14) The “pure feelings” point (shuddh bhaav)

He clarifies vocabulary:

  • shubhashubh bhaav = (in this context) he calls it “impure”
  • sh◌ुद्ध bhaav (shuddh bhaav) = soul-consciousness / passionless purity

He states clearly:

  • if you engage in shuddh bhaav, you receive the fruit of liberation

And he repeats:

  • Teh shubhashubh chhedta → upje moksh (leave the wording “swabhav” for a moment; the essence is: moksh manifests)

15) Direct answer to the disciple’s logic: “Why not liberated in infinite time?”

The disciple’s doubt:

  • infinite past + impurity still present → so moksh seems impossible

Guru’s answer is extremely direct:

  • Because the soul never attempted.

Meaning:

  • the soul didn’t put effort into:
    • withdrawing from impure feelings
    • abiding in the Self
    • reducing engagement

He repeats many verbs to hammer it in:

  • abandon / refrain / destroy / retire / retreat, etc.

Then he challenges them:

  • “Now that you heard it, let me check your next hour.”
  • The ears love listening, but the soul doesn’t attempt.

16) Weight-loss analogy (his “mathematics” example)

He uses overweight as an analogy for liberation effort:

He says:

  • Don’t say “destiny.”
  • Weight changes by two efforts:

1. stop adding more

2. remove what is already there

Examples:

  • “I exercise so much”—but what about the cheese pizza?
  • “I eat only two items”—but you don’t exercise.
  • “You walk like a pregnant woman… she would reach before you.”
  • “You walk only to put a tick mark.”
  • Put on a watch and see calories burned: simple math.

Point: Liberation is also “math” (cause-effect), not magic.

17) “Grace is not magic” + “crying is not devotion”

He draws a hard line:

  • Grace = for inspiration and clarity
  • Grace is not a word for magic
  • You can’t ask God to violate the cosmic law:
    • “I will keep impure feelings, but please smile at me.”

He says strongly:

  • Singing songs and crying is not devotion (by itself)
  • Emotional display is not the proof
  • Real devotion is in:
    • feelings
    • conduct
    • following commands

He even says:

  • “Don’t sing any song, don’t worship—just follow his commands. That is devotion.” (He is emphasizing obedience and transformation over emotion.)

18) “If I withdraw, will I become stone?” (important confusion he answers)

He anticipates a fear:

  • If I withdraw from feelings, will I become numb/stone-like?

Answer:

  • No. What manifests is pure feelings:
    • passionless feelings
    • no karma influence on feelings
  • Purity is your nature
  • Impure feelings are a distorted state, not your nature

He explains again in “latent → manifest” language:

  • pure nature was latent
  • withdrawing from impure allows it to manifest
  • full manifestation = liberation

19) Samvar & Nirjara (technical terms made simple)

He gives both:

  • Technical words:

1. Samvar = stoppage of influx of new karmas

2. Nirjara = shedding of old karmas

  • Simple explanation:

1. stop incurring new bondage

2. shed old karmas He says:

  • This is dharma: samvar–nirjara

20) Major myth he attacks: “Merits (punya) give liberation”

He says this is widespread in religious communities:

  • they talk charity, austerity, worship
  • which are largely auspicious activity (punya)

He is not saying “don’t do charity.” He is saying:

  • Punya can give comforts, luxuries, opportunities, pleasures
  • but it cannot give liberation.

He repeats the core:

  • Liberation is not the result of any karma.
  • Liberation is the result of destruction of all karmas.

He ties it back to the verse:

  • If auspicious were the path, why would Krupalu Dev say:
    • destroy both auspicious and inauspicious?

So he reframes:

  • merits cause influx (bondage)
  • dharma = samvar (withdrawal / nivrutti)

21) “Punya–Paap chhedta” + Navkar mantra homework

He translates shubhashubh into 2 Ps:

  • shubh = punya
  • ashubh = paap

So verse meaning becomes:

  • “By destroying punya and paap, liberation manifests.”

Then he gives homework:

  • In Navkar mantra it says: “savva pavappanasano” (destroys demerits)
  • Why does it mention paap, not punya, if liberation requires annihilating both?
  • He assigns this as thinking work.

22) The water analogy (his clearest “nature vs distortion” teaching)

He asks:

  • Nature of water? Coolness.
  • What do you do to keep it cool? Nothing—that is its nature.

Then:

  • Have you seen boiling water? Yes.
  • Boiling is not the nature—it is a distorted state.

Why does water boil?

  • Because it is put on the stove (instrumental cause)

How to cool it again?

Two options (he jokes about personality types):

  • “Lift pot and keep aside” (hard-working practical type)
  • “Just switch off the stove and wait” (MBA/brainy type)

Either way:

  • remove the heat-source → water returns to coolness on its own.

Mapping to spirituality

  • Water = soul
  • Wood/log / stove fuel = karmas
  • Fire/heat = passions, likes-dislikes
  • Boiling = impure/distorted state
  • Switching off stove = withdrawing from passions
  • Coolness returning = moksh swabhav manifesting

He warns:

  • Don’t think only in external/instrumental terms. The real fire is your passions.

So:

  • withdraw from likes-dislikes (teh nivrutti)
  • then safalta happens (fruitfulness)
  • the soul returns to its natural disposition → liberation

23) Final conclusion of the video (what he wants you to leave with)

He ends by summarizing today’s learning:

  • Transmigration cause: engaging in shubhashubh bhaav
  • End of transmigration: destroy/withdraw from shubhashubh bhaav
  • Dharma is not only activity. Dharma is also (and deeply): withdrawing from shubhashubh.

And he says:

  • With Guru’s association + satsang, change your approach to happiness/suffering and dharma.
  • Then sing the verse.

Two-step method:

1. Stop new bondage (samvar)

2. Shed old karmas (nirjara)

Water lesson:

  • Just remove the “stove” (passions), purity returns.
