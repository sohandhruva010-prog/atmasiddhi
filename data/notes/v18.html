Verse 18 (“Mānadī śatru mahā…”) — the easy, unfailing way to destroy inner enemies (especially pride).

One-line essence: The deadliest obstacles to liberation are inside—led by pride (mān) and its clan. You cannot uproot them by yourself (nij chhande). The unfailing remedy is to take refuge in a living Sadguru. In that refuge, these enemies fall away with little effort (alp prayāse). This verse bridges the previous three verses on swachhand (self-will, 15–17) and sets up the next two on vinay (humility, 19–20).

1) How Verse 18 fits the flow of the text

  • Verses 15–17: on swachhand (self-willed behavior).
    • Swachhand is a form of ego (mān).
    • “Swachhand, mat-āgrah” (self-will and opinionated obstinacy) block progress.
  • Verse 18: shows how to destroy that ego and its allies—not by solitary effort but by Sadguru-refuge.
  • Verses 19–20: move to vinay (humility): as mān goes, humility arises; humility is key for liberation.

Bridge statement: Pride connects backward to swachhand (15–17) and forward to humility (19–20). When mān goes, vinay comes.

2) Parsing the verse (word by word)

  • Mānadī = mān (“pride/ego”) + ādi (“etc.” → the rest of the passions).
    • “Ādi” covers the whole inner lineup—anger, deceit, greed, lust, jealousy, delusion, etc.
    • Two lists he referenced:
      • Vedāntic śadripu (six foes): lust, anger, pride, delusion, greed, envy/deceit.
      • Jain four kashāyas: anger (krodh), pride (mān), deceit (māyā), greed (lobh).
  • Śatru = enemies/foes.
  • Mahā = mighty, deadly, hard to remove.
  • Nij chhande = by one’s own effort (on your own steam); not “swachhande” here, but the idea is similar—your own doing.
  • Na maray = cannot be destroyed.
  • Jatā Sadguru śaraṇmā = by going to the refuge of a Sadguru (implied living Sadguru: the verb tense “jatā” points to present, living refuge, not just textual study).
  • Alp prayāse = with little/minimal effort—practically effortlessly.

3) Why “mān (pride)” is named first

  • The verse could have said “anger etc.” or “greed etc.” and meant the same group, but pride is:
    • Strongest among the four kashāyas.
    • The hardest to uproot.
    • The closest link to swachhand (self-importance, attachment to “my” views).
  • Swachhand = a type of ego.
    • Attachment to my opinions, my moods, my way.
    • “I feel it is this way.” (Teacher’s blunt aside: Be quiet—what you feel is not the point; you are not the measure of truth.)
    • The sting: “You think you are very important; no one else (family, society, saints, the Liberated) thinks so.”

4) The real obstacle: inside, not outside

  • Beginners assume the obstacles (bādhā) to Self-realisation are external—people, situations, logistics (“If this trip happens, my sādhanā will suffer,” etc.).
  • The Enlightened point out: the deadliest enemies live within.
  • Arihant literally means “destroyer of inner enemies.” There are no “outer” enemies on this path.

5) How strong are these enemies? The “HABIT” memory trick

He likens kashāyas to a habit:

  • HABIT → remove H = A BIT remains.
  • Remove A = BIT remains.
  • Remove B = IT remains.
  • Remove I = T remains (“tea”—the pun: you still want tea in the morning!). Point: Even when you stop expressing anger, the anger-seed may still remain. These instincts are tenacious.

6) Why self-effort alone can’t finish them

  • “Nij chhande na maray” — by your own effort they won’t die.
    • Even if you exert mightily, a new ego forms around your effort (“Look how hard I’m working on myself!”).
    • You get trapped in your own loop—convinced you’re right.

7) The unfailing, easy remedy: Sadguru-refuge

  • “Jatā Sadguru śaraṇmā” — go to the living Sadguru’s refuge. Not any random advice; not “elders”; not even scriptures alone.
  • Taking refuge ≠ turning up with folded hands. Taking refuge =
    • Understanding His insights,
    • Observing His state,
    • Following His ājñā (instructions/commands).
  • This “refuge” motif is universal:
    • Gītā 18.66: sarva-dharmān parityajya… śaraṇaṁ vraja (Take refuge in Me).
    • Buddhist: Buddhaṁ śaraṇaṁ gacchāmi.
    • Jain: Arihante śaraṇaṁ pavajjāmi. Message: Refuge is the way.

8) What “alp prayāse” looks like (real-life feel)

He stresses: in true refuge, the inner enemies go with minimal effort—often it feels effortless.

  • People report: “It just fell away; I didn’t even feel I’d done anything.”
  • Examples he gave (humorous & concrete):
    • Quitting alcohol → sipped apple cider, later coffee, now too busy writing notes—and you don’t even miss it.
    • Stopping root vegetables—effortless.
    • “American way of thinking” softening on its own (he teased a devotee about her Phoenix home with orange/grapefruit trees—“Fruits will come from trees, right?”). Even the urge to keep that lifestyle loosens.
  • No pride, no drama: not “I did it!”—just forgotten. That’s alp prayāse in action.

9) Why pride is the worst blocker

  • Pride won’t let you go to a Sadguru, much less understand or obey Him.
  • Meritorious karmas (good fortune) and ignorance can mask your misery, so you don’t see the problem.
  • Only when ego weakens a bit do you feel “I need a Sadguru’s insight and ājñā.”

10) Signs the medicine is working

In Sadguru-refuge, two movements run together:

1. Elimination of flaws: swachhand reduces; anger, pride, deceit, greed lose bite.

2. Manifestation of virtues:

  • Vinay (humility) – centerpiece of verses 19–20.
  • Vivek (discriminative clarity) – “what to think/speak/do—and what not to.”

You actually see: swachhand going, humility & vivek coming.

11) The two big themes of the introduction (recap of the larger section)

From earlier in the “upodghāt” (introduction) that runs to this point:

1. Two kinds of delusion (verses 3–8): people who look religious yet aren’t on the path of liberation.

2. Greatness of a Sadguru (verses 9–18): the centrality of living guidance.

Verse 18 sits in this Sadguru-centric arc and completes it.

12) Practical understanding of “Go to the Sadguru”

  • Not: “I paid a visit.”
  • Is: “I receive His insight, watch His inner posture, and follow His ājñā.”
  • That obedience (with faith, love, surrender) creates the alchemical condition where passions dissolve easily.

13) Why “external obstacles” lose their grip

  • Under Enlightened guidance, you realise: “Those aren’t real obstacles.”
  • The only deadly obstacles are inner—and they manifest in many forms (mān, krodh, māyā, lobh, lust, jealousy).

14) Memorable lines & hooks from the talk

  • HABIT mnemonic: H → A BIT → BIT → IT → T(tea!) — the residue remains unless grace intervenes.
  • “You cannot kill ego by yourself… because you’ll grow an ego of effort.”
  • “Jatā Sadguru śaraṇmā—that’s the unfailing solution.”
  • “Alp prayāse—you’ll say it felt effortless.”
  • “Swachhand will not let you go to the living Sadguru. Only death (or another birth) might show you what you missed.”

15) Take-home practice (implied by the talk)

  • Spot the inside job: Each time you blame a person/situation, ask: “What inner enemy is this scratching?”
  • One act of refuge today: Seek a clarification or instruction from the Sadguru (or His living representative/teaching), and do it.
  • Watch for effortless shifts: Notice any habit softening without fanfare. Guard against “ego of effort.”
  • Cultivate vinay & vivek: Consciously choose a humble response and a discriminating thought/speech/action in one situation today.

16) Compact recap (one breath)

Verse 18 teaches: Pride (with its entire clan) is the mighty inner enemy blocking liberation. You cannot finish it alone. Go to the living Sadguru’s refuge—understand, observe, obey—and then, with little effort, these enemies fall away, while humility and discernment appear. This completes the Sadguru section (9–18) and opens the path of vinay (19–20).

17) Key vocabulary (quick lookup)

  • Mān — pride/ego.
  • Ādi — “etc.” (the rest of the passions).
  • Śatru mahā — mighty enemies.
  • Nij chhande — by one’s own efforts.
  • Jatā… śaraṇmā — going to the refuge (of the Sadguru).
  • Alp prayāse — with little effort.
  • Swachhand — self-will; attachment to “my” views/moods/way.
  • Ājñā — the Master’s instruction/command.
  • Vinay — humility.
  • Vivek — discriminative knowledge.
  • Arihant — destroyer of inner enemies.

18) Where the talk ended

Having established the remedy (Sadguru-refuge) and its promise (alp prayāse), he said, “We’ll sing,” closing the exposition of Verse 18 and preparing the heart for the humility verses (19–20).
