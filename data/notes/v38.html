So far (verses 34–37) we’ve seen the inner world of an ātmārthī:

  • What he thinks of a Sadguru,
  • How he wants to surrender,
  • His conviction about the one supreme path,
  • His single-pointed purpose (kaam ek ātmārthnu).

Now Param Krupalu Dev goes one step further:

“You’ve seen the heart of a true seeker. But let Me now give you a clear checklist – so you can honestly see if you are an ātmārthī or not.”

This verse is meant for:

  • Self-diagnosis, not to judge others.
  • You can look for these traits in others (nice if you find many true seekers),
  • But mainly:

“This is for you to check yourself.”

He had given attributes of a bigot earlier in verse 32:

  • Those were “common attributes” of matārthīs:
    • Mechanical ritualists,
    • Dry intellectualists.

Now verse 38 is the mirror:

“What are the attributes of a true seeker?” And again, He doesn’t just say:

“Whatever is lacking in a bigot is present in an ātmārthī.” Instead, He gives a positive, direct list of virtues.

Example:

  • Bigot verse: “Nahi kashay upshānttā” – no subsidence of passions.
  • Here: “Kashayni upshānttā” – passions are subsiding.

Same field, opposite state.

2. The verse & its four qualities

The verse (in essence):

Kashayni upshānttā, matra moksh abhilāsh;
Bhavey khed, pranidayā, tya ātmārth nivās.

He unpacks four clear attributes:

1. Kashayni upshānttā – Subsidence of passions (anger, pride, deceit, greed are weakening).

2. Matra moksh abhilāsh – Only desire for liberation (“matra / only” is the real tapas here).

3. Bhavey khed – Grief for transmigration – deep tiredness of wandering in births and deaths, and of the causes of it (our faults).

4. Pranidayā – Compassion for all beings – not just for “my people”, but for all who have life.

And then:

Tya ātmārth nivās “There is the abode / house of Self-realisation.”

Or, very practically:

“Where these four are present – there lives true seeking. This is the ID of an ātmārthī.”

3. The “house” of Self-realisation – 4 walls, 1 floor, open roof

He plays with the word nīvās (house, abode):

  • Just as:
    • Sādhanā-nivās – building for sādhanā,
    • Devchandra-nivās – building named after Devchandbhai,
  • Here: Ātmārth nivās – the house where Self-realisation can reside.

A house needs:

  • Four walls → these four virtues.
  • Floor → he hints the 5th factor (faith in Sadguru) will come later (verse 40).
  • Roof? → Not needed:

“We just want open sky of grace, grace, grace…”

So the picture is:

Self-realisation is like a guest of honour. It will only “move in” to a heart-house that has:

  • Pacified passions,
  • Only desire for moksh,
  • Grief for bondage,
  • Universal compassion.

And he’s very strong:

“One who doesn’t have these virtues cannot even hope for Self-realisation.”

That’s not to discourage – it’s to force honesty.

  • Hoping for Self-realisation without cultivating these → he calls it almost a sin: “Then you just want a miracle. And it won’t happen.”

Also:

  • We want infinite grace,
  • But don’t want to cultivate even four virtues.

He pokes:

“Is this what you call devotion?”

So:

These four = basic requirement to be called a real mumukshu / ātmārthī. White clothes, forehead marks, mālās – do not make you a true seeker by themselves.

4. Where did these four come from? The five classical factors

He anticipates the doubt:

“From where did He pick exactly these four?”

He answers from Jain/letter teaching:

  • Many scriptures (and Letter 135) mention five factors needed for Self-realisation:

1. Sham – calming of passions.

2. Samveg – spiritual urgency / impetus.

3. Nirved – deep disillusion / weariness of samsār.

4. Anukampā – compassion for all.

5. Āstha – faith (especially in Sadguru and true path).

Verse 38:

  • Gives us the first four:
    • Sham → kashayni upshānttā
    • Samveg → matra moksh abhilāsh
    • Nirved → bhavey khed
    • Anukampā → pranidayā

The 5th – āstha – will come in verse 40, after Sadguru has been fully described and met.

So if you know the five factors list, verse 38 is very transparent.

5. 1st Attribute – Kashayni upshānttā (Sham)

Kashay = passions in Jain language:

  • Anger (krodh),
  • Pride / conceit (mān),
  • Deceit / masking (māyā),
  • Greed (lobh),
  • And their subtler forms.

Question: What to do with them?

Not utilise them “creatively” – but:

Weaken them, pacify them, calm them down.

He points to our experience:

  • When you are in kashay:
    • You feel impure,
    • Your mind is scattered & defocused,
    • No good thoughts come,
    • Sādhanā, study, seva – everything becomes dull.

If you want to use your mind in Guru’s ajna, that mind must be:

  • Happy,
  • Pure,
  • Focused.

Pacifying passions gives two big advantages:

1. No disturbance / impurity / distraction

  • You’re not constantly hijacked by:
    • Mood,
    • Hurt,
    • Ego spikes,
    • Greedy pulls.

2. Virtues blossom naturally

  • When anger subsides → forgiveness appears.
  • When pride subsides → humility / softness comes.
  • When deceit subsides → simplicity & innocence shine.
  • When greed subsides → the mind is free of “a thousand wants” and can hold one pure aim.

He says:

“If you can apologise quickly, you feel so good. Maybe the other person is slow – but you feel free.”

He also gives a strict warning:

  • If you are:
    • Impulsive (āveshī),
    • Acting on trivial whims and petty pleasures,
    • At the mercy of your moods,

Then:

“Salvation is just not possible. You shouldn’t even hope for Self-realisation.”

Not because God is cruel, but because:

  • Without calming passions, the inner space where truth can shine never opens.

Scriptural label:

  • This kashayni upshānttā is Sham – the first factor.

6. 2nd Attribute – Matra moksh abhilāsh (Samveg)

Meaning:

“Only desire for liberation.”

He jokes:

  • Put “only” everywhere:
    • Only desire,
    • Desire only,
    • Only liberation.

This is what makes the seeker genuine.

He clarifies:

  • It’s not that:
    • Life has gone badly,
    • He’s fed up with misery,
    • So he wants escape.
  • In fact, everything can be good:
    • Good spouse, parents, health,
    • Comfort, luxury, desires being fulfilled.

Yet, inwardly he sees:

  • This happiness is temporary,
  • It is dependent on external conditions,
  • It’s fragile – one event, one message, one loss and it collapses.

Example he gives:

  • Hemal is happy with Rushabh – and Bapa is happy she is happy.
  • Still, deep inside:
    • There can be a fear:
      • “What if he dies before me?”
      • “What if he meets an accident?”
      • “What if he starts liking someone else?”
  • So long as happiness is dependent, there is anxiety at the base.

Similarly:

  • When you lose someone dear:
    • You continue living, laugh also,
    • But underlying sorrow remains,
    • Showing how fragile that happiness was.

From all this, the ātmārthī concludes:

“Unobstructed, imperishable bliss is only possible within, because there it doesn’t depend on anything outside.”

That purest state of the soul is called moksh.

So:

“I want nothing else. Not more friends, not accomplishments, not objects. I want the purest state of my own Self.”

He makes an important psychological distinction:

  • If someone:
    • Doesn’t like outer things,
    • And also has no spark for inner liberation,
  • That’s not spiritual – that’s depression.

He categorises:

  • Worldly:
    • Likes only outside.
  • True seeker:
    • Likes inside; outside is secondary.
  • Depressed:
    • Likes neither outside nor inside.

Whenever he sees detachment, he checks:

“Is it true dispassion, or is this person just shut down and depressed?”

Test he uses:

  • Does the person:
    • Sparkle in Guru’s presence,
    • Light up in seva, satsang, sādhanā?
  • If not, then:
    • It’s not detachment; it’s numbness.

And he adds:

Absence of this strong longing for liberation = presence of worldly desires, whether you can detect them or not.

Scriptural name:

  • Samveg – intense spiritual impetus.
  • Easy phrase: matra moksh abhilāsh.

7. 3rd Attribute – Bhavey khed (Nirved)

He calls on Shiv: “Bhavey khed” =

Grief for transmigration, sorrow over birth–death wandering.

He links it to the bhajan (tune of Kati Patang):

“Jag mā janam kalank sam lāge mane” – “Taking birth again in this world feels like a stain to me.”

This seeker wants Pūrna Swarāj:

  • Complete freedom:
    • From births and deaths,
    • From the causes of them:
      • Inner enemies,
      • Faults and flaws.

Because of his understanding:

  • His view of sensual pleasures shifts:
    • He starts feeling tired of them.

He notes a practical sign:

  • You may still:
    • Do business 8 hours a day,
    • Work in hospital, run your profession.
  • But you feel:
    • A loss of interest in it compared to before,
    • While love for satsang & sādhanā has increased.

He teases:

  • Dr. Mansi & Dr. Hitesh were joking about anatomy and forgot where the heart is:
    • “That’s a good state – you’re losing head-space in that and gaining in spirituality!”
    • (But then he says: revise, or don’t work in hospital!)

He says:

The seeker starts seeing sensual pleasures as a disease.

For example:

  • One more party:
    • Feels like a waste of 2–3 hours.
    • So much mental noise:
      • “What will I wear?”
      • “What gift to take?”
      • “Need to keep up with gossip…”
  • He just doesn’t feel like getting entangled anymore.

He wants to cross the ocean of bhav:

  • If told “in 15 births” – he’s not okay with it.
  • His feeling:

“I don’t even want one more birth.”

Scriptural term:

  • This deep weariness of samsār is Nirved.
  • Simple phrase: bhavey khed.

He even mentions a boy from Ladakh:

  • Who had strong samveg and nirved,
  • He started calling him Nirved lovingly.
  • Now the boy:
    • Works in his region to free others from addictions,
    • Makes them chant Sahajatma Swaroop,
    • Shares satsang – even if not under official “Mission” label.

8. 4th Attribute – Pranidayā (Anukampā)

Meaning: Compassion for all beings.

Krish answers this one.

He clarifies:

  • Everyone has compassion for some people:
    • Family, close friends.
  • But the verse doesn’t say:
    • “Love for family,”
    • It says prani-dayā – compassion for all who have life.

Ideal feeling:

“No one in the world should suffer. What I want for myself, I want for everyone.”

He tells the sweet chips story:

  • He went to Community Gardens with 3–4 snack packets.
  • About 8–10 kids were nearby.
  • He gave those packets to Tia:

“Share among these kids.”

Within minutes:

  • A message goes on the kids’ WhatsApp group: “Bapa has sent prasād, all of you come!”
  • Then another group (Kirtan’s group) also gets the message.
  • But there were just 4 packets – not enough for 40–50 kids.

He scolds Nanu: “This was only for the ones in front of me – not for mass distribution!”

And her response is beautiful:

  • She doesn’t defend:
    • Starts with “Sorry, Bapa, I apologise.”
  • Then she shares her inner thought: “Our subject today is compassion and love. I see everyone like myself. What I wish for me, I wish for all. I don’t want anyone to suffer.”

He appreciates the root feeling but clarifies the minimum standard of pranidayā:

“At the very least, I will see that no one suffers because of me.”

So:

  • If someone is unhappy due to their own karmas:
    • He’ll do his best to help pull them out.
  • But minimum vow:
    • “Let them not be unhappy because of me.”
    • That much alertness & sensitivity is part of pranidayā.

He links this with the four bhāvanās:

1. Maitrī – friendliness to all.

2. Pramod – joy in others’ goodness.

3. Madhyasthata – indifference / neutrality to others’ negativity.

4. Karunā / Anukampā – compassion for the suffering.

When these fuel your compassion:

“Then you are ready for Self-realisation.”

He notes:

  • Softness in words and behaviour is only possible if there’s softness in thoughts.
  • Without compassion in the mind:
    • You can’t stay artificially polite forever,
    • Sooner or later the rudeness will spill out.

Scriptural name:

  • Anukampā – compassion.
  • Simple phrase: pranidayā – compassion for all beings.

9. The real mumukshu: not white clothes, but these four

He jokes:

  • In the ashram:
    • White clothes → you’re labelled “mumukshu”.
    • Coloured clothes → you’re a “visitor”.
  • Once Bhavin wore coloured clothes and got pushed into the “visitor” category.

But he says clearly:

“You do not become a mumukshu by your dress, tilak, or mālā. You become a true seeker when these four virtues are cultivated.”

Summing them up:

1. Kashayni upshānttā – calming of passions (Sham).

2. Matra moksh abhilāsh – only desire for liberation (Samveg).

3. Bhavey khed – grief of transmigration; tired of bondage (Nirved).

4. Pranidayā – compassion for all beings (Anukampā).

These:

  • Prepare the soil of the heart,
  • So that fruits of wisdom – Self-realisation – can grow.

Without them:

“Don’t dream of some miracle enlightenment. First, make your heart into ātmārth nivās – the house fit for Self-realisation to live in.”

That’s verse 38: The identity card of a true seeker.
