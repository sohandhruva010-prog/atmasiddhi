Absolutely. This satsang is especially important because it is essentially the conclusion of the Guru–disciple dialogue in this section of Atmasiddhi. The disciple has already expressed gratitude, offered himself to Gurudev, and declared himself the servant of the servant of His servant.

Now he looks back at the entire journey and identifies the single greatest thing Gurudev has done for him:

“Shat sthanak samjavine, bhinna batavyo Aap;
Myan thaki talvarvat, e upkar amaap.”

In essence:

“By explaining the Six Fundamental Truths, YOU revealed to me that I am separate from the body, just as a sword is separate from its sheath. This favour of YOURS is immeasurable.” Pasted text(20261107-182646)

That is really the entire satsang: What was the ultimate purpose of everything Gurudev taught? To take me from “I am this body” to the direct conviction and experience of “I am the pure soul.”

Atmasiddhi — “Bhinna Batavyo Aap”

1. The disciple is still continuously remembering Guru's upkar

Gurudev begins by saying the disciple is:

  • reminiscing,
  • recalling,
  • repeating,
  • recounting

the countless favours of his Sadguru.

His heart is overflowing with reverence.

The previous verses were:

“Aho! Aho! What immense upkar!”

↓

“What can I possibly offer YOU?”

↓

“From this moment, everything of mine is YOURS.”

↓

And now:

“What exactly is the greatest thing YOU did for me?”

The answer:

YOU showed me who I actually am. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

2. The essence of the entire Atmasiddhi

Gurudev says something extremely important:

Why did Sadguru explain all Six Fundamental Truths?

Why this entire dialogue?

Why all these verses?

Why all these arguments?

Why all these examples?

Why this entire Yajna?

Ultimately:

“Bhinna batavyo Aap.”

YOU showed me that I am separate from the body.

Gurudev calls this the:

  • essence,
  • gist,
  • purport,
  • main purpose

of Sadguru's teachings and of Shri Atmasiddhi Shastra itself. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

He even says that if you couldn't follow every verse and every philosophical argument, remember this much:

“Bhinna batavyo Aap.”

Because this contains the destination.

3. What were the Six Fundamental Truths ultimately doing?

Gurudev briefly revises them:

1. The soul exists.

2. The soul is eternal.

3. The soul is the doer.

4. The soul is the enjoyer.

5. Liberation exists.

6. There is a sure path to liberation.

Sadguru explained these from different viewpoints and angles until the disciple's doubts disappeared.

But Gurudev says the purpose wasn't merely:

“Now I know six correct philosophical statements.”

The Six Truths were taking the disciple toward right conviction regarding himself. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

That distinction matters enormously.

4. Knowing that “a soul exists” isn't enough

Gurudev makes a subtle point:

The soul existed whether you believed in it or not.

So merely saying:

“Yes, yes, I believe souls exist.”

isn't the accomplishment.

The transformation is:

Right faith regarding MYSELF has arisen.

Not:

“There is something called a soul.”

But:

“I AM that soul.”

The Guru establishes this conviction in the disciple.

And Gurudev says that is what is worth embracing from all the philosophy, logic, doubts, answers and examples. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

5. The attitude that should permeate EVERYTHING

Gurudev calls the most crucial thing an attitude of Self-awareness.

It shouldn't exist only during:

  • meditation,
  • satsang,
  • prayer,
  • scriptural study.

Its “fragrance” should permeate:

  • thoughts,
  • likes/dislikes,
  • decisions,
  • behavior,
  • internal behavior,
  • external behavior,
  • role-playing,
  • everyday activities.

Gurudev even asks:

While you're eating, do you realize that you are the soul?

From the worldly perspective:

“I am eating.”

But from the spiritual perspective:

I am the knower.

The body is engaged in the activity.

My fundamental nature is knowingness. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

6. The enormous shift: “I am body” → “I am soul”

Gurudev calls the difference vast:

Before:

I am the body.

Therefore:

What happens to body → happens to me.

What people say about body → affects me.

Pleasure through body → my happiness.

Pain to body → my misery.

Appearance → my worth.

Status → my identity.

Associations → mine.

After:

I am the pure, peaceful, blissful soul.

The body becomes something associated with me, but not what I fundamentally am.

This is the conviction Gurudev wants to permeate the disciple's entire life. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

7. Devotion isn't for unintelligent people Gurudev then connects this with the previous verse about complete surrender.

Some people may think:

“Bhakti is emotional. Knowledge is for intellectual people.”

Gurudev completely rejects this.

He says the path of devotion is for:

the most intelligent AND the most brave.

Why intelligent?

Because you have to recognize what is truly worth surrendering to.

Why brave?

Because genuine surrender means giving up:

your own insistence.

Gurudev says:

“Surrendership is needed first.”

It takes courage to genuinely say:

“Not what I want. What YOU want.” Pasted text(20261107-182646)

8. Kabirji's wife and the lamp in broad daylight

Gurudev gives an example to illustrate extraordinary surrender/devotion.

A young man asks Kabirji:

Should I marry and live as a householder, or become an ascetic/celibate?

Kabirji doesn't immediately lecture him.

Around noon, in broad daylight, he calls his wife and asks her to bring a lamp to look for his shirt.

There are two absurdities:

  • it's broad daylight, so no lamp is needed;
  • Kabirji apparently only had the shirt he was already wearing.

Yet she simply brings the lamp and searches.

Gurudev's point is her absence of argumentative ego.

She doesn't immediately begin:

“Why?” “That makes no sense.” “It's not my fault.” “Why should I?” “Can't you see?”

Gurudev contrasts that level of devotion with our tendency to constantly defend our own position. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

9. The 91-year-old disciple climbing the hill THREE times

Kabirji then shows the young man another example.

He calls an elderly disciple—around 90–91 years old—from a hut at the bottom of a hill.

The elderly man slowly climbs up with his stick.

Kabirji says essentially:

“Never mind. It was nothing.”

He goes back down.

Then Kabirji calls:

“Wait, I remembered! Come back.”

He climbs again.

Again:

“Never mind.”

And Gurudev says this happens three times.

The point isn't the physical hill.

It's the disciple's:

  • faith,
  • devotion,
  • dedication,
  • absence of “Why are YOU wasting my time?”

Gurudev is illustrating what surrender looks like when the “I” stops demanding justification for everything. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

10. “After everything I've surrendered, I've still given YOU almost nothing”

This is beautiful.

The Atmasiddhi disciple has already said:

My body, mind, speech, wealth—everything is YOURS.

We might think:

Wow. He gave everything.

But what does he think?

Essentially:

“YOU gave me the best. I have given YOU something inferior.”

Gurudev points out that even surrender benefits the disciple himself spiritually.

So he still doesn't feel:

“Look how much I've done for Guru.”

Instead:

“Even after giving everything, how could it compare to what YOU gave me?” Pasted text(20261107-182646)

This connects strongly to Gurudev's earlier teaching:

If you're counting your seva, you haven't understood the immeasurable upkar.

11. Guru did THREE increasingly deeper things

This part is very important.

Gurudev essentially identifies three levels:

1. Guru explained it.

He taught the nature of the soul.

↓

2. Guru dispelled my doubts.

Now intellectually I have no confusion.

↓

3. Guru enabled me to EXPERIENCE it.

And Gurudev says this third one is the most important.

The disciple isn't merely saying:

“I understand that the soul and body are theoretically different.”

He's saying:

“I experience myself as different from the body.” Pasted text(20261107-182646)

That's why the gratitude is so enormous.

12. The sword and sheath example

This is the central analogy of the entire lecture:

Body = sheath
Soul = sword

Gurudev spends significant time unpacking why this is such a powerful analogy.

A king may have a sheath made from:

  • gold,
  • jewels,
  • diamonds,
  • expensive decoration.

It may look beautiful.

Everyone sees it.

But in actual battle:

The sheath isn't what matters.

The sword inside might simply be made from steel.

Not glamorous.

Not visible.

But:

THAT is what actually matters. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

13. Why is the body like the sheath?

The body is:

Visible.

You can see:

  • height,
  • skin,
  • beauty,
  • strength,
  • weakness,
  • sex,
  • health,
  • age.

Like the decorative sheath.

But the soul?

Not visible through the physical senses.

Yet it is the actual conscious entity.

Gurudev says it can be experienced through spiritual insight — Atmadrishti.

So:

What is visible is not necessarily what is most valuable.

The sheath catches the eye.

The sword has the actual significance.

Likewise:

body catches our attention.

soul is what we actually are. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

14. Beautiful body? Ugly body? Tall? Short? Six-pack?

Gurudev then deliberately makes this practical.

The body can be:

  • attractive,
  • unattractive,
  • healthy,
  • unhealthy,
  • weak,
  • strong,
  • male,
  • female,
  • tall,
  • short.

It doesn't alter what the soul is.

He jokes about someone being proud of being six feet tall.

And then jokes with someone spending too much time working on his six-pack:

It's just the sheath!

Gurudev isn't saying neglect your health.

He explicitly says staying healthy is fine.

His point is:

Don't make decorating the sheath your identity and life's obsession.

The soul is the sword. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

15. Why sword/sheath instead of water/glass?

Gurudev jokingly asks:

Couldn't Param Krupalu Dev have used:

Water and glass?

The water is different from the glass.

Simple.

But Gurudev finds beauty in the sword/sheath analogy.

There is an important distinction:

The sheath can look more impressive than the sword, while the sword is actually more valuable and useful.

That's exactly our problem.

The body is:

  • immediately visible,
  • constantly attended to,
  • decorated,
  • protected,
  • praised,
  • identified with.

The soul isn't visible to the senses.

So we assign enormous importance to the sheath and almost none to the sword.

The analogy reverses our valuation. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

16. The coconut analogy

Gurudev also recalls Saubhagbhai's coconut example.

Shake a mature coconut.

You can feel the kernel moving separately inside the shell.

Shell ≠ kernel.

Likewise:

Body ≠ soul.

The disciple is now saying:

Gurudev, what YOU explained, I am experiencing.

The separation is no longer merely conceptual. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

17. “YOU gave me the vision to see this”

And now the gratitude becomes even deeper.

The soul was always there.

The body and soul were always distinct entities.

So what exactly did Guru give?

Vision.

Gurudev calls Him:

  • giver of vision,
  • giver of the right path,
  • giver of true religion,
  • giver of Self-awareness.

Without Atmadrishti, the soul cannot be experienced through ordinary sensory perception.

Therefore the disciple feels:

“YOU opened my eyes.” Pasted text(20261107-182646)

18. “My eyes were blinded by ignorance”

Gurudev recalls a prayer he chants on Gurupurnima:

The disciple's condition before Guru:

Darkness of ignorance.

He genuinely believed:

“I am the body.”

Not occasionally.

So deeply that Gurudev says he couldn't even feel himself as soul in his dreams.

Then Guru:

  • cleared the vision,
  • removed ignorance,
  • gave spiritual sight.

So the disciple bows:

“For this, O Gurudev, I offer my salutations.” Pasted text(20261107-182646)

19. The body can be sick while “I” remain blissful

This becomes one of the strongest consequences of the sword/sheath distinction.

Gurudev says:

The sheath is worn out, the body is sick, and I'm enjoying the bliss of the Soul.

Why is this possible?

Because:

Body's condition ≠ soul's condition.

If:

I = body

then:

sick body → “I am miserable.”

But when:

I = soul

the body may undergo its own condition while consciousness abides in its own pure nature.

This doesn't mean physical pain doesn't occur. Gurudev's point is about the fundamental locus of identity and bliss. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

20. “YOU made me the master of my bliss”

This is beautiful.

Before Self-realisation:

I was a slave of sensory pleasure.

My happiness depended on:

  • what I received,
  • what someone said,
  • what I saw,
  • what I ate,
  • what I experienced externally.

External world gives pleasure?

Happy.

External world takes it away?

Miserable.

So I'm a slave.

But Guru revealed the inner treasure.

Now the disciple says:

“YOU made me the master of my bliss.”

Why master?

Because bliss is no longer something I must beg from the external world.

It is intrinsic to the Self. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

Gurudev now names what Guru has ultimately bestowed:

Samyak Darshan — Right Faith / Self-realisation.

Like Kalpavriksha

Like nectar

Rejuvenating.

Gurudev jokingly calls Samyak Darshan a spiritual spa 😂:

Samyak Darshan is:

This is why the disciple calls Guru's favour:

Amaap — immeasurable. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

The deepest practical takeaway

There are really two things Gurudev wants to “match” within us at the end of this satsang.

The first is the disciple's Self-awareness:

Whatever role I am playing, can the fragrance of “I am the soul” remain somewhere within?

Not just during satsang.

While studying.

While doing seva.

While eating.

While talking.

While succeeding.

While someone praises me.

While someone criticizes me.

While the body looks good.

While the body doesn't.

While circumstances go my way.

While they don't.

The sheath keeps changing.

The sword remains distinct.

And the second thing Gurudev wants to match is the disciple's bhakti.

Because strangely, the disciple's realization of:

“I am the soul”

doesn't make him think:

“Now I don't need Guru.”

“Now I finally understand what YOU have done for me.”

So deeper Atma-bhan creates deeper Guru-upkar-bhan.

gratitude → humility → surrender → seva.

That's why Gurudev's final prayer is so beautiful. It isn't:

It's essentially:

Let me become the disciple HE wants me to become.

Or in Gurudev's final words:

May I very soon fulfil the dream my Gurudev has seen for me. Pasted text(20261107-182646)

That feels like the perfect conclusion to this whole sequence of satsangs.
