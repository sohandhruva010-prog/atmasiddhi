Verse 26

Romanized text Pratyakṣa Sadguru yogmā, varte dṛṣṭi vimukh;
asadgurune dṛaḍh kare, nij mānarthe mukhya.

Plain sense Even in the living presence of a true Sadguru, the bigot turns his view away and argues within. Then he fastens himself to an untrue teacher—mainly to feed his pride.

Padārth (word-by-word)
  • Pratyakṣa Sadguru = a living, present Enlightened Mentor
  • Yogmā = in association with, in His very presence/company
  • Varte = he behaves/acts (his inner stance)
  • Dṛṣṭi = view/opinion/way of seeing
  • Vimukh = turned away/opposed (perverse/contrary attitude)
  • Asadgurune = towards an untrue teacher (charlatan/ignorant “guru”)
  • Dṛaḍh kare = he strengthens/locks in faith and allegiance
  • Nij mānarthe = for his own pride/vanity
  • Mukhya = chiefly, mainly (this is the main driver)
Where this verse sits (the link from 24→25→26→27–28)
  • 24: Bigot’s wrong view of Guru (picks based on external renunciation or family/sect tradition).
  • 25: Bigot’s wrong view of God (focuses on externals—body, Samavasaran, glories).
  • 26: Back to Guru—now the sharper case: even with a true living Guru present, the bigot stays wrong because of ego.
  • 27–28: Then come the verses on Religion.

Why return to Guru? Because verse 24 only covered having a wrong guru. Verse 26 covers the more incisive failure: having the right Guru and still remaining a bigot.

The core mistake, step by step

1. He meets a living Sadguru (by past merit, interest in satsang, etc.).

2. Honeymoon phase: he enjoys the talks when nothing pinches his ego.

3. Reality phase: the Sadguru begins correcting his wrong notions—about religion, vows, seva, charity, austerity, work styles, opinions, self-willed behavior.

4. “Dṛṣṭi vimukh” awakens: instead of reflecting, he argues within, doubts, uses wrong logic, and mentally opposes the Guru.

5. Karmic fallout: this egoic opposition to Truth activates heavy karmic bondage (the inner quarrel against the Enlightened becomes a spiritual loss).

6. Flight to comfort: he leaves the discomfort of correction and re-attaches to an untrue teacher, where his pride is praised and pampered.

7. “Nij mānarthe mukhya”: the main motive is ego-preservation—respect, spotlight, first-row treatment, donor perks, applause.

8. Net result: tragic loss—he had a Sadguru and still regressed.

Every example he gives—and what it’s teaching A. “Dṛṣṭi vimukh” in real life
  • Inner dialogue: “I don’t agree with Him… He’s wrong… This isn’t practical.” → Teaching: When correction begins, ego argues, not to find truth, but to defend itself. “Not practical” becomes a cover for “Not pleasing to my pride.”
B. Honeymoon analogy
  • At first: sweet, inspiring satsang.
  • Then: the personal satsang—He looks you in the eye and says, “You’re wrong here.” → Teaching: True satsang must pierce the ego. If the relationship never pinches, it isn’t transformative.
C. Anger’s timing
  • Why does anger come now? It didn’t arise when He praised you. It flares right after a reprimand. → Teaching: This shows the root is ego, not the event. If it were the event, anger would appear earlier too.
D. Abhyākhyān (false accusation) warning
  • Making allegations against the Enlightened (abhyākhyān) is a serious sin among the 18 major sins. → Teaching: When ego insists “I’m right, He’s wrong,” bondage deepens—because the opposition is against Truth itself.
E. “Slap” scale analogy
  • Slapping a common person vs. a leader vs. an Enlightened One—the graver the purity you oppose, the heavier the bondage. → Teaching: Turning your view against a Sadguru is spiritually costly.
F. Pot-upside-down in rain
  • Plenty of pure rain, but the pot is inverted—no benefit. → Teaching: In the presence of Grace, unworthiness/ego prevents reception.
G. Human psychology: why the bigot prefers the untrue teacher
  • At the Sadguru’s feet: correction, no flattery; truth first.
  • At the charlatan’s dais: praise, first row, donor benefits, “You’re great.” → Teaching: Ego seeks environments that feed it. Hence dṛaḍh kare—he doubles down there.
H. “Same state” magnet
  • He and the untrue teacher share the same state: both ignorant, both ego-hungry; so they get along. → Teaching: We’re drawn to spaces that mirror our inner state.
I. “Never get along with your Guru” (paradox line)
  • If you effortlessly get along, either the “guru” is untrue, or you’re very near Self-realisation. → Teaching: A true Guru disrupts your delusion—friction is normal until you become inwardly aligned.
J. Salesmanship vs. sanctity (carried forward tone)
  • The untrue teacher uses stagecraft and sweetness to retain donors/volunteers; he won’t correct you if it risks losing your services. → Teaching: Comfort is cheap; truth is costly—only a Sadguru pays that price on your behalf.
The psychology behind “dṛṣṭi vimukh”
  • Arrogance as a disease: “I am right; my viewpoint is final.” This leaves no room for understanding.
  • Ego as a veil: it blocks reality, so you can’t even see that you’re wrong.
  • Boxed prejudice: the mind gets packed in prior beliefs; it won’t consider another angle.
  • Goal confusion: he wants importance, not liberation. Hence “nij mānarthe”—the main motive is me.
Short meaning you can memorise

Even with a living Sadguru, the bigot keeps a contrary inner stance. Stung by correction, he turns away and cements himself to an untrue teacher—chiefly to nourish his pride.

Symptoms checklist (to catch Verse-26 drift in yourself)
  • Do I argue inside as soon as I’m corrected?
  • Do I label spiritual guidance “impractical” when it pinches my ego?
  • Do I seek praise-heavy spaces and avoid places that demand introspection?
  • Do I feel relief with flatterers and resistance with truth-tellers?
  • After satsang, do I leave with resolve to change, or with a case file against what was said?
Correctives (how to turn Verse 26 into growth)

1. Instant pause when corrected: “My ego is flaring. I will not decide while burning.”

2. Switch from ‘prove’ to ‘learn’: “What is He seeing that I don’t? Show me my blind spot.”

3. One-line prayer: “Crush my vanity; keep me under truth.”

4. Seek substance over spotlight: choose tasks/roles where no one notices you—watch how your mind reacts.

5. Sankalp after satsang: write one specific change you’ll practice today (not tomorrow).

6. Company audit: reduce time in rooms that inflate you; increase time where virtue is demanded.

One-page recap
  • Verse: Pratyakṣa Sadguru yogmā…
  • Error: in front of a living Sadguru, he keeps a contrary view; then tightens to an untrue teacher.
  • Driver: ego/pride (nij mānarthe mukhya).
  • Examples: honeymoon → correction → anger; abhyākhyān risk; pot upside down;
    first row & donor perks; same-state magnet; “never get along with your Guru” unless you’re near realisation.
  • Result: not just no profit—huge loss (bondage against Truth).
  • Remedy: accept correction, prize inner change over praise, stay where truth costs you.
